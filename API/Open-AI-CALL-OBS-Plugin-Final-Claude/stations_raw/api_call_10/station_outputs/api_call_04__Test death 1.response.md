God can be proven only when the deepest rule of the universe meets someone it has no right to bind. Every created thing is under some law—matter under physics, bodies under decay, living things under death. A simple broken rule does not prove God, because a demon, a magician, or an advanced intelligence could break a rule. The exception must be *rightful*. It must look like the Law recognizing its own Author, not like cheating or hacking the system.

Death is the final law of a fallen, broken world. Death has jurisdiction—legal authority—over everything that belongs to that broken order. Sin is what gives death that jurisdiction. If there is no sin, death has no rightful claim. So the real proof is not that Jesus came back from the dead. The stronger proof is that death had no rightful authority over Him, and reality was forced to release Him. This turns resurrection from a mere supernatural event into a structural verdict—a judgment from the system itself.

A miracle can look like power, but power alone does not prove God. Power could be evil, alien, or deceptive. Rightful authority is different. If someone enters death innocently, bears it voluntarily, and death cannot keep Him because it has no claim on Him, then the event is not just force. It is justice. Reality itself says: *This one does not belong here.* That proves moral authority over the final law.

The shortest version is this: God is proven when the final law of the fallen world is forced to confess that one man is above it. Death examines Christ, finds no rightful claim, and loses. That is not just God showing off power. That is the system's deepest constraint testifying against itself. A true proof of God is not a rule being broken. It is the deepest rule meeting the one person it had no right to bind.
