# Peer Review: "It from Bit from Logos"

## A. CITATION QUALITY

**A1: 1 — Source Existence & Verifiability** | Comment: The paper cites no academic sources whatsoever. Wheeler's "it from bit" is mentioned but not cited to any specific publication. Biblical passages are quoted but not cited to any scholarly edition. No references, bibliography, or footnotes exist.

**A2: 1 — Source Authority & Relevance** | Comment: The only identifiable sources are Biblical texts (Matthew, John, Genesis, Proverbs) and an unnamed "Math Is Moral" paper from the same author's framework. No peer-reviewed physics, information theory, or philosophy sources are engaged.

**A3: 0 — Citation Formatting** | Comment: There is no citation formatting of any kind. No author-date, footnote, or numbered reference system is employed. Biblical citations are given as book-chapter-verse but without edition or translation information.

**A4: 0 — Citation Density & Distribution** | Comment: The paper contains zero citations per page. No academic literature is referenced anywhere in the document. The "Related Work" section lists only "Core article, supporting evidence, and broader context" with no actual references.

## B. FACTUAL ACCURACY

**B1: 2 — Empirical Claim Accuracy** | Comment: The paper makes empirical-sounding claims about thermodynamics, entropy, and information theory (e.g., "the Shannon formula is already running on it") without any empirical support. Claims about "thermodynamically unstable" false bits have no basis in established physics.

**B2: 1 — Historical/Contextual Accuracy** | Comment: Wheeler's "it from bit" is presented without historical context of its development, its critics, or its status within physics. The paper treats it as established fact rather than a speculative philosophical position.

**B3: 2 — Mathematical/Formal Accuracy** | Comment: The paper invokes Noether's theorem, Shannon entropy, and thermodynamic concepts but provides no mathematical formalism. Terms like "holographic boundary encoding" and "N_adversarial" are introduced without definition or mathematical specification.

**B4: 1 — Claim-Evidence Alignment** | Comment: Grand claims about "what survives entropy" are supported only by Biblical quotations and assertions about the author's own framework. No evidence is presented that could distinguish these claims from arbitrary theological speculation.

## C. LOGICAL RIGOR

**C1: 3 — Deductive Validity** | Comment: The paper attempts deductive chains (Wheeler → framework → consequence) but the premises are unestablished. The argument that "truth is invariant" and "invariance survives entropy" requires premises about the nature of truth and entropy that are neither defined nor defended.

**C2: 2 — Inductive Strength** | Comment: No inductive evidence is presented. The paper makes universal claims about all truth, all information, and all of reality based on no empirical observations.

**C3: 2 — Absence of Fallacies** | Comment: Multiple fallacies are present: equivocation on "invariance" (mathematical vs. theological), false analogy (thermodynamics of information vs. moral truth), and argument from authority (Biblical texts treated as formal proofs).

**C4: 2 — Argument Chain Integrity** | Comment: The argument chain from "it from bit" to "bit from Logos" to "what survives is what's true" contains multiple leaps without intermediate justification. Each step assumes what needs to be proven.

## D. EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY

**D1: 1 — Claim Strength vs Evidence Weight** | Comment: The paper makes claims of cosmic significance (what survives eternity, the nature of reality) with zero evidence. The ratio of claim strength to evidence weight is maximally imbalanced.

**D2: 0 — Counter-Evidence Engagement** | Comment: No counter-evidence, alternative explanations, or objections are considered. The paper does not acknowledge that Wheeler's framework is controversial, that information-theoretic approaches to physics face serious challenges, or that theological claims require different evidentiary standards.

**D3: 0 — Replication & Corroboration** | Comment: No replication or corroboration is cited. The paper relies entirely on the author's own unpublished framework ("The Convergence Series," "Math Is Moral" paper).

**D4: 0 — Statistical Rigor** | Comment: No statistical methods, data, or quantitative analysis are present. Claims about "thermodynamic pressure" and "entropic pressure of truth" are metaphorical, not statistical.

## E. SEMANTIC PRECISION

**E1: 2 — Term Definition Consistency** | Comment: Key terms ("Logos," "invariant," "alignment," "sign of the bit") are used repeatedly but never formally defined. "Invariant" shifts between mathematical invariance, moral constancy, and theological eternality without distinction.

**E2: 1 — Equivocation Detection** | Comment: The paper systematically equivocates on "truth" (mathematical truth, moral truth, propositional truth, theological truth), "invariance" (Noether invariance, logical invariance, moral constancy), and "entropy" (thermodynamic entropy, information entropy, metaphorical decay).

**E3: 2 — Domain Translation Fidelity** | Comment: Concepts from physics (Noether's theorem, Shannon entropy, thermodynamics) are translated into theological claims without justification that the translation preserves meaning. The paper assumes without argument that physical conservation laws map onto moral properties.

**E4: 3 — Hedging Calibration** | Comment: The paper uses no hedging language. Claims are presented as certain ("The thermodynamics are exact about this," "This is the formal statement of what Jesus meant"). No epistemic modesty is displayed.

## 1. THREE Most Critical Failures

**Failure 1: Complete absence of academic citation and engagement with existing literature.** The paper makes claims about physics, information theory, and philosophy without citing a single academic source. Wheeler's "it from bit" is mentioned but not cited to any publication. Noether's theorem is invoked without reference. The paper cannot be evaluated as scholarship because it does not situate itself within any scholarly conversation.

**Failure 2: Systematic equivocation on key terms.** "Invariance" is used to mean mathematical invariance (Noether's theorem), logical constancy, moral absoluteness, and theological eternality without distinguishing these meanings. "Entropy" shifts between thermodynamic entropy, information entropy, and metaphorical decay. "Truth" conflates mathematical truth, propositional truth, moral truth, and theological truth. The paper's central arguments depend on these equivocations.

**Failure 3: No falsifiability or evidentiary standards.** The paper makes claims about the nature of all reality, the survival of truth through eternity, and the thermodynamic properties of moral alignment, but provides no way to test, verify, or falsify any of these claims. No evidence is presented that could distinguish the framework from arbitrary speculation. The paper does not acknowledge this as a limitation.
