**Thesis:** The long-standing gap between science and religion is not a natural divide but a structural contradiction caused by science using borrowed theological assumptions while refusing to acknowledge them.

**Method:** The document performs a "bilateral audit" that holds both scientific and theological claims to the same standards of evidence, falsifiability (the ability to be proven wrong), and explanatory power.

**Key Finding:** When audited honestly, science leaks at six critical points—including consciousness, mathematical effectiveness, and moral obligation—while theology holds on predictive claims and moral dynamics.

**Significance:** This work offers the first formal method to test both science and religion by the same rules, potentially transforming how we understand their relationship.

---

The document argues that science currently owns about 80% of public discourse—logic, medicine, technology, and the definition of knowledge itself—while religion is confined to 20%: Sunday mornings, private morality, and personal comfort. But this arrangement is built on a hidden problem. Before science can run any experiment, it must borrow ten assumptions it cannot prove with its own methods: that the external world exists, that logic works, that human minds can reliably understand reality, and that truth matters. These assumptions describe a rational, ordered universe—exactly the kind of universe theology has described for thousands of years. Science borrowed this foundation and then used it to push theology out of bounds.

The bilateral audit then runs the test in both directions. When science is held to its own standards, it fails to explain why consciousness exists at all, why abstract mathematics describes physical reality with absurd precision, why we feel genuine moral obligation, where the information in DNA comes from, why the universe's physical constants are fine-tuned to one part in 10¹²⁰ (a number so large it has 120 zeros), and why the universe began in an impossibly ordered state. Meanwhile, theology holds up: fifteen specific biblical predictions about the physical world were written thousands of years before science could verify them, and five independent historical datasets confirm that societies decay when moral constraints are removed.

The document's greatest strength is its honesty about method. It names six specific places where science fails its own test, provides a formal verification system using Lean 4 (a computer proof-checker) with 942 lines of code and zero unproven gaps, and publicly lists the conditions that would falsify its own claims. This transparency is rare in science-religion dialogue.

However, the document makes several claims without sufficient evidence. The "coherence equation" derived from thermodynamics and population dynamics is presented as independently confirmed across three fields, but the document does not show the actual mathematical derivations or the raw data from the five historical datasets. The claim that seventy independent domains surface the same structure is stated but not demonstrated—no list of domains, no methodology for counting them, no explanation of how "independence" was verified. The document also does not address alternative explanations for its findings, such as confirmation bias (the tendency to notice evidence that supports what you already believe) or the possibility that the method was designed to produce the results it found.
