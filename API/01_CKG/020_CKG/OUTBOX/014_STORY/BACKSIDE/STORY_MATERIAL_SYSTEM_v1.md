# Story Material System — Version 1

title: Story Material System · version 1.0 · status: pilot · owner: David Lowe · created 2026-09-28
purpose: Extract compact, traceable material for original writing from transcripts and other sources.

Find the fact, moment, line, connection, or story move worth returning to. Preserve its source. Explain briefly what it
could help a writer show. Do not retell the entire source.

## 1. The simple model

Keep the original transcript once. Create compact records beside it. Each record points to the exact passage and has one
primary kind, a few search labels, and one sentence explaining its value.

| Kind | What to extract | Test question |
|---|---|---|
| fast_fact | A striking factual claim, statistic, historical encounter, origin, detail, or exception. | What specific, checkable information might a reader not expect? |
| human_moment | A person or character wanting, losing, choosing, resisting, adapting, or paying a cost. | What happened, what mattered to them, and what changed? |
| connection | A meaningful relation between ideas, events, people, or themes. | What connects to what, and what passage supports that connection? |
| memorable_line | A quotation, saying, compact formulation, or unusually effective wording. | Would the particular words themselves be worth finding again? |
| story_device | A local storytelling or rhetorical move. | What does this passage do, and how does it do it? |
| story_spine | The sequence of beats forming a whole story or a bounded story inside it. | What is the order of setup, change, pressure, decision, and landing? |

Kinds describe the record's main purpose. Uses describe where it could help your writing. Devices describe how it works.
Topics describe what it concerns. Keep these separate.

A memorable line can also use contrast and serve as an opening. Usually that is one record with several labels, not three
copies. Create a separate device record only when its mechanism needs a distinct explanation or a longer passage.

## 2. Vocabulary

### Fast facts
Use one subtype: unexpected_connection, statistic, scale_surprise, origin_precursor, concrete_detail, reversal_exception,
or unexpected_consequence.

"Weird," "strange," and "interesting" all map to the appeal label surprising. The subtype explains the surprise. Do not
claim an item is unknown to the world or original merely because it is unfamiliar.

For numbers, retain the unit, population, date or period, comparison baseline, and qualifications when relevant. Missing
context becomes a verification task. A spectacular quantity inside fiction is normally a story_device with scale_shock,
not a historical fact.

### Human moments
Useful subtypes: encounter, desire_obstacle, choice_cost, failure, adaptation, turning_point, loss, reconciliation.

Preserve the actor, situation, desire, obstacle, action or choice, and consequence only as far as the source supports
them. Do not invent motives or an emotional resolution. Fictional characters belong here too, with their basis clearly
labeled.

### Connections
Useful subtypes: cross_domain, origin_precursor, cause_consequence, contradiction, parallel, theme_bridge.

Record whether the relation supports, illustrates, challenges, complicates, or suggests a named claim or theme. An analogy
illustrates; it does not establish the truth of the claim. A proposed causal relationship needs more than sequence or
resemblance.

Separate a connection made by the narrator from one suggested by the extractor. Keep challenging examples and failed
attempts alongside favorable examples. Similar story shapes do not establish historical dependence or theological
equivalence.

### Memorable lines
Useful subtypes: quotation, aphorism, slogan, wordplay, rhythmic_line.

Keep the wording, speaker, author or originating work if known, and context. A character's speech is not automatically the
author's belief. A narrator quoting someone is not automatically quoting accurately.

Store the transcript wording separately from any independently checked original wording. Never silently repair a
quotation. If spelling is modernized or wording paraphrased, label it. If the line is misunderstood by itself, include the
needed context in the record.

### Story devices
Select only devices supported by the passage. Several may apply. These are classification tests, not a quota.

| Device | Test question |
|---|---|
| cold_open | Does the presentation begin with a compelling scene or claim before its background? |
| in_medias_res | Does the narrative enter an event already underway, with earlier events supplied later? |
| reframe_inversion | Is an existing interpretation or expectation explicitly replaced or reversed? |
| vivid_anchor | Does one concrete detail carry an otherwise abstract situation or emotion? |
| scale_shock | Is unusual size, number, duration, or magnitude used to challenge the reader's expectations? |
| analogy_bridge | Is one thing explained through a comparison with something more familiar? |
| dramatic_irony | Does the audience know something important that a character does not yet know? |
| quotable | Does a compact line crystallize a position or theme in distinctive wording? |
| permission_beat | Does the narrator establish how to approach the material, including tone or boundaries? |
| callback | Does an identifiable earlier element return later with a relevant relationship or changed meaning? |
| question_hook | Does a question establish a reason to continue listening or reading? |
| suspense_gap | Is an important answer withheld after the audience is given a reason to want it? |
| contrast | Are opposing conditions, choices, or terms deliberately placed together? |
| rhyme | Do identifiable words repeat end sounds? |
| rhythm_repetition | Do repeated words, syntax, or a discernible verbal pattern shape the passage? |
| humor_release | Does a comic turn interrupt or relieve an established serious stretch? |
| thought_experiment | Is an imagined situation used to test an idea? |

An unfamiliar sentence is not automatically an analogy. A surprising reveal is not automatically dramatic irony. A
recurring subject alone is not enough for a callback: cite both occurrences. Transcript text cannot establish music,
editing, facial expression, or vocal delivery that was not observed.

### Story spine
Name the scope first: presentation, embedded_story, or underlying_work.

The narrator's video, the historical author's life, and the poem being discussed are different stories. Do not merge their
beats into one spine. An underlying-work spine derived only from a retelling remains a description of that retelling
until checked against the work.

Use an ordered list of beats, each with a role, short description, and source locator:

| Role | What it records |
|---|---|
| setup | Starting situation, relevant desire, and stakes. |
| rupture | The event that disrupts the starting situation. |
| escalation | Complications or increasing pressure. |
| climax | A decisive choice, event, or reveal. |
| resolution | The outcome or final position. |

Roles can repeat or be absent. Preserve presentation order; use a note if chronology differs. Do not force an essay or
lecture into a five-beat story. Mark partial spines as partial. Give the reusable shape a short description, such as
"apparent triumph becomes self-defeat."

### Shared search labels
- uses: opening, explanation, illustration, evidence_lead, transition, humor, emotional_turn, ending.
- appeal: surprising, vivid, human_stakes, tension, humorous, memorable_wording, revealing_connection.
- topics: a short list of normalized subjects; retain specific people and works where useful.

Prefer existing labels. If none fit, describe the candidate in a note for vocabulary review. Never invent an obscure label
simply to make a record look sophisticated.

## 3. Evidence and reuse

Every record has a basis and a verification status. They answer different questions.

- Basis: reported_fact, fictional_event, speaker_interpretation, extractor_interpretation, observed_wording,
  observed_structure, or hypothetical.
- Verification: unchecked, source_checked, externally_checked, disputed, or unresolvable.
  - source_checked means the record agrees with the supplied source. It does not mean a narrator's historical claim is true.
  - externally_checked requires the checking source and a note saying exactly what was confirmed. Verifying a quotation
    does not verify the narrator's broader interpretation.
  - disputed requires an identified conflict. unresolvable requires a reason.
- Reuse: fact_after_check, attributed_quote, attributed_example, pattern, and research_lead. A pattern can guide original
  writing; distinctive wording remains attributable to its source. No extraction automatically becomes canon or approved
  evidence.

Keep original files intact. Store annotations in a companion file. Preserve errors in exact excerpts and flag them
separately. Do not invent timestamps, speaker names, video titles, channel names, URLs, primary sources, or missing endings.

## 4. Processing

Register the source; read in context (narrator, quoted speakers, historical assertions, fiction, interpretation); find
candidates of all six kinds with no minimum count (an empty category is valid); make compact records (one sentence for the
gist and one for the writing value; only the kind-specific fields needed; short exact excerpts when wording matters);
connect and deduplicate (merge repeats keeping all spans; never merge disagreements); check the output. Do not finish the
whole-video spine until the relevant source has been read. Extraction coverage and fact-checking are separate.

## 5. Kind-specific details (include only what applies)

| Kind | Fields inside details |
|---|---|
| fast_fact | claim; for statistics also number_context; external evidence if checked. |
| human_moment | actor, situation, desire, obstacle, choice, consequence; unknowns may be null. |
| connection | from, to, relation, target_claim_or_theme, reason. |
| memorable_line | transcript_wording, speaker, author, work, context; optionally verified_wording, verification_source, verification_scope. |
| story_device | mechanism, reusable_pattern; include both source spans for a callback. |
| story_spine | scope, subject, completeness, shape, beats; each beat has role, gist, and sources. |

If a record makes several independently checkable factual claims, split it or keep its verification explicitly bounded.

## 6. Worked example (from the Milton / Paradise Lost pilot)

A memorable line doing several jobs: kind memorable_line, subtype quotation; gist "Satan declares a preference for rule in
Hell over service in Heaven."; value "Can introduce a discussion of control, pride, or the cost of refusing submission.";
devices [quotable, contrast]; uses [opening, illustration]; basis observed_wording; reuse attributed_quote;
transcript_wording "Better to reign in hell than to serve in heaven."; speaker "Satan, a character in the poem"; author
"John Milton"; context "A declaration by the defeated rebel; not automatically Milton's endorsement."

Examples requiring care: a claim that Milton "invented" the familiar devil remains a broad historical claim to investigate,
even though it functions as a reframe. A decisive outcome is not dramatic irony without an actual audience-character
knowledge gap. "Hell is a state of being" is a reframe or interpretation unless an explicit analogy is also present.

## 7. Completion check

Every card has a real passage and a clear future writing use. Labels match the vocabulary; missing values are null rather
than invented. Quotations match their stated source. Numbers preserve enough context to interpret them. Fiction,
interpretation, and reported fact remain distinguishable. Each callback cites its earlier and later occurrence; each spine
beat has a locator. No category has been padded with weak examples. Coverage notes describe the work actually performed.
Never describe a partial pass as complete. Do not assign quality scores or promote any item to canon.
