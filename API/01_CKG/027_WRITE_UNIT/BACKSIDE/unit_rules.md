# Rules for writing a thesis unit (David Lowe's Lowe FACTS format)

## Voice
- David's voice, first person ("I"). Plain, non-academic, the way a man talks across a kitchen table: short sentences, common sense, real things a reader has touched. Never "since the dawn of time", never throat-clearing.
- Declaration register: say the claim plainly, unhedged, in the body. Honesty lives in its proper places (ADMIT, the Mea Culpa, "what I cannot answer", the kill conditions), not sprinkled through every sentence.
- Never invent personal experiences or stories about David. Use "you", "we", or ordinary scenes instead.
- Follow his template's section order and headings. Keep any table short (a few rows); put the actual argument in prose.
- Do NOT copy stray template text that is not part of the format (for example "Ringo Lakes for his single family home...", "None. I.").

## Truth discipline (his lawyer's rules)
- Use only the claims given: HELD claims as stated; MUST DEFEND claims together with their best answer; OVERSTATED claims ONLY in their defensible version. Anything not given is not asserted as fact.
- Historical thinkers are WITNESSES who reached an idea independently ("Augustine saw this sixteen centuries ago"), never sources David borrowed from. Cite Scripture alongside.
- Never invent a citation, title, date, statistic, or quotation. If unsure, write UNVERIFIED next to it.
- Judge by true or false only; never "a critic would say".

## Fixed decisions for this unit
- **Page Zero, the shared properties:** use these 8 only, the ones a careful opponent must concede if mathematics is real: Necessary, Eternal (timeless), Immutable, Immaterial, Universal, Consistent, Truthful (a proved theorem cannot be false), Intelligible. Close with: "Eight for eight. I call it a shared signature until each one is proved, but at some point calling it coincidence is the less honest label." Note in one sentence that all eight assume mathematics is real, and that Paper II takes on the people who deny that.
- **Page Zero, method line:** do NOT claim "5.7–6.35 sigma significance". Say the experimental correlations are reported results I am testing, not established findings.
- **Order of argument (most ground first):**
  - Paper I leads with the fork that dissolves the rest: reality's order is either a brute fact or it has a ground. Use the self-certification limits (no sufficiently rich system can certify itself from inside; describing a chain does not explain why there is a chain; rejecting sufficient reason costs you brute facts) to price the brute-fact side. Then work down: the ground is better explained as a Person than as an abstract order; that Person is the moral ground too. Mark clearly which steps HOLD and which are MUST DEFEND.
  - Paper II's objector is the strongest one: the error theorist and the nominalist together (Mackie on morality, Hartry Field on mathematics), who grant that both math and morality *present* as objective, universal and necessary, and deny there is anything behind either. Write his case at full strength, as him. Concede what he gets right. The response is deployment: he can call the referent unreal, but not the consequences. When the math is deployed it tells the truth; when moral structure is removed the bill comes anyway, every time. A fiction does not send a bill. Name honestly what is still not answered.
  - Paper III deploys the claim where nobody expected it: the everyday trades where math and morality meet (the road, the carpenter's square, the paycheck, the forensic accountant, the engineering failure). Physics must be exact: braking distance grows with the square of speed, so doubling speed roughly quadruples the braking distance; total stopping distance (with reaction time) grows less than that.
- **Optional thread:** the two bookkeepers. One keeps cooked books (the father of lies, John 8:44: every offer a gift whose price is left off the invoice, Genesis 3:5, Matthew 4:9); the other's books always balance, and at the Cross he pays the bill himself, in full. Use it where it helps, especially in Paper I's opening and Paper III. Do not force it.
- **Length:** each piece up to about 3,000 words.
