## Abstract

This probe investigates whether the structural components of evolutionary theory can be mapped onto the asymmetry framework previously established for the ten canonical physical laws. Unlike prior probes, which tested whether a purely physical model could generate free-will asymmetry terms for known laws, this investigation begins with evolutionary theory—which is not among the ten canonical laws—and asks whether its components admit structural isomorphism with the framework's spiritual analogs. The probe decomposes evolutionary theory into six structural components (variation, selection, inheritance, drift, time, and the absence of teleology), tests each for mapping viability, and applies the asymmetry probe to those components that admit partial or complete mapping. Results indicate that two of six components map cleanly (inheritance and time), two map partially (variation and drift), and two fail structurally (selection and the absence of teleology). The failures are not attributable to missing asymmetry terms but to fundamental differences in solution-space topology: specifically, the presence of a global attractor at \(\chi_{\text{max}}\) in the framework versus the local, context-dependent fitness landscape of evolutionary theory. This topological divergence constitutes the framework's strongest cosmological claim and yields a testable prediction regarding the equation of state of dark energy, to be evaluated with Euclid DR1 data (October 2026).

## I. Introduction: Rationale and Methodology

### 1.1 Motivation for the Probe

The nine-term asymmetry probe previously conducted for the canonical physical laws addressed a uniform question: for each law, can the corresponding spiritual term be derived from a purely physical model without importing agent choice? The structure of inquiry was consistent across all nine cases: a known physical law was paired with a known spiritual counterpart, and the question was whether the latter could be reduced to the former plus a free-will asymmetry term.

Evolutionary theory presents a distinct case. It is not enumerated among the ten canonical laws of the Theophysics framework. The intuition that evolutionary processes may not align straightforwardly with the framework's structure is the epistemically honest starting point. This probe therefore does not presuppose a known spiritual counterpart and test whether it reduces to physics. Rather, it begins with the structure of evolutionary theory as independently established by the biological sciences and asks: (1) does this structure map onto the framework at all? (2) Where mapping is possible, what form does it take? (3) Where mapping fails, what does the failure reveal about the relationship between evolutionary theory and the framework?

### 1.2 Methodological Approach

The methodology proceeds through four stages:

1. **Decomposition:** Evolutionary theory is decomposed into its essential structural components, each characterized by a mathematical form and a functional role within the theory.
2. **Structural Mapping:** Each component is tested for isomorphism with a candidate spiritual analog, identified through structural comparison of functional roles rather than superficial similarity.
3. **Asymmetry Probe:** For components that admit partial or complete mapping, the asymmetry probe is applied: can the spiritual analog be expressed as the physical mechanism plus a free-will asymmetry term, consistent with the structure of the other nine asymmetry terms?
4. **Failure Analysis:** For components that fail to map, the nature of the failure is characterized—specifically, whether it is a parametric difference (resolvable by adding a term) or a topological difference (requiring a transformation of the solution space).

## II. Structural Decomposition of Evolutionary Theory

Evolutionary theory, as formalized in the modern synthesis and extended by contemporary population genetics, comprises six essential structural components. Table 1 presents these components with their mathematical formalizations and functional descriptions.

**Table 1: Structural Components of Evolutionary Theory**

| Component | Description | Mathematical Form | Functional Role |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Variation | Random changes to inherited traits | Mutation rate \(\mu\), stochastic process | Generates diversity in the population |
| Selection | Differential reproduction based on fitness | \(\Delta f = \frac{\text{cov}(w, z)}{\bar{w}}\) (Price equation) | Filters variation by reproductive success |
| Inheritance | Transmission of traits to offspring | Heritability \(h^2 = \frac{V_A}{V_P}\) | Preserves and propagates selected traits |
| Drift | Stochastic change from finite population size | Diffusion in allele frequency space, effective population size \(N_e\) | Introduces non-adaptive change |
| Time | Deep time for cumulative change | Generational clock \(t_g\), geological timescale | Enables incremental accumulation |
| No teleology | Absence of goal, attractor, or direction | Fitness defined locally by environment; no global fitness function | Distinguishes evolution from directed processes |

The sixth component—the absence of teleology—merits particular attention. It is not merely one feature among others but constitutes evolutionary theory's defining philosophical commitment. Evolution does not move *toward* any state; it moves *away from* reproductive failure. The phrase "toward fitness" implies a directional attractor, whereas "away from death" implies only a pressure gradient. These formulations yield identical outputs under many conditions but differ fundamentally in the topology of the solution space: the former posits a global attractor, the latter does not.

## III. Structural Mapping and Asymmetry Probe

### III.1 Component 1: Variation (Partial Mapping)

#### 3.1.1 Physical Formulation

Mutation in biological systems is a stochastic process. The mutation rate \(\mu\) is a parameter that varies across taxa and genomic regions, but the direction of mutation is undirected with respect to fitness. A mutation does not "know" whether it will be beneficial; it is a random change to the genetic program.

#### 3.1.2 Spiritual Candidate

The spiritual analog of variation is the capacity for directed change in spiritual state—choices to grow, develop, or attempt transformation. This corresponds to the soul's variation in spiritual state across the state space \(\mathcal{S}\).

#### 3.1.3 Mapping Analysis

**Where the mapping works:** Both physical variation and spiritual variation describe change from a prior state that is subsequently evaluated by a selection process. Both generate diversity in the population (genetic diversity in the biological case; diversity of spiritual states in the community case).

**Where the mapping breaks:** Physical mutation is random with respect to fitness. Spiritual variation is directed—the agent can choose which direction to vary. An agent choosing to practice forgiveness is not mutating randomly; they are executing directed variation toward a specific target state.

#### 3.1.4 Asymmetry Term

If the mapping is forced, the asymmetry term takes the form:

\[
V_{\text{agent}} \in [0, 1]
\]

where \(V_{\text{agent}} = 0\) corresponds to purely random variation (physical form) and \(V_{\text{agent}} > 0\) corresponds to directed variation (spiritual form). The structure of this term is isomorphic to the asymmetry terms identified for the other nine laws: a free-will degree of freedom added to an otherwise identical mechanism.

**Verdict:** PARTIAL MAPPING. Structure is similar; direction of variation is fundamentally different.

### III.2 Component 2: Selection (Structural Failure)

#### 3.2.1 Physical Formulation

Natural selection is differential reproduction based on fitness. The environment performs the selection; the organism has no agency in the process. Fitness is entirely local and context-dependent—there is no global fitness function. The Price equation formalizes this as:

\[
\Delta f = \frac{\text{cov}(w, z)}{\bar{w}}
\]

where \(w\) is fitness, \(z\) is the trait value, and \(\bar{w}\) is mean fitness. The covariance is computed over the local population; there is no reference to an absolute or universal fitness metric.

#### 3.2.2 Spiritual Candidate

The spiritual analog of selection is the process by which choices that increase coherence persist and strengthen, while choices that decrease coherence decay. In the framework, God and circumstance provide selection pressure toward \(\chi_{\text{max}}\), the maximum coherence state.

#### 3.2.3 Failure Analysis

The mapping fails for a structural reason that is categorically different from the asymmetry-term failures observed in the other nine laws. In the physical case, selection pressure is local and context-dependent: what is fit in one environment may be unfit in another. In the spiritual case, selection pressure is toward a specific target: \(\chi_{\text{max}}\). This constitutes a global fitness function.

The difference is not parametric but topological. Physical selection operates on a fitness landscape with no global attractor; the landscape itself changes as the environment changes. Spiritual selection operates on a landscape with a fixed global attractor at \(\chi_{\text{max}}\). This is not an asymmetry term added to an otherwise identical structure; it is a structural transformation of the solution space topology.

**Verdict:** STRUCTURAL FAILURE. Topology difference: local versus global attractor. Not resolvable by a single asymmetry term.

### III.3 Component 3: Inheritance (Complete Mapping)

#### 3.3.1 Physical Formulation

Genetic inheritance is the transmission of traits from parent to offspring through DNA. The offspring inherits the genome mechanistically; there is no choice involved. Heritability \(h^2 = V_A / V_P\) quantifies the proportion of phenotypic variance attributable to additive genetic variance.

#### 3.3.2 Spiritual Candidate

The spiritual analog of inheritance is covenant transmission: spiritual states, capacities, and orientations pass through relationship, community, and family lines. This includes the generational transmission of faith, sin patterns, and spiritual capital.

#### 3.3.3 Mapping Analysis

**Where the mapping works:** Both describe intergenerational transmission of traits with fidelity and imperfection. Both describe how the current generation inherits from the prior generation. The mathematical structure of transmission—a mapping from parent state to offspring state with some noise—is isomorphic.

**Where the mapping breaks:** Genetic inheritance is mechanistic: the offspring inherits the genome, period. Spiritual inheritance is received or rejected. A child born into a faith community can walk away; the transmission is not automatic.

#### 3.3.4 Asymmetry Term

The asymmetry term takes the form:

\[
A_{\text{cov}} \in [0, 1]
\]

where \(A_{\text{cov}} = 0\) corresponds to complete rejection of spiritual inheritance and \(A_{\text{cov}} = 1\) corresponds to full reception. This term has the same structure as the asymmetry terms for the other nine laws: an agent-choice degree of freedom modulating an otherwise physical mechanism.

**Verdict:** COMPLETE MAPPING. Same structure; asymmetry term = agent choice to accept or reject transmitted capital.

### III.4 Component 4: Drift (Partial Mapping)

#### 3.4.1 Physical Formulation

Genetic drift is the stochastic change in allele frequency due to finite population size. It is modeled as a diffusion process in allele frequency space, with variance proportional to \(1/(2N_e)\) where \(N_e\) is the effective population size. Drift is pure stochastic noise with no direction or intent.

#### 3.4.2 Spiritual Candidate

The spiritual analog of drift is providential circumstance: the "random" events of a life that shape spiritual development without being chosen. Examples include the death of a loved one, an unexpected encounter, or a loss that precipitates transformation.

#### 3.4.3 Mapping Analysis

**Where the mapping works:** Both describe non-chosen influences on development. Both can have large effects on trajectory. Both are outside the agent's direct control.

**Where the mapping breaks:** Genetic drift is genuinely random—no direction or intent. Providential circumstance may not be random at all. The framework's claim, grounded in Romans 8:28 (NRSV: "We know that all things work together for good for those who love God, who are called according to his purpose"), is that God operates in what appears random. If providence is real, drift is not pure noise but carries a signal component.

#### 3.4.4 Asymmetry Consideration

This is not an asymmetry-term dispute. It is a claim about whether apparent randomness is actually random. The physical mechanism (stochastic noise) and the spiritual mechanism (directed signal masked as noise) are categorically different at the level of underlying mechanism, even if they are observationally indistinguishable in many cases.

**Verdict:** PARTIAL MAPPING. Surface similarity; underlying mechanisms categorically different (noise versus directed signal).

### III.6 Component 6: No Teleology (Core Structural Failure)

#### 3.6.1 Physical Formulation

Evolution has no goal, no attractor, and no direction in fitness space. A complex organism is not "better" than a simple organism in any absolute sense; eyes evolved because they were useful in a particular environment, not because vision is "toward" anything. Evolution can produce whatever fitness selects for; there is no fixed direction.

#### 3.6.2 Framework Position

The framework posits an explicit global attractor: \(\chi_{\text{max}} = C\) (Christ/coherence). Every spiritual law has its resolution at \(\chi_{\text{max}}\). The direction of the spiritual analog is fixed: toward the Logos. This is not environmentally determined but structurally fixed by the framework.

#### 3.6.3 Materialist Attempt at Resolution

One might argue that attractors exist in physical systems and that \(\chi_{\text{max}}\) is simply the maximum entropy state—the natural attractor of any system tending toward equilibrium. However, the framework explicitly describes an open system (Law 5: grace provides negentropy). Maximum entropy is not the attractor of an open system.

More fundamentally, \(\chi_{\text{max}}\) is not maximum entropy but maximum coherence. Maximum entropy corresponds to maximum disorder; maximum coherence corresponds to maximum order. The attractor is toward the opposite of what physical thermodynamics predicts for a closed system.

For a materialist to derive \(\chi_{\text{max}}\) as the natural attractor, they must explain why the system's natural tendency is toward maximum coherence rather than maximum entropy. The only physical answer is that the system receives a negentropy input that drives it toward order. But then the attractor's position depends on the source of that input—and an inexhaustible, fixed-direction source is exactly what the framework identifies as the Logos.

#### 3.6.4 Failure Characterization

The materialist cannot derive a fixed global attractor toward maximum coherence without importing an inexhaustible directed source—which is the Logos by another name. This is not a parametric difference resolvable by an asymmetry term; it is a topological difference in the structure of the solution space.

**Verdict:** CORE STRUCTURAL FAILURE. The absence of teleology in evolutionary theory and the presence of teleology in the framework constitute a fundamental disagreement about solution-space topology.

## V. Implications and Findings

### 5.1 Finding 1: Evolution Does Not Require a Distinct Law

The structural components of evolutionary theory that map cleanly onto the framework (inheritance, time) are already covered by existing laws. The components that map partially are covered by the interaction of Laws 5, 6, 9, and 10. Evolution, in framework terms, is an emergent phenomenon arising from the operation of multiple laws over deep time on a biological system. No Law 11 is required.

Specifically:
- **Law 5 (Thermodynamics)** provides selection pressure: entropy eliminates low-fitness states.
- **Law 6 (Information)** provides the information-accumulation mechanism.
- **Law 9 (Weak Force)** provides the transformation structure.
- **Law 10 (Coherence)** provides the attractor: \(\chi_{\text{max}}\) is what physical evolution is pointing toward, even if the process is locally undirected.

### 5.2 Finding 2: The Teleology Failure Constitutes the Framework's Strongest Cosmological Claim

Evolutionary theory and the Theophysics framework disagree at the level of solution-space topology. Evolution asserts that the fitness landscape has no global attractor. The framework asserts that reality has a global attractor at \(\chi_{\text{max}}\).

This disagreement is empirically testable. If the chi-field cosmology is correct—if the chi-field acts as quintessence dark energy driving the universe toward a coherence-maximizing state—then the universe's trajectory is teleological at the cosmological scale, even if individual evolutionary processes appear undirected at the local scale. The Euclid DR1 test (anticipated October 2026) is partially testing this hypothesis. An evolving dark energy equation of state (\(w(z) \neq -1\)) would be consistent with a universe moving toward a fixed cosmological attractor.

### 5.3 Finding 3: The Teleology Asymmetry Differs in Kind from the Other Nine

When an asymmetry term was forced for variation (\(V_{\text{agent}}\)), it exhibited the same structure as the other nine asymmetry terms: a free-will degree of freedom added to a physically identical mechanism. This is the standard pattern.

The teleology asymmetry, however, is not of this form. It constitutes a change in the topology of the solution space—from no global attractor to a fixed global attractor at \(\chi_{\text{max}}\). This is a deeper structural transformation than any of the nine laws require.

The spiritual analog of evolution is therefore not "evolution plus one free-will term." The spiritual analog of evolution is sanctification—not a new law but the operation of all ten laws together on a soul over time.

### 5.4 Finding 4: Evolution as the Materialist's Strongest Argument

The materialist employs evolution to argue that the appearance of design is an illusion. This argument carries genuine epistemic force.

The framework's response is that the teleological attractor is not visible at the scale of individual organisms. It is a property of the coherence field operating at the cosmological scale. Biological evolution is a local sample of a globally directed process—analogous to the way a gas molecule moving randomly is a local sample of a thermodynamic process moving toward equilibrium. The individual molecule is undirected; the thermodynamic process is directed. Both descriptions are simultaneously true at their respective scales.

The framework does not deny that biological evolution is undirected at the local scale. It claims that the local process is embedded in a global process with a fixed attractor—and that attractor is what Law 10 identifies.

## VI. Verdict and Open Questions

### 6.1 Summary Verdict

**Does evolution map to the asymmetry structure?** Partially, but not cleanly. The inheritance component maps completely, with the same structure and the same asymmetry term (agent choice to accept or reject transmitted capital). The time component is already integrated into the framework. Two components fail to map because they represent structural differences, not asymmetry-term differences: selection (local versus global attractor) and the absence of teleology (the core philosophical divergence).

### 6.2 Four Conclusions

1. **Evolution is not a Law 11.** It is an emergent description of Laws 5, 6, 9, and 10 operating on a biological system over deep time. The ten laws contain evolution.

2. **The teleology failure is the sharpest point of contact** between evolutionary theory and the framework. They disagree not about a parameter but about solution-space topology. This disagreement is testable at the cosmological scale (Euclid DR1).

3. **The spiritual analog of evolution is sanctification**—the operation of all ten laws together on a soul. The asymmetry terms already in the framework (resistance, acceptance, betrayal, consent, faith threshold, will) are exactly the degrees of freedom that distinguish sanctification from mechanical evolution.

4. **Evolution is the materialist's strongest argument** because it correctly identifies that biological processes are locally undirected. The framework's response is that locally undirected processes can be globally directed if they operate inside a coherence field with a fixed attractor. Whether this is true is what the cosmological tests are measuring.

### 6.3 Open Question: Testable Prediction Regarding Spiritual Heritability

The inheritance component yielded the cleanest mapping. The asymmetry term \(A_{\text{cov}}\) represents the agent's choice to accept or reject transmitted spiritual capital.

This raises a specific, testable prediction: the transmission of spiritual capital (faith, wisdom, covenant orientation) should exhibit a different heritability structure than genetic traits, because its transmission is accepted rather than mechanically inherited. Genetic heritability \(h^2\) is determinable from variance components and is independent of offspring choice. Spiritual heritability should exhibit choice-dependent structure—with significantly higher variance in spiritual trait expression than genetic models predict, and that excess variance should correlate with the offspring's own faith engagement rather than random environmental factors.

This prediction is currently untested. It is proposed for inclusion in the open questions registry for future investigation.
