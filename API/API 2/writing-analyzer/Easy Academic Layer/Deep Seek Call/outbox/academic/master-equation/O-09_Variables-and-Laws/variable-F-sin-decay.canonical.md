## §1 The Dual Projection

### 1.1 Physical Domain: Weak Nuclear Force and Radioactive Decay

The weak nuclear force governs the process of radioactive decay, specifically beta decay, wherein a neutron transforms into a proton, an electron, and an antineutrino. This process is characterized by the exponential decay law:

\[
N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}
\]

where \(N(t)\) represents the number of undecayed nuclei at time \(t\), \(N_0\) is the initial quantity at \(t = 0\), and \(\lambda\) (units: s\(^{-1}\)) is the decay constant specific to the isotope. The half-life \(t_{1/2} = \ln(2)/\lambda\) provides a characteristic timescale for the transmutation process. The weak force thus induces transmutation—one elemental species becomes another through decay.

### 1.2 Theological Domain: Conviction and the Metanoia Mechanism

The theological correlate is articulated in 2 Corinthians 7:10 (ESV): "For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation without regret, whereas worldly grief produces death." This verse establishes a causal mechanism wherein comprehension of meaning (godly grief) induces a transformation (repentance, *metanoia*) that decomposes sin. The structural claim is that sin is not a static state but a dynamic condition that decomposes when exposed to meaning. The cure (repentance) requires the disease (sin) as its precondition, formalized as a \(1/F\) singularity: in the absence of meaning, no transformation occurs.
