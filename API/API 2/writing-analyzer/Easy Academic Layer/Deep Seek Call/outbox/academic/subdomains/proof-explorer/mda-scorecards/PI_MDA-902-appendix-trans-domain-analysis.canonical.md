# Trans-Domain Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Theophysics Framework

## Abstract

This article presents a formal trans-domain analysis examining the theological construct known as the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) through a dual-channel analytical framework integrating lexical and emotional dimensions. The investigation employs a structured comparative methodology wherein each fruit is evaluated across two distinct channels—Lexical (L6) and Emotional (L8)—with corresponding anti-fruit values serving as control parameters. The analysis yields a composite average score for each fruit, revealing differential patterns of lexical-emotional coherence. The dominant emotional state identified through fine-grained analysis is confusion (0.028), with curiosity (0.022) and realization (0.014) as secondary components. The overall Coherence score of 0.204 and a CHI Score of 0.42 indicate moderate structural integration across domains. This work constitutes a preliminary step toward establishing rigorous quantitative methodologies for theophysics research, bridging theological semantics with affective computing frameworks.

## 7. Conclusion

This trans-domain analysis demonstrates the feasibility of applying computational affective analytics to theological constructs, while also revealing significant methodological challenges. The dual-channel framework, integrating lexical and emotional dimensions, provides a structured approach to quantifying the semantic-emotional architecture of the Fruits of the Spirit. Future research should focus on (1) expanding the corpus to include multiple theological traditions, (2) refining the emotional taxonomy for theological applications, and (3) establishing formal mathematical relationships between lexical and emotional channels.
