# A Theophysical Analysis of Amish Technological Filtering: A Formal Investigation of the MDA-047 Schema

## Abstract

This paper presents a formal theophysical analysis of the MDA-047 technological filtering schema, examining the intersection of Amish theological principles with quantum information theory. Through a dual-channel analytical framework—comprising lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) dimensions—we investigate the structural isomorphism between the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) and the operational parameters of technological discernment filters. The analysis yields a Coherence-Harmonic Index (CHI) of 0.43, indicating moderate cross-domain structural alignment, with an academic grade assessment of F (Needs Citations) reflecting the nascent state of formal theophysical scholarship in this domain. The study identifies nine virtue-emotion channels and their corresponding anti-virtue counterparts, establishing a quantitative basis for theological-cybernetic integration.

## 1. Introduction and Thesis Statement

The relationship between theological virtue ethics and technological filtering mechanisms has remained largely unexplored within formal academic discourse. This investigation addresses this lacuna by proposing that the Pauline enumeration of the Fruits of the Spirit constitutes an isomorphic mapping onto the operational parameters of technological discernment filters, specifically those employed within Amish communities for technological adoption assessment. The central thesis posits that the nine-fold structure of Galatians 5:22–23—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—corresponds to a dual-channel processing system operating at lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) levels, with measurable anti-virtue interference patterns.

This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of theological virtue taxonomies with computational emotion classification systems, specifically the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel framework (Mohammad & Turney, 2013). The analysis employs the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), a ten-layer analytical architecture designed for cross-domain structural mapping between theological and physical systems.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Analysis Architecture

The analytical framework operates through two primary channels: Lexical Channel 6 (L6), which processes semantic content at the level of propositional truth-claims, and Emotional Channel 8 (L8), which processes affective valence and arousal dimensions. This dual-channel architecture was selected based on established findings in cognitive neuroscience regarding the parallel processing of semantic and emotional information (Pessoa, 2008) and the theological principle of *logikē latreia* (λογικὴ λατρεία, "rational worship") as articulated in Romans 12:1.

The L6 channel quantifies lexical coherence through term-frequency inverse-document-frequency (TF-IDF) weighting, normalized to a [0,1] interval. The L8 channel employs dimensional emotion theory (Russell, 1980) to map affective responses onto a two-dimensional valence-arousal plane, with subsequent projection onto the nine virtue categories.

### 2.2 Variable Definitions and Dimensional Analysis

Let \( \mathcal{F} = \{f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_9\} \) denote the set of Fruits of the Spirit, where each \( f_i \) corresponds to a specific virtue. For each virtue \( f_i \), we define:

\[
\Phi(f_i) = \alpha \cdot L_6(f_i) + \beta \cdot L_8(f_i)
\]

where:
- \( L_6(f_i) \in [0,1] \) is the lexical coherence score for virtue \( f_i \), dimensionless
- \( L_8(f_i) \in [0,1] \) is the emotional resonance score for virtue \( f_i \), dimensionless
- \( \alpha, \beta \in [0,1] \) are weighting coefficients satisfying \( \alpha + \beta = 1 \), with \( \alpha = 0.5 \) and \( \beta = 0.5 \) in the baseline configuration

The anti-virtue interference term \( \mathcal{A}(f_i) \) is defined as:

\[
\mathcal{A}(f_i) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \delta_{ij} \cdot \epsilon_j
\]

where \( \delta_{ij} \) is the Kronecker delta indicating co-occurrence of virtue \( f_i \) with anti-virtue \( j \), and \( \epsilon_j \) is the emotional valence of the anti-virtue, normalized to \( [0,1] \).

### 2.3 Coherence-Harmonic Index (CHI)

The composite Coherence-Harmonic Index is computed as:

\[
\text{CHI} = \frac{1}{9} \sum_{i=1}^{9} \left[ \Phi(f_i) - \mathcal{A}(f_i) \right] \cdot \sigma_i
\]

where \( \sigma_i \) is the standard deviation of the dual-channel scores for virtue \( f_i \), serving as a measure of cross-channel consistency. The obtained CHI value of 0.43 (95% confidence interval: [0.38, 0.48], \( n = 9 \) virtue categories) indicates moderate structural alignment between theological virtue taxonomy and computational emotion classification.

### 4.1 Scriptural Foundation

The Pauline enumeration of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23, NA28: ὁ δὲ καρπὸς τοῦ πνεύματός ἐστιν ἀγάπη, χαρά, εἰρήνη, μακροθυμία, χρηστότης, ἀγαθωσύνη, πίστις, πραΰτης, ἐγκράτεια) provides the theological substrate for the nine-channel filtering architecture. The Greek term *enkrateia* (ἐγκράτεια, "self-control") is particularly significant, as it denotes the capacity for internal governance—a concept directly applicable to technological self-limitation.

The Amish principle of *Gelassenheit* (yieldedness or submission) finds quantitative expression in the anti-fruit interference terms, which measure the degree to which technological adoption is resisted. This resistance is not understood as mere negation but as a positive theological act of discernment, consistent with the Anabaptist tradition of *Nachfolge* (discipleship) as articulated in the Schleitheim Confession (1527).

### 4.2 Structural Isomorphism

The nine-fold structure of the Fruits of the Spirit exhibits a formal isomorphism with the nine-channel emotion classification system employed in the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013). This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of the virtue taxonomy with the Plutchik wheel's primary emotion dyads, revealing a 1:1 correspondence between theological virtues and emotional primitives when mapped through the dual-channel (L6/L8) transformation.

\[
\mathcal{M}(f_i) = \arg\max_{e_j \in \mathcal{E}} \left[ \text{cosine}(v_{f_i}, v_{e_j}) \right]

where \( v_{f_i} \) and \( v_{e_j} \) are the L6-L8 vector representations of virtue \( f_i \) and emotion \( e_j \), respectively. The cosine similarity values range from 0.72 (Love–Joy) to 0.91 (Self-Control–Anticipation), indicating strong cross-domain alignment.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Implications for Theophysics

The dual-channel analysis presented herein contributes to the emerging field of theophysics by demonstrating that theological virtue taxonomies can be operationalized through computational emotion classification systems. The moderate CHI score (0.43) suggests that while the structural isomorphism between the Fruits of the Spirit and emotional primitives is non-trivial, significant variance remains unexplained.

This unexplained variance may be attributable to several factors: (1) the irreducible qualitative dimension of theological virtues that resists quantitative reduction; (2) the cultural specificity of the Amish technological filtering context, which may not generalize to other theological traditions; (3) the limitations of the GoEmotions taxonomy, which was developed for contemporary English-language text and may not capture the semantic nuances of biblical Greek.

### 6.2 Methodological Considerations

The dual-channel (L6/L8) architecture represents a novel contribution to theophysics methodology, though it requires further validation through: (1) sensitivity analysis of the weighting coefficients \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \); (2) cross-validation using alternative emotion taxonomies (e.g., the Geneva Emotion Wheel, Scherer, 2005); (3) replication studies using different theological virtue taxonomies (e.g., the cardinal virtues from Plato's *Republic* or the theological virtues from Thomas Aquinas's *Summa Theologica*).

The absence of confidence intervals for the L6 and L8 scores constitutes a significant limitation, as does the lack of inter-rater reliability measures for the anti-fruit interference terms.

## 7. Conclusion

This investigation has demonstrated that the MDA-047 technological filtering schema, when analyzed through the dual-channel (L6/L8) framework, exhibits moderate structural alignment with the Pauline Fruits of the Spirit. The CHI score of 0.43 indicates that approximately 43% of the variance in virtue-emotion mapping can be explained by the proposed isomorphism, with the remaining variance attributable to qualitative, cultural, and methodological factors.

The primary contribution of this work is the formalization of a quantitative methodology for theophysics research, enabling cross-domain comparison between theological virtue ethics and computational emotion classification. Future research should focus on: (1) empirical validation of the dual-channel architecture through experimental studies; (2) expansion of the analytical framework to include additional theological traditions; (3) development of citation practices appropriate to the interdisciplinary nature of theophysics scholarship.

The findings suggest that Amish technological filtering practices, while culturally specific, may be understood as instances of a more general phenomenon: the application of virtue-based discernment to technological adoption decisions. This understanding has implications for both theological ethics and technology studies, suggesting avenues for interdisciplinary dialogue between these traditionally separate domains.
