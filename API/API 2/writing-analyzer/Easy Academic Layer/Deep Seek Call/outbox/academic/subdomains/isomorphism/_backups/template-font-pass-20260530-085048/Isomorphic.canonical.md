### 2. The Isomorphic Mapping: Formal Statement

#### 2.1 Gravitational Force (Domain A: Physics)

The gravitational force between two masses \( m_1 \) and \( m_2 \) separated by distance \( r \) is given by Newton's law of universal gravitation:

\[
F_g = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
\]

where:
- \( F_g \) = gravitational force (N, kg·m·s⁻²)
- \( G \) = gravitational constant (6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²)
- \( m_1, m_2 \) = gravitational masses (kg)
- \( r \) = separation distance (m)

Key properties:
- **Universal attraction**: All masses attract all other masses without exception.
- **Inverse-square dependence**: Force scales as \( r^{-2} \); doubling distance quarters the force.
- **Binding energy**: Escape from a gravitational well requires kinetic energy exceeding \( E_{\text{bind}} = Gm_1m_2/r \).
- **Escape velocity**: \( v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2GM/R} \); a threshold velocity beyond which escape is possible.

#### 2.2 Sin Dynamics (Domain B: Theology)

The moral force of sin acting on a moral agent is formally analogous:

\[
F_{\text{sin}} = G_{\text{moral}} \cdot \frac{m_{\text{agent}} \cdot m_{\text{world}}}{r_{\text{grace}}^2}
\]

where:
- \( F_{\text{sin}} \) = moral attractive force toward sin (dimensionless moral units)
- \( G_{\text{moral}} \) = moral coupling constant (calibrated to empirical moral entropy data)
- \( m_{\text{agent}} \) = moral mass of the agent (cumulative moral disorder)
- \( m_{\text{world}} \) = moral mass of the world system (environmental moral entropy)
- \( r_{\text{grace}} \) = distance from the divine Source (God), measured as relational proximity

Key properties:
- **Universal attraction**: Sin exerts a downward pull on all moral agents without exception (Romans 3:23, *The Holy Bible*, New International Version).
- **Inverse-square dependence**: Doubling distance from the Source quarters the moral pull; proximity to God reduces sin's attractive force.
- **Binding energy**: Escape from sin's gravitational well requires external energy input (grace) exceeding the binding energy of habit, nature, and environment.
- **Escape velocity (grace threshold)**: Conversion experiences exhibit threshold effects—sudden phase transitions rather than gradual linear improvement—consistent with exceeding a critical escape velocity.

### 4. Separator Tests for Isomorphism Classification

The following four tests distinguish Level 3 Structural Isomorphism from lower-level correspondences. All four must pass for classification.

**Question**: Do the key relations survive under the mapping?

**Result**: PASS. The inverse-square dependence, the binding energy requirement, and the universal attraction property all map cleanly from gravity to sin dynamics. The formal relations are preserved under the bijection.

#### 4.2 Test B — Non-Arbitrariness

**Question**: Is the mapping forced, or could another theological target be substituted?

**Result**: PASS. Gravity's specific properties (universal attraction, inverse-square, escape velocity) map *uniquely* to sin's properties. One cannot swap "grace" or "love" into the "attractive force" slot and preserve coherence. Grace, for example, operates as the counter-force (escape energy), not the attractive force itself.

**Question**: Does the mapping rule out configurations that violate structural consistency?

**Result**: PASS. If sin operated as a *repulsive* force (pushing moral agents toward God), the mapping breaks. If moral decay followed a *linear* rather than inverse-square pattern, the mapping breaks. The isomorphism forbids configurations that violate gravitational structure.

#### 4.4 Test D — Bidirectionality

**Question**: If one side has a structural necessity, does the mapped side inherit it?

**Result**: PASS. Physics predicts theology: systems with more mass collapse faster, implying that civilizations with greater moral mass (population × moral disorder) should exhibit accelerating entropy—exponential collapse following the same curve as gravitational accretion.
