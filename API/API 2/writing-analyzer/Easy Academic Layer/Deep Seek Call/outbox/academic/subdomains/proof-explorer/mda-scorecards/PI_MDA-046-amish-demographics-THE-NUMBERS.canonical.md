# A Theophysical Analysis of Demographic Patterns in Amish Communities: A Dual-Channel Framework for Assessing Spiritual and Structural Coherence

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical analysis of demographic patterns within Amish communities, employing a dual-channel analytical framework that integrates lexical and emotional dimensions of textual data. Through the application of the Fruits of the Spirit taxonomy (Galatians 5:22–23) as a structural metric, we examine the coherence between semantic content and affective valence across nine virtue categories. The analysis yields a Coherence score of 0.227, an Academic Grade of D (Light), and a Composite Knowledge Grade (CKG) Tier of F (Needs Work), based on a corpus of 2,498 words. The dominant emotional valence, as measured by the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy, is disappointment (0.020), with approval (0.013) and realization (0.013) as secondary modalities. The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis corroborates these findings. The study identifies two principal claims within the text and characterizes the authorial posture as "confident but spiritually rotten; high-claim, low-support; precise but lifeless; deep but poorly sequenced; broad but shallow." This paper argues that the observed discrepancy between lexical precision and emotional coherence constitutes a measurable theophysical signature, indicative of a structural misalignment between propositional content and affective grounding.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of physics and theology—herein termed *theophysics*—provides a methodological framework for examining the structural properties of spiritual claims through formal analytical tools. The present study applies this framework to demographic data concerning Amish communities, specifically through the lens of the Fruits of the Spirit as delineated in Galatians 5:22–23 (Nestle-Aland 28th edition, 2012). The central thesis is that textual artifacts, when subjected to multi-layered analysis, reveal latent coherence metrics that correlate with spiritual integrity.

The analysis was conducted using the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), a ten-layer analytical system designed to assess lexical, emotional, and structural dimensions of theological texts. The pipeline generates a Composite Knowledge Grade (CKG) and a Coherence score, both of which serve as proxies for the epistemic and spiritual robustness of the claims under examination.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Data Source and Preprocessing

The corpus under analysis comprises 2,498 words of text pertaining to Amish demographic patterns, extracted from a schema dated 2026.04.07-B. The text was processed through ten analytical layers (L1–L10, with L13 as an auxiliary layer), each designed to assess distinct features including lexical density, emotional valence, and structural coherence. The pipeline identified zero contradictions within the text, indicating internal logical consistency despite low coherence scores.

### 2.2 Dual-Channel Analytical Framework

The dual-channel approach distinguishes between two primary modalities of textual analysis:

1. **Lexical Channel (L6):** Measures the frequency and distribution of terms associated with the nine Fruits of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.
2. **Emotional Channel (L8):** Assesses the affective valence of the text using the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy (27 categories) and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel.

The Fruits Comparison Table (Table 1) presents the dual-channel scores for each virtue category, along with corresponding anti-fruit scores and arithmetic means.

### 2.3 Emotional Taxonomy

Emotional analysis was conducted using two complementary systems:

- **GoEmotions (Demszky et al., 2020):** A 27-category fine-grained emotion taxonomy. The dominant emotion identified was disappointment (0.020), followed by approval (0.013) and realization (0.013). Additional emotions included disgust (0.008) and annoyance (0.002).
- **NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013):** A dimensional model mapping emotions onto eight primary categories. The NRC analysis corroborated the GoEmotions findings, though specific numerical values for the NRC top emotions are not provided in the source data.

The data reveal a marked discrepancy between lexical and emotional channels. With the exception of "peace" (L6 = 0.058), all lexical scores are zero, indicating no direct lexical reference to the Fruits of the Spirit within the text. The anti-fruit scores are uniformly low (range: 0.001–0.007), indicating minimal presence of opposing emotional valences. This suggests that the text, while lacking explicit virtue language, does not actively contradict the emotional profile of the Fruits of the Spirit.

### 3.2 Coherence and Academic Metrics

The overall Coherence score is 0.227, which falls below the threshold for academic acceptability. The Academic Grade is D (Light), corresponding to an 11th–12th grade reading level. The CKG Tier is F (Needs Work), indicating significant deficiencies in structural organization and epistemic support.

The Idea Density score is +0.397 (Fruit minus Anti), suggesting a net positive emotional valence despite the low coherence. The Vocabulary Diversity score is 90, indicating moderate lexical variation.

### 3.3 Character Profile

The pipeline generated the following character profile for the authorial voice:

> "mixed spiritual posture; confident but spiritually rotten; high-claim, low-support; precise but lifeless; deep but poorly sequenced; broad but shallow."

This profile synthesizes the observed discrepancy between lexical precision (high claim density) and emotional coherence (low support), as well as the structural disorganization (poor sequencing) relative to the breadth of claims.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Theophysical Interpretation of the Dual-Channel Discrepancy

The observed divergence between lexical and emotional channels—high L8 scores with near-zero L6 scores—constitutes a measurable theophysical signature. In the framework of theophysics, this signature indicates a text that evokes the emotional resonance of the Fruits of the Spirit without explicitly articulating them. This may be interpreted as a form of implicit spiritual coherence, wherein the affective structure of the text aligns with virtue categories even in the absence of direct lexical reference.

However, the low Coherence score (0.227) and the character profile ("high-claim, low-support") suggest that this implicit coherence is insufficient to compensate for structural deficiencies. The text may be characterized as possessing *emotional fidelity* without *lexical integrity*, a condition that theophysical analysis identifies as spiritually precarious.

### 4.2 Implications for Amish Demographic Studies

The application of theophysical analysis to demographic data raises methodological questions regarding the assessment of spiritual communities through textual artifacts. The present findings suggest that demographic texts, even when factually accurate, may exhibit low theophysical coherence if they lack explicit engagement with virtue language. This does not necessarily indicate a deficiency in the community under study, but rather a limitation in the textual representation of that community's spiritual life.

### 4.3 Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the corpus size (2,498 words) is relatively small, and the findings may not generalize to larger or more diverse textual corpora. Second, the GoEmotions and NRC taxonomies are derived from secular affective science and may not fully capture the theological nuances of the Fruits of the Spirit. Third, the pipeline's character profile is generated algorithmically and may reflect biases inherent in the training data.

## 5. Conclusion

This study demonstrates the application of a dual-channel theophysical framework to the analysis of Amish demographic texts. The results reveal a significant discrepancy between lexical and emotional channels, with high emotional coherence in the absence of explicit virtue language. The overall Coherence score of 0.227 and the CKG Tier of F indicate that the text, while emotionally resonant, lacks the structural and lexical integrity required for robust theophysical coherence. Future research should explore larger corpora and refine the analytical pipeline to better capture the interplay between lexical precision and affective grounding in theological texts.
