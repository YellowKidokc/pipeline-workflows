# Semantic Collapse in Theophysical Systems: An Analysis of MDA-024

## Abstract

This paper presents a formal analysis of semantic collapse phenomena within the framework of theophysics, examining the intersection of lexical-semantic structures and theological constructs through quantitative modeling. The investigation focuses on the MDA-024 dataset, which demonstrates a coherence score of 0.268 and a CHI score of 0.46, indicating significant structural fragmentation in the mapping between linguistic and theological domains. Through a dual-channel analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit taxonomy (Galatians 5:22–23, *Novum Testamentum Graece*, 28th ed.) and the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion classification system (Demszky et al., 2020), this study identifies a systematic collapse in semantic differentiation across nine theological virtues. The findings suggest that the current lexical representation of these virtues fails to achieve the necessary dimensional separation required for robust cross-domain mapping, with implications for both theological hermeneutics and computational semantics.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Implications for Theophysical Mapping

The systematic semantic collapse observed in this analysis poses significant challenges for theophysical mapping. The dual-channel framework, which was designed to establish robust correspondences between theological concepts and physical representations, fails to achieve the necessary dimensional separation. This failure suggests that either (a) the current lexical encoding is insufficiently granular, (b) the emotional channel lacks the resolution to capture theological distinctions, or (c) the Fruits of the Spirit, as theological constructs, resist reduction to discrete semantic categories.

### 4.2 The Goodness Anomaly

The anomalous behavior of Goodness (L6 = 0.941) warrants further investigation. This single data point suggests that the lexical channel is capable of differentiation, but only for this particular virtue. The mechanism underlying this differentiation remains unclear, though it may be related to the polysemous nature of "goodness" in both theological and secular contexts.

## 5. Conclusion

This analysis has identified a systematic semantic collapse within the MDA-024 dataset, wherein eight of nine Fruits of the Spirit become indistinguishable within the dual-channel representational space. The coherence score of 0.268 and CHI score of 0.46 indicate significant structural fragmentation, with implications for the broader project of theophysical mapping. The current findings suggest that the Fruits of the Spirit, as represented in this dataset, do not support the cross-domain mappings required for robust theophysical analysis.
