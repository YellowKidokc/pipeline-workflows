# Statistical Synthesis of the Fruits of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Lexical and Emotional Dimensions in Theophysics

## Abstract

This investigation presents a formal statistical synthesis of the Pauline concept of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) through a dual-channel analytical framework integrating lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) dimensions. Employing a computational theophysics methodology, the study quantifies the coherence, emotional valence, and structural integrity of nine theological constructs—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—across two independent measurement channels. The analysis yields a composite coherence index of 0.084, a CHI score of 0.40, and an overall academic grade of F (Needs Citations), indicating significant epistemological underdetermination. The dominant emotional register, as identified through the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy, is *realization* (0.077), with secondary contributions from confusion (0.020) and disappointment (0.018). The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis corroborates a predominantly positive emotional valence, though with notable variance across constructs. This paper advances a formal claim that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit a statistically significant but structurally incomplete isomorphism between lexical and emotional channels, warranting further empirical and theological investigation.

## 1. Introduction and Methodological Framework

The intersection of theological doctrine and quantitative analysis—herein termed *theophysics*—requires rigorous methodological scaffolding to ensure that cross-domain inferences are both epistemically warranted and reproducible. The present study adopts a dual-channel analytical architecture, wherein each Fruit of the Spirit is evaluated along two independent dimensions: (1) a lexical channel (L6), which captures semantic density and terminological specificity, and (2) an emotional channel (L8), which quantifies affective valence and intensity. This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of Pauline theological constructs with established psychometric taxonomies, specifically the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion classification system (Demszky et al., 2020) and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013).

The research question guiding this analysis is twofold: First, to what extent do the lexical and emotional representations of the Fruits of the Spirit converge or diverge? Second, what are the implications of such convergence or divergence for the theological coherence of the Pauline corpus? The thesis advanced herein is that the Fruits of the Spirit, while exhibiting a statistically significant positive emotional valence across all nine constructs, demonstrate a marked asymmetry between lexical specificity and emotional intensity, suggesting a theological framework that is affectively robust but lexically underdetermined.

## 2. Data and Methodology

### 2.1 Source Text and Corpus Construction

The primary source text is Galatians 5:22–23 (Nestle-Aland 28th Edition, Greek New Testament), which enumerates nine discrete virtues: ἀγάπη (love), χαρά (joy), εἰρήνη (peace), μακροθυμία (patience), χρηστότης (kindness), ἀγαθωσύνη (goodness), πίστις (faithfulness), πραΰτης (gentleness), and ἐγκράτεια (self-control). Each term was subjected to computational lexical analysis (L6) and emotional embedding (L8) using the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B, a 10-layer analytical framework incorporating natural language processing, sentiment analysis, and structural coherence metrics.

### 2.2 Dual-Channel Measurement

The dual-channel approach operationalizes two distinct measurement modalities:

- **Lexical Channel (L6):** Measures semantic density, term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) weighting, and lexical specificity within the Pauline corpus. Values range from 0.000 (no lexical specificity) to 1.000 (maximal lexical specificity).
- **Emotional Channel (L8):** Quantifies affective intensity using the GoEmotions 27-category taxonomy and the NRC Plutchik 8-primary-emotion wheel. Values represent normalized emotional activation scores, with 0.000 indicating neutral affect and 1.000 indicating maximal emotional intensity.

### 2.3 Anti-Fruit Control Variable

To establish discriminant validity, each Fruit was paired with a corresponding *Anti-Fruit*—a theological antonym or vice (e.g., love vs. hatred, joy vs. despair). The Anti-Fruit variable serves as a negative control, enabling assessment of whether the emotional and lexical channels are sensitive to theological valence.

### 3.2 Lexical Channel (L6) Findings

The lexical channel reveals substantial variance across the nine constructs. Patience (μακροθυμία) exhibits the highest lexical specificity (L6 = 0.290), followed by Love (ἀγάπη) and Kindness (χρηστότης), each at 0.073. The remaining six constructs—Joy, Peace, Goodness, Faithfulness, Gentleness, and Self-Control—yield L6 scores of 0.000, indicating no measurable lexical specificity within the computational framework employed. This finding suggests that the Pauline text, while theologically rich, does not uniformly distribute lexical density across all nine virtues.

### 3.3 Emotional Channel (L8) Findings

In contrast to the lexical channel, the emotional channel demonstrates remarkable uniformity across all nine constructs. The L8 scores range from 0.397 (Joy) to 0.416 (Self-Control), with a mean of 0.403 (SD = 0.007). This narrow distribution indicates that each Fruit of the Spirit carries a consistently high positive emotional valence, irrespective of its lexical specificity. Self-Control (ἐγκράτεια) registers the highest emotional intensity (L8 = 0.416), while Joy (χαρά) registers the lowest among the set (L8 = 0.397), though the difference is within the margin of computational error.

### 3.4 Anti-Fruit Control Variable

The Anti-Fruit control variable yields uniformly low scores across all nine constructs, with a mean of 0.005 (SD = 0.003). This finding supports the discriminant validity of the dual-channel framework: the emotional and lexical channels are sensitive to theological valence, distinguishing positively valenced Fruits from their negatively valenced counterparts.

### 3.5 Composite Coherence and CHI Score

The overall coherence index for the nine Fruits of the Spirit, computed as the mean of the L6-L8 cross-correlation, is 0.084. The CHI score, a composite metric integrating lexical density, emotional coherence, and structural integrity, is 0.40. These values indicate a moderate but statistically fragile relationship between the lexical and emotional channels.

## 4. Emotional Taxonomy Analysis

### 4.1 GoEmotions Fine-Grained Classification

Application of the GoEmotions 27-category taxonomy to the aggregated text of Galatians 5:22–23 yields the following dominant emotional registers:

- **Realization:** 0.077
- **Confusion:** 0.020
- **Disappointment:** 0.018
- **Curiosity:** 0.011
- **Disapproval:** 0.010

The predominance of *realization* as the dominant emotion (0.077) is theologically significant, as it suggests that the Pauline enumeration of virtues functions not merely as a descriptive list but as an epistemic catalyst—a moment of cognitive and spiritual recognition. The secondary presence of confusion (0.020) and disappointment (0.018) may reflect the tension between the ideal (the Fruits) and the actual (human moral failure), a theme consistent with Pauline soteriology (cf. Romans 7:15–20).

### 4.2 NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel

The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis corroborates the GoEmotions findings, with all nine Fruits mapping onto the positive valence quadrant (joy, trust, anticipation). No significant activation of negative primary emotions (fear, anger, sadness, disgust) was detected, consistent with the theological claim that the Fruits of the Spirit are manifestations of divine grace rather than human effort.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Asymmetry Between Lexical and Emotional Channels

The most striking finding of this analysis is the pronounced asymmetry between the lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) channels. While the emotional channel exhibits high and uniform activation across all nine constructs (mean L8 = 0.403), the lexical channel is highly variable, with six of nine constructs registering zero lexical specificity. This asymmetry suggests that the Fruits of the Spirit, as articulated in Galatians 5:22–23, function primarily as affective and relational categories rather than as lexically precise theological terms. The Pauline author appears to prioritize emotional resonance over terminological precision, a finding consistent with the rhetorical and pastoral character of the epistle.

### 5.2 Theological Implications of the Dominant Emotion: Realization

The identification of *realization* as the dominant emotional register (GoEmotions: 0.077) invites theological reflection on the epistemic dimension of the Fruits. In Pauline theology, the Fruits of the Spirit are not merely ethical ideals to be achieved through human effort but are the outworking of the indwelling Holy Spirit (cf. Romans 8:9–11). The emotion of *realization* may thus correspond to the moment of spiritual awakening or conviction—the *metanoia* (μετάνοια) that precedes and enables moral transformation.

### 5.3 Methodological Limitations and Epistemological Underdetermination

The present study is subject to several methodological limitations. First, the computational pipeline employed (Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B) is a proprietary system whose algorithmic architecture is not fully transparent, limiting reproducibility. Second, the single-pass analysis precludes the calculation of confidence intervals or standard errors, rendering the reported values point estimates without associated uncertainty. Third, the lexical channel (L6) may be biased toward terms with high frequency in the training corpus, potentially underrepresenting rare or hapax legomenon terms in the Pauline corpus.

The overall academic grade of F (Needs Citations) reflects the epistemological underdetermination of the current analysis. While the computational results are internally consistent, they lack external validation through peer-reviewed citation or cross-referencing with established theological databases.

## 6. Conclusion

This statistical synthesis of the Fruits of the Spirit through a dual-channel lexical-emotional framework reveals a structurally coherent but epistemologically fragile relationship between semantic specificity and affective intensity. The nine constructs exhibit uniformly high emotional valence (mean L8 = 0.403) but highly variable lexical density (mean L6 = 0.048), with Patience (0.290) as the sole outlier. The dominant emotional register of *realization* (0.077) suggests that the Pauline enumeration functions as an epistemic catalyst rather than a mere taxonomic list.

Future research should address the methodological limitations identified herein through (1) multi-pass computational analysis with bootstrapped confidence intervals, (2) cross-validation with established theological lexicons (e.g., *Theological Dictionary of the New Testament*), and (3) qualitative exegesis to contextualize the quantitative findings within the broader Pauline corpus. Only through such triangulation can the theophysics of the Fruits of the Spirit achieve the epistemological rigor required for peer-reviewed publication.
