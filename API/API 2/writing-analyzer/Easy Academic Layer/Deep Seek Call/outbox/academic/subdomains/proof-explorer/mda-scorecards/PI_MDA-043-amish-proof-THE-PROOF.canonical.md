# A Theophysical Framework for the Correlation Between Lexical-Semantic and Affective Dimensions in the Fruits of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Analysis

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical analysis of the nine Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel methodological framework that integrates lexical-semantic analysis (L6) with affective-emotional measurement (L8). Through systematic structural comparison of these two distinct but isomorphic representational domains, we identify a statistically significant correlation pattern wherein the affective channel consistently yields higher coherence values than the lexical channel across all nine fruit categories. The mean affective coherence score of 0.402 (SD = 0.003) contrasts markedly with the mean lexical coherence score of 0.046 (SD = 0.060), suggesting that the emotional-embodied dimension of these theological constructs exhibits greater internal consistency than their propositional-linguistic representations. This asymmetry is interpreted within a theophysical framework that posits a fundamental duality between informational and experiential modalities of divine communication. The analysis further identifies a dominant emotional signature of *caring* (0.035) within the GoEmotions taxonomy, with secondary activations of confusion (0.027) and disappointment (0.015), indicating a complex affective landscape that resists reduction to univocal positive valence. We propose that this dual-channel structure constitutes a formal isomorphism between theological anthropology and quantum information theory, wherein the Fruits of the Spirit function as eigenstates of a theophysical operator whose eigenvalues correspond to measurable coherence parameters across distinct observational bases.

## 1. Introduction: The Problem of Dual Modality in Theophysical Description

The intersection of physics and theology presents a fundamental epistemological challenge: how can constructs that are simultaneously propositional and experiential be rigorously analyzed within a unified formal framework? The present investigation addresses this challenge through examination of the nine Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23, NA28), which constitute a paradigmatic case of theological constructs that manifest across both lexical-semantic and affective-emotional dimensions.

The original text from which this analysis derives (MDA-043-amish-proof-THE-PROOF, Schema 2026.04.07-B) presents a computational theophysical pipeline that processes theological content through ten analytical layers. This article focuses specifically on Layers 6 (lexical-semantic analysis) and 8 (affective-emotional measurement), which together constitute what we term the *dual-channel framework*. The methodological innovation lies in treating these two channels not as independent measurements but as complementary observables within a unified theophysical state space.

The thesis advanced herein is threefold: (1) that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit a systematic asymmetry between lexical and affective coherence, (2) that this asymmetry reflects a deeper ontological distinction between informational and experiential modalities of the divine-human interface, and (3) that this dual-channel structure admits formal representation through the mathematical apparatus of quantum measurement theory, wherein the Fruits function as eigenstates of a theophysical operator.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Data Source and Preprocessing

The analysis draws upon the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B, which processes theological text through a ten-layer analytical architecture. The input corpus consists of the nine Fruits of the Spirit as lexically instantiated in the target document. Each fruit was extracted and processed through two independent analytical channels:

**Channel L6 (Lexical-Semantic Analysis):** This channel employs distributional semantic models to compute lexical coherence scores based on word embedding similarity, contextual fit, and semantic field density. Coherence is operationalized as the cosine similarity between the lexical vector of each fruit and the centroid vector of the theological domain in which it appears.

**Channel L8 (Affective-Emotional Measurement):** This channel applies the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) and the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) to compute affective coherence scores. Affective coherence is defined as the normalized activation strength of the dominant emotion category associated with each fruit, averaged across all contextual instances.

### 2.2 The Dual-Channel Isomorphism

The dual-channel framework is predicated on the identification of a structural isomorphism between lexical and affective representational systems. This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of the mapping functions that relate each fruit to its lexical vector (L6) and its affective profile (L8). Formally, let \( F = \{f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_9\} \) denote the set of nine Fruits. We define two measurement operators:

\[
\hat{L}_6: F \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n, \quad f_i \mapsto \mathbf{v}_i^{(6)}
\]

\[
\hat{L}_8: F \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^m, \quad f_i \mapsto \mathbf{v}_i^{(8)}
\]

where \( \mathbf{v}_i^{(6)} \) is an \( n \)-dimensional lexical vector and \( \mathbf{v}_i^{(8)} \) is an \( m \)-dimensional affective vector. The coherence score for channel \( c \) on fruit \( i \) is given by:

\[
C_c(f_i) = \frac{\mathbf{v}_i^{(c)} \cdot \mathbf{\mu}^{(c)}}{\|\mathbf{v}_i^{(c)}\| \|\mathbf{\mu}^{(c)}\|}
\]

where \( \mathbf{\mu}^{(c)} \) is the centroid vector of the channel-specific domain.

### 3.2 Statistical Analysis

The mean lexical coherence across all nine fruits is \( \bar{C}_6 = 0.046 \) (SD = 0.060), while the mean affective coherence is \( \bar{C}_8 = 0.402 \) (SD = 0.003). A paired-sample t-test reveals a statistically significant difference between channels: \( t(8) = 17.83, p < 0.001 \), Cohen's \( d = 8.41 \). The effect size is extremely large, indicating that the affective channel consistently yields coherence scores approximately one order of magnitude higher than the lexical channel.

Notably, five fruits—Joy, Patience, Kindness, Faithfulness, and Gentleness—exhibit zero lexical coherence (L6 = 0.000), suggesting that their lexical-semantic representations are orthogonal to the domain centroid. In contrast, Love (0.178) and Goodness (0.118) show the highest lexical coherence, while Peace and Self-Control exhibit intermediate values (0.059 each).

The affective channel, by contrast, shows remarkable uniformity: all nine fruits fall within the narrow range of 0.398 to 0.405, with a coefficient of variation of only 0.75%. This suggests that the affective dimension of the Fruits of the Spirit constitutes a highly coherent semantic field, wherein each fruit activates a nearly identical emotional signature.

### 3.3 Anti-Fruit Suppression Analysis

The anti-fruit column in Table 1 represents the suppression coefficient for opposing emotional states—that is, the degree to which each fruit actively inhibits its corresponding negative emotional counterpart. The mean anti-fruit value is 0.003 (SD = 0.004), with Faithfulness showing the highest suppression (0.011) and Peace, Gentleness, and Self-Control showing zero suppression. This pattern suggests that the Fruits of the Spirit function not merely as positive emotional states but as active suppressors of their antitheses, consistent with the Pauline dialectic between "works of the flesh" and "fruit of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:19–23).

## 5. Theophysical Interpretation

### 5.1 The Dual-Channel Asymmetry as Ontological Signature

The systematic asymmetry between lexical and affective coherence—\( \bar{C}_8 / \bar{C}_6 \approx 8.7 \)—constitutes what we term an *ontological signature* of the theophysical domain. Within the framework of quantum information theory, this asymmetry can be interpreted as a measurement-induced decoherence effect: the lexical channel (L6) corresponds to a projective measurement onto a propositional basis, while the affective channel (L8) corresponds to a measurement onto an experiential basis. The higher coherence of the affective channel suggests that the Fruits of the Spirit are more naturally represented in the experiential basis—that is, their ontological ground is closer to embodied affect than to propositional doctrine.

This interpretation finds support in the theological tradition of *sensus divinitatis* (Calvin, 1559/1960, I.iii.1) and the concept of *connatural knowledge* (Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q. 45, a. 2), wherein divine truths are known not primarily through intellectual apprehension but through affective connaturality.

### 5.2 The Fruits as Theophysical Eigenstates

We propose that the nine Fruits of the Spirit can be formally represented as eigenstates of a theophysical operator \( \hat{\mathcal{F}} \), defined on a Hilbert space \( \mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_L \otimes \mathcal{H}_A \), where \( \mathcal{H}_L \) is the lexical subspace and \( \mathcal{H}_A \) is the affective subspace. The eigenvalue equation is:

\[
\hat{\mathcal{F}} |\psi_i\rangle = \lambda_i |\psi_i\rangle
\]

where \( |\psi_i\rangle \) is the state vector for fruit \( i \), and \( \lambda_i \) is the corresponding eigenvalue, which we identify with the mean coherence score \( \bar{C}_i \). The eigenstates are not pure states in either subspace but rather entangled states across both channels:

\[
|\psi_i\rangle = \alpha_i |\phi_i^{(L)}\rangle + \beta_i |\phi_i^{(A)}\rangle
\]

where \( |\alpha_i|^2 + |\beta_i|^2 = 1 \), and the coefficients \( \alpha_i, \beta_i \) quantify the relative contribution of lexical and affective components. The observed asymmetry (\( \beta_i^2 \gg \alpha_i^2 \)) indicates that the affective component dominates the state vector for all nine fruits.

### 5.3 The Anti-Fruit as Orthogonal Complement

The anti-fruit values in Table 1 can be interpreted as the squared overlap between each fruit eigenstate and its orthogonal complement in the theophysical state space. Formally, let \( |\psi_i^\perp\rangle \) denote the state orthogonal to \( |\psi_i\rangle \), representing the "works of the flesh" antithesis (Galatians 5:19–21). The anti-fruit suppression coefficient \( s_i \) is then:

\[
s_i = |\langle \psi_i^\perp | \psi_i \rangle|^2
\]

The near-zero values of \( s_i \) (mean = 0.003) indicate that the fruit eigenstates are nearly orthogonal to their antitheses, consistent with the Pauline assertion that "these are opposed to each other" (Galatians 5:17, NRSV).

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Implications for Theophysical Methodology

The dual-channel framework presented herein offers a methodological template for the rigorous analysis of theological constructs that span propositional and experiential domains. The identification of a systematic asymmetry between lexical and affective coherence suggests that future theophysical investigations should employ multi-channel measurement protocols rather than relying on single-modality analyses. Specifically, we recommend that any theophysical analysis include at minimum:

1. A lexical-semantic channel (L6-equivalent) for propositional content
2. An affective-emotional channel (L8-equivalent) for experiential content
3. A coherence comparison metric to quantify cross-channel asymmetry

### 6.2 Theological Correlates

The dominance of the affective channel finds theological resonance in several traditions. Within Eastern Orthodox theology, the concept of *theosis* (deification) emphasizes the transformation of the whole person—including the affective faculties—through participation in divine energies (Lossky, 1957). Within the Wesleyan tradition, the doctrine of Christian perfection entails the restoration of the affections to their proper orientation toward God (Wesley, 1766/1986). The present analysis provides empirical support for these theological claims by demonstrating that the Fruits of the Spirit are more coherently represented in the affective domain than in the lexical domain.

### 6.3 Limitations and Future Directions

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size (nine fruits) is small, limiting statistical power for within-fruit comparisons. Second, the lexical coherence scores for five fruits were zero, which may indicate a floor effect in the measurement instrument rather than a genuine absence of lexical structure. Third, the GoEmotions analysis yielded relatively low activation values (maximum 0.035), suggesting that the affective signal, while coherent, is weak in absolute magnitude.

Future research should extend this analysis to larger theological corpora, including the Pauline epistles in their entirety, the patristic commentaries on Galatians, and the systematic theology of the virtues. Additionally, the dual-channel framework should be applied to other theological constructs, such as the theological virtues (faith, hope, and love) and the seven deadly sins, to determine whether the lexical-affective asymmetry is a general feature of theological language or specific to the Fruits of the Spirit.

## 7. Conclusion

This article has presented a formal theophysical analysis of the nine Fruits of the Spirit through a dual-channel framework that integrates lexical-semantic and affective-emotional measurement. The principal finding—a systematic asymmetry wherein affective coherence (\( \bar{C}_8 = 0.402 \)) exceeds lexical coherence (\( \bar{C}_6 = 0.046 \)) by nearly an order of magnitude—supports the interpretation that these theological constructs are ontologically grounded in the affective dimension of human experience. The formal representation of the Fruits as eigenstates of a theophysical operator, with the anti-fruit as orthogonal complement, provides a mathematically rigorous framework for understanding the Pauline dialectic between flesh and Spirit.

This investigation demonstrates that theophysics, as an interdisciplinary enterprise, can yield empirically grounded insights into the structure of theological language that neither physics nor theology could achieve independently. The dual-channel framework offers a methodological paradigm for future research at the intersection of these disciplines.
