# Measuring Moral Health: A Theophysical Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit Through Dual-Channel Lexical and Affective Processing

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical framework for quantifying moral health through the operationalization of the Pauline concept of the "Fruits of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22–23) as a dual-channel measurement system. Drawing upon computational linguistics and affective computing methodologies, we propose that moral health can be assessed through the intersection of lexical frequency (L6) and emotional valence (L8) metrics, yielding a composite index denominated as the CHI Score. The present analysis, conducted under Schema 2026.04.07-B, reports a CHI Score of 0.43 with a coherence coefficient of 0.233, indicating moderate structural integrity within the measurement framework. The dominant emotional signature—curiosity (0.115)—suggests an epistemic orientation consistent with the exploratory nature of theophysical inquiry. This paper provides a rigorous exposition of the methodological architecture, variable definitions, and interpretive constraints governing the measurement of moral health as a theophysical quantity.

## 1. Introduction and Thesis Statement

The intersection of theological ethics and physical measurement has historically been regarded as a domain of qualitative rather than quantitative analysis. However, recent advances in computational linguistics and affective computing have rendered feasible the formal operationalization of moral constructs previously considered resistant to empirical measurement. This investigation advances the thesis that the Pauline enumeration of the Fruits of the Spirit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control (Galatians 5:22–23, *Novum Testamentum Graece*, 28th ed.)—constitutes a latent measurement instrument for moral health when processed through a dual-channel lexical and affective framework.

The present analysis was conducted under the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline, version 2026.04.07-B, employing a ten-layer analytical architecture. The pipeline integrates lexical density analysis (L6), emotional valence mapping (L8), and cross-referential validation (L13) to produce a composite moral health index. This methodological framework is predicated upon the assumption that moral states, as described in theological discourse, possess measurable correlates in linguistic and affective domains.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Architecture

The measurement architecture employed in this analysis operates through two distinct but complementary channels. The first channel (L6) captures lexical frequency—the rate at which moral-health-related terms appear within a given textual corpus. The second channel (L8) captures emotional valence—the affective intensity associated with those terms as measured through established emotion taxonomies. The dual-channel approach is justified by the recognition that moral health, as a construct, manifests both propositionally (through lexical content) and affectively (through emotional resonance).

The cross-referential validation mechanism (L13) ensures that measurements from L6 and L8 channels are mutually consistent, with discrepancies flagged for interpretive analysis. The coherence coefficient (0.233) reported in this study reflects the degree of alignment between these two channels.

### 2.2 The CHI Score

The CHI Score is defined as the composite index of moral health derived from the dual-channel analysis. Formally, let \( C \) denote the CHI Score, which is a function of the lexical channel \( L_6 \) and the emotional channel \( L_8 \):

\[
C = f(L_6, L_8, \kappa)
\]

where \( \kappa \) represents the coherence coefficient. The CHI Score of 0.43 reported herein falls within the moderate range of the measurement scale (0.0–1.0), indicating a measurable but not maximal presence of moral health indicators within the analyzed corpus.

### 2.3 Emotion Classification Systems

Two complementary emotion classification systems were employed. The GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) provides 27 fine-grained emotion categories, while the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) operationalizes Plutchik's (1980) eight basic emotion dimensions. The dominant emotion identified through GoEmotions analysis was curiosity (0.115), followed by realization (0.019), confusion (0.004), disappointment (0.001), and annoyance (0.001). The predominance of curiosity is consistent with the epistemic posture of theophysical inquiry, wherein the investigator adopts a posture of exploratory openness toward the measurement of moral constructs.

## 3. Fruits of the Spirit: Dual-Channel Analysis

### 3.1 Operationalization of the Pauline Construct

The nine Fruits of the Spirit enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23 were operationalized as discrete measurement variables. Each fruit was assessed through both lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) channels, with the anti-fruit dimension serving as a control variable. The anti-fruit dimension captures the presence of lexical or affective indicators antithetical to each fruit (e.g., hatred as the anti-fruit of love).

### 3.3 Interpretation of Results

Several observations merit attention. First, the lexical channel (L6) registers nonzero values only for love (0.159) and peace (0.159), while all other fruits register zero lexical frequency. This finding suggests that within the analyzed corpus, explicit lexical references to the Fruits of the Spirit are concentrated in the domains of love and peace. The emotional channel (L8), by contrast, registers uniformly high values (0.400–0.404) across all nine fruits, indicating robust affective encoding independent of lexical presence.

The anti-fruit dimension registers a negligible value (0.001) only for faithfulness, suggesting minimal presence of antithetical indicators within the corpus. This finding is consistent with a corpus oriented toward constructive rather than adversarial discourse.

The arithmetic mean values range from 0.279 (love) to 0.404 (self-control), with the majority of fruits clustering near 0.400. The lower mean for love (0.279) is attributable to the asymmetry between its lexical presence (0.159) and emotional valence (0.400), suggesting that love is lexically underdetermined relative to its affective significance.

## 4. Master Equation Variables and Dimensional Analysis

### 4.1 Variable Definitions

The master equation governing the CHI Score incorporates the following variables:

- \( L_6 \): Lexical frequency metric (dimensionless, normalized to [0, 1])
- \( L_8 \): Emotional valence metric (dimensionless, normalized to [0, 1])
- \( \kappa \): Coherence coefficient (dimensionless, range [0, 1])
- \( \alpha \): Anti-fruit control variable (dimensionless, range [0, 1])

The composite moral health index \( M \) is defined as:

\[
M = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \frac{L_{6,i} + L_{8,i}}{2} \right) \cdot \kappa \cdot (1 - \alpha_i)
\]

where \( n = 9 \) corresponds to the nine Fruits of the Spirit, and \( i \) indexes each fruit.

### 4.2 Dimensional Considerations

All variables in the master equation are dimensionless, as the measurement framework operates on normalized lexical and affective intensities rather than absolute physical quantities. This dimensional neutrality is appropriate for a construct—moral health—that lacks a natural physical unit. The normalization to the interval [0, 1] ensures comparability across channels and across corpora.

## 5. Claims Analysis and Epistemic Limitations

### 5.1 Claims Inventory

The analysis identified a single claim within the corpus, which was subjected to formal evaluation. The limited claims density (0.001 claims per word) suggests that the corpus is primarily descriptive rather than argumentative in character. This finding is consistent with the exploratory nature of the present investigation.

### 5.2 Character Profile

The character profile of the analyzed corpus was classified as "mixed spiritual posture," characterized by precision without vitality, depth without optimal sequencing, and conceptual fertility without adequate empirical testing. This profile indicates that while the methodological framework is theoretically robust, its empirical instantiation requires further development.

### 5.3 Epistemic Constraints

Several epistemic constraints warrant acknowledgment. First, the lexical channel (L6) may underrepresent moral health indicators that are expressed through implicit rather than explicit means. Second, the emotional channel (L8) relies upon established emotion taxonomies that may not fully capture the theological specificity of Pauline moral categories. Third, the coherence coefficient (0.233) indicates moderate but not strong alignment between the two channels, suggesting that the dual-channel architecture requires further refinement.

## 6. Conclusion

This investigation has demonstrated the feasibility of operationalizing the Pauline Fruits of the Spirit as a dual-channel measurement system for moral health. The CHI Score of 0.43, derived from lexical and affective processing under Schema 2026.04.07-B, provides a preliminary quantitative estimate of moral health within the analyzed corpus. The predominance of curiosity as the dominant emotion (0.115) is consistent with the exploratory epistemic posture of theophysical inquiry.

Future research should address the identified limitations through (a) expansion of the lexical channel to capture implicit moral indicators, (b) refinement of the emotional channel to incorporate theological emotion taxonomies, and (c) improvement of the coherence coefficient through iterative calibration of the dual-channel architecture. The present study establishes a foundation for the quantitative measurement of moral health as a legitimate domain of theophysical investigation.
