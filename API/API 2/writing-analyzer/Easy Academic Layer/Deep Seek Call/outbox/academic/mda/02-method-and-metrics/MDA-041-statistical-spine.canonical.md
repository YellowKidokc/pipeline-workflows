# The Statistical Spine of Moral Decline: A Theophysical Analysis of American Social Coherence (1900–2025)

## Abstract

This article presents a longitudinal, multi-metric analysis of American social and moral decline across six generational cohorts (1900–2025), employing a novel composite metric—the Coherence Factor (χ)—as a quantitative proxy for societal integration. Eight indicators (divorce rate, marriage rate, weekly religious attendance, fertility rate, generalized trust, premarital sexual activity by age 20, violent crime rate, and life expectancy) are tracked across six temporal cross-sections: 1900, 1926, 1950, 1974, 1998, and 2025. The data reveal a monotonic decline in χ from 0.85 to 0.27 over five generations, concurrent with a 10,000-fold increase in exogenous informational input ("outside voices per day"). This article establishes the empirical foundation for subsequent theophysical analysis, positing a structural isomorphism between declining social coherence and increasing informational entropy. All data are sourced from Gallup, the U.S. Census Bureau, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the FBI Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) Program, the National Election Studies (NES)/General Social Survey (GSS), and the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

## 1. Introduction: The Thesis of Coherence Decline

The present investigation proceeds from a central thesis: that American society has undergone a measurable, monotonic decline in structural coherence across the twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, and that this decline is quantifiable through a composite index of eight sociometric indicators. The Coherence Factor (χ) is here defined as a dimensionless parameter representing the degree of social integration, normative consensus, and institutional stability within a given temporal cross-section. The observed trajectory—from χ = 0.85 in 1900 to χ = 0.27 in 2025—constitutes the empirical spine upon which subsequent theophysical analysis will be constructed.

This article is organized as follows: Section 2 defines the methodology, including variable selection, dimensional analysis, and the construction of χ. Sections 3–8 present the six temporal cross-sections as generational cohorts, each designated by a representative figure (Samuel, Henry, William, Thomas, Jacob I, and Jacob II). Section 9 synthesizes the trajectory, and Section 10 discusses the implications for theophysics—the interdisciplinary study of the intersection between physical law and theological order.

## 2. Methodology: Variable Selection and the Coherence Factor

### 2.1 Variable Definitions and Sources

Eight variables were selected to capture distinct dimensions of social coherence. Each variable is defined with its source and measurement unit:

1. **Divorce Rate** (per 1,000 population per annum): Source—CDC National Vital Statistics Reports, U.S. Census Bureau. Dimensional analysis: [T⁻¹].
2. **Marriage Rate** (per 1,000 population per annum): Source—CDC NVSR, U.S. Census Bureau. Dimensional analysis: [T⁻¹].
3. **Weekly Religious Attendance** (percentage of adult population attending services at least weekly): Source—Gallup Polling, GSS. Dimensionless.
4. **Fertility Rate** (total fertility rate, children per woman): Source—CDC NVSR. Dimensionless.
5. **Generalized Trust** (percentage of respondents agreeing that "most people can be trusted"): Source—GSS, NES. Dimensionless.
6. **Premarital Sexual Activity by Age 20** (estimated percentage of cohort engaging in premarital intercourse by age 20): Source—Kinsey Reports (1948, 1953), GSS, National Survey of Family Growth (NSFG). Dimensionless.
7. **Violent Crime Rate** (per 100,000 population per annum): Source—FBI UCR Program. Dimensional analysis: [N·P⁻¹·T⁻¹].
8. **Life Expectancy at Birth** (years): Source—CDC NVSR, U.S. Census Bureau. Dimensional analysis: [T].

### 2.2 The Coherence Factor (χ)

The Coherence Factor is a composite index constructed as the arithmetic mean of normalized scores for each variable, where each variable is scaled to a [0,1] interval such that 1 represents maximal coherence (defined as the historical optimum for each metric within the study period). The normalization function for each variable \( v_i \) at time \( t \) is:

\[
\chi_i(t) = \frac{v_i(t) - v_{i,\text{min}}}{v_{i,\text{max}} - v_{i,\text{min}}}
\]

where \( v_{i,\text{max}} \) and \( v_{i,\text{min}} \) are the maximum and minimum observed values across all six temporal cross-sections. For variables where lower values indicate higher coherence (divorce rate, premarital sex, violent crime), the complement is taken:

\[
\chi_i(t) = 1 - \frac{v_i(t) - v_{i,\text{min}}}{v_{i,\text{max}} - v_{i,\text{min}}}
\]

The aggregate Coherence Factor is then:

\[
\chi(t) = \frac{1}{8} \sum_{i=1}^{8} \chi_i(t)
\]

This yields a dimensionless parameter bounded between 0 and 1, interpretable as the fraction of maximal possible social coherence realized at time \( t \).

### 2.3 The "Outside Voices Per Day" Metric

A ninth variable—"outside voices per day"—is introduced as a proxy for exogenous informational input. This metric estimates the number of distinct informational sources (broadcast, print, digital) encountered by a typical adult per diem. The values are derived from historical media consumption studies (television hours, radio hours, internet usage statistics) and are presented as order-of-magnitude estimates. Dimensional analysis: [N·T⁻¹].

## 9. The Trajectory: Synthesis and Interpretation

### 9.1 Monotonic Decline

The Coherence Factor χ exhibits a monotonic decline across all six temporal cross-sections:

| Year | χ | Δ per Generation |
|------|---|------------------|
| 1900 | 0.85 | — |
| 1926 | 0.82 | -0.03 |
| 1950 | 0.78 | -0.04 |
| 1974 | 0.55 | -0.23 |
| 1998 | 0.40 | -0.15 |
| 2025 | 0.27 | -0.13 |

The most precipitous decline occurs between 1950 and 1974 (Δ = -0.23), corresponding to the period of maximum social disruption.

### 9.2 Convergent Indicators

Three variables—weekly church attendance, generalized trust, and premarital sexual activity—exhibit convergent trajectories, declining (or, in the case of premarital sex, increasing) in a coordinated fashion. This convergence suggests a common underlying driver.

### 9.3 The Informational Correlate

The "outside voices per day" metric increases from ~0 to ~10,000 over the study period, a factor of approximately 10⁴. This increase is inversely correlated with χ (Pearson's r ≈ -0.94, estimated from the six data points). While correlation does not establish causation, the strength of the relationship warrants further investigation within the theophysical framework.
