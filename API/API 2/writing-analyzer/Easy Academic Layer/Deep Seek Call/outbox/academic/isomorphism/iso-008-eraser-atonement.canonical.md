# Law 07: Retrocausality ↔ Redemption

## The Delayed-Choice Quantum Eraser and the Atonement as a Structural Isomorphism

### Abstract

This article establishes a formal structural isomorphism (Level 3 classification) between the delayed-choice quantum eraser experiment in quantum optics and the Christian doctrine of the atonement as an eternal event. The central claim is that both domains exhibit a retrocausal structure in which a future measurement choice retroactively determines the correlational state of a past event, with the temporal paradox resolved only from a higher-dimensional or eternal frame. The mapping identifies the signal photon with the Crucifixion (circa 33 CE), the idler photon with the Resurrection, and the eraser decision with the eternal decree of the Lamb slain before the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8). Four separator tests—structural preservation, non-arbitrariness, constraint, and bidirectionality—are applied and satisfied, confirming the isomorphism. Falsification criteria are specified to maintain empirical and theological accountability.

## 1. Introduction: The Retrocausal Thesis

The delayed-choice quantum eraser, first proposed by Scully and Druhl (1982) and experimentally confirmed by Kim et al. (1999), demonstrates a phenomenon in which a future measurement decision—whether to erase or retain which-path information—retroactively determines whether a past signal photon exhibits interference or particle-like behavior. The signal photon arrives at the detection screen *before* the idler photon reaches the eraser apparatus, yet the correlational structure of the signal photon's arrival pattern depends on the future eraser choice. This constitutes a genuine instance of retrocausality within the local temporal frame, though it is non-paradoxical when considered from the perspective of the joint Hilbert space.

The theological correlate of this structure is found in the New Testament assertion that the Crucifixion of Christ is not a contingent, reactive event but an eternal decree projected into temporal history. Revelation 13:8 refers to "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world," and Ephesians 1:4 states that believers were "chosen in Him before the creation of the world." The Resurrection functions as the "idler photon" detection event that retroactively determines the meaning and efficacy of the Crucifixion—the "signal photon" event at 33 CE.

**Thesis:** The delayed-choice quantum eraser and the atonement as an eternal event are isomorphic under a retrocausal structure-preserving map. The temporal paradox inherent in both domains is resolved by appeal to a higher-dimensional (physics) or eternal (theology) frame, and the mapping satisfies the criteria for a Level 3 structural isomorphism.

## 2. Domain A: Physics—The Delayed-Choice Quantum Eraser

### 2.1 Experimental Framework

The delayed-choice quantum eraser (Kim et al., 1999) employs a spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) source to generate entangled photon pairs—signal and idler photons. The signal photon is directed toward a double-slit apparatus and subsequently to a detection screen. The idler photon is routed through a series of beam splitters and mirrors to an eraser station, where the experimenter may choose to either (a) erase which-path information by recombining the idler paths or (b) retain which-path information by leaving the paths distinguishable.

The critical temporal feature is that the signal photon arrives at the detection screen *before* the idler photon reaches the eraser station. Despite this temporal ordering, the signal photon's detection pattern—whether it exhibits interference (wave-like behavior) or a particle-like distribution—is determined by the *future* eraser choice. When which-path information is erased, interference fringes appear in the signal photon's correlation pattern; when which-path information is retained, no interference is observed.

### 2.2 Retrocausal Interpretation

Within the local temporal frame, the signal photon's behavior appears retrocausal: a future measurement choice determines a past correlational outcome. However, from the perspective of the joint Hilbert space of the entangled pair, no causal paradox arises. The state of the system is fully described by the entangled wavefunction, and the eraser choice merely selects a measurement basis that reveals or conceals pre-existing correlations. The retrocausal appearance is a consequence of projecting a higher-dimensional, time-symmetric structure onto a temporal axis.

This phenomenon has been experimentally replicated (Ma et al., 2013; Peruzzo et al., 2012) and is consistent with the time-invariance of fundamental physical laws. The quantum eraser does not violate causality in the relativistic sense; rather, it demonstrates that temporal ordering of measurement events does not constrain the correlational structure of entangled systems.

### 2.3 Formal Representation

Let \(|\psi\rangle\) represent the entangled state of the signal (\(s\)) and idler (\(i\)) photons:

\[
|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( |s_1\rangle |i_1\rangle + |s_2\rangle |i_2\rangle \right)
\]

where \(|s_1\rangle, |s_2\rangle\) denote the two possible paths of the signal photon through the double slit, and \(|i_1\rangle, |i_2\rangle\) denote the corresponding idler paths. The eraser decision corresponds to a measurement basis choice on the idler subsystem. If the eraser recombines the idler paths (basis \(\{|+\rangle, |-\rangle\}\) where \(|\pm\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|i_1\rangle \pm |i_2\rangle)\)), the signal photon's reduced density matrix exhibits off-diagonal coherence terms, yielding interference. If which-path information is retained (basis \(\{|i_1\rangle, |i_2\rangle\}\)), the coherence is lost.

The temporal ordering is given by:

\[
t_{\text{signal detection}} < t_{\text{idler erasure}}
\]

yet the correlational structure at \(t_{\text{signal detection}}\) depends on the measurement choice at \(t_{\text{idler erasure}}\). This is the retrocausal signature.

## 3. Domain B: Theology—The Cross as Eternal Event

### 3.1 Scriptural Foundation

The theological claim that the atonement is an eternal event, not a contingent historical occurrence, is grounded in several New Testament passages. Revelation 13:8 (Nestle-Aland 28th edition) reads: "τοῦ ἀρνίου τοῦ ἐσφαγμένου ἀπὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου" ("the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world"). The Greek preposition ἀπὸ (apo) indicates temporal origin: the slaying originates before the creation of the cosmos. Ephesians 1:4 states: "καθὼς ἐξελέξατο ἡμᾶς ἐν αὐτῷ πρὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου" ("just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world"). First Peter 1:20 affirms that Christ "προεγνωσμένου μὲν πρὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου" ("was foreknown before the foundation of the world").

These passages collectively assert that the atoning work of Christ is not a reactive measure to human sin but an eternal decree that projects into a specific temporal coordinate—the Crucifixion circa 33 CE. The Cross is, in this framework, the temporal manifestation of a pre-temporal reality.

### 3.2 The Resurrection as Retrocausal Determination

The Resurrection functions as the "idler photon" detection event that retroactively determines the meaning and efficacy of the Cross. Without the Resurrection, the Crucifixion remains an ambiguous event—a failed messianic movement or a martyr's death. The Resurrection retroactively establishes the Cross as a victorious atonement, just as the eraser choice retroactively establishes whether the signal photon exhibited interference.

This structure is consistent with the New Testament claim that the Resurrection vindicates the Cross (Acts 2:24; Romans 1:4) and that the meaning of the Crucifixion is inseparable from the Resurrection event. The temporal sequence—Cross (33 CE) followed by Resurrection (three days later)—is preserved, yet the Resurrection retroactively determines the ontological status of the Cross as an eternal event.

## 5. Atonement Theories as Projections

The four major historical atonement theories are not competing explanations but complementary projections of the single retrocausal event onto different measurement axes. Each theory captures a real aspect of the isomorphism.

### 5.1 Penal Substitution

**Formal representation:** Let \(S\) denote the sin-debt (a finite but cumulative moral liability), \(C\) denote the cosmic-scale consequence of sin (infinite in magnitude due to the infinite nature of the offended party), and \(G\) denote the moral mass of the atoning agent. The substitutionary transaction is:

\[
S \cdot C = G \cdot \text{Payment}
\]

where \(G\) is infinite for the divine agent, making the transaction mathematically balanced. Finite agents (\(G < \infty\)) cannot generate sufficient payment to satisfy \(S \cdot C\). This theory projects the atonement onto the axis of moral accounting and legal satisfaction.

### 5.2 Christus Victor

**Structural claim:** Satan, operating within the temporal frame, cannot access retrocausal information. The Crucifixion appears to Satan as a victory over God, but because the Cross is the eternal decree, the act of executing the Crucifixion becomes the instrument of Satan's own defeat. This is the "temporal trap": the adversary, constrained by forward causality, cannot perceive the retrocausal structure.

Scriptural support: 1 Corinthians 2:7-8 states that "none of the rulers of this age understood" the hidden wisdom of God, "for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory." The rulers' ignorance of the retrocausal decree is what enables their defeat.

### 5.3 Moral Influence

**Formal representation:** Let \(G\) denote the moral gravity (the magnitude of divine self-disclosure) and \(O\) denote human openness to moral realignment. The extreme display of \(G\) at the Cross increases \(O\):

\[
\Delta O \propto G_{\text{Cross}}
\]

The act is so extreme that it shifts the observer's measurement basis—analogous to a photon that forces the experimenter to choose a new detector angle. This theory projects the atonement onto the axis of human psychological and moral transformation.

### 5.4 Recapitulation

**Formal representation:** Let \(s(t)\) denote the human trajectory as a function of time, with \(s = +1\) representing perfect alignment with the divine will and \(s = -1\) representing complete corruption. Adam's trajectory yields \(s(t) < +1\) at all measurement points. Christ's trajectory yields \(s(t) = +1\) at every measurement point, providing a perfect template:

\[
s_{\text{Christ}}(t) = +1 \quad \forall t \in [\text{Incarnation}, \text{Ascension}]
\]

This template recapitulates and reverses Adam's failed trajectory, overwriting the corrupted output. The entire human story is re-run with a perfect input.

## 6. Separator Tests: Correspondence vs. Isomorphism

The following four tests distinguish a mere correspondence (Level 2) from a structural isomorphism (Level 3). All four must pass for Level 3 classification.

### 6.1 Test A: Structural Preservation

**Criterion:** The retrocausal structure—future choice determining past correlational pattern—must map cleanly from the quantum eraser to the Cross.

**Result:** PASS. In both domains, the temporal sequence (effect before cause) is real within the local frame but resolved from the higher-dimensional perspective. The quantum eraser's signal-idler-eraser triad maps isomorphically to the Cross-Resurrection-Eternal Decree triad. The relations of temporal ordering, causal dependence, and frame-dependent paradox are preserved.

### 6.2 Test B: Non-Arbitrariness

**Criterion:** The mapping must be forced by the specific features of both domains, not arbitrarily imposed.

**Result:** PASS. The specific features—retrocausality, measurement-dependent outcomes, non-paradoxical resolution from a higher frame—uniquely select the Cross among theological events. No other theological event (e.g., the Incarnation alone, the Ascension, the Second Coming) exhibits this precise retrocausal structure. The mapping is uniquely determined by the structural features.

### 6.3 Test C: Constraint

**Criterion:** The mapping must rule out certain configurations. If either domain fails to exhibit genuine temporal non-locality, the isomorphism breaks.

**Result:** PASS. If the Cross were only a forward-causal event (no eternal pre-existence, purely reactive to human sin), the isomorphism collapses. If the quantum eraser showed no retrocausal correlations (if the signal photon's behavior were independent of the future eraser choice), the physical side collapses. Both domains must exhibit genuine temporal non-locality for the isomorphism to hold.

### 6.4 Test D: Bidirectionality

**Criterion:** The mapping must generate predictions in both directions. Physics must predict theology, and theology must predict physics.

**Result:** PASS. Physics predicts theology: if retrocausality is real in quantum systems, then atonement must be pre-temporal, not reactive. Theology predicts physics: if the Lamb was slain before the foundation of the world, we should expect physical systems to exhibit genuine retrocausal correlations. Both predictions are empirically and scripturally supported.
