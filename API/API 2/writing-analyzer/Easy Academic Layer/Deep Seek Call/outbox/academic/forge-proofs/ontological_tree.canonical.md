# The Ontological Decision Tree: A Formal Theophysical Argument from First Principles

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized decision-theoretic framework for evaluating competing metaphysical and physical worldviews through an exhaustive branching structure originating from the most primitive ontological question. The methodology employs four distinct question types—binary verification, ontological identification, explanatory grounding, and constraint derivation—each governed by four death conditions: self-refutation, infinite regress, empirical contradiction, and logical incoherence. The analysis demonstrates that secular physics and theophysics traverse an identical decision tree, diverging at a precisely identifiable node (Q4) concerning the grounding of structural order. From the self-grounding branch, eight necessary boundary conditions are derived, culminating in an abductive identification of the terminal observer with the concept of God as articulated within Christian theism. The framework yields twelve testable predictions and acknowledges six honest blanks requiring further formal development.

---

## 1. Introduction and Methodology

### 1.1 The Decision Tree Framework

The present analysis constructs an exhaustive ontological decision tree wherein every node enumerates all logically possible answers to a given question. Each branch is evaluated according to four death conditions:

1. **Self-Refutation (SR)**: The proposition undermines its own conditions of possibility.
2. **Infinite Regress (IR)**: The answer defers the question indefinitely without resolution.
3. **Empirical Contradiction (EC)**: The claim conflicts with observable phenomena.
4. **Logical Incoherence (LI)**: The claim contradicts previously established nodes in the tree.

Branches surviving all death conditions are designated as *explanatory terminals* (logically coherent stopping points) or *continuing branches* (requiring further interrogation). The tree bifurcates into two principal families: System A (secular physics) and System B (theophysics), which diverge at a structurally identifiable node.

### 1.2 Question Typology

Four question types govern the branching structure:

**Type 1: Binary Verification** — "Does X hold?" Maximum four branches: Yes / No / Meaningless / Illusory.

**Type 2: Ontological Identification** — "What IS X?" Requires exhaustive enumeration of philosophical categories: material, mental, informational, relational, mathematical, brute, composite.

**Type 3: Explanatory Grounding** — "What grounds X?" Branches: nothing (brute fact), external (regress risk), itself (self-grounding), illusory, necessary.

**Type 4: Constraint Derivation** — "What must X be?" Deductive inference from survivors; non-branching.

## 2. Level 0: The Primitive Question

### Q0: Does anything exist?

This question is self-justifying in the sense that the act of posing it presupposes a questioner. The Cartesian *cogito ergo sum* (Descartes, 1641) establishes the minimal epistemic foundation: the doubting subject is itself something that exists.

**Q0-A: No, nothing exists.** *Death condition: Self-Refutation.* The proposition "nothing exists" requires a denier to instantiate the claim, which constitutes something. The proposition destroys itself upon utterance.

**Q0-B: Yes, something exists.** *Continues.* This proposition is undeniable; even radical skepticism terminates here. The act of doubting is itself an existent phenomenon.

**Q0-C: The question is meaningless.** *Death condition: Self-Refutation.* Declaring meaninglessness requires a meaning-bearing entity to issue the declaration. The proposition presupposes what it denies.

**Q0-D: Existence is an illusion.** *Collapses to Q0-B.* An illusion requires something for which the illusion exists. The illusion itself exists as illusion, routing back to "something exists."

**Consensus:** Both System A (secular physics) and System B (theophysics) accept A1.1: "Something exists rather than nothing." No divergence at this level.

## 3. Level 1: The Distinction Problem

### Q1: Can that which exists be distinguished from that which does not?

Forced by Q0-B. If "something" exists, the term implies contrast with "nothing." The question concerns the reality of this distinction.

**Q1-A: No — being and non-being are indistinguishable.** *Death condition: Logical Incoherence.* Contradicts Q0-B. If existence and non-existence are indistinguishable, "something exists" has no content.

**Q1-B: Yes — the distinction between being and non-being is real.** *Continues.* This is constitutive of what "existence" means: to exist is to be distinguishable from not-existing.

**Q1-C: The distinction is real at one level but illusory at another.** *Recursion noted.* Advaita Vedanta occupies this branch: empirical distinction is real, ultimate distinction is illusory. However, stating this requires distinguishing the two levels, generating recursion. This cost is carried forward.

**Consensus:** Both systems accept A1.2: "Existence requires distinguishability." Observables in physics are defined by distinguishability (e.g., quantum state discrimination).

## 4. Level 2: The Information Fork

### Q2: What IS a distinction?

Forced by Q1-B. This constitutes the first major kill zone; six worldviews are eliminated simultaneously.

**Q2-A: A property of matter — physical objects differ.** *Circular.* Presupposes matter, which has not been established. Matter itself is a pattern of distinctions.

**Q2-B: A property of mind — observers create difference.** *Circular.* Presupposes consciousness, which has not been established.

**Q2-C: A distinction IS information — the minimum unit of difference is the bit.** *Continues.* Shannon (1948) formalized information as the reduction of uncertainty between distinguishable states. One distinction = one bit. No additional primitives required.

**Q2-D: A brute, unanalyzable primitive.** *Collapses.* A "brute distinction" carrying no informational content is indistinguishable from no distinction (violates Q1-B). If it carries content, it restates Q2-C.

**Q2-E: A mathematical relation.** *Merges with Q2-C.* Mathematical relations are informational structures; this is Q2-C in different language.

**Q2-F: A structural relation (Ontic Structural Realism; Ladyman & Ross, 2007).** *Collapses.* "Relations all the way down" requires relata. If relata are eliminated, relations without content collapse to Q2-D. If relata carry content, "structure" = "pattern of distinctions" = Q2-C.

**Consensus:** System B accepts A1.3: "Distinguishability IS information; information is ontologically primitive." System A operates in Q2-A territory, which is functionally adequate but logically circular at this foundational level.

## 5. Level 3: Order and Structure

### Q3: Does information require structure, or can it exist as pure randomness?

Forced by Q2-C. Pure noise corresponds to maximum Shannon entropy, yielding zero usable content.

**Q3-A: Information can be purely random and still ground reality.** *Death condition: Empirical Contradiction.* Random information carries no structure. A reality grounded in pure noise has no laws, no regularities, no physics — contradicting observed reality.

**Q3-B: Information must have structure — pattern, regularity, compression.** *Continues.* Both systems agree: reality has structure. The question concerns its explanation.

**Q3-C: Structure is observer-imposed, not inherent.** *Deferred.* Presupposes observers. For the information itself, it is random until observed, collapsing to Q3-A.

**Consensus:** Both systems accept A3.1: "Order is required; coherence is built into the substrate."

## 6. Level 4: The Great Fork — Grounding Structure

### Q4: What grounds the structure?

This constitutes the primary divergence point between secular physics and theophysics. Both branches are logically valid, but one terminates and one continues.

**Q4-A: Nothing — the substrate is a brute fact.** *Explanatory Terminal.* Secular physics occupies this branch. "The laws are what they are." Logically coherent; epistemically closed. No further questions are answerable. This is not a contradiction but a boundary condition.

**Q4-B: Another structure grounds it — *turtles all the way down*.** *Death condition: Infinite Regress.* Every "ground" requires a deeper ground; no explanation is ever complete.

**Q4-C: The substrate grounds itself — self-consistent and self-referencing.** *Continues.* Self-grounding: the "why" and the "what" are identical at the deepest level. The system could not fail to exist — mathematical necessity.

**Q4-D: A multiverse generates all possible substrates — anthropic selection.** *Death conditions: Unfalsifiable + Regress.* (a) No observable test distinguishes this from Q4-A. (b) The multiverse mechanism itself requires grounding. (c) Violates parsimony.

**Q4-E: The question is illegitimate — "why" has no answer.** *Collapses to Q4-A.* Saying "no answer exists" is accepting brute fact.

**Divergence:** System B accepts A2.2: "Self-grounding substrate → D2.1 Logos Field χ(x,t)." System A accepts Q4-A as an honest stopping point. Physics describes; it does not explain ground.

## 7. Level 5: Properties of the Self-Grounding Substrate

### Q5: What are the necessary properties of a self-grounding substrate?

This is a deductive (Type 4) question. Self-consistency constrains what the ground can be. These are logical constraints, not theological claims.

**Derived Properties:**

1. **Self-consistent** — A self-contradictory ground destroys itself (A2.2).
2. **Contains its own explanation** — Incomplete self-reference cannot close the loop (falls to Q4-B).
3. **Non-material** — Any specific material configuration is contingent, not necessary (G1.3).
4. **Non-temporal** — If the ground depends on time, time requires grounding, generating regress (G1.2).
5. **Maximally coherent** — Incoherent self-grounding fragments and destroys itself (A3.1, D3.1).
6. **Non-deceptive** — Deception entails maintaining P and ¬P toward subsystems, constituting internal incoherence. This follows from maximal coherence, not self-consistency alone (G2.4).
7. **Generative** — Must produce the observed world. An inert ground explains nothing (D3.3, E3.1).

**System B:** Properties match — universal, eternal, immaterial, coherent, non-deceptive, generative.

**System A:** Cannot derive these properties, having terminated at Q4-A.

## 8. Level 6: The Observer Requirement

### Q6: Does the ground require actualization — does information become actual on its own?

This addresses the measurement problem: the transition from potential to actual.

**Q6-A: Information self-actualizes — no observer needed.** *Problematic.* Contradicts quantum mechanics. If information self-actualizes, superposition should not persist.

**Q6-B: Information requires observation to become actual.** *Continues.* Participatory universe (Wheeler, 1983). Potential requires actualization; actualization requires an observer.

**Q6-C: Decoherence handles it — spontaneous collapse.** *Incomplete.* Explains the *appearance* of collapse, not the phenomenon itself. Wigner's friend paradox: where does the observer chain terminate?

**Q6-D: No potential/actual distinction — everything is always actual.** *Death condition: Empirical Contradiction.* Superposition is experimentally verified; interference patterns require it.

**System B:** Accepts A5.1: "Observer required for actualization." A5.2: "Observers participate."

**System A:** Split — Many-Worlds (Q6-A), Copenhagen (Q6-B), Decoherence (Q6-C). No consensus.

## 9. Level 7: The Terminal Observer

### Q7: Does the observer chain terminate?

The von Neumann chain: if observers are physical systems requiring observation, where does it end?

**Q7-A: No — infinite chain of observers.** *Death condition: Infinite Regress.* Same structure as Q4-B. Nothing is ever actually observed.

**Q7-B: Yes — the chain terminates in a TERMINAL OBSERVER.** *Continues.* Derived properties: always actual (never in superposition), self-grounding, infinite observational capacity (Φ = ∞), non-material. All four deviation modes equal zero.

**Q7-C: The chain loops — observers observe each other circularly.** *Collapses.* Requires a bootstrap mechanism unless always running, which routes to Q7-B.

**Q7-D: The chain dissolves — consciousness has no bottom level.** *Incomplete.* Functionalism avoids the question but does not resolve the measurement problem.

## 12. Level 10: Sign Structure

### Q10: Can a system change its own moral orientation?

Given sign structure σ = ±1, can an agent flip its own sign?

**Q10-A: Yes — self-improvement can change orientation.** *Death condition: Contradicts T3.1.* Coherence cannot self-increase in a closed system. Self-generated operations commute with the sign operator: [σ̂, Û] = 0.

**Q10-B: No — sign change requires an external operator (grace).** *Continues.* An external, non-unitary operator (Ĝ) is required. This constitutes the grace requirement (C8.2): works salvation is impossible.

**Q10-C: No moral orientation exists — the question is meaningless.** *Death condition: Logical Incoherence.* Contradicts Q1-B. Distinction is real; moral distinctions are a subclass.

### Q11: What is the asymptotic fate — where does each trajectory terminate?

Sign determines basin of attraction. Two-attractor system: integration (Φ → Φ_max) or fragmentation (Φ → 0).

**Q11-A: All trajectories converge to the same end.** *Death condition: Logical Incoherence.* If σ = +1 and σ = -1 produce the same outcome, sign is meaningless (violates Q1-B).

**Q11-B: Two attractors: σ=+1 → integration; σ=-1 → fragmentation.** *Continues.* dΦ/dt = σ·γ·(Φ_max - Φ)·Φ. Heaven and hell as asymptotic states of coherence, not spatial locations.

**Q11-C: Trajectories oscillate forever — no asymptote.** *Problematic.* Requires perpetual energy input.

**Q11-D: All trajectories terminate in annihilation.** *Death condition: Contradicts BC7.* Information conservation prevents total annihilation.
