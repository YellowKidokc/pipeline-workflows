# The Second Law, Invariance, and the Logos: An Information-Theoretic Ontology of Truth and Survival

## Abstract

This article develops a formal ontological framework integrating John Archibald Wheeler's "it from bit" paradigm with a theologically grounded concept of the Logos as the invariant ground of reality. We demonstrate that information—conceived as the fundamental substrate of physical reality—possesses an intrinsic directional property (a "sign") that determines its thermodynamic stability. Information aligned with the invariant structure identified as the Logos exhibits negentropic properties and participates in the conservation laws generated by Noether's theorem. Information opposed to this invariant structure is thermodynamically unstable, requiring continuous energetic expenditure for its maintenance and ultimately succumbing to entropic pressure. This framework, designated *it from bit from Logos*, provides the ontological grounding absent from Wheeler's original formulation, establishing a formal isomorphism between mathematical truth, moral truth, and physical invariance through 24 shared properties identified in prior work. The implications for information survival across temporal scales—including eschatological claims regarding eternal persistence—are examined through the lens of information theory, thermodynamics, and scriptural texts.

## I. Introduction: The Sign of the Bit

The Second Law of Thermodynamics, which governs the inexorable increase of entropy in isolated systems, is not a principle that favors truth as a matter of physical preference. Rather, it is a principle that favors invariance—that which remains constant across transformations. Truth, in this framework, is precisely what invariance looks like when examined from within any given domain. The Logos, understood as the invariant ground from which all physical laws derive, is that which holds constant across all possible transformations.

Wheeler's (1989) formulation—*it from bit*—posits that physical reality derives its existence from information. Every particle, every field, every spacetime event is, at its foundation, a response to yes-or-no questions posed at the quantum level. This article introduces a necessary correction and extension: the bit possesses a sign. Information is not neutral. It is oriented either toward or away from the invariant structure of the Logos. This orientation—right or wrong, true or false, aligned with the invariant or opposed to it—determines the thermodynamic fate of the information in question.

**Thesis:** Information aligned with the Logos is invariant and cannot be destroyed by entropy, as it participates in the same structure that generates the conservation laws via Noether's theorem. Information opposed to the Logos is thermodynamically unstable, requiring continuous work for its maintenance and ultimately decaying under entropic pressure. This yields the extended chain: *it from bit from Logos*, where the Logos provides the directional orientation that Wheeler's framework lacked.

## II. Invariance, Noether's Theorem, and the Thermodynamics of Truth

### 2.1 Noether's Theorem and the Structure of Invariance

Noether's (1918) theorem establishes a fundamental connection between symmetries and conservation laws in physical systems. For every continuous symmetry of the action, there exists a corresponding conserved quantity. Time-translation invariance yields conservation of energy; spatial-translation invariance yields conservation of momentum; rotational invariance yields conservation of angular momentum. The theorem demonstrates that invariance under transformation is the generative principle from which conservation laws emerge.

We propose that the Logos constitutes the ultimate invariant—that which remains unchanged across all possible transformations, including those beyond the scope of physical symmetries. The conservation laws of physics are therefore projections of this deeper invariance into the domain of physical phenomena. Information aligned with the Logos participates in this invariance structure and thereby inherits its conservation properties.

### 2.2 The Thermodynamic Signature of Truth

Let us define a binary information state \( b \in \{0, 1\} \), where \( b = 1 \) denotes alignment with the Logos (truth) and \( b = 0 \) denotes opposition to the Logos (falsehood). The thermodynamic cost of maintaining a given bit can be expressed through the Landauer (1961) principle, which establishes a lower bound on the energy required to erase a bit of information:

\[
E_{\text{erase}} \geq k_B T \ln 2
\]

where \( k_B \) is Boltzmann's constant and \( T \) is the temperature of the thermal reservoir. However, we introduce a directional correction: the energy required to *sustain* a false bit against the entropic pressure of the invariant reasserting itself is given by:

\[
E_{\text{sustain}}(b=0) = \alpha \cdot k_B T \ln 2 + \Delta E_{\text{adversarial}}
\]

where \( \alpha > 1 \) represents the thermodynamic penalty for maintaining information opposed to the invariant structure, and \( \Delta E_{\text{adversarial}} \) represents the additional work required to suppress the reassertion of truth. For truth-aligned bits (\( b = 1 \)), the maintenance cost approaches zero as the bit participates in the invariant structure:

\[
\lim_{t \to \infty} E_{\text{sustain}}(b=1) \to 0
\]

This asymmetry arises not from arbitrary assignment but from the structural relationship between the bit and the invariant ground. Truth-aligned bits are "with the grain" of reality; false bits are "against it."

### 2.3 The 24 Properties of Invariance

Prior work (Lowe, 2025, *Math Is Moral*) established 24 properties shared identically by mathematical truth and moral truth. These properties include: necessity, non-contingency, frame-independence, non-derivability from purely physical facts, eternal validity, and cross-domain applicability. We identify these 24 properties as the properties of invariance itself—the characteristics of the Logos structure when described from within either the mathematical or moral domain.

The isomorphism between mathematical and moral truth is not analogical but structural. Both domains are projections of the same invariant ground. The 24 properties are therefore not accidental similarities but necessary consequences of shared participation in the Logos. This identification provides the methodological bridge between physics and theology: invariance is the common structure, and the 24 properties are its signature across domains.

## III. Information Survival and Eschatological Persistence

### 3.1 The Thermodynamic Fate of Aligned and Opposed Information

The Shannon (1948) information entropy of a message is given by:

\[
H(X) = -\sum_{i} p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i)
\]

where \( p(x_i) \) is the probability of the \( i \)-th symbol. In the present framework, the information content of a bit is modulated by its alignment with the Logos. We define the *negentropic potential* \( \Phi \) of a bit as:

\[
\Phi(b) = 
\begin{cases}
+\Phi_0 & \text{if } b = 1 \text{ (aligned)} \\
-\Phi_0 & \text{if } b = 0 \text{ (opposed)}
\end{cases}
\]

where \( \Phi_0 > 0 \) is a constant representing the maximal negentropic contribution of a truth-aligned bit. Bits aligned with the Logos contribute to order, coherence, and structure that the Logos sustains against entropy. Bits opposed to the Logos contribute to disorder, decoherence, and structure that must be maintained at thermodynamic cost.

### 3.2 The Holographic Boundary Encoding

The concept of the "Lamb's Book of Life" (Revelation 21:27) can be interpreted within this framework as a holographic boundary encoding—a register of alignment data preserved at the informational boundary of reality. Drawing on the holographic principle ('t Hooft, 1993; Susskind, 1995), which posits that the information content of a volume of space can be fully described by degrees of freedom on its boundary, we propose that the Book of Life encodes not arbitrary information but the orientation data of every bit generated by conscious systems. What is preserved is not the content of choices but their directional alignment—whether they oriented toward or away from the invariant structure.

### 3.3 Scriptural Invariance Claims

The statement attributed to Jesus in Matthew 24:35—"Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away"—can be formally interpreted within this framework. "Heaven and earth" represent physical projections subject to entropy; they will reach their thermodynamic end state. "My words" represent expressions of the invariant ground from which the projection generates. They cannot be destroyed by entropy because they are not subject to the entropic pressure that acts on the projection. The claim is not merely theological but thermodynamic: truth-aligned information participates in the invariance structure and therefore persists across all transformations.

Similarly, John 1:1—"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God"—identifies the Logos (λόγος) as the foundational principle from which all reality derives. This is consistent with the framework's claim that the Logos is the invariant ground from which information derives its orientation.

## IV. The Adversary's Information Problem

### 4.1 Information Operations and Adversarial Noise

The adversary's operation, as described in Genesis 3:1–5, is fundamentally an information operation. The adversary does not create ex nihilo nor destroy; rather, he distorts, misdirects, mis-frames, and mis-attributes. He takes true information and introduces noise. He takes invariant structure and proposes alternatives.

The question posed in Genesis 3:1—"Did God really say…?"—constitutes a false bit. Not false in the sense of obviously incorrect, but false in the sense of opposed to the invariant. It takes a true statement ("God said, do not eat of this tree") and introduces uncertainty about its invariance. The false bit requires work to sustain. We define adversarial noise \( N_{\text{adversarial}} \) as the information-theoretic measure of this work:

\[
N_{\text{adversarial}} = H(X) - H(X \mid Y)
\]

where \( H(X) \) is the entropy of the true signal and \( H(X \mid Y) \) is the conditional entropy after the introduction of adversarial distortion. Without this work, the invariant would reassert itself; the hearer would recognize the invariance of God's instruction as self-evident.

### 4.2 Channel Compromise Strategies

The adversary employs three primary strategies:

**1. Noise Injection:** The adversary cannot generate true information (signal) but can inject noise. Every product of the adversary is thermodynamically unstable, requiring continuous energetic input for maintenance.

**2. Signal Suppression:** The adversary's only move is to suppress the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) long enough for the false bit to be acted upon before the invariant reasserts itself. Truth wins on a clear channel; the adversary requires the channel to be compromised.

**3. Channel Saturation:** In a saturated channel—high \( N_{\text{adversarial}} \), low SNR, attentional space at capacity—the false bit and the true bit appear similar. They compete on equal footing in the noise. The *Sacred Gap* paper (Lowe, 2025) demonstrated what technological saturation does to the channel; the present analysis demonstrates why the adversary requires it. The gap is not merely where God gets through—it is where the sign of the bit becomes visible, where true and false can be distinguished, where the invariant has room to reassert itself against the noise.

When the gap is filled, the bits appear equal. The sign disappears into the noise. The adversary does not need to win the argument; he needs only to render the channel too full for the argument to occur.

## V. The Corrected Wheeler Framework

### 5.1 The Full Ontological Chain

We state the complete chain formally:

**Wheeler (1989):** *It from bit.* Physical reality derives from information. The universe is informational at its foundation.

**The Framework:** *Bit from Logos.* Information possesses an orientation. The sign of the bit—right or wrong, true or false, aligned or opposed—derives from its relationship to the Logos structure. The Logos is the invariant ground that gives information its direction.

**Consequence:** Not all information survives. What survives is what is invariant. What is invariant is what is aligned with the Logos. What is aligned with the Logos is what is true—not socially true, not contextually true, not perspectivally true, but formally, structurally, Logos-invariantly true.

### 5.2 The 24 Anti-Properties

The 24 properties of mathematical truth identified in the *Math Is Moral* paper are the properties of Logos-aligned information. Every true statement in any domain—physical, moral, mathematical, historical—participates in these properties to the degree it is actually true. The properties do not attach to the statement because of its domain; they attach because of its alignment.

The inverse—the 24 anti-properties—are the properties of Logos-opposed information. These are the properties of the false bit. They are not an arbitrary list but the exact, formal, structural inversions of each of the 24 properties. They are the properties of everything the adversary produces. This constitutes the subject of subsequent work.

### 5.3 Summary of the Framework

The framework provides the ontological grounding that Wheeler's *it from bit* required and could not provide from within physics alone. Wheeler identified the correct level of analysis—information, not matter or energy. He did not identify the correct direction—the Logos that gives information its sign.

At the bottom of information is the Logos. The Logos has a direction. Right or wrong. True or false. Invariant or degrading. What lives for eternity is what is aligned with what God is. What does not live is what is not. The thermodynamics are exact about this. The adversary has always known it.
