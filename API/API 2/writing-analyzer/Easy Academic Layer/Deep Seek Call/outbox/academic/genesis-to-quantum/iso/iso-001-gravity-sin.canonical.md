## 2. The Isomorphic Mapping: Formal Specification

### 2.1 Domain A: Classical Gravitation

Newton's law of universal gravitation states that every point mass attracts every other point mass with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them:

\[
F_g = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
\]

where:
- \(F_g\) = gravitational force (N, kg·m·s⁻²)
- \(G\) = gravitational constant (6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²)
- \(m_1, m_2\) = masses of the two bodies (kg)
- \(r\) = distance between centers of mass (m)

The gravitational binding energy of a system—the minimum energy required to separate two masses to infinity—is given by:

\[
U_g = \frac{G m_1 m_2}{r}
\]

Escape velocity from a gravitational well is:

\[
v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}
\]

### 2.2 Domain B: Hamartiology (Theology of Sin)

Within Christian theological tradition, sin is understood as a condition of moral separation from God (the Source) that exerts a "downward pull" on moral agents. This pull is characterized by three properties isomorphic to gravitational attraction:

1. **Universality**: Sin acts on all moral agents without exception (Romans 3:23, *New International Version*).
2. **Attractive character**: Sin draws moral agents away from the Source rather than repelling them toward it.
3. **Distance-dependent intensity**: The moral "force" of sin increases as the agent's distance from the Source increases.

The isomorphic mapping is formalized as follows:

\[
F_{\text{sin}} = G_{\text{moral}} \cdot \frac{m_{\text{agent}} \cdot m_{\text{world}}}{r_{\text{grace}}^2}
\]

where:
- \(F_{\text{sin}}\) = moral "force" of sin (dimensionless in theological units)
- \(G_{\text{moral}}\) = moral coupling constant (calibrated to empirical theological data)
- \(m_{\text{agent}}\) = moral mass of the agent (a measure of moral capacity and culpability)
- \(m_{\text{world}}\) = moral mass of the world system (collective moral entropy)
- \(r_{\text{grace}}\) = distance from the Source (God), mediated by grace

The binding energy of sin—the minimum "energy" required to escape the moral gravitational well—corresponds to the grace required for salvation. Escape velocity from sin's gravitational field is achieved when grace input exceeds the binding energy of habit, nature, and environment.

## 4. Separator Tests: Classification Criteria

The following four tests constitute the gatekeeping mechanism between Correspondence (Level 2) and Isomorphism (Level 3) within the Theophysics Classification Framework. All four must pass for Level 3 classification.

### 4.1 Test A — Structural Preservation

**Criterion**: Do the key relations survive under the mapping?

The inverse-square dependence, binding energy requirement, and universal attraction property all map from gravitational dynamics to sin dynamics. The relation \(F \propto 1/r^2\) in physics corresponds to the observation that moral distance from the Source increases the attractive pull of sin in a nonlinear, accelerating manner. The binding energy requirement \(U_g = Gm_1m_2/r\) maps to the theological claim that grace must exceed a threshold to effect salvation.

**Result**: PASS — Relations Preserved

### 4.2 Test B — Non-Arbitrariness

**Criterion**: Is the mapping forced, or could another theological target be substituted?

Gravity's specific properties—universal attraction, inverse-square dependence, escape velocity thresholds—map uniquely to sin's properties. Substituting "grace" or "love" into the "attractive force" slot fails to preserve coherence, as grace and love are theologically understood as *counter-forces* to sin rather than isomorphic to it.

**Result**: PASS — Mapping is Forced

### 4.3 Test C — Constraint

**Criterion**: Does the mapping rule out configurations that violate structural coherence?

If sin operated as a repulsive force (pushing moral agents toward God), the mapping breaks. If moral decay followed a linear rather than inverse-square pattern, the mapping breaks. The isomorphism thus forbids configurations that violate gravitational structure.

**Result**: PASS — Rules Out Violations

### 4.4 Test D — Bidirectionality

**Criterion**: Does structural necessity on one side entail structural necessity on the mapped side?

Physics predicts theology: systems with more mass collapse faster, implying that civilizations with greater moral mass (population × moral disorder) should exhibit accelerating entropy. Theology predicts physics: grace must exceed binding energy, implying that conversion experiences should exhibit threshold effects (discontinuity) rather than gradual transitions.

**Result**: PASS — Bidirectional Consequence
