# MacArthur and the Equation: A Two-Phase Resolution of the Calvinist-Arminian Debate via the Coherence Equation

## Abstract

For approximately sixteen centuries, the Christian theological tradition has sustained an unresolved dialectic between Calvinist and Arminian soteriological frameworks. This article proposes that the apparent antinomy between divine sovereignty and human responsibility—articulated most recently by John MacArthur as an irreconcilable tension—admits formal resolution through a single differential equation governing the dynamics of spiritual coherence. The Coherence Equation, defined as \(dC/dt = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C\), describes two sequential transitions: a monergistic phase (\(s: -1 \to 0\)) in which divine grace unilaterally opens a closed system, and a synergistic phase (\(s: 0 \to +1\)) in which human response within a grace-enabled channel contributes genuinely to coherence growth. Through structural mapping of MacArthur's theological claims onto specific equation operations, and through systematic alignment with Augustine's four-state taxonomy of the will, we demonstrate that Calvinist and Arminian positions are not contradictory but complementary descriptions of distinct phases within a single dynamical process. Falsification criteria are specified, including a proposed partition test of scriptural proof texts by phase.

## 1. Introduction: The Persistent Antinomy

The Calvinist-Arminian debate represents one of the most enduring theological controversies in Christian history, engaging figures including Augustine of Hippo, Martin Luther, John Calvin, Jonathan Edwards, John Wesley, and—in the contemporary Reformed tradition—John MacArthur. The central tension may be stated succinctly: Calvinist exegesis emphasizes divine sovereignty in election, effectual calling, and the inability of the spiritually dead to initiate salvation (Ephesians 1:4; Ephesians 2:1; Romans 9:16), while Arminian exegesis emphasizes genuine human response, universal divine desire for salvation, and the reality of choice (John 3:16; 2 Peter 3:9; Joshua 24:15; Revelation 3:20). Both traditions claim extensive scriptural support and accuse the opposing tradition of neglecting complementary biblical data.

MacArthur, representing the "hard edge" of the Reformed tradition, explicitly characterizes this tension as an "antinomy"—two truths that appear contradictory but are not, with their reconciliation lying beyond finite human comprehension (MacArthur, *Ephesians Commentary*). He affirms simultaneously that "God's sovereign election and man's exercise of responsibility in choosing Jesus Christ seem opposite and irreconcilable truths" and that both must be held without resolution.

This article advances the thesis that the antinomy dissolves when salvation is understood as comprising two sequential phases, each described by one term of a single differential equation. The Calvinist tradition describes the first phase (initiation, monergistic); the Arminian tradition describes the second phase (response, synergistic). Neither alone is sufficient; both are mathematically required by the dynamics of the system.

## 2. The Coherence Equation: Formal Definition

The Coherence Equation, introduced in the parent article "Free Will in Two Frames" (Article 04), governs the time evolution of spiritual coherence \(C\), defined as alignment with the divine will:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C
\]

where:

- \(C\) = coherence (dimensionless, \(C \in [0,1]\)), representing the degree of alignment with God
- \(O\) = openness (dimensionless, \(O \in [0,1]\)), representing the human reception variable
- \(G\) = grace (dimensionless, \(G > 0\)), representing divine input
- \(S\) = entropy/sin (dimensionless, \(S > 0\)), representing decay pressure
- \((1-C)\) = room to grow (dimensionless), representing remaining capacity for coherence increase

The surrender parameter \(s\) ranges from \(-1\) (fully closed, autonomous) to \(+1\) (fully surrendered). The coupling function \(\alpha(s)\) determines the proportion of grace that reaches the system:

\[
\alpha(s) = \frac{1+s}{2}, \quad \alpha: [-1, +1] \to [0, 1]
\]

At \(s = -1\), \(\alpha(-1) = 0\); at \(s = 0\), \(\alpha(0) = 0.5\); at \(s = +1\), \(\alpha(+1) = 1\). The growth term thus becomes \(\alpha(s) \cdot G(1-C)\), requiring multiplicative coupling among all three factors.

## 3. Phase 1: The Calvinist Domain (\(s: -1 \to 0\))

### 3.1 The State of Spiritual Death

At \(s = -1\), the coupling function evaluates to zero: \(\alpha(-1) = 0\). The Coherence Equation reduces to:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = -S \cdot C
\]

This is a pure decay equation. The system exhibits no internal mechanism for recovery; coherence decreases monotonically toward zero. This corresponds precisely to MacArthur's exegesis of Ephesians 2:1: "dead in trespasses and sins." The state is not metaphorical illness but dynamical death—the system cannot reverse its own trajectory. No internal generation of \(G\) is possible.

### 3.2 Effectual Calling as External Intervention

The transition from \(s = -1\) to \(s = 0\) requires external action upon the system. Divine grace (\(G\)) acts unilaterally on the surrender parameter \(s\), increasing it from \(-1\) toward \(0\). This operation is monergistic: the system at \(s = -1\) possesses no capacity to initiate or cooperate with this transition. MacArthur's theological vocabulary maps directly onto this operation:

| MacArthur's Term | Equation Mapping |
|-----------------|------------------|
| "God wills" | \(G\) initiates change in \(s\) |
| "God draws" | \(G\) applies force to closed system |
| "God grants" | \(G\) provides the transition |
| "God calls" | \(G\) acts on \(s\) |
| "God appoints" | \(G\) determines the target state |
| "God prepares" | \(G\) enables the transition |
| "God causes" | \(G\) produces the change |

This phase corresponds to what the Reformed tradition terms "irresistible grace" or "effectual calling." The system at \(s = -1\) offers no active resistance—it is simply closed. Grace opens what was shut. The transition always succeeds for the elect because the operation is unilateral.

## 4. Phase 2: The Arminian Domain (\(s: 0 \to +1\))

### 4.1 The Open Channel

Once \(s\) has been moved from \(-1\) to a value in \((0, 1]\), the coupling function \(\alpha(s)\) becomes nonzero. Grace now reaches the system. The full Coherence Equation applies:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = \alpha(s) \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C
\]

The growth term \(\alpha(s) \cdot G(1-C)\) requires both divine input (\(G\)) and human openness (embedded in \(\alpha(s)\) via \(s\)). Neither alone produces growth. This is the domain of synergism: God's grace and human response together build coherence.

### 4.2 Genuine Human Agency

Within this phase, the human variable \(O\) (represented through \(\alpha(s)\)) is genuinely free. The person can choose to increase \(s\) toward \(+1\) (surrender, belief, obedience) or allow \(s\) to decrease toward \(0\) (resistance, complacency). Arminian proof texts find their natural home here:

- "Whoever believes in Him" (John 3:16) — the response variable \(O\) is real
- "Choose this day whom you will serve" (Joshua 24:15) — choice within the open channel
- "If anyone hears my voice and opens the door" (Revelation 3:20) — the open channel requires human response

The Wesleyan concept of "prevenient grace" finds precise formal expression: grace precedes and enables the choice, but the choice itself is genuinely the person's.

## 5. Augustine's Four States: A Historical Confirmation

Twelve centuries prior to the formalization of the Coherence Equation, Augustine of Hippo mapped four states of the human will that correspond precisely to specific parameter values:

| State | Description | \(s\) Value | \(\alpha(s)\) | Dynamical Regime |
|-------|-------------|-------------|---------------|------------------|
| Pre-Fall | *Posse peccare, posse non peccare* (able to sin, able not to sin) | Free | Variable | Full freedom; \(S \approx 0\) |
| Post-Fall | *Non posse non peccare* (unable not to sin) | \(-1\) | 0 | Pure decay; locked |
| Under Grace | *Posse non peccare* (able not to sin) | \((0, 1]\) | \((0, 1]\) | Growth possible; synergistic |
| In Glory | *Non posse peccare* (unable to sin) | \(+1\) | 1 | Perfect coupling; \(S = 0\) |

This mapping demonstrates that the four-state taxonomy—developed through theological reflection on Scripture and human experience—is isomorphic to four parameter settings of a single dynamical equation. The "freedom" to choose sin (decay) is revealed as the freedom to choose system degradation; its loss in glory represents not diminished freedom but enhanced capacity for coherence.

## 6. The Double Predestination Question

MacArthur, consistent with the Reformed tradition, rejects double predestination—the proposition that God actively sends individuals to perdition. His position distinguishes between active election and passive reprobation: God actively chooses the elect, while the non-elect are "passed by" (Romans 9:22-23).

The Coherence Equation provides formal support for this asymmetry. At \(s = -1\) with \(\alpha(s) = 0\), the equation reduces to \(dC/dt = -S \cdot C\). The system decays autonomously. No divine push is required for destruction; the system's own dynamics carry it downward. Salvation, by contrast, requires external input—\(G\) acting on \(s\) to produce the \(-1 \to 0\) transition.

The grammatical asymmetry in Romans 9:22-23 is thus reflected in the dynamics:

- **Active election** (v. 23): "vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory" — active verb, corresponding to \(G\) acting on \(s\)
- **Passive reprobation** (v. 22): "vessels of wrath fitted for destruction" — passive verb (or middle voice), corresponding to the system remaining at \(s = -1\) under its own decay dynamics

The asymmetry is not arbitrary but reflects the fundamental structure: salvation requires external input; destruction requires only the absence of that input.

## 7. Philippians 2:12-13 as Summary Equation

The Apostle Paul's formulation in Philippians 2:12-13 encapsulates the entire two-phase process in a single sentence:

> "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure." (Philippians 2:12-13, ESV)

The mapping to the Coherence Equation is as follows:

| Phrase | Equation Component | Interpretation |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| "Work out your salvation" | Maintain \(s > 0\); sustain openness | Human responsibility within Phase 2 |
| "With fear and trembling" | \(S\) is always active; complacency closes the channel | Entropy requires vigilance |
| "For it is God who works in you" | \(G\) is the power source | Grace enables all coherence growth |
| "Both to will and to work" | Even the desire to respond is grace-enabled | \(G\) precedes and sustains \(O\) |

The passage contains both phases: the divine initiative that enables all response (Phase 1) and the genuine human activity of "working out" salvation within that enabled space (Phase 2). The apparent tension between divine sovereignty and human responsibility is resolved when both are recognized as operating in distinct but sequential phases of a single dynamical process.

## 8. Falsification Criteria

Every claim in this analysis is held to explicit falsification standards. The following kill conditions are specified:

### 8.1 Load-Bearing Claims (High Confidence)

**Kill Condition 1:** If a systematic tabulation of Calvinist and Arminian proof texts cannot be partitioned cleanly by phase—if Calvinist verses do not consistently describe initiation events (\(s: -1 \to 0\)) and Arminian verses do not consistently describe response/growth events (\(s: 0 \to +1\)) under any robust exegetical reading—then the structural claim of the framework fails.

*Status:* Confirmed (qualitative) · *Confidence:* HIGH

**Kill Condition 2:** If Augustine's four-state taxonomy cannot be cleanly mapped onto specific \(s\) values without distorting either Augustine's original formulation or the equation's parameter space, the historical confirmation claim fails.

*Status:* Confirmed · *Confidence:* HIGH

### 8.2 Suggestive Claims (Medium Confidence)

**Kill Condition 3:** If the Romans 9:22-23 active/passive verb asymmetry is shown to be a Greek grammatical convention with no theological weight, or if the asymmetry holds but the framework's mapping to "\(G\) acting on \(s\)" versus "\(s\) left at \(-1\)" is not the most parsimonious account, the double predestination mapping is weakened.

*Status:* Open · *Confidence:* MEDIUM

### 8.3 Destructive Test (Framework-Level)

**Kill Condition 4:** If the perseverance question—whether \(O\) (or \(s\)) can return to zero in the elect—is shown to require the equation to take sides between Calvinism and Arminianism rather than remaining neutral, the structural-neutrality claim fails. The framework's claim is that the equation contains both phases but does not resolve perseverance; that question is set by initial conditions, not by the dynamics. If careful analysis shows the dynamics force one position, the framework's neutrality claim is invalidated.

*Status:* Open · *Severity:* FRAMEWORK-LEVEL

## 9. Conclusion

The Calvinist-Arminian debate, sustained for approximately sixteen centuries, admits formal resolution through the Coherence Equation—a single differential equation with two terms governing two sequential phases of spiritual dynamics. Calvinist theology describes the monergistic Phase 1 (\(s: -1 \to 0\)), in which divine grace unilaterally opens a closed system. Arminian theology describes the synergistic Phase 2 (\(s: 0 \to +1\)), in which human response within a grace-enabled channel contributes genuinely to coherence growth. Both phases are mathematically required; neither alone is sufficient.

John MacArthur's characterization of the tension as an "antinomy" is accurate within a single-phase framework. The Coherence Equation demonstrates that the antinomy dissolves when the two-phase structure is recognized. The harmonization that MacArthur assigned to divine mystery is, in fact, encoded in the dynamics of the system itself.
