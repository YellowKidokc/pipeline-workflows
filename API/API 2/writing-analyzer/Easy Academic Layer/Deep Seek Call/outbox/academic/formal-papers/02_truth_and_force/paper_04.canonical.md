# Theophysics of Relational Bonding: A Structural Isomorphism Between the Strong Nuclear Force and Covenant Love

## Abstract

This investigation establishes a formal structural mapping between the strong nuclear force, as characterized by the Yukawa and Cornell potentials, and the phenomenology of spiritual bonding—specifically love, covenant, addiction, and captivity. Through comparative analysis of the mathematical properties governing quark confinement and asymptotic freedom, we demonstrate that genuine love exhibits a formally analogous "asymptotic freedom," wherein maximal relational liberty obtains at minimal interpersonal distance. Conversely, addiction and captivity are identified as pathological regimes arising from confinement breakdown and vacuum-tearing phenomena, respectively. A free will parameter \( W \) is introduced to modulate the effective potential between covenant and captivity states. The Gottman ratio is interpreted as a Shannon signal-to-noise threshold governing the phase transition from relational coherence to pathological confinement.

## 1. Introduction

Love has been characterized across theological, philosophical, and psychological traditions as the preeminent binding force within human experience (1 Corinthians 13:4–8, *Novum Testamentum Graece*). The "No-Drift" framework posits that the strong nuclear force constitutes the literal physical invariant corresponding to this relational reality. By examining the unique mathematical properties of the Yukawa and Cornell potentials, we may rigorously delineate the mechanics of healthy commitment from those of destructive obsession. This paper proceeds by first establishing the physical formalism of confinement, then mapping these structures onto constructive and destructive relational regimes, and finally introducing a free will parameter to account for human agency in bond quality.

## 2. The Physics of Confinement

The strong nuclear force, governing interactions between quarks and gluons, is described by the Cornell potential (Eichten et al., 1975):

\[
V_{\text{strong}}(r) = -\frac{\alpha_s}{r} + kr,
\]

where \( r \) denotes the interparticle separation (in units of femtometers, fm), \( \alpha_s \) is the dimensionless strong coupling constant (running with energy scale, typically \( \alpha_s \approx 0.118 \) at the \( Z^0 \) mass scale), and \( k \) is the string tension (approximately \( 0.9 \ \text{GeV/fm} \)). The first term, \( -\alpha_s / r \), represents the Coulomb-like one-gluon exchange dominant at short distances; the second term, \( kr \), models the linear confinement potential arising from the gluon flux tube stretched between separated color charges.

Unlike gravitational or electromagnetic interactions, which diminish monotonically with increasing separation, the strong force exhibits two distinctive regimes:

1. **Asymptotic freedom** (Gross & Wilczek, 1973; Politzer, 1973): At sufficiently small \( r \) (high momentum transfer), the coupling \( \alpha_s \) becomes logarithmically small, rendering quarks effectively non-interacting. This is formalized by the renormalization group equation:

\[
\alpha_s(Q^2) = \frac{4\pi}{\beta_0 \ln(Q^2 / \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2)},
\]

where \( Q^2 \) is the momentum transfer squared, \( \beta_0 = 11 - \frac{2}{3} n_f \) (with \( n_f \) the number of active quark flavors), and \( \Lambda_{\text{QCD}} \approx 200 \ \text{MeV} \) is the QCD scale parameter.

2. **Confinement**: As \( r \) increases, the linear term \( kr \) dominates, requiring infinite energy to isolate a single quark. The force between quarks remains constant at large separation (string tension \( k \)), leading to hadronization—the spontaneous creation of quark-antiquark pairs from the vacuum when the stored energy exceeds the pair production threshold.

## 3. The Constructive Regime: Love and Covenant

Within the theological framework, the strong force potential maps onto the spiritual bond of **Love** and **Covenant** (בְּרִית, *berith*; διαθήκη, *diathēkē*). The property of asymptotic freedom provides a formal resolution to the paradox of freedom within commitment: as two souls approach maximal relational proximity, the binding interaction asymptotically vanishes, granting maximal liberty for individual identity expression. Love, in this formalism, does not constrain at close range; rather, it enables authentic selfhood.

The linear confinement term \( kr \) corresponds to the **Covenant Pull**. If one party begins to drift or separate, the restoring force increases linearly with separation distance. This monotonic increase in restorative potential explains the phenomenological experience of "tearing" associated with the dissolution of genuine covenant relationships (Malachi 2:16, *Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia*). The structural violation of the field—the attempted separation of bound entities—incurs an energetic cost that escalates with distance, mirroring the impossibility of isolating a single quark.

## 4. The Destructive Regime: Addiction and Captivity

When the strong force is driven beyond its stable regime, two pathological states emerge, each with a precise physical analogue:

### 4.1 Captivity (Overcoupling)

If the short-range attractive term \( -\alpha_s / r \) becomes pathologically intensified—whether through environmental modification of \( \alpha_s \) or through suppression of the asymptotic freedom regime—asymptotic freedom is lost. Closeness ceases to enable freedom and instead becomes control. The bond transitions from a covenant to a prison, wherein the subjects can no longer move freely within the potential well. This state corresponds formally to a potential with a suppressed short-range screening, such that \( V(r) \approx -\alpha_s' / r + kr \) with \( \alpha_s' \gg \alpha_s \), eliminating the regime where \( dV/dr \approx 0 \) at small \( r \).

### 4.2 Addiction (Vacuum Tearing)

When an agent attempts to break a strong-force bond through brute force, the energy required to overcome the linear confinement term \( kr \) becomes sufficient to exceed the pair production threshold \( 2m_q c^2 \), where \( m_q \) is the constituent quark mass. The vacuum itself "tears," spontaneously creating new quark-antiquark pairs (hadronization). The critical separation for pair production is given by:

\[
r_c \approx \frac{2m_q c^2}{k}.
\]

For light quarks (\( m_q \sim 5 \ \text{MeV}/c^2 \)), \( r_c \sim 1 \ \text{fm} \). The result is not the liberation of free quarks but the formation of replacement bonds—new hadrons. This constitutes the mathematical definition of **addiction**: the attempt to break one destructive bond results in the spontaneous creation of another (e.g., swapping one substance for another). The vacuum-tearing mechanism ensures that the system cannot return to a non-interacting state; it merely reconfigures its binding topology.

## 5. Free Will and Bond Quality

Human agency determines the state of the relational bond through the **Free Will variable** \( W \), a dimensionless parameter modulating the effective potential:

\[
V_{\text{eff}}(r) = W \cdot V_{\text{covenant}}(r) + (1 - W) \cdot V_{\text{captivity}}(r),
\]

where \( V_{\text{covenant}}(r) = -\alpha_s / r + kr \) (the standard Cornell potential) and \( V_{\text{captivity}}(r) \) represents a modified potential with suppressed asymptotic freedom. The parameter \( W \) is constrained to the following regimes:

- **\( W = 1 \)**: Stable Covenant. The system exhibits healthy binding with maximum freedom at the center, corresponding to the standard QCD vacuum.
- **\( W = 0 \)**: Pure Confinement. Asymptotic freedom is entirely suppressed; the bond is experienced as a prison. The effective potential reduces to \( V_{\text{eff}}(r) \approx kr \) with no short-range cancellation.
- **\( W > 1 \)**: Sacrificial Cohesion. The bond becomes so strong that it generates a "residual force" binding the surrounding community. This is analogous to the nuclear force between nucleons, which arises as a residual effect of the strong force binding quarks within nucleons (Yukawa, 1935). The effective coupling becomes \( \alpha_s' = W \alpha_s \), and the residual interaction mediates community-level cohesion.

The dimensional consistency of \( V_{\text{eff}} \) requires that \( W \) be dimensionless, and the transition between regimes occurs at critical values of \( W \) determined by the balance between the Coulomb and linear terms.

## 6. Relational Signal-to-Noise

The stability of these relational bonds can be quantified via the **Gottman ratio** (Gottman & Levenson, 1992), defined as the ratio of positive to negative interactions within a dyadic relationship. Empirically, a threshold of approximately 5:1 (positive to negative) is associated with stable marital relationships. This ratio may be interpreted as a Shannon signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for relational coherence:

\[
\text{SNR}_{\text{relational}} = \frac{P_{\text{positive}}}{P_{\text{negative}}},
\]

where \( P_{\text{positive}} \) and \( P_{\text{negative}} \) represent the power (or frequency-weighted magnitude) of constructive and destructive interactions, respectively. Below a critical SNR threshold—empirically identified as \( \text{SNR}_c \approx 5 \)—the system undergoes a confinement transition: the "noise" of betrayal, neglect, or abuse triggers the collapse of the covenant regime into captivity or vacuum tearing. This phase transition is formally analogous to the loss of asymptotic freedom when the coupling exceeds a critical value.

Love, as characterized within this theophysical framework, is not an emotion but a fundamental architectural principle governing relational binding. By mapping the phenomenology of love and covenant onto the strong nuclear force, we observe that the most stable structures in the physical universe (protons, with a mean lifetime exceeding \( 10^{34} \) years) and the most stable structures in the human soul (covenants) are governed by isomorphic laws of binding and freedom. The formal isomorphism between asymptotic freedom and relational liberty, confinement and captivity, and vacuum tearing and addiction provides a rigorous foundation for interdisciplinary inquiry at the intersection of quantum chromodynamics and spiritual theology.
