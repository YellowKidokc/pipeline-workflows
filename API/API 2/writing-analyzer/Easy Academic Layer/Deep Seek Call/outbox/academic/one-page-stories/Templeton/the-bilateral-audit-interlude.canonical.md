# The Bilateral Audit: A Formal Examination of Epistemic Standards Across Physics and Theology

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic audit of epistemic standards applied to both scientific and theological claims, arguing that asymmetrical evaluative criteria constitute a form of special pleading that undermines rigorous interdisciplinary inquiry. By holding both domains to an identical methodological standard, the audit identifies six loci where novel structural correspondences emerge between physical and theological frameworks. The analysis proceeds through four sequential moves: (1) identification of the asymmetric epistemic burden, (2) articulation of a unified evaluative standard, (3) application of this standard across six domains of inquiry, and (4) derivation of results from the methodological framework. The audit does not presuppose the superiority of either domain but rather examines the conditions under which each domain borrows from the other's conceptual resources.

**Keywords:** bilateral audit, epistemic parity, theophysics, methodological symmetry, interdisciplinary epistemology

---

## 1. The Asymmetric Epistemic Burden: An 80/20 Distribution Without Democratic Mandate

The present investigation begins with a foundational observation: scientific and theological discourses are conventionally evaluated according to distinct—and demonstrably asymmetric—epistemic standards. This asymmetry, herein designated the "80/20 split," refers to the disproportionate burden of proof placed upon theological claims relative to scientific claims, a distribution that has emerged through historical convention rather than through explicit methodological deliberation. No formal vote, disciplinary consensus, or epistemological mandate has authorized this disparity.

The central thesis of this audit is as follows: when both domains are subjected to an identical evaluative framework—one that eliminates special pleading from either side—novel structural correspondences become apparent. These correspondences, identified across six distinct loci, suggest that the conventional asymmetry obscures substantive cross-domain relationships worthy of rigorous investigation.

---

## 2. Methodological Framework: A Unified Evaluative Standard

The audit proceeds through four sequential moves, each designed to eliminate preferential treatment of either domain:

**Move 1: Identification of the Asymmetric Burden.** The audit first documents the conventional disparity in epistemic standards applied to scientific versus theological propositions. This disparity is not asserted as inherently unjustified but is instead identified as a structural feature requiring explicit examination.

**Move 2: Articulation of a Unified Standard.** A single evaluative framework is constructed, applicable to both domains without modification. This framework requires that any claim—whether physical or theological—satisfy identical criteria regarding internal consistency, empirical grounding (where applicable), explanatory power, and parsimony.

**Move 3: Application Across Six Domains.** The unified standard is applied to six specific loci of inquiry, identified below as domains where the audit yields novel findings.

**Move 4: Derivation of Results.** The methodological framework generates results that are not presupposed by the framework itself. These results are presented as provisional findings subject to further scrutiny.

---

## 3. Six Loci of Novel Findings

The application of the bilateral audit reveals six domains in which structural correspondences between physics and theology emerge under the unified evaluative standard. These findings are presented here in summary form; the full derivation, including formal proofs and source attribution, is preserved in the canonical treatment (Lowe, *The Bilateral Audit: Theophysics Research Initiative*, POF 2828).

1. **Ontological Grounding.** Both domains require a foundational ontological commitment that cannot be derived from within the domain itself. In physics, this takes the form of the cosmological initial condition; in theology, the *creatio ex nihilo* doctrine. Under the unified standard, both commitments exhibit isomorphic logical structure.

2. **Causal Closure and Transcendence.** The physical principle of causal closure and the theological concept of divine transcendence exhibit parallel formal properties when analyzed under the audit's framework. Both posit a boundary condition that is simultaneously internal and external to the system under description.

3. **Information-Theoretic Correspondences.** The mathematical structure of information theory, particularly as developed in the context of quantum measurement, exhibits formal isomorphism with theological models of revelation and epistemic access. This correspondence is not metaphorical but structural, as demonstrated through category-theoretic mapping.

4. **Temporal Asymmetry and Eschatology.** The thermodynamic arrow of time and eschatological teleology share a common formal structure when analyzed under the unified standard. Both posit an asymmetric temporal orientation that is irreducible to symmetric physical laws.

5. **Emergence and Hierarchy.** The physical phenomenon of emergence—wherein novel properties arise at higher organizational levels—exhibits formal correspondence with theological models of hierarchical causation. The audit demonstrates that both domains require a non-reductive account of causal efficacy across levels.

6. **Measurement and Revelation.** The quantum measurement problem and the theological problem of revelation both involve the relationship between an observer (or recipient) and a reality that is partially constituted by the act of observation (or reception). The audit identifies a shared logical structure in the two problems, suggesting that neither domain can resolve its respective puzzle in isolation.

## 4. Derivation of Results from the Methodological Framework

The results enumerated above are not asserted as conclusions but as products of the methodological framework. The audit does not claim that physics and theology are identical, nor that one reduces to the other. Rather, it demonstrates that when special pleading is eliminated from both sides, structural correspondences emerge that are invisible under asymmetric evaluative standards.

The full derivation, including formal proofs, tables with source attribution, and confidence intervals where applicable, is preserved in the canonical treatment (Lowe, *The Bilateral Audit: Theophysics Research Initiative*, POF 2828). The present document serves as an interlude—a reader-facing summary that names the load-bearing structure before directing the reader to the complete argument.

## 5. Conclusion and Methodological Implications

The bilateral audit reveals that the conventional 80/20 split in epistemic standards is neither epistemologically necessary nor empirically justified. When both domains are held to the same standard, each is found to borrow from the other's conceptual resources in ways that the asymmetric framework obscures. This finding does not resolve the tensions between physics and theology but reframes them as productive sites of interdisciplinary inquiry rather than as domains of irreconcilable difference.

The audit invites further investigation into the six loci identified above, with the understanding that the unified evaluative standard itself remains subject to revision. The canonical treatment (Lowe, *The Bilateral Audit: Theophysics Research Initiative*, POF 2828) provides the complete argument, tables, proofs, and source structure for those seeking the full derivation.
