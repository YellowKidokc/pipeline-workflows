## 2. Pair I: Gravity ↔ Grace · Resurrection ↔ Expansion (G, R)

### 2.1 Physics Frame

**Newtonian Gravitation.** The gravitational force between two masses is given by:

$$F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

where \(G\) is the gravitational constant (\(6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2\)), \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) are masses, and \(r\) is the separation distance. The force is attractive, follows an inverse-square law, and exhibits a characteristic escape velocity:

$$v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$$

which defines the threshold kinetic energy required to escape the gravitational potential well.

**General Relativistic Curvature.** The Einstein field equations describe gravitation as spacetime geometry:

$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}$$

where \(G_{\mu\nu}\) is the Einstein tensor encoding spacetime curvature, \(\Lambda\) is the cosmological constant, \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is the metric tensor, \(T_{\mu\nu}\) is the stress-energy tensor, and \(c\) is the speed of light. In this formulation, mass-energy tells spacetime how to curve, and spacetime tells mass-energy how to move along geodesics.

$$v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2G_s M_{\text{sin}}}{r}}$$

representing the threshold faith-energy required to break free from habitual sin.

$$\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\text{grace}}\, g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G_s}{c_s^4}\, \mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}$$

where \(\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}\) is the moral curvature tensor, \(\Lambda_{\text{grace}}\) is the grace constant (associated with negentropy), \(\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}\) is the moral-energy tensor, and \(c_s\) is the spiritual propagation speed.
