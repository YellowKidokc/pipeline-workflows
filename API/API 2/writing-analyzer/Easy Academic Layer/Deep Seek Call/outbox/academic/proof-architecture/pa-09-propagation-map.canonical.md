# Theophysics · Propagation Analysis

## Substrate Propagation Map: A Formal Evaluation of Ontological Grounding Through Sequential Constraint Satisfaction

### Abstract

This analysis presents a systematic evaluation of six candidate ontological substrates—Matter, Mind, Information, Mathematical Structure, Relational Structure, and Brute Primitive—through a twelve-stage sequential constraint satisfaction framework (Q0–Q12). Each substrate is assessed for its capacity to answer successive foundational questions without internal contradiction, circularity, or reliance on extraneous explanatory resources. The propagation principle is defined as follows: the substrate selected at Q2 must provide coherent answers to all subsequent questions (Q3–Q12) without borrowing from alternative ontological vocabularies (strain), generating unresolvable blanks, or producing self-contradiction (death). Published secular theories are embedded at each node to demonstrate where contemporary physics converges upon open questions that exhibit the formal structure of boundary conditions (BC1–BC8). Results indicate that only the Information substrate survives the full propagation sequence, yielding eight derived boundary conditions that exhibit an 8/8 correspondence with the theological framework of Christianity. All other substrates terminate at or before Q5 due to explanatory closure, circularity, causation gaps, or property conflicts.

---

### The Propagation Principle: Formal Definition

Let \( S \) denote the substrate selected at Q2. For each subsequent question \( Q_n \) (\( n \in \{3, \ldots, 12\} \)), define the evaluation function:

\[
E(S, Q_n) \in \{\text{PASS}, \text{STRAIN}, \text{FAIL}, \text{TERMINAL}\}
\]

where:

- **PASS**: \( S \) provides a coherent, self-contained answer to \( Q_n \) without recourse to external substrates.
- **STRAIN**: \( S \) answers \( Q_n \) only by borrowing terminology or explanatory resources from another substrate, incurring cumulative explanatory debt.
- **FAIL**: \( S \) produces a self-contradiction or violates a previously satisfied constraint.
- **TERMINAL**: \( S \) reaches explanatory closure—logically coherent but incapable of addressing further questions.

The accumulated cost of strain positions becomes unsustainable by Q6–Q8, as the number of borrowed explanatory resources exceeds the substrate's internal coherence budget.

### Conclusion

The propagation analysis demonstrates that among the six candidate ontological substrates, only Information satisfies the sequential constraint satisfaction criteria through all twelve stages (Q0–Q12). The Information substrate exhibits self-grounding at Q4, yields the necessary properties of a self-grounding entity at Q5 (non-temporal, non-material, maximally coherent, non-deceptive, generative), and supports the derivation of eight boundary conditions (BC1–BC8) that exhibit an 8/8 correspondence with the theological framework of Christianity. All other substrates terminate at or before Q5 due to explanatory closure (Matter), property conflicts (Mind), causation gaps (Mathematical), or collapse into Information (Relational, Brute Primitive). The analysis further reveals that every unsolved problem in contemporary physics—including the Hard Problem of Consciousness, the Arrow of Time, the Measurement Problem, and the Free Will vs. Determinism debate—exhibits the formal structure of a boundary condition that the Information substrate alone can accommodate.
