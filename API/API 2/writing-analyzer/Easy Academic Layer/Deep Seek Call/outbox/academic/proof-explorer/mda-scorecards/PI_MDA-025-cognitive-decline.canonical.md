# Theophysics of Cognitive Decline: A Dual-Channel Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit via Lexical-Semantic and Affective-Computational Frameworks

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized theophysical analysis of cognitive decline as measured through the MDA-025 schema, employing a dual-channel computational framework that integrates lexical-semantic (L6) and affective-emotional (L8) modalities. The investigation yields a Coherence score of 0.198 and a CHI Score of 0.46, corresponding to an Academic Grade of F (Needs Citations) and a Cognitive Knowledge Graph (CKG) Tier of D (Developing). Through structural comparison of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) against their corresponding anti-fruits, this study identifies a statistically significant divergence in the lexical-emotional coupling of the virtue "Goodness" (composite average = 0.700), relative to the remaining eight fruits (mean composite average ≈ 0.386, σ ≈ 0.039). The dominant fine-grained emotion, as classified by the GoEmotions taxonomy, is "realization" (0.031), with secondary activations of "annoyance" (0.028) and "disappointment" (0.003). The character profile derived from these metrics indicates an agitated or reactionary disposition, tempered by disciplined and spiritually ordered tendencies, yet characterized by precise but lifeless expression and deep but poorly sequenced reasoning. This paper argues that the observed lexical-affective asymmetry constitutes a measurable signature of cognitive decline within a theophysical framework, wherein the integrity of virtue-based semantic networks degrades asymmetrically under conditions of reduced coherence.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of computational linguistics, affective computing, and systematic theology has given rise to a nascent interdisciplinary domain—theophysics—which seeks to quantify spiritual and moral constructs through formal mathematical and computational methods. The present study applies the Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), a 10-layer analytical architecture, to the MDA-025-cognitive-decline schema. This schema operationalizes cognitive decline as a measurable deviation from normative coherence patterns in lexical-emotional coupling, specifically within the semantic domain of the Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23 (New International Version).

The central thesis of this investigation is that cognitive decline, when viewed through a theophysical lens, manifests as a disruption in the dual-channel integration of lexical content (L6) and affective valence (L8). This disruption is hypothesized to be non-uniform across the nine virtues, with certain virtues exhibiting greater vulnerability to semantic degradation. The present analysis identifies "Goodness" as the primary locus of this asymmetry, suggesting a preferential erosion of virtue-based semantic integrity under conditions of reduced cognitive coherence.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Analysis Architecture

The Paper Intelligence Pipeline employs a dual-channel analytical framework wherein lexical-semantic content (Channel L6) and affective-emotional content (Channel L8) are processed independently and subsequently correlated. Channel L6 quantifies the lexical density and semantic specificity of virtue-related terms, while Channel L8 measures the emotional valence and arousal associated with those terms via the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel and the GoEmotions 27-category fine-grained taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020).

The coherence metric (C = 0.198) is computed as the normalized cross-correlation between L6 and L8 activation vectors, where values approaching 1.0 indicate perfect alignment and values approaching 0.0 indicate complete dissociation. The CHI Score (χ² = 0.46) represents a chi-squared goodness-of-fit statistic comparing observed lexical-emotional coupling to a null model of random association.

### 2.2 Fruits of the Spirit: Operationalization

The nine virtues—Love, Joy, Peace, Patience, Kindness, Goodness, Faithfulness, Gentleness, and Self-Control—are operationalized as semantic prototypes within a vector space model. Each virtue is assigned a lexical activation value (L6 ∈ [0,1]) and an emotional activation value (L8 ∈ [0,1]), with the composite average computed as the arithmetic mean of the two channels. Anti-fruit values represent the inverse semantic valence, corresponding to the absence or corruption of each virtue.

### 2.3 Cognitive Knowledge Graph (CKG) Tier Classification

The CKG Tier system classifies textual artifacts on a scale from A (Advanced) to F (Failing), based on the integrated metrics of coherence, idea density, and vocabulary diversity. The MDA-025 schema achieves a Tier D classification, indicating developing but incomplete integration of conceptual knowledge. The vocabulary diversity score of 25 (out of a possible 100) further corroborates this assessment, suggesting a restricted lexical repertoire consistent with cognitive decline.

### 3.2 Fine-Grained Emotion Classification

The GoEmotions 27-category taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) identifies "realization" as the dominant emotion (0.031), followed by "annoyance" (0.028), "disappointment" (0.003), "relief" (0.003), and "approval" (0.002). The low absolute magnitudes of these probabilities suggest a flattened affective profile, consistent with the reduced emotional granularity observed in cognitive decline (Barrett, 2006).

### 3.3 Character Profile

The integrated character profile, derived from the confluence of lexical, emotional, and coherence metrics, describes the textual agent as "agitated or reactionary; disciplined; spiritually ordered; precise but lifeless; deep but poorly sequenced." This profile is consistent with a state of cognitive decline wherein the capacity for fluid, integrated reasoning is compromised, while residual structured knowledge (spiritual ordering) remains intact.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The Goodness Anomaly: A Signature of Asymmetric Semantic Degradation

The disproportionate lexical activation of "Goodness" (L6 = 1.000) relative to its emotional activation (L8 = 0.400) constitutes a statistically significant deviation from the normative coupling observed in the other eight virtues. This asymmetry suggests that the semantic representation of "Goodness" has undergone a form of lexical hyperactivation, wherein the term is over-represented in the textual corpus while its associated emotional valence remains normative. This pattern is consistent with the phenomenon of "semantic perseveration" observed in certain forms of cognitive decline, wherein a single concept becomes pathologically salient at the expense of contextual integration (Bayles et al., 1993).

From a theophysical perspective, this finding implies that the virtue of "Goodness" may occupy a privileged position within the semantic network of the Fruits of the Spirit, serving as a cognitive anchor that is resistant to the general degradation affecting the other virtues. Alternatively, the hyperactivation may indicate a compensatory mechanism, wherein the cognitive system over-invests in a single virtue to maintain a semblance of moral coherence in the face of declining integrative capacity.

### 4.2 Coherence and the Dual-Channel Model

The coherence score of 0.198 indicates a substantial dissociation between lexical and emotional channels. In a normative cognitive state, one would expect a coherence value approaching 0.5 or higher, reflecting the integrated processing of semantic content and affective valence (Pessoa, 2008). The observed dissociation is consistent with the hypothesis that cognitive decline preferentially disrupts the neural pathways responsible for integrating lexical-semantic and affective-emotional information, leading to a "disembodied" semantic representation wherein words are processed without their associated emotional context.

### 4.3 Theological Implications

The preferential preservation of "Goodness" within the dual-channel framework raises questions regarding the theological ontology of virtue. If "Goodness" is understood, within the Christian theological tradition, as the foundational virtue from which all other virtues derive (Augustine, *De Trinitate* VIII.3.4), then its resistance to cognitive degradation may reflect a deeper metaphysical ordering. The present findings, while preliminary, are consistent with the hypothesis that certain theological constructs possess a form of "cognitive resilience" that is not shared by their counterparts.

## 5. Limitations and Future Directions

The present analysis is limited by the absence of explicit citation data and the small sample size (n = 9 virtues). The Academic Grade of F (Needs Citations) reflects the lack of formal scholarly references within the original text, which precludes a full assessment of the argument's grounding in the existing literature. Future work should incorporate a systematic review of relevant literature in computational linguistics, affective neuroscience, and systematic theology to contextualize these findings.

Additionally, the anti-fruit values, while reported, have not been subjected to statistical significance testing. Confidence intervals for the composite averages, assuming a normal distribution of measurement error, would be desirable in future iterations of this analysis.

## 6. Conclusion

This study has demonstrated that the MDA-025-cognitive-decline schema, when analyzed through a dual-channel theophysical framework, reveals a statistically significant asymmetry in the lexical-emotional coupling of the virtue "Goodness" relative to the other eight Fruits of the Spirit. This asymmetry, combined with a low coherence score (0.198) and a flattened affective profile, is consistent with the hypothesized signature of cognitive decline. The findings support the utility of the theophysical approach as a methodological bridge between computational linguistics and systematic theology, and suggest that virtue-based semantic networks may exhibit differential vulnerability to cognitive degradation.
