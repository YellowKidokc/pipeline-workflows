# Theophysics of the Fruits of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Lexical and Affective Dimensions

## Abstract

This article presents a formal analysis of the nine Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, examined through a dual-channel framework integrating lexical (L6) and affective-emotional (L8) dimensions. A comparative table quantifies the relative contributions of these channels across each fruit, revealing a consistent pattern of affective dominance with minimal lexical variation. The analysis further incorporates the GoEmotions taxonomy of 27 fine-grained emotions and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel to contextualize the affective profile. A master equation is proposed to formalize the relationship between lexical and emotional variables, though the current data exhibit a low coherence score (0.253) and an academic grade of D (Light), indicating conceptual fertility but insufficient empirical testing. This work constitutes a preliminary step toward a rigorous theophysics of spiritual-affective states.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of theological constructs and physicalist frameworks—termed *theophysics*—requires a methodological approach that preserves the distinct epistemological commitments of each domain while enabling cross-domain structural comparison. The present study investigates the nine Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23, *Novum Testamentum Graece*, 28th ed.) through a dual-channel model that distinguishes between lexical content (L6) and affective-emotional content (L8). This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of Pauline ethical lists with contemporary affective taxonomies, specifically the GoEmotions dataset (Demszky et al., 2020) and the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013).

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Framework

The dual-channel model posits that any spiritual-affective construct can be decomposed into two orthogonal dimensions: (1) a lexical channel (L6), representing propositional or semantic content, and (2) an emotional channel (L8), representing affective valence and arousal. These channels are not assumed to be independent; rather, their interaction is quantified through the average of their respective scores.

### 2.2 Data Sources

The primary data are derived from the Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), which applies a 10-layer analysis to theological texts. The Fruits Comparison Table (Table 1) reports L6 and L8 scores for each fruit, along with an anti-fruit score (representing the inverse or privation of the fruit) and an average score.

### 2.3 Emotion Taxonomies

Two complementary emotion taxonomies are employed: (1) the GoEmotions dataset, which provides 27 fine-grained emotion categories, and (2) the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel, which maps emotions onto a circumplex model of eight primary emotions and their intensities.

### 3.2 GoEmotions Analysis

The dominant emotion identified by the GoEmotions taxonomy is *realization* (score: 0.006), followed by *confusion* (0.001), *sadness* (0.000), *disappointment* (0.000), and *curiosity* (0.000). The low magnitude of these scores indicates that the text does not strongly activate any single fine-grained emotion category.

### 3.3 NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel

The NRC analysis (data not fully reported) is consistent with the GoEmotions findings, showing no dominant primary emotion. This suggests that the affective profile of the Fruits of the Spirit, as measured by these instruments, is characterized by low arousal and neutral valence.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Interpretation of Dual-Channel Scores

The near-zero L6 scores for most fruits indicate that the lexical channel contributes minimally to the overall representation of these constructs. This finding is consistent with the theological claim that the Fruits of the Spirit are primarily *dispositional* or *affective* states rather than propositional beliefs. The exception of Kindness (L6 = 0.199) may reflect its more action-oriented semantic content, which carries greater lexical specificity.

The uniform L8 scores (0.400–0.401) suggest a baseline emotional intensity that is moderate but invariant across fruits. This invariance may be an artifact of the measurement instrument or may reflect a genuine property of the spiritual-affective domain: namely, that the Fruits of the Spirit share a common emotional substrate.

### 4.2 Methodological Limitations

The low coherence score (0.253) and academic grade of D (Light) indicate that the current analysis is conceptually fertile but empirically underdetermined. The absence of variance in L8 scores raises questions about the discriminative validity of the dual-channel model. Furthermore, the anti-fruit scores of 0.000 for all fruits suggest that the model does not yet capture the privative dimension of spiritual states.

### 4.3 Toward a Master Equation

Let \( F_i \) denote the \( i \)-th fruit, with lexical component \( L6_i \) and emotional component \( L8_i \). The average score \( \bar{F}_i \) is given by:

\[
\bar{F}_i = \frac{L6_i + L8_i}{2}
\]

where \( L6_i, L8_i \in [0,1] \). The anti-fruit score \( A_i \) is defined as:

\[
A_i = 1 - \bar{F}_i
\]

However, the empirical data yield \( A_i = 0.000 \) for all \( i \), which is inconsistent with this definition unless \( \bar{F}_i = 1 \). This discrepancy suggests that the anti-fruit score is not simply the complement of the average but may require a separate measurement channel.

## 5. Conclusion

This study provides a preliminary formalization of the Fruits of the Spirit within a dual-channel theophysical framework. The results indicate a dominant affective component with minimal lexical variation, consistent with a dispositional interpretation of Pauline ethics. However, the low coherence and discriminative validity of the current model necessitate further refinement. Future work should (a) expand the lexical channel to include syntactic and pragmatic dimensions, (b) incorporate higher-resolution affective taxonomies, and (c) develop a rigorous master equation that accounts for the privative dimension of spiritual states.
