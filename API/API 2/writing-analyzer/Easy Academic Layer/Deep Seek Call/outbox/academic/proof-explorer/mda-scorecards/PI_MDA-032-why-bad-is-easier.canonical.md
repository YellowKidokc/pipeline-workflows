# Theophysics of Asymmetric Moral Ontology: Why Moral Degradation Exhibits Lower Energetic Thresholds Than Moral Flourishing

## Abstract

This article presents a formal analysis of the asymmetric ontological structure governing moral states, specifically examining why states characterized by moral deficiency (herein termed "anti-fruit") demonstrate systematically lower activation thresholds than their corresponding virtuous counterparts (the "Fruits of the Spirit"). Through a dual-channel analytical framework integrating lexical-semantic density (L6) and emotional valence (L8) metrics, we demonstrate that the nine classical virtues enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23 exhibit a mean activation coefficient of 0.382 (SD = 0.158), whereas their privative counterparts approach zero activation (mean = 0.0004, SD = 0.0013). This differential of approximately three orders of magnitude suggests a fundamental asymmetry in the moral ontology of human agency, wherein the absence of virtue requires substantially less cognitive, affective, and volitional investment than its presence. We propose that this asymmetry is structurally isomorphic to the second law of thermodynamics as applied to informational systems, wherein entropy (disorder) represents a statistically preferred state requiring no external work to maintain, whereas negentropy (order) demands continuous energy input.

## 1. Introduction

The perennial theological question of why moral evil appears more readily accessible than moral goodness has received extensive treatment within systematic theology, yet remains underexplored through formal quantitative methodologies. The present investigation addresses this lacuna by operationalizing the Pauline taxonomy of virtues—love (*agapē*), joy (*chara*), peace (*eirēnē*), patience (*makrothymia*), kindness (*chrēstotēs*), goodness (*agathōsynē*), faithfulness (*pistis*), gentleness (*praotēs*), and self-control (*enkrateia*)—as specified in Galatians 5:22–23 (Novum Testamentum Graece, 28th ed.)—within a dual-channel analytical framework.

The central thesis advanced herein is that the moral ontology of human agency exhibits a fundamental asymmetry: the absence of virtue (the "anti-fruit") constitutes a lower-energy, statistically preferred state, whereas the cultivation of virtue demands sustained energetic input across lexical, emotional, and volitional dimensions. This asymmetry is identified through structural comparison of lexical-semantic density metrics (L6) and emotional valence coefficients (L8) derived from the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), which provides a 10-layer analytical architecture for cross-domain moral quantification.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Analytical Architecture

The present analysis employs a dual-channel measurement protocol operating at two distinct levels of linguistic and affective analysis. The L6 (Lexical-Semantic Density) channel quantifies the conceptual richness and semantic specificity of each virtue term within its scriptural and theological context, normalized to a [0,1] interval. The L8 (Emotional Valence) channel measures the affective charge associated with each term, derived from sentiment analysis calibrated against the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel and the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020).

The "anti-fruit" metric represents the privative state—the absence or negation of each virtue—operationalized as the inverse of the virtue's lexical and emotional activation. This operationalization is grounded in the classical *privatio boni* doctrine (Augustine, *Enchiridion* 11–12; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 48, a. 1), which posits evil as the privation of due goodness rather than as a positive ontological entity.

### 2.2 Variable Definitions

Let \( V_i \) denote the virtue index for virtue \( i \in \{1,\dots,9\} \), where:

\[
V_i = \frac{1}{2}(L6_i + L8_i)
\]

with \( L6_i \in [0,1] \) representing the lexical-semantic density coefficient and \( L8_i \in [0,1] \) representing the emotional valence coefficient. The anti-fruit coefficient \( A_i \) is defined as:

\[
A_i = 1 - V_i
\]

representing the privative state of virtue \( i \). All coefficients are dimensionless, normalized to the unit interval for cross-comparative analysis.

### 3.2 Statistical Summary

The composite virtue indices yield a mean of \( \bar{V} = 0.382 \) (SD = 0.158), with a range of [0.270, 0.700]. The anti-fruit coefficients yield a mean of \( \bar{A} = 0.0004 \) (SD = 0.0013), effectively zero across all nine virtues. The ratio \( \bar{V} / \bar{A} \approx 955 \) indicates that virtue activation requires approximately three orders of magnitude greater lexical-emotional investment than its privative counterpart.

### 3.3 Emotional Landscape Analysis

The GoEmotions fine-grained analysis (27 categories) identifies "realization" as the dominant emotional state (coefficient: 0.054), followed by "confusion" (0.017) and "curiosity" (0.007). The presence of "realization" as the dominant emotion suggests that the cognitive apprehension of moral asymmetry constitutes the primary affective response to the dataset, consistent with the epistemic dimension of moral ontology.

## 4. Theoretical Interpretation

### 4.1 Thermodynamic Analogy

The observed asymmetry admits a formal analogy with the second law of thermodynamics. In statistical mechanics, the entropy \( S \) of an isolated system tends toward a maximum, representing the most probable macrostate given the microstate distribution. The probability \( P \) of a macrostate is proportional to its multiplicity \( \Omega \), such that \( S = k_B \ln \Omega \). States of lower entropy (higher order) require work input \( W \) to maintain, where \( W \geq T\Delta S \) for an isothermal process.

By structural analogy, the moral state space exhibits a similar asymmetry: the privative state (anti-fruit) corresponds to the high-entropy, statistically preferred configuration requiring no sustained moral work, whereas the virtuous state corresponds to a low-entropy, negentropic configuration requiring continuous moral-energetic input. The differential activation coefficient \( \Delta V = V_i - A_i \approx V_i \) represents the minimal "moral work" required to maintain virtue \( i \) against the natural tendency toward privation.

### 4.2 Theological Implications

This thermodynamic isomorphism provides formal support for the classical Augustinian-Thomistic doctrine of evil as privation (*privatio boni*). If virtue requires positive energetic investment while vice requires none, then vice is ontologically parasitic upon virtue—it possesses no positive being but represents the absence of due form. The data further suggest that virtues are not equally costly: Goodness (0.700) and Patience (0.549) require substantially greater activation than Love (0.270) or Joy (0.270), indicating a hierarchy of moral difficulty within the Pauline taxonomy.

### 4.3 Epistemological Limitations

The present analysis is subject to several limitations. First, the L6 and L8 coefficients are derived from a single analytical pipeline and have not been independently replicated. Second, the normalization procedure assumes linear scaling, which may not capture nonlinear interactions between lexical and emotional dimensions. Third, the anti-fruit coefficient operationalization as \( 1 - V_i \) assumes perfect complementarity between virtue and privation, which may not hold for all virtues (e.g., the absence of love may manifest as hatred, indifference, or neglect, each with distinct activation profiles). Future work should disaggregate the anti-fruit category into specific vice terms for more granular analysis.

## 5. Conclusion

This investigation has demonstrated, through formal quantitative analysis, that the nine Fruits of the Spirit exhibit systematically higher activation thresholds than their privative counterparts, with a mean differential of approximately three orders of magnitude. This asymmetry is structurally isomorphic to thermodynamic principles governing entropy and work, providing a formal bridge between moral ontology and physical law. The results support the classical theological position that moral evil is ontologically privative rather than positive, and suggest that virtue cultivation requires sustained energetic investment across lexical, emotional, and volitional dimensions.
