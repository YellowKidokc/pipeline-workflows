## 2. The Six-Step Deductive Chain

### 2.1 Proposition 1: Universal Moral Realism

**Claim:** Every human being, across all cultures and historical epochs, possesses an innate and universal awareness of the objective reality of good and evil.

**Logical Derivation:** The phenomenology of moral awareness is pre-theoretical. A child of approximately three years of age, prior to formal moral instruction, exhibits recognition of fairness violations (e.g., protesting "that's not fair" when a toy is taken). This observation is cross-culturally robust and has been documented in developmental psychology (Haidt, 2001; Hamlin, 2013). Even individuals who espouse moral relativism in abstract discourse exhibit behavioral responses consistent with moral realism when personally affected (e.g., anger at being cut off in traffic). This indicates that the belief in objective moral values is deeper than explicit philosophical commitment.

**Entailment:** For good and evil to be *real*—that is, to possess ontological status independent of subjective opinion—there must exist a fixed reference point or standard against which moral judgments are measured. Without such a standard, moral claims reduce to expressions of preference (Ayer, 1936; Mackie, 1977), which contradicts the universal phenomenology of moral experience.

**Physics Mapping — Electromagnetic Signal Propagation:** In electromagnetic theory, a signal propagates coherently only if it maintains phase integrity relative to a reference frame. Truth, in this analogy, is not a matter of opinion but of measurement: the wave either arrives intact (coherent propagation) or is cancelled (destructive interference). Good and evil, analogously, are real signals in a moral field, not social constructions subject to arbitrary redefinition.

### 2.2 Proposition 2: The Consciousness Threshold and Free Will

**Claim:** Moral weight requires agency, specifically the capacity for free choice. Non-human animals, lacking this capacity, operate outside the moral domain.

**Logical Derivation:** Consider two events with identical physical outcomes: a lion kills a gazelle, and a human kills another human. The first event is not classified as murder; the second is. The difference cannot be attributed to the physical action itself, which is isomorphic (a predator killing prey). The difference lies in the agent's capacity to have done otherwise. The lion operates on stimulus-response mechanics—instinct, predation drive, \( F = ma \)—without reflective self-awareness or alternative possibilities. The human, by contrast, possesses the capacity for counterfactual choice: the human *could have refrained*.

**Entailment:** Moral weight requires agency. Without free will, there can be no sin, no virtue, and no love—only deterministic sequences of cause and effect. This is consistent with the standard philosophical position that moral responsibility presupposes libertarian free will (Kane, 1996; van Inwagen, 1983).

**Physics Mapping — The Free Will Variable \( W \):** We introduce a dimensionless variable \( W \), defined as the capacity for counterfactual choice. For non-human animals operating on deterministic physics, \( W = \text{N/A} \)—the variable does not exist in their dynamical equations. For humans, \( W \in [0, \infty) \), where \( W = 0 \) corresponds to complete determinism (no moral agency) and \( W \to \infty \) corresponds to maximal freedom. This single variable constitutes the formal distinction between the physical domain (Tier 1) and the moral domain (Tier 2).

### 2.3 Proposition 3: Mixed Moral Nature (The Corruption Thesis)

**Claim:** Every human agent exhibits a mixed moral nature—capable of both good and evil, often simultaneously—indicating a systemic corruption that leaves the capacity for moral agency intact but compromises its execution.

**Logical Derivation:** Empirical observation reveals that no human agent is purely good or purely evil. A good man lies; a criminal shows kindness; a pastor steals; a drug dealer feeds the homeless. This pattern is universal and has been documented across cultures (Peterson & Seligman, 2004). The system is *live but broken*: the hardware (consciousness, free will, moral awareness) functions, but the software (moral execution) is corrupted. Something has occurred that left the capacity intact but the execution compromised.

**Entailment:** The free will variable \( W \) exists in humans, but it is biased toward the destructive regime. This bias is not a property of \( W \) itself but of the system in which \( W \) operates.

**Physics Mapping — Native Duality in Physical Laws:** The same gravitational equation (\( F = G m_1 m_2 / r^2 \)) produces both stable planetary orbits and black hole singularities. The mathematics contains both possibilities; the initial conditions and boundary conditions determine which regime manifests. Analogously, the free will variable \( W \) determines whether the moral system operates in the constructive or destructive regime. The universal bias toward the destructive regime constitutes what theology terms "original sin" or "the fall."

### 2.4 Proposition 4: The Impossibility of Self-Perfection

**Claim:** No closed system can reverse its own entropy; analogously, no human agent can achieve moral perfection through self-directed effort alone.

**Logical Derivation:** A survey of human history yields no documented case of a human agent achieving moral perfection through effort alone. Philosophers (e.g., the Stoics, Kant) attempted systematic moral self-cultivation and acknowledged failure. Monastic traditions across religions report the same. The more one attempts moral self-improvement, the more acutely one becomes aware of the gap between aspiration and achievement. This is not a contingent empirical finding but a structural necessity: self-improvement is real and possible; self-perfection is not.

**Entailment:** The system requires an external source of correction. A closed system cannot reverse its own degradation.

**Physics Mapping — The Second Law of Thermodynamics:** In a closed system, entropy obeys \( dS \geq 0 \), where \( S \) is entropy. The system always moves toward greater disorder. Without an external energy source, decay is inevitable and irreversible. Mathematically, one cannot lift oneself by one's own bootstraps: the equations do not permit it. The moral analog is that moral entropy (the tendency toward corruption) increases in a closed moral system without external intervention.

### 2.5 Proposition 5: The Necessity of Ultimate Justice

**Claim:** If moral realism is true (Proposition 1), and if this life is the only realm of existence, then justice is impossible—contradicting Proposition 1. Therefore, a realm beyond this life must exist where moral accounts are settled.

**Logical Derivation:** Empirical observation reveals that good people suffer and evil people prosper with systematic regularity. Children die in poverty while tyrants die in luxury. If justice is real (Proposition 1), and if this life constitutes the entirety of existence, then justice is a lie—because it is clearly not served here. Two options present themselves: either justice is an illusion (contradicting Proposition 1), or there exists a realm beyond this one where accounts are settled. These are the only two logical possibilities.

**Entailment:** Conservation of moral energy requires a domain of resolution beyond the present life.

**Physics Mapping — Conservation Laws:** In physics, energy is neither created nor destroyed—only transformed (\( \Delta E_{\text{system}} = 0 \) for an isolated system). If moral "justice-energy" exists (and Propositions 1–4 establish that it does), then it cannot disappear. Every action persists in the system; every choice is conserved. Conservation laws do not allow moral energy to vanish; it must resolve somewhere. This resolution requires a domain beyond the present physical universe.

### 2.6 Proposition 6: The Justice-Mercy Paradox and Its Unique Solution

**Claim:** A perfect Judge faces an impossible dilemma: perfect justice requires that every violation be answered; perfect mercy requires that forgiveness be possible. These constraints contradict unless a mechanism exists that satisfies both simultaneously. The only such mechanism is substitution.

**Logical Derivation:** Let \( J \) represent the justice constraint (every violation must receive proportional consequence) and \( M \) represent the mercy constraint (forgiveness must be possible). These constraints are contradictory under standard logic: \( J \land M \to \bot \) (a contradiction). The substitution mechanism operates as follows: an agent who meets the standard perfectly (i.e., \( W = 1 \) sustained across an entire life, including the moment of death) voluntarily absorbs the penal consequences of the violation. The debt is paid—justice is satisfied. The debtor is freed—mercy is satisfied. Both properties are preserved simultaneously.

**Entailment:** This is not a religious claim but a mathematical solution to a system of two contradictory constraints. The solution is unique: no other configuration satisfies both \( J \) and \( M \) simultaneously.

**Physics Mapping — Quantum Entanglement:** When two quantum systems interact, they share a single wavefunction from that point forward. The substitute's wavefunction (perfectly aligned, \( W = 1 \)) entangles with the individual's wavefunction (corrupted, \( W < 1 \)). After entanglement, the following holds:

This is not metaphorical but structural: entangled particles share identity in the quantum mechanical sense. The solution transfers through quantum entanglement—the formal analog of substitutionary atonement.
