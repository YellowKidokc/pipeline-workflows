# The Moral Decline of America

Nine areas of life. 125 years of data. Five generations of one family. A guided tour through measurable changes in trust, family structure, moral language, institutions, and the possibility of recovery.

## What This Series Is About

This series tracks how America fell apart — not as a feeling or an opinion, but as something you can measure. Think of it like a fever chart for a whole country. We look at nine separate areas of life (called "domains") and track what happened to each one over the last 125 years.

The big idea: America didn't slowly get worse. It hit a tipping point between 1968 and 1973. Before that, things held together. After that, they came apart. This is the story of how that happened, why it happened, and what it means.

## The Physics of Decline

Think of a block of ice sitting on a warm counter. For a long time, nothing seems to happen. Then, all at once, the ice turns to water. That's a **phase transition** — a sudden change in the state of a system. This series argues that America went through a phase transition in its social and moral structure between 1968 and 1973. It didn't just get a little worse each year. It changed state.

We use the math from physics and information theory (the study of how information is stored and sent) to measure this. The same equations that describe how heat moves or how radio signals get scrambled can describe how trust, family, and moral language break down in a society.

## The Nine Domains

These are the nine areas we measure. Each one has its own data going back to 1900.

1.  **Family** — Marriage rates, divorce rates, single-parent households, how kids turn out.
2.  **Religion** — Church attendance, what people believe, trust in clergy, knowledge of the Bible.
3.  **Institutions** — Trust in government, media, schools, doctors, corporations.
4.  **Language** — How often people use moral words like "honor," "duty," "virtue," and "character."
5.  **Education** — SAT scores, how many people can read well, critical thinking skills.
6.  **Economy** — How much debt families carry, savings rates, home ownership.
7.  **Health** — Life expectancy, mental health, addiction rates, how many kids people have.
8.  **Crime** — Violent crime, prison rates, child abuse, suicide.
9.  **Constitution** — How people interpret the Constitution, whether they stick to the original meaning or change it to fit the times.

## The Key Discovery: Language Breaks First

Before any institution falls apart, its vocabulary falls apart first. Think of it like a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal. Researchers Kesebir and Kesebir found that American moral vocabulary declined 73% between 1900 and 2000. Words like "honesty," "patience," "compassion," and "self-control" showed up less and less in books and newspapers.

This matters because you can't think clearly about something you don't have words for. If you lose the word "honor," you lose the ability to recognize honorable behavior. The language collapse came first. The behavior collapse followed.

## The Phase Transition: 1968-1973

This is the key moment. Between 1968 and 1973, everything changed at once. Think of it like a hill — you don't get pushed, the ground itself tilts so you naturally move toward the bottom. Grace changes the shape of the ground under your feet. In this case, the ground tilted away from coherence (things holding together) and toward decoherence (things falling apart).

The data shows it clearly:
- Divorce rates shot up
- Church attendance dropped sharply
- Trust in government collapsed
- Crime rates surged
- Moral vocabulary continued its decline

Before 1968, these trends were stable or slowly improving. After 1973, they all went in the same bad direction at the same time. That's a phase transition.

## The Amish Proof

The Amish are not just an interesting group of people. They are a **control group** — like the group that gets the sugar pill in a drug study. By rejecting the technologies that changed modern America (television, internet, cars, mass media), the Amish preserved the old way of life.

The result? The Amish outperform the national average on 8 out of 9 coherence domains. They have stronger families, lower crime, better mental health, more stable finances, and higher trust. This is not an accident. It's evidence that the technology and culture changes of the last century are directly linked to the decline.
