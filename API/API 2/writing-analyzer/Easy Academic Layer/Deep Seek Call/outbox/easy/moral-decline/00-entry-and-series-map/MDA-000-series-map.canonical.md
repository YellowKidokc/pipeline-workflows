[** Why the Bad Is Easier Than the Good Published **](../04-collapse-mechanisms/MDA-032-why-bad-is-easier.html)
The asymmetry problem — entropy, shortcuts, and the structural cost of virtue. Think of it like this: thermodynamics (the study of heat and energy flow) tells us that things naturally move toward disorder. Like heat flowing from hot to cold — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system. That's judgment. Bad actions are like letting heat flow naturally. Good actions are like trying to push heat uphill. It costs more.

[** The Coherence Metric Published **](../02-method-and-metrics/MDA-038-coherence-metric.html)
A physics-based framework for measuring structural integrity across domains. Imagine a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal. This metric measures how clear the signal is.

[** The Empirical Evidence Published **](../03-evidence-chronology/MDA-005-empirical-evidence.html)
Four predictions, four tests, all confirmed — R̄ = 0.986, p < 10⁻⁸. That R̄ (pronounced "R-bar") is a statistical measure of how well the model fits the data. 1.0 would be a perfect fit. 0.986 is almost perfect. The p-value (probability value) tells you the chance that this happened by random luck. Less than 10⁻⁸ means less than one in 100 million. That's not luck.
