## DOMAINS

**Domain A:** Mathematics / Physics
**Domain B:** Christian Theology
**Concept A:** Terminus Sui — Closed System Incompleteness. Six separate theorems prove that closed systems (systems that don't get help from outside) cannot: check their own correctness, define what's true for themselves, predict when they'll stop working, reverse their own decay, process information without using energy, or explain why they exist at all.
**Concept B:** Grace — Help that comes from outside, is enough to fix the problem, costs the helper not the helped, doesn't depend on how good the system has been, and arrives before the system can do anything to earn it (Ephesians 2:8-9).

* * *

## THE MAPPING

**The Six Theorems (Domain A):**

1. **Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems** — No math system that's powerful enough to do basic arithmetic can prove that it's correct just by using its own rules. You can't check yourself from the inside.
2. **Tarski's Undefinability Theorem** — No system can define what "true" means using only its own language. You need a bigger language outside the system to talk about truth inside it.
3. **Turing's Halting Problem** — No computer program can figure out whether it will ever finish running just by looking at its own code. Self-prediction is impossible.
4. **Clausius / Second Law of Thermodynamics** — In a sealed-off system, disorder (entropy) always increases or stays the same. It never goes down on its own. Like heat flowing from hot to cold — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system.
5. **Landauer's Principle** — Every time a computer erases information, it has to release heat. Information processing isn't free — erasing always costs energy.
6. **Leibniz's Contingency Argument** — Nothing that could have been different (contingent things) can explain why it exists just by looking at itself. You need something that couldn't have been different (non-contingent) to explain the whole chain.

**Formal Statement:**

S(X) = V(X) ∧ T(X) ∧ P(X) ∧ R(X) ∧ E(X) → ¬Coherent(X)

Where:

- V(X) = X validates its own consistency (checks its own correctness)
- T(X) = X defines its own truth predicate (defines what "true" means for itself)
- P(X) = X predicts its own termination (figures out when it will stop)
- R(X) = X reverses its own entropy (undoes its own decay)
- E(X) = X explains its own existence (says why it exists without looking outside)

Any system claiming all five is incoherent (self-contradictory). A closed system cannot do all five at once. This is like saying you can't lift yourself off the ground by pulling on your own shoelaces.

**The Five Properties of Grace (Domain B) — Derived by Inverting the Six Theorems:**

| Theorem | What closure lacks | What grace provides | Grace Property |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gödel | Self-validation (can't check itself) | External validation (outside check) | G1: Externality (comes from outside) |
| Tarski + Turing | Self-defined truth, self-prediction (can't define truth or predict own end) | Sufficient external truth/halting (outside source that's complete) | G2: Sufficiency (enough to fix the problem) |
| Landauer | Cost-free computation (can't process info for free) | Cost borne by the intervener (helper pays the price) | G3: Asymmetric cost (cost is uneven — helper pays, not helped) |
| Clausius (2nd Law) | Self-reversal of entropy (can't undo its own decay) | Reversal independent of system merit (decay reversal doesn't depend on system's performance) | G4: Merit-independence (doesn't depend on how good you've been) |
| Leibniz | Self-explanation of existence (can't explain why it exists) | Temporally prior ground (outside cause that existed before the system) | G5: Temporal priority (comes before the system can do anything) |

**Mapping to Ephesians 2:8-9:**

- "For by grace" → G1: Externality (the help comes from outside the system, not from within)
- "you have been saved" → G2: Sufficiency (the rescue is complete, not partial — like a full rescue, not a half-rescue)
- "this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God" → G3: Asymmetric cost (the giver pays the whole price, like a parent paying for a child's surgery)
- "not by works" → G4: Merit-independence (the system's performance doesn't matter — like getting a gift whether you've been good or bad)
- "so that no one can boast" → G5: Temporal priority (the rescue happens before the system can produce any output — like a lifeguard reaching you before you can swim to shore)

**Coupling Coefficient:**

α(u) where u = autonomy parameter (how much the system insists on running itself):

- α(u=1) = 0 — Maximum autonomy (fully closed system) = zero grace coupling. The system says "I can handle it myself" and gets exactly the breakdown the six theorems guarantee. Like a person who refuses all help and then drowns.
- α(u=0) = 1 — Surrender of autonomy = maximum coupling. The system opens to outside input and receives what it cannot generate internally. Like a person who admits they can't swim and lets the lifeguard pull them to shore.

This is not a metaphor. The coupling coefficient describes how much a system lets outside help in. A fully closed system (u=1) gets none. A fully open system (u=0) gets maximum restoration of coherence (everything working together properly).

**Shared Structure:** Both domains describe systems that are built in a way that they cannot save themselves. The properties needed for rescue (externality, sufficiency, asymmetric cost, merit-independence, temporal priority) are not optional extras — they are the logical opposites of the impossibility theorems. Grace is what you get when you ask: "Since self-rescue is impossible, what must the rescue look like?"

**What Is NOT Claimed:**

- NOT claiming "grace is a theorem" — grace is a real theological thing; the theorems just describe the structural need for something with grace's properties
- NOT claiming the six theorems were meant to prove grace — they were discovered separately across centuries by people who weren't trying to prove theology
- NOT claiming this proves Christianity is true — it shows that the Christian description of grace has exactly the right structure that formal impossibility results require of any outside rescue
- NOT claiming other religions couldn't map here — but Pelagianism (the idea that people can save themselves) fails the same way a perpetual motion machine fails, and semi-Pelagianism (the idea that people can partly save themselves) fails the way partial self-validation fails
- NOT claiming the coupling coefficient α(u) can be measured in theology — it describes the shape of the relationship, not something you can put numbers on

## TESTS

**Swap Test:** Can you swap the five properties?

No. Each property connects to a specific theorem:

- Externality is not the same as sufficiency. Gödel proves you need an OUTSIDE validator; Tarski/Turing prove the outside validator must be COMPLETE (knows all truth, can predict halting). You can have outside help that isn't enough to fix the problem.
- Asymmetric cost is not the same as merit-independence. Landauer proves computation has a cost that must be paid SOMEWHERE; Clausius proves the reversal doesn't depend on what the system has DONE. A costly rescue could still depend on merit (you pay for the good students). A rescue that costs the helper but doesn't depend on the system's performance is specifically what the theorems require.
- Temporal priority is not the same as externality. Something can be outside but not come before the system's collapse (a rescue that arrives after the system has already broken). Leibniz's argument requires the ground to be before the chain of contingent things.

**Swap test result: PASSED.** The mapping is one-to-one, not many-to-one. Each property is non-redundant (you need all of them, and none can replace another).

**Prediction in Domain B (Theology):**

- Pelagianism (pure self-salvation) is structurally incoherent — it is the theological equivalent of a perpetual motion machine
- Semi-Pelagianism (partial self-salvation) is structurally incoherent — it is the theological equivalent of claiming a system can partially validate its own consistency

- The Reformation debate (faith alone vs. faith + works) maps to: does the coupling coefficient α require u=0 (pure reception — just receiving) or can u be nonzero (partial system contribution — the system helps a little)? The six theorems say u must be 0 for coherence — any nonzero u brings back the self-validation problem

**Bidirectional:** Yes. The mathematics predicts that any viable rescue must have these five properties. The theology describes a rescue with exactly these five properties. Information flows both ways:

- Math → Theology: limits which theories of salvation are structurally possible
- Theology → Math: suggests that closed-system impossibility results are not bugs but features — they create the structural opening through which outside coherence enters

**Falsification:**

Show ANY ONE of the following:

1. A closed formal system that proves its own consistency (breaks Gödel → G1 unnecessary)
2. A formal language that defines its own truth predicate (breaks Tarski → G2 unnecessary)
3. A program that decides its own halting (breaks Turing → G2 unnecessary)
4. An isolated thermodynamic system that spontaneously decreases its entropy (breaks Clausius → G4 unnecessary)
5. A computation that erases information with zero energy cost (breaks Landauer → G3 unnecessary)
6. A contingent entity that fully explains its own existence without reference to anything external (breaks Leibniz → G5 unnecessary)

Breaking any single theorem removes the necessity of the corresponding grace property. Breaking all six eliminates the structural necessity for grace entirely. None have been broken in the history of mathematics or physics. Each has been independently strengthened over time.
