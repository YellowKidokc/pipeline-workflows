# Proof V · Full Explanation

## Thought Costs Energy

Information is physical. Erasing one tiny piece of information (one bit) costs real energy. You can't think your way out of decay for free. Landauer proved it.

In 1961, IBM physicist Rolf Landauer proved something that connects the world of ideas to the world of matter: information is not free. Every bit of information that gets processed, stored, or erased has a minimum cost in heat. Thinking — computing — any kind of mental work — all produce heat. All increase entropy (the measure of disorder in a system).

E ≥ kT · ln(2)

Erasing one bit of information releases at minimum  
kT · ln(2) joules of heat (~3 × 10⁻²¹ J at room temperature)

This number is tiny — 3 zeptojoules (a billionth of a billionth of a joule). But it's not zero. And that non-zero minimum is everything. It means that processing information has a cost you cannot avoid. You cannot compute without paying entropy.

## Why This Matters

Landauer's Principle is the fifth proof in Terminus Sui because it connects two worlds that people used to think were separate: the world of ideas (logic, information, computation) and the physical world (energy, heat, entropy).

Before Landauer, you could imagine that information was somehow "above" physics — that logical operations happened in some perfect, imaginary realm untouched by thermodynamics. Landauer destroyed that escape route. Information is physical. Computation is physical. Even *thinking* is physical, bound by the same laws that govern steam engines.

This means Gödel's incompleteness (limits of logic), Tarski's undefinability (limits of language), and Turing's halting problem (limits of computation) are not just abstract curiosities — they have physical consequences. The limits of self-reference aren't just logical — they cost energy. Every attempt a system makes to evaluate itself burns resources and increases the disorder it's trying to fight.

**The connection to the Second Law:**

The Second Law says disorder (entropy) always increases. Landauer shows that even the ACT OF THINKING about how to decrease disorder itself increases disorder. You can't out-think decay. The tool you'd use to fight disorder (thinking) is itself subject to disorder. The system is trapped.

## The Theological Translation

Landauer proves something theologians have always known: you can't save yourself by your own efforts. Not because of moral weakness, but because of the structure of reality itself. Every effort you make to maintain order costs energy that increases disorder somewhere else.

Self-improvement is a thermodynamic treadmill. You're burning energy to organize locally while the universe disorganizes globally. You can stay ahead for a while — a lifetime, maybe — but the Second Law always wins. Unless someone outside the system pays the cost.

Grace isn't just a theological concept. It's the name for what physics calls externally sourced negentropy (order that enters the system from outside) — covering the thermodynamic cost that the system cannot pay for itself.

Think of it like the slope of a hill — you don't get pushed, the ground itself tilts so you naturally move toward the bottom. Grace changes the shape of the ground under your feet. You can't climb the hill on your own forever. Someone from outside has to change the slope.

**Landauer's Principle in one sentence:**

You cannot think your way out of decay, because thinking IS decay. The solution must come from outside the system that's thinking.
