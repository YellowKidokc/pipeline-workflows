## The Five Proofs

Each proof below was found on its own. None of them started out trying to prove God. Each one just asked a narrow, technical question—and found that the answer needed something from outside the system being studied.

### 1. Gödel — Incompleteness

Any consistent formal system (a set of rules for math or logic) that's strong enough to do basic arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove. You can't have both consistency (no contradictions) and completeness (proving everything true) at the same time.

[Read the plain-language walkthrough →](godel.html)

### 2. Tarski — Undefinability of Truth

A language cannot define its own truth without creating a paradox (like "this sentence is false"). The idea of "truth" for a system has to come from a metalanguage (a language outside the first one).

### 3. Turing — Undecidability

No general algorithm (a step-by-step computer program) can figure out whether any random program will stop running or run forever. Predicting the system's own behavior needs something the system can't contain.

### 4. Second Law of Thermodynamics

Entropy (a measure of disorder) in an isolated system (one with no energy or matter coming in or out) never goes down. Order falls apart, information scatters, and usable energy runs downhill—and you can't reverse it.

[See why the universe hasn't reached heat death →](entropy.html)

### 5. Landauer's Principle

Erasing one bit of information costs kT·ln(2) joules (a tiny but real amount of energy). Information is physical, and processing it has an irreversible thermodynamic price (you can't get that energy back).

[Read why thought costs energy →](landauer.html)

## The Shared Structure

Every proof has the same shape: **draw a box around any finite system, and the box leaks**. It needs an outside source for consistency, truth, predictability, order, and even the ability to process information. The universe has all of these—and has had them for 13.8 billion years. Either it's the exception to five proven theorems, or it was never a closed box.

## Therefore

If the universe is not a closed system, then something outside it keeps it going. The five proofs don't tell us _what_ that something is. They only make one thing unavoidable: something is.
