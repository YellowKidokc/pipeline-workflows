The attached document argues that God can stand as the primitive axiom of a formal system. Take that as granted.

But a system that starts from God has to say more than "God is." It has to say what is true about God, about what God made, about what went wrong, and about whether God provided a way to fix it. Some of those things are God's nature. They could not have been otherwise. Others are things God freely did, or things that happened in history. A careful system keeps those apart, the way physics keeps its laws separate from initial conditions and events.

Work through four parts. For every premise you list, give:
- the premise, in one sentence
- where Scripture reveals it (Old Testament and New Testament)
- its kind: NATURE (true of God necessarily) / ACT (something God freely did or will do) / CREATURE (true of what God made) / EVENT (something that happened in history)
- which rival accounts it rules out (name them: deism, pantheism, Spinoza, Aristotle, Islam, Judaism, Gnosticism, Manichaean dualism, naturalism, or others). Say "none" if it rules out none.
- what breaks in the account if it is left out

Part 1. What must be true about God?
Part 2. What must be true about what God created, including human beings?
Part 3. What must be true about evil: what it is, where it came from, what it did?
Part 4. What must be true about the repair: did God provide a way, what is it, and what must be true for it to work?

Part 5. Honest accounting.
a) Which premises do the real work of picking out this God and this account, and which are shared with other monotheisms? Do not claim every premise is distinguishing if it is not.
b) The smallest set of premises that still picks out the Christian account and no rival.
c) Which premises reason can reach, and which are known only by revelation.
d) Where stating a premise precisely creates a real formal or logical difficulty. Be exact about how hard it is: say whether there are known formal treatments, rather than saying it cannot be done.
