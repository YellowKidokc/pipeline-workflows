The attached document argues that God can stand as the primitive axiom (A0) of a formal derivation system, the way Euclid's postulates or Peano's axioms stand at the start of theirs. For this question, take that as granted.

An ordinary axiom is short. Peano needs a handful of statements and everything else is derived. But if the axiom is the God of the Christian Scriptures, the God of the Old Testament who is revealed in Jesus Christ in the New Testament, then "God is" alone does not say which God. A deist first cause, Spinoza's substance, Aristotle's unmoved mover, and the God of Islam could all be called "God."

So: what must be stated in the axiom itself, so that the axiom is this God and no other?

List the premises you think are essential. For each one:
- the premise, in one sentence
- where it is revealed (Old Testament and New Testament)
- why it has to be in the axiom rather than derived later
- what would go wrong in a system that left it out

Then answer:
1. What is the smallest set that still picks out this God and no other?
2. Which premises could reason reach on its own, and which are known only by revelation?
3. Where does stating any of these premises precisely create a real logical or formal difficulty?

Answer from the Scriptures and the historic Christian confession. Be concrete. Stay under about 3,500 words.
