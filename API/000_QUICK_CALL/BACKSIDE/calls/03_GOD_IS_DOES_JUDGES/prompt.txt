# ============================================================
#  SAVED CALL 03: God is, God does, God judges - and where
#  the physical meets the moral. No input file; prompt alone.
# ============================================================

Take as the starting axiom of a formal system: God is - the God revealed in the Christian canon of Scripture, the sixty-six books of the Old and New Testaments, made known fully in Jesus Christ. Quote Scripture from the King James Version.

An ordinary axiom is a few short statements. This one names a living God, so it needs more said before anything can be derived from it. List the premises that define this God, in four parts:

1. What God is: his nature and being.
2. What God does: his acts in creation, history, and redemption.
3. What God judges: what he holds the world to, and how he judges it.
4. Where the physical meets the moral: how the laws of the physical universe relate to the moral order, as you understand it.

Give a full paragraph for each premise: what it says, where Scripture reveals it (Old and New Testament), why it must be stated rather than derived from the others, and what would go wrong if it were left out. Draw first from the Bible. The historic creeds, the Church Fathers, and plain reasoning may support it, but Scripture leads.

Then say which premises are God's nature (true of him necessarily) and which are his free acts (true because he chose them), and why that difference matters for a formal system.

Be concrete. Do not argue whether God exists. Stay under about 5,000 words.
