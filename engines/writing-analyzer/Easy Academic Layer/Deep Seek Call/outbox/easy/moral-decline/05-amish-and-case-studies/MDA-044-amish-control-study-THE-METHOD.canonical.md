### "It only works because they're small."

They have grown 48 times in a century — from 8,000 to 385,000 — across 622 settlements in 32 states. The model spreads by splitting: when a district grows too big for the buggy radius, it divides. There is no theoretical limit to how big they can get.

### "I wouldn't want to live like that."

That is not the question this study answers. The question is whether rules produce order. Your personal preference does not change the data. And 85–90% of people raised in the system *do* want to live like that, after experiencing the alternative.

### "They free-ride on the outside world."

They pay taxes and take no benefits. They give more to the system than they take. Their model does not require isolation; it requires *rules*. Remove the rules, the results disappear with them.

### "This is cherry-picking. They have problems too."

They do. Genetic diseases from a small starting population. Limited formal education (balanced by 100% literacy and 5% business failure). Occasional abuse cases (at lower rates than the surrounding population). None of these cancel out a systematic advantage that holds across *every* measured area.
