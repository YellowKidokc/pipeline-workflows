# The Peak Window: 1940–1958

The numbers all point to a peak window of coherence (when parts of a system work together smoothly) between about 1940 and 1958. This doesn't mean the era was morally perfect — it wasn't. Jim Crow laws were still in place. Women had limited freedom. Mental illness was hidden and shamed. These are real, serious moral failures.

But coherence and justice are different measurements. Coherence measures how well the parts of a system fit together and support each other. A system can be coherent and still be unjust. What it cannot be is incoherent and stable.

Here's what peak coherence looked like in the data:

### Family

- Divorce rate: 2.2 per thousand people (1960), down from earlier spikes
- Only 28% of adults were unmarried
- Non-marital birth rate for white babies: 3.1% (1965)
- Couples living together without marriage: almost none
- Children raised by both biological parents: the overwhelming norm

### Religious

- Weekly church attendance peaked at 49% in 1958 (Gallup poll)
- Christian identification: about 87%
- "Nones" (people claiming no religion): less than 10%
- The church was the center of community life, not just one option among many

### Institutional Trust

In 1958, 73% of Americans told the National Election Study they trusted the federal government to do the right thing "most of the time" or "just about always." Seventy-three percent. Think about that number. Today it's under 20%.

### Education

- SAT verbal scores: 478
- High school graduation rate was climbing
- Schools taught a shared cultural story — flawed and incomplete, but _shared_

### Media

- The Hays Code (movie censorship rules) was fully enforced
- TV was new and had FCC rules treating broadcasting as a public trust
- No swearing on public media
- Pornography was illegal and hidden
- The Pornography Access Index (a combined measure of legal status, technology, and cultural spread) sat at zero

### Economic

- Union membership: 34.8%
- The gold standard tied money to physical reality
- Personal savings rates were high
- Home ownership grew through programs (VA loans, FHA) that built wealth rather than taking it

**Composite Coherence Index: 89–91 out of 100 — Peak Window**

## What Made It Work (The Physics)

Let me pause and explain what was really happening using the language of the equation. This isn't nostalgia — it's thermodynamics (the science of how energy and order work).

In any coherent system, you need three things working at the same time:

### First: A Strong Negentropic Source (G)

Negentropy (ne-gen-tro-pee) is the opposite of entropy — it's order being pumped into a system. Something has to continuously push order into the system against the natural drift toward chaos. In America's peak window, that source was spread across many institutions that reinforced each other:
- The church provided moral order
- The family provided relational order
- The legal system provided behavioral order
- The economic system provided material order

These weren't separate. They were _coupled_ (connected so they affect each other). The church supported the family. The family supported community involvement. Community involvement supported trust in institutions. Trust supported economic stability. It was a positive feedback loop of coherence — like the slope of a hill: you don't get pushed, the ground itself tilts so you naturally move toward the bottom. Grace changes the shape of the ground under your feet.

### Second: High Observer Receptivity (O)

The population was tuned in to the signal. Church attendance wasn't just a social habit — it was a weekly reset. Families ate dinner together. Neighbors knew each other's names. The observer function (people's ability to receive and respond to the coherence signal) was high because the social setup _demanded_ engagement with the sources of coherence. Like a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal.

### Third: Managed Entropy (S)

Entropy (en-tro-pee) is the natural tendency of things to fall apart and become disordered. It never goes to zero — that's the second law of thermodynamics (the rule that disorder always increases in a closed system). But in a well-functioning system, entropy is absorbed, processed, and spread out before it builds up past a dangerous level. Social problems existed in the 1940s and 1950s. But they were contained by how institutions responded — not perfectly, not always fairly, but structurally. The system had ways to handle disorder without being destroyed by it.

The equation says this setup produces rising coherence: dC/dt > 0 (the rate of change of coherence over time is positive). And that's what the data shows. From 1900 to 1958, in every measurable area, the trends moved toward greater integration.

## What Was Already Fragile

Now I need to push back against the "good old days" reading. Because the equation also shows something the nostalgia misses: the system was _brittle_ (easily broken).

High coherence that's maintained by outside enforcement — rather than by people's inner beliefs — is structurally weak. Think of it like a crystal: highly ordered, but one sharp hit in the right place shatters the whole thing.

Three specific weaknesses were already present in the peak window:

### 1. Conformity as a Substitute for Conviction

Much of the behavioral order of the 1940s and 1950s came from social pressure, not from genuine moral growth. People went to church because that's what you did. People stayed married because divorce was shameful. When the outside pressure was removed — as it would be, catastrophically, in 1968-1973 — the behavioral order collapsed almost overnight. That speed of collapse tells you the inner coherence was lower than the outside measurements suggested.

In equation terms: the observed C (coherence you can measure) was artificially raised by institutional enforcement. The _actual_ C — the coherence held by individual conviction — was lower. The gap between observed and actual coherence is how you measure the system's fragility.

### 2. Justice Deficits as Entropy Seeds

Jim Crow. Women's limited roles. The systematic exclusion of whole groups from the coherence system didn't just harm those groups — it planted seeds of entropy in the system itself. A moral system that says "all men are created equal" while enforcing racial hierarchy contains an internal contradiction. That contradiction is stored entropy, waiting for something to trigger its release.

The civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s was, in this framework, not the cause of moral decline but the _exposure_ of an entropy reservoir that had been building for generations. The system had to process this entropy. The question was whether it could do so without falling apart. The answer, as we'll see in Part 4, was no — because the entropy release from fixing injustice happened at the same time as entropy release from many other sources. Like a shattered glass — you can't unshatter it, and you can't hide the pieces. Every shard is still there, accounted for. What broke stays broken until someone comes with glue from outside.

### 3. The Single-Signal Problem

Almost all of the order-pumping input came through one cultural channel: Protestant Christianity filtered through American civic religion. There was no backup signal. No backup plan. No second source of coherence that could keep the system going if the main source weakened. When church attendance started falling, there was nothing to catch the system.

In information theory terms (the math of how messages are sent and received): the system had no error correction (a way to fix mistakes in the signal). A single-source signal with no backup is maximally vulnerable to noise (interference that messes up the message). When the noise came — and it came from every direction at once — the signal was overwhelmed.

The starting point wasn't paradise. It was a coherent system with real weaknesses, real injustices, and real entropy reserves that hadn't been processed. But it was _coherent_. The pieces supported each other. The equation was positive. dC/dt > 0 (the rate of change of coherence over time was positive).

What happened next — the hairline cracks of 1958-1967 — is the story of how a system that looked unbeatable from the outside was already coming apart at the seams.
