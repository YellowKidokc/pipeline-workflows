# Critical Thresholds

## 1962

**Engel v. Vitale** — The Supreme Court removes prayer from public schools. Within one generation, every FACTS metric starts to decline.

## 1968

**Phase Transition Begins** — SAT scores hit their peak. Social trust starts to fall. The divorce rate speeds up. This is the turning point across many separate sets of data.

## 1971

**Nixon Shock** — The dollar is taken off the gold standard. The link between money and real value is broken. Real wages stop keeping up with productivity. Economic morality becomes a phantom — something that looks real but isn't.

## 1973

**Roe v. Wade** — The sanctity of life is redefined by a court decision, not by a vote. Moral consequences are separated from personal choices by a government decree.

## 2008

**Financial Crisis & Smartphone Saturation** — Trust in institutions collapses below the point where it can recover. Algorithms start capturing human attention. The observer — individual consciousness — begins to dissolve.

## A Note on Methodology

This series uses thermodynamic modeling (the math of heat and energy) on social data. This is not a metaphor. It is a structural match — the same pattern shows up in both places. When entropy (disorder) increases in a closed system (a system with no outside energy coming in), it does not reverse on its own. The same math that governs phase transitions in physics (ice turning to water, water turning to steam) maps exactly onto the social data we see: slow decay, then a speed-up past critical thresholds, then no way back without energy from outside the system.

Every claim has a source. Every measurement is tracked over time. The FACTS framework was built to be falsifiable — if any pillar showed signs of recovery, the argument would get weaker. None has.
