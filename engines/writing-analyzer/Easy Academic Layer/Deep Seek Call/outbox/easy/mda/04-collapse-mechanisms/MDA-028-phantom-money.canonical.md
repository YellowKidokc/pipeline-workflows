## Baseline: 1971 (The Last Anchored Moment)

On August 15, 1971, President Nixon cut the dollar's tie to gold. This one action sent shockwaves through every American institution that had been built on the idea of *stable value*.

Before 1971:

*   $1 USD = 1/35th of an ounce of gold
*   Money represented *claims on something real*
*   Inflation was held back by physical reality
*   Taking on debt had natural limits (you couldn't borrow forever against a limited supply of gold)

**Economic Coherence in 1971: 76**

| Real Wage Growth | Net Worth to Income | Top 1% Wealth Share | Home Price to Income |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2.1%/yr | 4.2x | 35% | 3.0x |

These numbers weren't perfect. But they were *held back by reality*. You couldn't claim more value than you produced for very long—the gold limit forced things to correct.

## The Severing: 1971–1980

When the gold standard broke, it wasn't just a money change. It was a **removal of reality's limits**.

For the first time in American history, the government could create wealth *symbolically* instead of *productively* (by actually making things).

The Federal Reserve immediately started what it called "monetary accommodation." The rest of us call it: printing money to keep spending more than we produce.

**The Mechanism**

1.  The government wants to spend more than it gets from taxes.
2.  Instead of raising taxes, it borrows from the Fed.
3.  The Fed creates new money (out of nothing) to buy government bonds.
4.  That new money flows into the economy.
5.  Prices go up (inflation), but wages don't keep up.
6.  The net result: real wealth moves from people who earn wages to people who own assets and the government.

This isn't capitalism. It isn't socialism. It's **taking wealth through money trickery**.

By 1980:

| 4.1x | 37% |

Notice: the economy was growing on paper, but workers were *losing ground* even as they produced more.
