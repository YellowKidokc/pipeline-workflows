## What Coherence Means in Physics

In physics, **coherence** means how much the parts of a system are correlated and in phase with each other. A coherent system is one where the parts act together — not as random, independent pieces.

This is not a metaphor. It is a measurable property with a clear definition.

In a laser, photons (particles of light) are coherent when they all have the same phase and frequency. In a superconductor, electrons are coherent when they form Cooper pairs (pairs of electrons that move together). In a ferromagnet (like a fridge magnet), atomic spins are coherent when they all point in the same direction.

* * *

## The Order Parameter (χ)

Physicists measure coherence using an **order parameter** (Landau, 1937). This is a number (or set of numbers) that is exactly **zero** in the disordered phase (high-entropy, high-symmetry — think of a messy room), **non-zero** in the ordered phase (low-entropy, broken-symmetry — think of a neatly organized room), and changes in a **non-smooth way** at the phase transition.

Order Parameters Across Systems

Ferromagnet

χ = net magnetization (how strongly the magnet pulls)

Superconductor

χ = Cooper pair density (how many electron pairs exist)

Crystal

χ = crystallographic order parameter (Bragg peak intensity — how perfectly the atoms are lined up)

## The Phase Transition

When the control parameter (temperature T in thermal systems, or a similar variable in social systems) crosses the **critical threshold** Tc, the order parameter goes through its characteristic transition:

Phase Transition Equation

χ ∝ |T − Tc|β for T < Tc (ordered phase)

χ → 0 for T > Tc (disordered phase)

T = temperature | Tc = critical temperature | β = critical exponent (usually 0.3–0.5)

**What this equation means:** The order parameter (χ) follows a power-law pattern based on how far the temperature is from the critical temperature. When the system is in the ordered phase (below Tc), χ is non-zero and grows as you move further from Tc. When the system is in the disordered phase (above Tc), χ drops to zero.

The transition is either sudden (first-order) or follows a power-law divergence (second-order/continuous) — it is not gradual. The key point: **the math is the same no matter what the system is made of.** The same power-law scaling governs ferromagnetism, superconductivity, and (this paper argues) civilizational coherence. The transition is **predictable** once you find Pc.

## A Concrete Example: The Superconductor

Below Tc — Ordered

Electrons form Cooper pairs. Electrical resistance drops to zero. The system shows quantum coherence (particles acting together) at a huge scale.

Above Tc — Disordered

Cooper pairs break apart. Resistance comes back. Coherence is lost.

"The mathematics does not care what the substrate is. It only tracks the order parameter."
