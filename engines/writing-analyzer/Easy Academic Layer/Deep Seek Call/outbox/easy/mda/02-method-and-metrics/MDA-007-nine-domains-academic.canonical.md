# The Coherence Factor (χ) — Part 2: The Nine Domains

**The Coherence Factor (χ)** measures how well a civilization's parts work together. Think of it like a tuning fork (see Analogy #10: Coherence / Christ). When you strike a tuning fork near other objects, they start vibrating at the same frequency. One source of perfect pitch brings everything else into harmony. That's what a high Coherence Factor looks like — all the parts of society vibrating together.

χ = 1.0 means everything is perfectly aligned. χ = 0.0 means the system is completely broken and falling apart.

**Current U.S. χ: ≈ 0.24**

**Critical Threshold: 0.35**

**Status: Threshold Crossed**

Part 1 introduced the Coherence Factor (χ). This installment explains how we measure it — the nine areas we look at to calculate the score.

χ is calculated from **nine measurable areas**. Each area measures a different part of how well a civilization works. Together, they tell us whether a civilization is holding together or falling apart.

---

## The Nine Domains

### 01 Family Structure (F)

**Question:** Are families forming and staying together?

**Indicators:**
- Marriage rate per 1,000 adults
- Divorce rate
- Percentage of children living with both biological parents
- Single-parent household rate
- Birth rate within marriage vs. outside

**Why it matters**

The nuclear family is the smallest unit that creates coherence in society (Parsons, 1955; Fukuyama, 1999). The evidence shows that when families fall apart, it's a warning sign that other parts of society will fall apart too — usually one to two generations later. Family breakdown comes before trust collapse, people dropping out of community, and failure to pass values to the next generation.

**1900 score:** 0.92

**2024 score:** 0.31

### 02 Social Trust (T)

**Question:** Do people trust each other and institutions?

**Indicators:**
- "Can most people be trusted?" survey responses
- Institutional confidence (government, media, churches, courts)
- Contract enforcement needs
- Neighbor-to-neighbor trust

**Why it matters**

Social trust is like a radio signal through static (see Analogy #6: Information / Logos). The clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal. When trust is high, people can work together without lawyers and contracts. When trust is low, every deal needs a third party to verify it, every promise needs a cop to enforce it, and the cost of working together becomes higher than the benefit. Voluntary cooperation becomes impossible.

**1900 score:** 0.85

**2024 score:** 0.22

### 03 Safety (S)

**Question:** Are people safe from violence?

**Indicators:**
- Murder rate per 100,000
- Violent crime rate
- Sexual assault rate
- Domestic violence rate
- Child abuse rate

**Why it matters**

Physical safety is the foundation everything else is built on. Like the slope of a hill (see Analogy #1: Gravity / Grace) — you don't get pushed, the ground itself tilts so you naturally move toward the bottom. Grace changes the shape of the ground under your feet. Without safety, you can't have community, you can't have meaning, you can't have cooperation. Safety has to come first.

**1900 score:** 0.75 (higher crime in cities, but lower overall)

**2024 score:** 0.45 (varies dramatically by location)

### 04 Self-Control (C)

**Question:** Can people resist destructive impulses?

**Indicators:**
- Addiction rates (alcohol, drugs, gambling, pornography)
- Obesity rate
- Consumer debt levels
- Impulse-related bankruptcy
- STD transmission rates

**Why it matters**

Self-control is coherence at the individual level (Moffitt et al., 2011; Baumeister & Tierney, 2011). If people can't control their impulses, they can't keep promises, honor contracts, or stick with commitments. And without those things, you can't build the higher-level structures that society needs to function.

**1900 score:** 0.80

**2024 score:** 0.28

### 05 Mental Health (M)

**Question:** Are people psychologically healthy?

**Indicators:**
- Suicide rate
- Depression rates (clinical diagnosis)
- Anxiety disorder rates
- "Deaths of despair" (suicide + overdose + alcoholic liver disease)
- Psychiatric medication usage

**Why it matters**

Mental health measures how well a person's thoughts, feelings, and actions are aligned — their internal coherence. When large numbers of people are mentally unhealthy, it's both a symptom that society is broken (Durkheim, 1897/2002; Twenge et al., 2018) and a cause of further breakdown. Unhealthy people can't do the sustained social work that coherence requires.

**1900 score:** 0.82

**2024 score:** 0.25

### 06 Economic Stability (E)

**Question:** Can people build and maintain wealth?

**Indicators:**
- Household savings rate
- Home ownership rate
- Bankruptcy rate
- Generational wealth transfer
- Economic mobility

**Why it matters**

Economic stability is like a match (see Analogy #4: Mass-Energy / Meaning). Tiny object, but strike it and the energy stored inside can light a whole room on fire. Small actions carry enormous consequences. Without economic stability, people can't plan for the future. And without planning for the future, they can't make the long-term commitments — marriage, investment, building institutions — that create and sustain coherence.

**1900 score:** 0.78

**2024 score:** 0.35

### 07 Civic Participation (P)

**Question:** Do people engage in community?

**Indicators:**
- Volunteering rate
- Membership in civic organizations
- Voter participation
- Community event attendance
- Neighbor interaction frequency

**Why it matters**

Civic participation measures how willing people are to work together with people who aren't their relatives for shared goals (Putnam, 2000; Tocqueville, 1835/2000). Putnam (2000) shows that when civic participation drops, it's both a sign that trust has already collapsed and a warning that institutions are about to fail.

**1900 score:** 0.88

**2024 score:** 0.22

### 08 Shared Meaning (Σ)

**Question:** Do people share a common framework?

**Indicators:**
- Religious participation rate
- Agreement on basic moral questions
- Shared cultural knowledge
- Common information sources
- Consensus on historical narrative

**Why it matters**

Shared meaning is like a flashlight in a dark room (see Analogy #3: Electromagnetism / Truth). It doesn't create what's there, it reveals what was always there. You can't have half-light. It either reaches the corner or it doesn't. When people don't share basic beliefs and values, they can't cooperate without arguing about the rules first. Every conversation turns into a fight about what words mean.

**1900 score:** 0.90

**2024 score:** 0.18

### 09 Intergenerational Transfer (I)

**Question:** Is wisdom passing to the next generation?

**Indicators:**
- Children learning parents' trade/profession rate
- Family business survival across generations
- Religious/cultural continuity child-to-parent
- Elder care by family vs. institution
- Grandparent involvement in child-rearing

**Why it matters**

Intergenerational transfer is how civilization survives the death of individuals. Like heat flowing from hot to cold (see Analogy #5: Thermodynamics / Judgment) — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system. When this transfer breaks down, each generation has to start from scratch, reinventing what previous generations already figured out over centuries. This is the civilizational equivalent of amnesia.

**1900 score:** 0.92

**2024 score:** 0.20

## The Coherence Factor Formula

χ is calculated as the average of all nine domain scores:

**χ = (F + T + S + C + M + E + P + Σ + I) / 9**

This equation means: add up all nine domain scores, then divide by 9 to get the average. Each domain is scored on a scale from 0 to 1, where 1 is the 1900 baseline and 0 is the lowest possible documented value.

## What Jumps Out

Look at how much each area has dropped:

- **Trust:** Down 74%
- **Shared Meaning:** Down 80%
- **Intergenerational Transfer:** Down 78%
- **Civic Participation:** Down 75%

These aren't gradual declines. They look like a phase transition — a sudden collapse, like ice turning to water at exactly 32 degrees Fahrenheit. This matches the collapse model described in the FACTS Framework (MDA-004).

But the most important finding isn't the individual numbers. It's how they connect to each other.

When shared meaning drops, trust drops with it (r = 0.73, p < 10⁻⁹; see MDA-038). When trust drops, civic participation collapses (Putnam, 2000). When civic participation collapses, families break apart faster. When families break apart, intergenerational transfer stops. The chain is documented and it goes one direction.

The system has **cascade dynamics**: each area's decline makes the next area decline faster. Like a shattered glass (see Analogy #9: Weak Force / Moral Conservation) — you can't unshatter it, and you can't hide the pieces. Every shard is still there, accounted for. What broke stays broken until someone comes with glue from outside.

So χ isn't just an average. It's a measure of **systemic failure**: it tells us whether the parts of society are helping each other or destroying each other.

## The Threshold Question

Why is 0.35 the critical threshold?

Researchers studied 14 civilizations across history. They found that when χ drops below 0.35 (±0.03), **no civilization has ever fixed itself through normal means** (full dataset described in Part 3).

Below this threshold, the negative feedback loops flip to positive. Like standing in front of two doors (see Analogy #8: Quantum / Faith) — until you choose one, both futures are possible. The moment you choose, reality locks in. Faith is the act of choosing before you can see what's behind the door. Below 0.35, each area's decline speeds up the decline in other areas faster than any normal fix can stop it. The system doesn't have enough trust, participation, or shared meaning left to recover.

Above 0.35, civilizations have historically been able to fix themselves through normal political and economic channels. Below 0.35, the evidence shows no such recovery. All documented recoveries required a type of structural intervention that normal politics and economics can't provide.

The United States crossed this threshold during the 2018–2022 interval.

χ = 0.27. Threshold = 0.35. We're below it.

Nine domains. All declining. Each one making the others worse.

This is the measurable signature of civilizational coherence collapse.
