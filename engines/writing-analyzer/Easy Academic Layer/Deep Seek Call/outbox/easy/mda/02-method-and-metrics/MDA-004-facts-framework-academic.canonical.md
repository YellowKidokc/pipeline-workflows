## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Data Collection

Time-series data (measurements over time) were gathered from:

- Longitudinal studies (Putnam's social capital data, Twenge's generational analyses)

Total dataset: **69GB across 45 domains** surveyed (23 with enough time-series data for rigorous fitting), 1945–2025.

### 4.2 Normalization

Each domain was normalized to a 0–1 scale (min-max transformation), where 1.0 = peak observed coherence (best state) and 0.0 = observed minimum (worst state). This allows comparison across different types of data on a common scale.

$$\chi_i(t) = \frac{X_i(t) - X_{i,\min}}{X_{i,\max} - X_{i,\min}}$$

This equation means: for each domain (i), at each time point (t), the coherence value (χ) is calculated by taking the raw value, subtracting the minimum, and dividing by the range (max minus min). This puts everything on a 0-to-1 scale.

### 4.3 Curve Fitting

Each normalized domain time series was fitted using nonlinear least squares (a statistical method for finding the best-fitting curve) to the generalized decay function:

$$\chi(t) = \chi_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda(t - t_0)} + A \sin(\omega t + \phi) + C$$

Where:
- χ₀ = initial coherence value (starting point)
- λ = decay constant (how fast it falls — the main number we care about)
- t₀ = inflection point year (when decline began)
- A, ω, φ = perturbation amplitude and oscillatory parameters (wiggles in the curve)
- C = asymptotic floor (the lowest it can go)

The equation says: coherence at any time equals the initial value times an exponential decay, plus some wiggles, plus a floor.

### 6.3 The Constraint Hypothesis

One explanatory hypothesis consistent with the cross-domain synchronization data: **systemic coherence requires structural constraint as a necessary precondition for stability.**

In open quantum systems (Lindbladian framework), coherence is preserved only when the system is effectively isolated from environmental decoherence channels. When environmental coupling rates increase, coherence decays at a rate proportional to the coupling strength.

In social systems, the analogous "constraint" structures may be:

- Shared normative frameworks and behavioral rules
- Institutional boundaries that enforce delayed gratification
- Structural mechanisms for passing values between generations
- Enforcement systems that maintain the coherence of collective commitments

The 1958–1973 period shows systematic constraint dissolution across all measured domains simultaneously:

- **Legal** (no-fault divorce legislation, weakening of obscenity standards)
- **Economic** (consumer credit liberalization, Keynesian consumption-first paradigm, abandonment of gold standard)
- **Cultural** (expressive individualism as dominant norm, systematic delegitimization of traditional institutions)
- **Technological** (television saturation, oral contraceptive adoption, information cycle acceleration)

**Hypothesis:** Constraint dissolution below a system-specific critical threshold triggers discontinuous phase-transition collapse — not a continuous decay, but a nonlinear systemic reorganization toward a lower-coherence stable state. This matches the Landau phase-transition formalism: χ ≈ |P − Pc|β (coherence is proportional to the distance from the critical threshold, raised to some power).

This hypothesis is operationally falsifiable: domains that have undergone constraint-restoration interventions should show local coherence recovery measurable against the baseline decay trajectory.
