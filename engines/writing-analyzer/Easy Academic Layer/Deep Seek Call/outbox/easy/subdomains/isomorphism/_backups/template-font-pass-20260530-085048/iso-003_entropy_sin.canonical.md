# ISO-003: Entropy and Sin — The Same Shape of Decay

**ID:** ISO-003
**Date:** 2026-03-10
**Status:** Testing

---

## DOMAINS

**Domain A:** Physics — Thermodynamics (the study of heat, energy, and disorder)
**Domain B:** Christian Theology — Hamartiology (the study of sin)

**Concept A:** The Second Law of Thermodynamics — in any isolated system (one that doesn't exchange energy with the outside), disorder always increases. Order breaks down unless energy comes in from outside.

**Concept B:** Sin as moral entropy — moral order breaks down without outside help (grace). God's moral reality (the coherence field) is always perfect and doesn't degrade. But our ability to receive that reality gets worse and worse when we're cut off from it.

---

## THE MAPPING

**Mathematical Form A:**

dS/dt ≥ 0 in isolated systems

S stands for thermodynamic entropy (measured in joules per kelvin, J/K). In any isolated system, entropy never goes down on its own. Order turns into disorder naturally. The only way to make local order increase is to bring in energy from outside the system — but that increases entropy somewhere else. The total never decreases.

**Mathematical Form B:**

dD/dt > 0 where D = moral disorder in systems cut off from grace

In any person or community that is completely closed to outside moral input (grace), moral disorder keeps going up. The coherence field C is conserved (div(C) = 0 — God's moral reality doesn't degrade). But the system's ability to receive and line up with that coherence gets worse. Sin isn't destroying the moral field. It's the system losing its ability to connect with it.

**Shared Structure:**

Both describe the same kind of breakdown in closed systems. You need outside help to reverse it.

1. **Directionality** — Both processes only go one way in closed systems. Entropy goes up. Moral disorder goes up. Neither reverses by itself.
2. **Closure is the problem** — The breakdown isn't caused by what's inside the system. It's caused by the system being closed. An open thermodynamic system can decrease local entropy. An open moral system (one receiving grace) can decrease moral disorder.
3. **Outside input reverses breakdown** — In physics: outside energy. In theology: grace. Both restore local order, and the source pays the cost.
4. **Total conservation** — In physics: total entropy of the universe plus system never decreases. In theology: div(C) = 0 — the coherence field is conserved. What changes is the system's ability to connect, not the field itself.
5. **Irreversibility without help** — A broken egg doesn't put itself back together. A fallen person doesn't fix themselves. Both need work from outside the system.

**What Is NOT Claimed:**

- NOT claiming sin IS entropy — sin works on moral coherence, entropy works on thermodynamic microstates. The measurement units are completely different (J/K vs. the coherence variable C).
- NOT claiming moral decay can be measured in joules per kelvin — the isomorphism (same shape) is about the pattern of breakdown, not the units.
- NOT claiming thermodynamics has moral content — the Second Law is morally neutral. The parallel is in the shape of how closed systems break down.
- NOT claiming this proves total depravity — it shows that SOME degree of moral breakdown is unavoidable in closed systems, but how fast and how bad depends on the coupling coefficient α(u).
- NOT claiming all entropy is bad — thermodynamic entropy enables heat flow, chemical reactions, life itself. The parallel is specifically about ISOLATED system entropy, where no outside input compensates.
- NOT claiming this is a new idea — the entropy/sin parallel has been noted before. What's new is the formal structural mapping with the coupling coefficient and the conservation of the coherence field.

## TESTS

**Swap Test:** Can you put moral decay in the physics slot?

No. Thermodynamic entropy is measurable in joules/kelvin using Boltzmann's equation S = k_B ln(Ω). Moral entropy works on the coherence variable C, which can't be reduced to counting microstates (or if it can, we don't know how yet). You can't plug moral disorder into a heat engine calculation and get a meaningful answer.

However, the topology (shape of the relationship) is identical:

- Steady increase in closed systems: yes (both)
- Reversal only through outside input: yes (both)
- Conservation of the source field: yes (both — total energy conserved, total coherence conserved)
- Breakdown is of the system's connection, not the field: yes (both — entropy doesn't destroy energy, sin doesn't destroy God's moral reality)

The swap test PASSES at the topological level and FAILS at the metric level. This is expected for a structural isomorphism (same shape) rather than identity (same substance).

**Swap test result: PASSED (topological). FAILED (metric). This is expected for a cross-domain structural isomorphism.**

**Prediction in Domain A (Physics):**

- No perpetual motion machines. Every attempt to build a self-sustaining closed system that maintains or increases its own order will fail. This has been tested for about 170 years with zero exceptions.
- Every living system maintains order only by importing energy from outside (food, sunlight) and exporting entropy (heat, waste). Life is an open system. Closed biological systems die.
- The heat death of the universe (maximum entropy, no usable energy left) is the thermodynamic endpoint of a system with no outside input. If the universe is a closed system, heat death is inevitable.

**Prediction in Domain B (Theology):**

- No self-salvation (Pelagianism fails). A person fully closed to grace cannot reverse their own moral decay, for the same structural reason a closed thermodynamic system cannot reverse its entropy. Pelagianism is the moral equivalent of a perpetual motion machine.
- Every morally ordered community maintains that order only by staying open to outside moral input (grace, revelation, worship). Communities that close themselves to these inputs decay morally over time, no matter how good they started. History confirms this pattern again and again.
- "Total depravity" in Reformed theology maps to heat death — the endpoint of a moral system with zero grace coupling (α = 0) for long enough. Not that every action is maximally evil, but that no part of the system is untouched by the decay. Just like heat death doesn't mean zero energy but zero usable energy.
- Sanctification (becoming more holy) maps to refrigeration — local entropy decrease achieved by connecting to an outside energy source. The cost is paid by the source (Christ), not the system.

**Bidirectional:** Yes.

- Physics → Theology: The Second Law limits which salvation theories are structurally possible. Any theology claiming self-salvation violates the structural equivalent of energy conservation.
- Theology → Physics: The doctrine of sin predicts that any physical system modeling moral agents must include an entropy-like variable that increases in isolation. This can be tested in computer models of agents and social dynamics simulations.

**Falsification:**

1. **In Physics:** Show thermodynamic entropy spontaneously decreasing in a truly isolated system (not a tiny fluctuation, but a sustained large-scale decrease). This would break the Second Law and remove the structural need for outside input.
2. **In Theology:** Show a moral community that maintains or increases its moral order over many generations with zero outside moral input — no revelation, no grace, no contact with any moral source beyond its own internal resources. If such a community exists, the moral entropy thesis fails.
3. **Break the topology:** Show that the structural features (steady increase, dependence on closure, reversal-by-outside-input, conservation of source field) do not actually match between the domains. This would require showing that one domain has a structural feature the other lacks at the topological level.
