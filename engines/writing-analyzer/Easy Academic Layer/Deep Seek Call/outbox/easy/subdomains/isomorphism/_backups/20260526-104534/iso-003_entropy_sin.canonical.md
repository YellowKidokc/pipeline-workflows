# ISO-003: Entropy and Sin — The Same Shape of Decay

## Domains

**Domain A:** Physics — Thermodynamics (the study of heat, energy, and disorder)
**Domain B:** Christian Theology — Hamartiology (the study of sin)

**Concept A:** The Second Law of Thermodynamics — in isolated systems (systems with no energy coming in or out), disorder always increases. Order breaks down unless energy comes from outside.

**Concept B:** Sin as moral entropy — moral order breaks down unless help comes from outside (grace). God's moral reality (the coherence field) never degrades, but our ability to receive it gets worse over time in closed systems.

## The Mapping

**Mathematical Form A:**

dS/dt ≥ 0 in isolated systems

*Here, S stands for thermodynamic entropy (measured in joules per kelvin). In any isolated system, entropy never goes down. Order naturally turns into disorder. The only way to decrease disorder in one place is to bring energy in from outside, which increases disorder somewhere else. The total disorder of the whole system never decreases.*

**Mathematical Form B:**

dD/dt > 0 where D = moral disorder in systems decoupled from grace

*Here, D stands for moral disorder. In any person or community that is completely closed off from outside moral help (grace), moral disorder keeps getting worse. The coherence field C is conserved (div(C) = 0 — God's moral reality doesn't break down). But the system's ability to connect with that coherence gets worse over time. Sin isn't destroying the moral field. Sin is the system losing its ability to tune in to it.*

**Shared Structure:**

Both describe the same kind of irreversible breakdown in closed systems. Both need outside input to reverse.

1. **Directionality** — Both processes only go one way in closed systems. Entropy goes up. Moral disorder goes up. Neither reverses on its own.

2. **Closure is the problem** — The breakdown isn't caused by what's inside the system. It's caused by the system being closed. An open thermodynamic system (one that lets energy in) can decrease local entropy. An open moral system (one that receives grace) can decrease moral disorder.

3. **External input reverses degradation** — In physics: energy from outside. In theology: grace. Both restore local order, and the source pays the cost.

4. **Total conservation** — In physics: the total entropy of the universe plus the system never goes down. In theology: div(C) = 0 — the coherence field is conserved. What changes is the system's ability to connect, not the field itself.

5. **Irreversibility without intervention** — A broken egg doesn't put itself back together. A fallen person doesn't fix themselves. Both need work from outside the system.

**What Is NOT Claimed:**

- NOT claiming sin IS entropy — sin works on moral coherence, entropy works on thermodynamic microstates (tiny particle arrangements). They use different measurement units (joules per kelvin vs. the coherence variable C).
- NOT claiming moral decay can be measured in joules per kelvin — the match is in the *shape* of the breakdown (topology), not the measurement units (metrics).
- NOT claiming thermodynamics has moral content — the Second Law is morally neutral. The parallel is in the structure of how closed systems break down.
- NOT claiming this proves total depravity — it shows that *some* moral decay is unavoidable in closed systems, but how fast and how far depends on the coupling coefficient α(u).
- NOT claiming all entropy is bad — thermodynamic entropy makes heat flow, chemical reactions, and life itself possible. The parallel is specifically about ISOLATED system entropy, where no outside input compensates.
- NOT claiming this is a new idea — the entropy/sin parallel has been noted before. What's new is the formal structural mapping with the coupling coefficient and the conservation of the coherence field.

## Tests

**Swap Test:** Can you put moral decay in the physics slot?

No. Thermodynamic entropy is measurable in joules per kelvin using Boltzmann's equation S = k_B ln(Ω). Moral entropy works on the coherence variable C, which can't be reduced to counting particle arrangements (or if it can, we don't know how yet). You can't plug moral disorder into a heat engine calculation and get a meaningful answer.

However, the *shape* is identical:

- Steady increase in closed systems: yes (both)
- Reversal only through outside input: yes (both)
- Conservation of the source field: yes (both — total energy conserved, total coherence conserved)
- Breakdown is of the system's connection, not the field: yes (both — entropy doesn't destroy energy, sin doesn't destroy God's moral reality)

The swap test PASSES at the topological level (same shape) and FAILS at the metric level (different units). This is what you expect for a structural isomorphism (same shape) rather than identity (same substance).

**Swap test result: PASSED (topological). FAILED (metric). This is expected for a cross-domain structural isomorphism.**

**Prediction in Domain A (Physics):**

- No perpetual motion machines. Every attempt to build a self-sustaining closed system that maintains or increases its own order will fail. This has been tested for about 170 years with zero exceptions.
- Every living system maintains order only by importing energy from outside (food, sunlight) and exporting entropy (heat, waste). Life is an open system. Closed biological systems die.
- The heat death of the universe (maximum entropy, no usable energy left) is the thermodynamic endpoint of a system with no outside input. If the universe is a closed system, heat death is inevitable.

**Prediction in Domain B (Theology):**

- No self-salvation (Pelagianism fails). A moral agent fully closed to grace cannot reverse its own moral decay, for the same structural reason a closed thermodynamic system cannot reverse its entropy. Pelagianism is the moral equivalent of a perpetual motion machine.
- Every morally ordered community maintains that order only by staying open to outside moral input (grace, revelation, worship). Communities that close themselves to these inputs decay morally over time, no matter how good they started. History confirms this pattern again and again.
- "Total depravity" in Reformed theology maps to heat death — the endpoint of a moral system with zero grace coupling (α = 0) for long enough. Not that every action is maximally evil, but that no part of the system is untouched by the decay. Like heat death doesn't mean zero energy — it means zero usable energy.
- Sanctification (the process of becoming more holy) maps to refrigeration — local entropy decrease achieved by connecting to an outside energy source. The cost is paid by the source (Christ), not the system.

**Bidirectional:** Yes.

- Physics → Theology: The Second Law limits which salvation theories are structurally possible. Any theology claiming self-salvation violates the structural equivalent of energy conservation.
- Theology → Physics: The doctrine of sin predicts that any physical system modeling moral agents must include an entropy-like variable that increases in isolation. This is testable in computer models of agents and social dynamics simulations.

**Falsification:**

1. **In Physics:** Show thermodynamic entropy spontaneously decreasing in a truly isolated system (not a tiny random fluctuation, but a sustained large-scale decrease). This would break the Second Law and remove the structural need for outside input.
2. **In Theology:** Show a moral community that maintains or increases its moral order over many generations with zero outside moral input — no revelation, no grace, no contact with any moral source beyond its own internal resources. If such a community exists, the moral entropy thesis fails.
3. **Break the topology:** Show that the structural features (steady increase, dependence on closure, reversal by outside input, conservation of the source field) do not actually match between the two domains. This would require showing that one domain has a structural feature the other lacks at the topological level.
