## Truth as a Signal

Think of truth like a radio wave. When you send a signal, it travels through the air. The signal can get weaker over distance. That's called **attenuation** (the signal losing power as it travels).

Here's the equation for how a signal weakens:

**P_received = P_transmitted × e^(-αd)**

This means: The power you receive equals the power you sent out, times a number that gets smaller as distance increases. The "α" (alpha) is how fast the signal fades. The "d" is the distance.

In plain English: The farther you are from the source of truth, the weaker it gets. But it's still the same truth — just quieter.

Deception works differently. Deception adds **noise** (random interference) to the signal. It doesn't just make the truth quieter — it changes the message.

Think of it like the **Information / Logos** analogy: "Like a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal."

When someone lies, they're adding static to the channel. The truth is still there, but now it's mixed with garbage. You can't tell what's real anymore.

## Love as a Force

Love works like the **strong nuclear force** (the force that holds atoms together). This force is weird. Most forces get weaker as you move apart. Gravity gets weaker. Magnetism gets weaker. But the strong nuclear force gets *stronger* as you try to pull things apart.

Think of the **Strong Nuclear Force / Love** analogy: "Like a parent holding a child's hand — loose grip when you're walking together, iron grip when you try to run into traffic. The closer you stay, the more freedom you feel."

That's love. When you're close to someone you love, it feels easy. When you try to pull away, love pulls you back. The more distance you try to create, the stronger the pull gets.

Here's the equation for the strong nuclear force:

**F_strong ≈ g²/r × e^(-r/λ)**

This means: The force (F) equals a constant (g²) divided by the distance (r), times a number that drops off as distance increases. But the key part is the 1/r — as r gets smaller (you get closer), the force gets bigger.

In plain English: Love gets stronger the closer you are. It's not weak at a distance — it's strongest up close.

## Addiction as a Vacuum Tear

Addiction is the opposite of love. Love holds things together. Addiction tears things apart.

In physics, a **vacuum tear** is when empty space itself rips open. This doesn't happen in normal life, but the math says it could. When it happens, everything near the tear gets sucked in. There's no escape.

Addiction works the same way. The thing you're addicted to becomes a hole in your life. Everything else — family, work, health — gets sucked into that hole. You can't pull away because the pull is too strong.

Think of the **Quantum / Faith** analogy: "Like standing in front of two doors — until you choose one, both futures are possible. The moment you choose, reality locks in. Faith is the act of choosing before you can see what's behind the door."

Addiction locks you into one choice. You can't see the other doors anymore. All possibilities collapse into one — the addiction.

## The Physics of Deception

Deception isn't just lying. It's creating **destructive interference** (waves that cancel each other out). When two waves meet and one is upside-down compared to the other, they cancel. You get nothing.

That's what deception does. It creates a wave that cancels out the truth. You end up with silence where the truth should be.

Here's the equation for destructive interference:

**ψ_total = ψ₁ + ψ₂ = 0 when ψ₁ = -ψ₂**

This means: The total wave equals wave one plus wave two. If wave one is the exact opposite of wave two, they add up to zero. Nothing.

In plain English: A lie that perfectly opposes the truth cancels the truth out. You're left with nothing real.
