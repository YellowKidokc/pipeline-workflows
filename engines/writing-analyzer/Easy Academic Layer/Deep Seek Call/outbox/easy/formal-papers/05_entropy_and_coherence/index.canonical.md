# Entropy and Coherence

The adversary, one-directional entropy, and the master integral that holds the series together.

## The One-Directional Drain: The Adversary and Unopposed Entropy

Here's the problem. Everything falls apart. Not sometimes — always. Your coffee cools down. Your phone battery dies. Your body ages. Your relationships fray. This isn't just bad luck. It's a law of physics.

The Second Law of Thermodynamics says that in any closed system (a system with no energy coming in from outside), disorder always increases. Scientists call this increase in disorder "entropy." Think of it like heat flowing from hot to cold — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system. That's the thermodynamics/judgment analogy. Judgment isn't God being mean. It's the natural direction of things when left alone.

The Bible describes an adversary — a force that opposes life, order, and connection. In Genesis, the serpent introduces chaos into a perfect garden. In the New Testament, Jesus calls Satan "the father of lies" (John 8:44). Lies break things. They create static in the signal. They increase entropy in relationships, in minds, in societies.

Here's the connection: entropy is the physical signature of the adversary. It's the one-directional drain. You can't reverse it on your own. Every system you put your hand to will eventually run down. Your body will die. Your reputation will fade. Your best efforts will be forgotten.

The equation for entropy change in a closed system is:

ΔS ≥ 0

This means the change in entropy (ΔS) is always greater than or equal to zero. It never goes negative on its own. Disorder only increases.

This isn't a metaphor. It's a measurable fact. Every experiment ever done confirms it. And it matches the biblical story: the world is running down, and we can't stop it.

## The Master Integral: Christ and Ultimate Coherence

Now here's the good news. What if there's an outside intervention? What if something — someone — from outside the system can reverse the drain?

In mathematics, an integral is a way of adding up many small pieces to get a whole. If you have scattered points, the integral gathers them into a smooth curve. If you have scattered data, the integral shows you the big picture.

Christ functions as the "master integral" — the one who takes all the scattered, broken pieces of creation and brings them back into coherence (things working together in harmony). Coherence is the opposite of entropy. Where entropy scatters, coherence gathers. Where entropy breaks down, coherence builds up.

Think of it like a tuning fork. When you strike it near other objects, they start vibrating at the same frequency. One source of perfect pitch brings everything else into harmony. That's the coherence/Christ analogy. Christ is the tuning fork. Everything else tunes to him.

The Apostle Paul describes this in Colossians 1:17:

> "He is before all things, and in him all things hold together."

Not "some things." Not "spiritual things." All things. The same Christ who walked on water is the one who keeps protons stuck to neutrons. He is the coherence that holds the universe together at every level.

Here's the key equation. In information theory, the amount of information in a signal is measured by how much it reduces uncertainty. The clearer the signal, the more information it carries. Sin is the static that scrambles the message. Truth is the signal. Christ is the one who clears the channel.

The cross is where the one-directional drain gets plugged. Like a shattered glass — you can't unshatter it, and you can't hide the pieces. Every shard is still there, accounted for. What broke stays broken until someone comes with glue from outside. That's the weak force/moral conservation analogy. The cross is the glue from outside.

The resurrection is the proof that the reversal worked. Death is the ultimate entropy — the complete breakdown of the biological system. If Christ reversed that, he can reverse anything.

## What This Means

You don't have to pretend the world isn't falling apart. It is. That's not your fault, and you can't fix it. But you can plug into the one who can.

The master integral doesn't just add up your good deeds. It takes your broken pieces — your sins, your failures, your entropy — and integrates them into a story that ends in resurrection.

The choice is simple. You can keep trying to reverse entropy on your own, which is like trying to push water uphill. Or you can accept the outside intervention that already did the work.

The physics is clear. The theology is clear. The math is clear.

The universe is running down. But the one who holds it together is still holding.
