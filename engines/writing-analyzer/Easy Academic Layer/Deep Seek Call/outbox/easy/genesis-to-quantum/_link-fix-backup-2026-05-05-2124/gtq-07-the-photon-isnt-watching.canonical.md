# System Audit

This paper challenges the idea that your mind directly controls quantum experiments. It argues for a more limited view of what "measurement" really means. The correction is strong, but some of its conclusions go beyond what the evidence actually shows.

## What Holds

- The idea that measurement does **not** need a human mind to happen is correct. This matches what most physicists believe.
- Separating "watching" from "wanting" is an important and necessary step.
- Criticizing overblown claims about consciousness affecting quantum systems is fair and justified.

## What Needs Proof

- The data from the PEAR and Global Consciousness Project needs much stricter math to back it up.
- The line between a physical measurement and an information-based interpretation needs to be drawn more clearly.
- The role of consciousness in larger system dynamics is still an open question.

## Where We Overreached

- Statements that say consciousness has **no** role at all in physical systems go beyond what most scientists agree on.
- Dismissing all strange data as "not physical influence" might be too quick a judgment.
- Some conclusions are based on interpretation, not on controlled experiments.
