# How Lies Kill: The Mathematics of Sin

Everyone who has ever told a lie knows the feeling that follows. Not guilt — that comes later. The immediate feeling is like building something. You told one lie, and now you need to remember it. You need a second lie to back it up if someone asks a follow-up question. Then a third to cover the gap between the first two. Then you need to remember all three, and which person heard which version, and what the truth actually was so you don't accidentally tell it.

One lie is a seed. Within hours, it's a structure. Within weeks, it's a load-bearing wall in a building you never meant to build.

This article argues that this experience is not a metaphor for something deeper. It *is* the deeper thing. It is the Second Law of Thermodynamics (the law that says disorder always increases) working in the moral world, and it kills by the same mechanism that makes every engine stop and every star burn out.
