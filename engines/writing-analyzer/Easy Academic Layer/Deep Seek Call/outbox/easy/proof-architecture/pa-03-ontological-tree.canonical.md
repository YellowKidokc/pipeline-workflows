# The Ontological Decision Tree

This is a logical tree that starts from "nothing" and branches out to "God." At every fork, we list every possible answer. Each dead branch shows exactly why it dies. Regular physics and Theophysics (the physics of God) climb the same tree. They only split apart at certain points.

### Method: Four Types of Questions × Four Ways to Die

**Question Type 1: "Does X hold?"**
A yes/no question with escape routes. Branches: Yes / No / Meaningless / Illusory. Max 4 branches.

**Question Type 2: "What IS X?"**
Asks what something really is. Run every category: material / mental / informational / relational / mathematical / brute / composite.

**Question Type 3: "What grounds X?"**
Asks where something comes from. Branches: nothing (brute fact) / external (risk of infinite regress) / itself (self-grounding) / illusory / necessary.

**Question Type 4: "What must X be?"**
Not branching — deduction. Given what survived, figure out what properties it must have.

**Death Condition: Self-Refutation**
The claim destroys itself when you say it.

**Death Condition: Infinite Regress**
The answer just pushes the question back without ever solving it.

**Death Condition: Empirical Contradiction**
The claim goes against what we actually observe.

**Death Condition: Logical Incoherence**
The claim contradicts something already proven in the tree.

### Level 0 — Bedrock

**Q0: Does anything exist?**

This question proves itself. The act of asking it assumes there is someone asking.

- **Q0-A: No, nothing exists.** → **DEAD END (Self-Refutation)** To say "nothing exists," you have to exist to say it. The statement destroys itself.
- **Q0-B: Yes, something exists.** → **CONTINUES** This is undeniable. Even the most extreme skeptic (like Descartes with his "I think, therefore I am") ends up here. The act of doubting is itself something that exists. This forces the next question.
- **Q0-C: The question is meaningless.** → **DEAD END (Self-Refutation)** Saying something is meaningless requires a meaning-making being to say it. The statement assumes what it denies.
- **Q0-D: Existence is an illusion.** → **COLLAPSES into Q0-B** An illusion requires something to be an illusion *for*. The illusion itself exists as an illusion. This routes back to "something exists."

**SYS-B:** A1.1 — "Something exists rather than nothing."
**SYS-A:** Agrees completely. No split here.

**Worldview Survival:** 18 tracked — 16 accept, 2 partial, 0 eliminated.

### Level 1 — Distinction

**Q1: Can that which exists be told apart from that which does not?**

This is forced by Q0-B. If "something" exists, the word implies it is not "nothing." But is that difference real?

- **Q1-A: No — being and non-being are the same.** → **DEAD END (Logical Incoherence)** This contradicts Q0-B. If existence and non-existence are the same, then "something exists" means nothing. You've destroyed your own starting point.
- **Q1-B: Yes — the difference between being and non-being is real.** → **CONTINUES** This is what "existence" *means*. To exist is to be different from not-existing. This forces the next question.
- **Q1-C: The difference is real at one level but an illusion at another.** → **COSTLY** Some philosophies (like Advaita Vedanta) live here: the everyday difference is real, but the ultimate difference is an illusion. But saying THIS requires telling the two levels apart — which is a recursion. It carries a cost forward.

**SYS-B:** A1.2 — "Existence requires distinguishability."
**SYS-A:** Agrees. We define things we can observe by how they are different from other things.

**Worldview Survival:** 15 accept, 2 partial, 1 rejects.

### Level 2 — The Information Fork

**Q2: What IS a distinction?**

Forced by Q1-B. Distinction is real. What is it, at its most basic level? This is the first major kill zone — six worldviews die here at the same time.

- **Q2-A: A property of matter — physical objects differ.** → **CIRCULAR** This assumes matter exists. We haven't proven matter exists yet. We only have "something exists" and "distinction is real." What IS matter, if not a pattern of distinctions?
- **Q2-B: A property of mind — observers create difference.** → **CIRCULAR** This assumes observers exist. We haven't proven consciousness exists yet. What tells the observer apart from the non-observer?
- **Q2-C: A distinction IS information — the smallest unit of difference is the bit.** → **CONTINUES** This is the definition. Claude Shannon (1948) formalized exactly this: information = reducing uncertainty between two states you can tell apart. One distinction = one bit. No other building blocks needed. This forces the next question.
- **Q2-D: A brute, unanalyzable primitive.** → **DEAD END (Collapses)** A "brute distinction" that carries no information is the same as no distinction (violates Q1-B). If it DOES carry content, you've just restated Q2-C.
- **Q2-E: A mathematical relation.** → **MERGES into Q2-C** Mathematical relations ARE informational structures. This is Q2-C in different words. Not wrong — just not a separate branch.
- **Q2-F: A structural relation (Ontic Structural Realism).** → **COLLAPSES into Q2-C** "Relations all the way down." But relations between what? If you get rid of the things being related, you have relations with no content → Q2-D (dead). If the things carry content, then "structure" = "pattern of distinctions" → Q2-C. Not a separate branch.

**SYS-B:** A1.3 — "Distinguishability IS information; information is the most basic building block of reality."
**SYS-A:** Operates in Q2-A territory. It works, but it's logically circular at this level.

**First Major Kill Zone:** 6 worldviews reject A1.3 for the same structural reason. 2 fully accept, 9 partial, 7 reject.
