# Coherence as Cosmic Intelligence: A Formal Architecture for Interdisciplinary Verification

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized research framework that bridges theoretical physics and philosophical theology through a machine-verifiable coherence architecture. The central thesis posits that structural isomorphisms between quantum coherence phenomena and theological concepts of divine intelligence can be formalized into a testable, falsifiable research program. Rather than advancing analogical reasoning, this work proposes a claim ladder supported by a formal proof layer, amenable to computational verification. The argument proceeds through four sequential moves: (1) articulation of the foundational question, (2) identification of the load-bearing proof structure, (3) construction of a three-tier claim hierarchy, and (4) derivation of a master equation governing cross-domain coherence. This framework is designed to meet institutional standards for interdisciplinary research funding, particularly those established by the Templeton Foundation.

The inquiry motivating this research program may be stated as follows: *What would persuade an institution that this is not analogy but a research program?* This question, hereafter referred to as Templeton's Question, establishes the epistemic threshold for the present investigation. ## 3. The Three-Tier Claim Ladder

The claim ladder constitutes the central argumentative structure of this research program. It is organized into three hierarchical tiers, each of which must be established before progression to the subsequent tier:

**Tier 1: Empirical Coherence** – Demonstrates that quantum coherence phenomena exhibit structural properties isomorphic to those attributed to divine intelligence in classical theological frameworks. This tier is established through formal comparison of mathematical structures governing coherence dynamics (e.g., density matrix evolution, entanglement entropy) with theological models of omniscience and omnipresence.

**Tier 3: Predictive Extension** – Derives testable predictions from the formal isomorphism, thereby transforming the framework from a descriptive model into a predictive research program. These predictions are, in principle, falsifiable through experimental or computational investigation.
