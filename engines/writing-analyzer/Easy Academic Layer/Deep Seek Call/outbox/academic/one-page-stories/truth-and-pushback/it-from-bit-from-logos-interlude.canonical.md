# What Lives for Eternity and What Does Not: The Adversary's Information Problem, the Correction Wheeler's Framework Required, and the Logos Grounding of Informational Ontology

## Abstract

This article addresses a foundational lacuna in John Archibald Wheeler's "It from Bit" ontological program. While Wheeler posited that physical reality derives from informational processes, his framework does not account for the metaphysical ground that renders information stable, truthful, and temporally persistent. The present work proposes a necessary extension: the transition from "It from Bit" to "Bit from Logos," wherein the Logos—understood as the divine rational principle articulated in Johannine theology—serves as the ontological substrate that grounds informational truth. This extension is developed through a four-move structural argument: (1) identification of the persistence problem for informational ontology, (2) formalization of the adversary's information problem as a constraint on any purely physicalist information theory, (3) articulation of the correction required within Wheeler's framework, and (4) the positing of the Logos as the necessary ground. The argument is presented as an interlude preceding the full canonical treatment, which contains complete proofs, tables, and source structure.

## 01. What Lives for Eternity and What Does Not

### Thesis Statement

If reality is fundamentally informational in nature, then the question arises: what metaphysical condition ensures that information is sufficiently true, stable, and coherent to persist across time? Wheeler's "It from Bit" thesis—the claim that every physical entity (the "It") derives its existence from information-theoretic processes (the "Bit")—constitutes a significant advance in ontological physics. However, the framework remains incomplete insofar as it does not address the ground of information itself. The present work argues that a further step is required: "Bit from Logos," wherein the Logos (λόγος) functions as the transcendent principle that grounds the truth conditions of information.

### Formal Statement of the Problem

Let \( \mathcal{I} \) denote the set of all possible informational states, and let \( \mathcal{P} \) denote the set of physical configurations realizable in spacetime. Wheeler's thesis can be formalized as a mapping:

\[
\Phi: \mathcal{I} \rightarrow \mathcal{P}, \quad \Phi(i) = p,
\]

where \( i \in \mathcal{I} \) and \( p \in \mathcal{P} \). The mapping \( \Phi \) asserts that for every physical configuration \( p \), there exists an informational state \( i \) such that \( p \) is the instantiation of \( i \). The question left unresolved is: what grounds the truth-preserving properties of \( \mathcal{I} \) itself? That is, under what conditions does an informational state \( i \) correspond to a physically realizable configuration \( p \) in a manner that is stable across temporal evolution?

### The Persistence Condition

Define a persistence operator \( \mathcal{T}_t \) that maps an informational state at time \( t_0 \) to its counterpart at time \( t_1 \):

\[
\mathcal{T}_{t_1 - t_0}: \mathcal{I}_{t_0} \rightarrow \mathcal{I}_{t_1}.
\]

For information to be "true enough to last," the following condition must hold:

\[
\forall i \in \mathcal{I}_{t_0}, \quad \exists i' \in \mathcal{I}_{t_1} : \mathcal{T}(i) = i' \land \Phi(i') \in \mathcal{P}_{t_1}.
\]

This condition is nontrivial. Without a grounding principle, there is no guarantee that informational states remain coherent under temporal transformation. The present work identifies the Logos as the necessary ground for this persistence condition.

## 02. The Adversary's Information Problem

### Structural Exposition in Four Moves

The argument proceeds through four logically sequential moves:

**Move 1: What Lives for Eternity and What Does Not.** This move establishes the persistence problem: informational states, if ungrounded, are subject to degradation, contradiction, or collapse. Only information that participates in a transcendent truth condition can persist eternally.

**Move 2: If Reality Is Information, What Makes Information True Enough to Last?** This move formalizes the epistemic requirement: for any informational ontology to be coherent, there must exist a criterion of truth that is invariant under transformations of the informational substrate.

**Move 3: The Adversary's Information Problem.** This move identifies a specific constraint: any purely physicalist account of information must contend with the possibility of adversarial informational environments—contexts in which information is systematically distorted, degraded, or falsified. The adversary represents any mechanism (entropic, stochastic, or malign) that undermines the fidelity of informational transmission across time.

**Move 4: The Correction Wheeler's Framework Required.** This move argues that Wheeler's "It from Bit" requires a supplementary grounding principle—"Bit from Logos"—to resolve the adversary's information problem.

### Formalization of the Adversary's Information Problem

Let \( \mathcal{C} \) be a communication channel through which informational states are transmitted across time. Let \( \mathcal{N} \) be a noise operator representing adversarial interference. The received informational state \( i' \) is given by:

\[
i' = \mathcal{N}(i) + \epsilon,
\]

where \( \epsilon \) represents stochastic error. The adversary's information problem states that for any finite \( \mathcal{I} \), there exists a noise operator \( \mathcal{N}^* \) such that:

\[
\forall i \in \mathcal{I}, \quad \mathcal{N}^*(i) \notin \mathcal{I}_{\text{true}},
\]

where \( \mathcal{I}_{\text{true}} \subset \mathcal{I} \) is the subset of truth-preserving informational states. Without a transcendent ground, the existence of such an adversarial operator cannot be ruled out.

## 03. The Correction Wheeler's Framework Required

### The "Bit from Logos" Extension

The correction to Wheeler's framework consists in the introduction of a grounding operator \( \Lambda \), representing the Logos, such that:

\[
\Lambda: \mathcal{I} \rightarrow \mathcal{I}_{\text{true}}, \quad \Lambda(i) = i_{\text{true}},
\]

where \( i_{\text{true}} \) is the informational state that satisfies the persistence condition under all admissible noise operators. The full ontological chain becomes:

\[
\text{Logos} \rightarrow \text{Bit} \rightarrow \text{It},
\]

or, in formal notation:

\[
\forall p \in \mathcal{P}, \quad \exists i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{true}} : \Phi(i) = p, \quad \text{where } i = \Lambda^{-1}(p).
\]

### Scriptural Grounding

The identification of the Logos as the ground of informational truth finds support in the Johannine prologue: "In the beginning was the Word [Λόγος], and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1, Nestle-Aland 28th edition). The term λόγος carries connotations of rational principle, ordering structure, and communicative truth—properties that correspond precisely to the grounding function required by the persistence condition.

### Methodological Context

The isomorphism between the informational ontology of Wheeler and the Logos theology of the Johannine tradition was identified through structural comparison of the respective frameworks. Wheeler's "It from Bit" posits a derivation of physical reality from information; the Logos tradition posits a derivation of created order from divine reason. The present work formalizes this correspondence as a necessary extension rather than a mere analogy.
