# The 24 Anti-Properties: A Structural Taxonomy of Moral Inversion

## Abstract

This article proposes that if mathematical and moral truth share twenty-four isomorphic properties, then evil may be formally characterized as their exact structural inversion. Rather than treating evil as an exclusively emotional or behavioral category, the present analysis defines it through a rigorous structural framework derived from cross-domain isomorphism between physical law and moral ontology. The result is a taxonomy—not a list of vices—in which evil becomes legible as a systematic inversion of truth's formal properties. This paper presents the abbreviated reader-facing exposition; the complete canonical treatment, including full derivations, proofs, and source structures, is referenced herein.

## 1. Introduction: The Structural Definition of Anti-Truth

The central thesis of this investigation may be stated as follows: If mathematical and moral truth share twenty-four properties, then evil constitutes their exact inverse structure. This claim is advanced not as a metaphorical or rhetorical device, but as a formal isomorphism identified through structural comparison of axiomatic systems across the domains of physics and theology.

The question motivating this inquiry is: *What would anti-truth look like if it were defined structurally instead of emotionally?* The present work argues that evil becomes legible as structural inversion, not merely as bad behavior. This reframing permits a rigorous, domain-independent characterization of moral opposition that is amenable to formal analysis.

## 3. Why This Is a Taxonomy, Not a List of Vices

It is essential to distinguish the present framework from conventional catalogues of moral failings. A list of vices enumerates behaviors or dispositions judged to be morally deficient within a given ethical system. A taxonomy, by contrast, identifies the formal structural relationships that generate and constrain the domain of possible moral inversions.

The twenty-four anti-properties are not derived from empirical observation of immoral acts; they are deduced from the formal inversion of the twenty-four properties shared by mathematical and moral truth. This deductive approach ensures that the resulting taxonomy is exhaustive with respect to the structural space defined by the original properties, and that it is independent of cultural, historical, or psychological contingencies.

## 4. The Fruits as the Exact Opposite

The structural inversion described herein yields what may be termed the *fruits* of anti-truth—that is, the observable manifestations of the anti-properties in moral and physical reality. These fruits are the exact opposite of the fruits of truth, as the anti-properties are the exact inverses of the properties.
