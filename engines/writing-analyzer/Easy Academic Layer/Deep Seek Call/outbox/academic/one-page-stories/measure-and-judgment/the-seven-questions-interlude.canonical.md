# The Seven Questions: A Methodological Framework for Claim Inspection

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic methodology—designated the Seven Questions (7Q) Framework—for the structural inspection of truth-claims across disciplinary boundaries. The framework posits that every propositional claim possesses an underlying architecture of assumptions, mechanisms, testability conditions, and failure criteria that typically remain implicit in ordinary discourse. By rendering these load-bearing structures explicit, the 7Q method transforms inquiry from an intuitive process into a repeatable, inspectable procedure. The framework is here introduced in its formal structure, with subsequent application to physical and theological domains.

---

## I. The Foundational Question

**If a claim is true, what else must be true underneath it?**

This question constitutes the operational axiom of the 7Q method. A claim, properly understood, is not reducible to its surface-level propositional content. Rather, it entails a hierarchical structure comprising:

- **Assumptions**: presuppositions about reality, language, and methodology
- **Definitions**: the semantic boundaries within which the claim operates
- **Mechanisms**: the causal or logical processes by which the claim is instantiated
- **Testability conditions**: the empirical or formal criteria by which the claim may be evaluated
- **Limits**: the domain of applicability beyond which the claim ceases to hold
- **Consequences**: the logical and practical implications entailed by the claim
- **Failure conditions**: the specific circumstances under which the claim would be falsified

The objective of the 7Q framework is not to render claims superficially sophisticated, but to render them *inspectable*—that is, to expose every load-bearing surface to systematic examination.

---

## II. Proof by Classification: The Seven Questions

The method proceeds through seven sequential interrogations, each designed to expose a distinct structural layer of the claim under investigation.

### Q1: Name the Claim

**Objective:** Identify the precise propositional sentence that constitutes the claim under examination, prior to any evaluative or defensive operations.

This step establishes the *explanandum*—the claim to be analyzed—in its most explicit form. Ambiguity at this stage propagates through all subsequent analysis.

### Q2: Expose Assumptions

**Objective:** Articulate what the claim already presupposes about:
- **Reality**: ontological commitments (e.g., the existence of physical laws, the uniformity of nature, the reality of abstract objects)
- **Language**: semantic and syntactic presuppositions (e.g., referential stability, truth-conditional semantics)
- **Method**: epistemological and methodological commitments (e.g., the validity of inductive inference, the reliability of empirical observation)

### Q3: Define the Mechanism

**Objective:** Specify the causal, logical, or dynamical process by which the claim is supposed to operate.

This step prohibits the substitution of labels for explanations. The mechanism must be articulated in sufficient detail to permit evaluation of its coherence and plausibility.

### Q4: Identify Kill Conditions

**Objective:** Enumerate the specific tests or observations that, if honestly conducted and found negative, would falsify the claim.

These conditions constitute the claim's *falsification criteria* in the Popperian sense. A claim that cannot specify its kill conditions is, by this framework, not yet fully inspectable.

### Q5–Q7: Consequences, Inspection, and Identity

The remaining three questions complete the analytical sequence:

- **Q5: What does the claim produce?** — The logical, empirical, or practical consequences that follow from the claim's truth.
- **Q6: What survives inspection?** — The residue of the claim after all kill conditions have been applied and all assumptions examined.
- **Q7: What identity does the surviving structure earn?** — The classification or designation that the inspected claim merits, based on the outcome of the preceding six questions.

The sequence is not a collection of discrete inquiries but a *machine*—a procedural algorithm that transforms any claim into an inspectable structure.

---

## III. The Instrument and Its Application

The 7Q framework functions as an *instrument of epistemic hygiene*. Its application does not guarantee the truth of any claim, but it does guarantee that the claim has been subjected to a repeatable, transparent inspection protocol. The method renders inquiry reproducible: any investigator applying the 7Q sequence to the same claim should, in principle, arrive at the same structural analysis.

The full walkthrough of the 7Q method, including worked examples from physical theory, is provided in the companion document, *The 7Q Framework Explained Through Physics* (Lowe, FP-029). The subsequent application of the framework to destructive testing—wherein claims are evaluated by their capacity to survive their own kill conditions—is developed in *Prove by Destruction* (Lowe, 7Q Framework Series).
