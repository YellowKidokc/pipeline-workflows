# The Reverse Method: A Formal Framework for Destructive Proof in Theophysics

## Abstract

This article introduces and formalizes the *Reverse Method*, a proof strategy that prioritizes the systematic elimination of competing hypotheses prior to the affirmation of a target proposition. Rather than constructing a cumulative defense of a claim, this method begins by enumerating all plausible counterarguments, falsification conditions, and structural vulnerabilities—termed *Kill Conditions*—and permits the claim to stand only after each such condition has been addressed or eliminated. The method is presented as a generalizable epistemic protocol applicable to interdisciplinary domains, including theophysics, where claims often span both physical and theological registers. This treatment provides the structural outline, methodological justification, and canonical reference architecture for the approach, with full derivations and formal proofs reserved for the accompanying canonical document.

## 1. Introduction: The Epistemic Priority of Destructive Testing

### 1.1 Thesis Statement

The central thesis of this work is that proof may be more rigorously established through the attempted destruction of a claim than through its direct defense. This approach—hereafter termed the *Reverse Method*—inverts the conventional epistemic trajectory: rather than accumulating evidence in favor of a proposition, the investigator first identifies and tests every condition under which the proposition would fail. The proposition is provisionally accepted only after all such conditions have been systematically eliminated.

### 1.2 Methodological Context

This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of proof strategies across physics, formal logic, and theological epistemology. In physics, the principle of falsifiability (Popper, 1959) provides a precedent: a theory is scientific only insofar as it can be empirically falsified. The Reverse Method extends this logic by operationalizing falsification as the *primary* mode of validation. In formal logic, *reductio ad absurdum* similarly proceeds by assuming the negation of a proposition and deriving a contradiction. The Reverse Method generalizes this strategy to include empirical, conceptual, and theological domains.

### 1.3 Scope and Limitations

This article presents the structural outline of the Reverse Method in four moves: (1) the Forward Method, (2) the Reverse Method proper, (3) Kill Conditions, and (4) Survivor Identity. The full canonical treatment—including formal proofs, tables, source attribution, and dimensional analysis—is provided in the accompanying document, *The Reverse Method: Prove by Destruction* (Lowe, 2024). ## 2. The Forward Method: Conventional Proof Strategy

### 2.1 Definition and Characterization

The *Forward Method* denotes the conventional approach to proof, in which the investigator begins with a claim and proceeds to accumulate supporting evidence, logical arguments, or empirical data in its favor. The reader is asked to trust the conclusion based on the cumulative weight of this evidence.

### 2.2 Epistemic Limitations

The Forward Method, while widely employed, suffers from a structural vulnerability: it does not systematically account for the possibility of unexamined counterarguments. The reader must trust that the investigator has considered and addressed all plausible objections, but this trust is not epistemically secured by the method itself. The Reverse Method addresses this limitation by making the elimination of counterarguments the explicit and primary mechanism of validation.

## 3. The Reverse Method: Proof by Destruction

### 3.1 Formal Definition

The Reverse Method is defined as a proof strategy comprising the following sequential steps:

1. **Enumerate Kill Conditions**: Identify all propositions, conditions, or scenarios that, if true, would falsify the target claim.
2. **Test Each Condition**: For each Kill Condition, determine whether it holds under the relevant empirical, logical, or theological constraints.
3. **Eliminate or Accommodate**: If a Kill Condition is found to hold, the target claim is either modified to accommodate it or abandoned. If a Kill Condition is found not to hold, it is eliminated from consideration.
4. **Survivor Identification**: The target claim is provisionally accepted only after all Kill Conditions have been eliminated.

### 3.2 Epistemic Justification

The Reverse Method derives its epistemic strength from the principle of *falsification resistance*: a claim that survives systematic attempts at destruction is more robust than one that has merely been supported by positive evidence. This is analogous to the statistical concept of *hypothesis testing*, where the null hypothesis is rejected only when the evidence against it exceeds a threshold. In the Reverse Method, the target claim functions as the null hypothesis, and the Kill Conditions represent alternative hypotheses that must be rejected.

## 4. Kill Conditions: Systematic Enumeration of Falsification Criteria

### 4.1 Definition

A *Kill Condition* is any proposition, empirical observation, logical contradiction, or theological constraint that, if verified, would falsify the target claim. Kill Conditions are enumerated prior to any attempt to defend the claim, ensuring that the proof process is exhaustive rather than selective.

### 4.2 Taxonomy of Kill Conditions

Kill Conditions may be classified into the following categories:

- **Empirical Kill Conditions**: Observations or experimental results that contradict the target claim.
- **Logical Kill Conditions**: Internal contradictions or inconsistencies within the claim itself.
- **Theological Kill Conditions**: Doctrinal or scriptural constraints that render the claim untenable within a given theological framework.
- **Structural Kill Conditions**: Failures of the claim to satisfy formal criteria such as parsimony, coherence, or explanatory power.

### 4.3 Methodological Role

The reader witnesses the elimination of Kill Conditions in real time, rather than being asked to trust that such elimination has occurred. This transparency constitutes the primary epistemic advantage of the Reverse Method over the Forward Method.

---

## 5. Survivor Identity: The Claim After Destruction

### 5.1 Definition

The *Survivor* is the target claim after all Kill Conditions have been systematically eliminated. Its identity is defined not by the evidence in its favor but by the set of alternatives that have been ruled out.

### 5.2 Epistemic Status

The Survivor is provisionally accepted as the most robust available proposition, subject to the discovery of new Kill Conditions. This provisional status is consistent with the fallibilist epistemology that underlies both scientific and theological inquiry.
