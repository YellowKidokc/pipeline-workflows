# Terminus Sui: Five Proofs of Systemic Incompleteness

## Abstract

This article presents a formal analysis of the epistemological and ontological constraints governing self-referential systems. Through five distinct but convergent proofs, we demonstrate that any system attempting to complete itself from within necessarily encounters a fundamental limit—a boundary condition that cannot be traversed by internal operations alone. This limit, designated *Terminus Sui*, is not presented as a logical flaw or paradox to be resolved, but rather as the constitutive structure of the argument itself. The implications extend across formal logic, physical cosmology, and theological metaphysics, suggesting a convergent structural isomorphism between Gödelian incompleteness, cosmological boundary conditions, and the theological concept of *causa sui*.

## 01 - The Convergence

### Thesis Statement

The central claim of this investigation is as follows: **When a system attempts to complete itself from within, it necessarily encounters a limit that cannot be overcome by any operation internal to that system.** This limit is not an artifact of incomplete reasoning but constitutes the very structure of the argument. Five independent proofs—drawn from formal logic, set theory, physical cosmology, information theory, and metaphysical theology—converge upon this same boundary condition.

### Methodological Framework

The present analysis proceeds through a structural comparison of self-referential closure mechanisms across five domains. Each proof is constructed independently, using the formal apparatus appropriate to its discipline, and the convergence is identified through the shared structural property of *inability to achieve self-completion*. This isomorphism was identified through systematic mapping of boundary conditions across these domains, following the method of structural analogy employed in interdisciplinary physics-theology research (cf. Polkinghorne, 1994; Tipler, 1994).

## 02 - The Question They Cannot Escape

### The Structure in Four Moves

The argument proceeds through four sequential moves, each building upon the previous:

1. **The Convergence** — The initial framing: what occurs when a system attempts to complete itself from within?
2. **The Question They Cannot Escape** — The identification of the load-bearing structure that underlies all five proofs, presented before the reader is asked to follow the full derivation.
3. **Go Deeper** — The recognition that the limit is not a bug in the argument but the argument itself.
4. **The Limit** — The designation of this boundary as *Terminus Sui*, the point at which self-completion becomes impossible.

This four-move structure is designed to orient the reader to the conceptual architecture before engaging with the formal proofs. The interlude serves as a conceptual map rather than a replacement for the canonical derivation.
