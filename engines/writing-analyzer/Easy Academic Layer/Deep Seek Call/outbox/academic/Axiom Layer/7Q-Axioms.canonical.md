# The 7Q Method: A Formal Epistemic Framework for Cross-Domain Inquiry

## Abstract

This article presents the 7Q Method, a structured epistemic framework comprising seven sequential interrogative operations (Q0–Q7) designed for the classification, evaluation, and falsification of claims across disciplinary boundaries. The framework operates through forward and reverse modalities: forward application classifies any propositional claim according to its type, domain, precision, evidentiary status, dependency structure, generative capacity, and axiomatic grounding; reverse application reconstructs the conditions under which a claim would constitute proof. A precondition (Q0) establishes the epistemic posture of the inquirer as a necessary condition for valid inquiry. The framework integrates three evidentiary channels—phenomenon strength, explanatory depth, and experiential coherence—into a multiplicative completeness factor, and specifies five distinct death conditions under which a claim fails. A composite truth score (T) is derived entirely from fields generated by the interrogative sequence, with hard caps enforcing minimal thresholds for explanatory depth, falsifiability, and generative capacity. The framework is demonstrated through worked application to general relativity (GR), yielding a formal classification of GR as a causal rather than descriptive theory and surfacing its hidden dependency on the smooth manifold assumption at Planck scale.

## 1. Introduction: The Epistemic Precondition (Q0)

### 1.1 Statement of the Precondition

The 7Q Method begins with an operation that lies outside the interrogative sequence proper. Let Q0 denote the precondition that the inquirer cannot serve as the ground of inquiry. Formally:

\[
\text{Q0} \notin \{\text{Q1}, \text{Q2}, \text{Q3}, \text{Q4}, \text{Q5}, \text{Q6}, \text{Q7}\}
\]

The inquirer must adopt a posture of epistemic openness prior to the initiation of inquiry. This is not a normative appeal to intellectual humility as a virtue, but an operational requirement: the inquirer who arrives with a decided conclusion cannot, by construction, subject that conclusion to genuine falsification. The condition is expressed as:

\[
\text{posture}(\text{decided}) \rightarrow \text{dead framework}
\]
\[
\text{posture}(\text{open}) \rightarrow \text{breakthrough potential}
\]

### 1.2 Historical Justification

The necessity of Q0 is established through historical analysis of frameworks that ossified. Logical positivism arrived decided that metaphysical propositions were meaningless, thereby foreclosing inquiry into domains that subsequently proved empirically tractable (e.g., quantum foundations). Behaviorism arrived decided that consciousness was irrelevant to the explanation of behavior, a position that cognitive science subsequently overturned. In both cases, the framework's death was encoded in its initial posture, not in any subsequent empirical failure.

The operational significance of Q0 is that it is simultaneously the weakest element of the framework (non-enforceable through any procedural mechanism) and the most structurally important. It cannot be scored, only acknowledged. Its function is to prevent the error that every dead framework committed: arriving at inquiry with the conclusion already in hand.

## 2. The Interrogative Sequence

### 2.1 Q1: Definition and Classification

#### 2.1.1 Classification Operation

Q1 requires the inquirer to name the claim, classify its type, and assign it a tier within a knowledge hierarchy. The operation transforms an undefined proposition into a defined object of inquiry. Misclassification at Q1 propagates through all subsequent questions, as each subsequent operation depends on the type and tier assigned at this stage.

The permitted types are: causal, descriptive, ontological, mathematical, predictive, mechanistic, and normative. Each type carries distinct epistemic commitments and correspondingly distinct kill conditions.

#### 2.1.2 Tier Assignment

Claims are assigned to one of four tiers within a knowledge hierarchy:

- **Foundational**: axiom, postulate, definition
- **Derived**: theorem, corollary, hypothesis
- **Constraint**: boundary condition
- **Support**: evidence bundle, connection (bridge relationship)

The tier determines how a claim is tested. A foundational claim requires different kill conditions than a derived claim. A hypothesis treated as an axiom will never be traced to its dependencies, because axioms do not have dependencies—they *are* dependencies.

#### 2.1.3 Worked Example: General Relativity

Application of Q1 to general relativity yields a reclassification from descriptive to causal. Standard textbook presentations treat GR as a description of spacetime geometry. Q1 reclassifies it: GR asserts that mass-energy *causes* curvature. This is a causal claim—a stronger commitment than "spacetime is curved." The reclassification immediately sharpens the kill conditions:

\[
\text{classify}(\text{GR}) = \text{causal} > \text{descriptive}
\]

A causal claim entails that removing the cause should remove the effect. This is testable in ways that a descriptive claim is not.

### 2.2 Q2: Domain Anchoring and Isomorphism Detection

#### 2.2.1 Domain and Scale Specification

Q2 establishes the primary domain, additional domains, and scale of operation for the claim. The critical question is whether the claim appears in more than one domain, as cross-domain presence triggers the isomorphism test.

Permitted domains include: physics, theology, consciousness studies, information theory, mathematics, biology, and economics. Scale ranges from quantum through molecular, neural, individual, social, civilizational, to cosmic.

#### 2.2.2 The Isomorphism Test

The isomorphism test distinguishes analogy from structural identity. Two structures A and B are isomorphic if and only if for all relations R, the relations in A correspond to relations in B under variable substitution:

\[
\text{iso}(A,B) \iff \forall R: R_A \leftrightarrow R_B \text{ under variable substitution}
\]

The test requires that the mathematics preserve topology, boundary conditions, and conservation laws under variable substitution. Analogies suggest similarity; isomorphisms demonstrate structural identity. If the same tensor equations and conservation laws hold when domain labels are swapped, the relationship is structural, not metaphorical.

Riemannian geometry provides a paradigmatic example: it appears in physics (general relativity), machine learning (manifold learning), economics (curved utility surfaces), and information theory (Fisher information geometry). The same mathematics operates in all four domains.

### 2.3 Q3: Precision and Negation

#### 2.3.1 Precision Requirement

Q3 forces the claim into a single precise statement, classified by certainty level (proven, well-supported, tentative, speculative) and scope (universal, domain-specific, context-dependent). The opponent restatement test serves as validation: a serious, informed opponent must be able to restate the claim accurately. If not, the claim is not sufficiently precise to test.

#### 2.3.2 The Negation Test

Every claim carries its own death warrant through automatic generation of its logical opposite. For a claim of the form \(A \land B\):

\[
\neg(A \land B) = \neg A \lor \neg B
\]

If the negation decomposes the original into independently testable components, bundled claims hidden within a single statement have been identified. For GR:

\[
\neg(\text{GR}) = \neg(\text{mass-energy curves spacetime}) \lor \neg(\text{objects follow geodesics})
\]

Two independently killable components are revealed automatically.

### 2.4 Q4: Evidentiary Channels

#### 2.4.1 Channel 1: Phenomenon Strength (PS)

Phenomenon strength measures what is reliably observed. It is computed as:

\[
\text{PS} = \text{reproducibility} \times 0.4 + \text{effect size} \times 0.3 + \text{measurement quality} \times 0.3
\]

Reproducibility receives the highest weight because if a phenomenon cannot be repeated, it does not exist as evidence.

#### 2.4.2 Channel 2: Explanatory Depth (ED)

Explanatory depth measures mechanistic understanding. It is computed as:

\[
\text{ED} = \text{mechanism clarity} \times 0.4 + \text{constraint consistency} \times 0.3 + \text{scope} \times 0.3
\]

The "why-penalty" operates as follows: if ED = 0, evidence is capped at 50% regardless of PS. This formalizes the observation that every dead framework refused this question, while every breakthrough came from someone who held it. Pre-1927 scientists who asked "why" moved the needle; post-1927 instrumentalism correlates with disciplinary fragmentation.

#### 2.4.3 Channel 3: Experiential Coherence (EC)

Experiential coherence measures whether the claim produces stable structure across time, behavior, and agents. It is computed as:

\[
\text{EC} = \text{internal consistency} \times 0.25 + \text{longitudinal stability} \times 0.25 + \text{behavioral transformation} \times 0.25 + \text{intersubjective pattern} \times 0.25
\]

This is not a measure of subjective feeling ("I feel strongly") but of whether the evidence produces consistent downstream effects across time, behavior, and multiple observers.

#### 2.4.4 The Completeness Factor

The three channels multiply rather than add. The completeness factor (CF) is:

\[
\text{CF} = (0.5 + 0.5 \times \text{ED}) \times (0.5 + 0.5 \times \text{EC})
\]

The final evidence score is:

\[
E_{\text{final}} = \text{PS} \times \text{CF}
\]

A tree with infinite rain but no sunlight dies. Zero in one channel cannot be compensated by maximum in another. When ED = 0 and EC = 0, CF = 0.25, capping evidence at 25%. When ED = 1 and EC = 1, CF = 1.0, earning full credit. Observed does not equal explained; repeatable does not equal understood.

### 2.5 Q5: Dependency Chain Analysis

#### 2.5.1 Chain Terminus Identification

Q5 traces the dependency chain of the claim to its terminus. The terminus types are:

- **Axiom**: self-evident → solid ground
- **Brute fact**: accepted without proof → honest but ungrounded
- **Circularity**: loops back → STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITY FLAGGED
- **Open**: never terminates → INCOMPLETE FLAGGED

#### 2.5.2 The Fragility Map

For each dependency, the fragility map assesses the consequence of its failure:

- Immediate collapse: critical
- Graceful degradation: the claim still partially works
- Independent survival: the dependency was not actually needed

Application to GR reveals the following dependencies: equivalence principle, Riemannian geometry, constancy of the speed of light, energy-momentum conservation, and the smooth manifold assumption. The hidden dependency is the smooth manifold assumption, which remains unverified at Planck scale (\(\sim 10^{-35}\) m). This has been driving unsolved physics for a century. Q5 surfaces it in one question.

### 2.6 Q6: Generative Capacity

#### 2.6.1 Prediction Generation

Q6 asks what must be true if the claim is true. The generative maturity of a claim is measured by:

\[
\text{generative maturity} = \frac{\text{confirmed predictions}}{\text{total predictions}}
\]

A claim that forces nothing downstream explains nothing—it occupies conceptual space without generating knowledge. If Q6(claim) = ∅, this constitutes an explanatory failure signal.

#### 2.6.2 Cross-Domain Forcing

If a Q6 consequence preserves structural relationships in another domain, an isomorphism candidate has been detected. This creates a detection loop with Q2:

\[
\text{Q6 consequence} \in \text{domain}_B \rightarrow \text{flag for Q2 isomorphism test}
\]
\[
\text{Q2} \leftrightarrow \text{Q6 form a detection loop}
\]

Standard disciplinary boundaries hide these connections; the framework makes them structurally visible.

### 2.7 Q7: Death Conditions and Adversarial Testing

#### 2.7.1 Death Condition 1: Self-Refutation

\(P \rightarrow \neg P\). The claim destroys itself when stated. The denial presupposes what it denies. "There is no truth" is itself a truth claim. "Logic is unreliable" requires logic to state. If the claim self-refutes, nothing else matters.

#### 2.7.2 Death Condition 2: Infinite Regress

\(\text{answer}(Q) \rightarrow \text{new instance of } Q \rightarrow \infty\). The claim pushes the question backward without ever resolving it. Each answer generates a new instance of the same question. This is distinct from a deep dependency chain—regress means the chain never terminates by design.

#### 2.7.3 Death Condition 3: Empirical Contradiction

\(\text{prediction}(\text{claim}) \neq \text{observation} \rightarrow \text{falsified}\). This is the traditional mode of falsification formalized by Popper (1934/1959). Necessary but not sufficient alone: a claim can survive empirical testing and still fail on other conditions.

#### 2.7.4 Death Condition 4: Logical Incoherence

\(\text{claim} \land \neg(\text{consequence of claim}) \rightarrow \text{incoherent}\). The claim contradicts something already established in its own chain—a prior dependency, a forced consequence, or itself at a different point in the argument. This is distinct from self-refutation (which is immediate) and catches delayed contradictions that only appear when the full chain is traced.

#### 2.7.5 Death Condition 5: Explanatory Failure

\(\text{Q6}(\text{claim}) = \emptyset\). The claim cannot answer forced downstream questions from Q6. It stops generating. Kuhn (1962) described this as anomaly accumulation; the 7Q system formalizes it. A theory that stops generating is dying. This is distinct from empirical failure—the data might still fit, but the theory can no longer explain.

#### 2.7.6 Adversarial Test Requirement

Surviving sympathetic review is categorically different from surviving hostile review. The adversarial status of a claim must be declared. A claim that has survived adversarial testing by informed opponents is categorically different from one examined only by sympathetic parties. Most claims in most fields have not been adversarially tested. A claim with no adversarial history is unproven, not confirmed.

## 3. Synthesis: The Truth Score

### 3.1 Composite Score

The truth score T is derived from six variables, all obtained from the Q1–Q7 fields:

\[
T = w_1 S + w_2 E + w_3 L + w_4 D + w_5 P + w_6 C
\]

Where:
- \(S \leftarrow\) Q7 survivability
- \(E \leftarrow\) Q4 \(E_{\text{final}}\) with why-penalty
- \(L \leftarrow\) Q3 precision × Q4 type
- \(D \leftarrow\) Q5 dependency stability
- \(P \leftarrow\) Q6 confirmed prediction ratio
- \(C \leftarrow\) Q2 cross-domain coherence

Every variable comes from fields the inquirer has already filled. No external judgment is needed—the mathematics handles it.

\[
T = \sum w_i V_i \quad \text{where } V \in \{S, E, L, D, P, C\}
\]

This maps to: \(T = \chi\), confidence = \(\kappa\), cluster variance = \(\rho\).

### 3.2 The Modulator

The 7Q framework score itself modulates the truth score:

\[
T_{\text{enhanced}} = T \times (0.6 + 0.4 \times \text{7Q\_score})
\]

Zero 7Q markers yield 60% of the truth score; full 7Q yields 100%. This is not bias but formalization of the principle that structured epistemic practice constitutes better epistemic practice.

### 3.3 Hard Caps

Weak dimensions cap the total. Three hard caps enforce completeness:

- If ED < 0.3: \(\max(T) = 0.70\) (no explanation)
- If Q7 < 0.3: \(\max(T) = 0.60\) (unfalsifiable)
- If Q6 < 0.3: \(\max(T) = 0.70\) (no consequences)

The system cannot be gamed by maximizing one dimension while neglecting others.

## 4. Conclusion

The 7Q Method provides a formal, structured epistemic framework for the classification, evaluation, and falsification of claims across disciplinary boundaries. Its five death conditions extend Popperian falsification (1934/1959) by adding self-refutation, infinite regress, logical incoherence, and explanatory failure as independent failure modes. The multiplicative completeness factor formalizes the requirement that observation, explanation, and experiential coherence must all be present for full epistemic credit. The isomorphism test provides a rigorous mechanism for detecting cross-domain structural identities that standard disciplinary boundaries would obscure. The framework is demonstrated through application to general relativity, yielding a reclassification from descriptive to causal and surfacing the hidden smooth manifold dependency that underlies the quantum gravity problem.
