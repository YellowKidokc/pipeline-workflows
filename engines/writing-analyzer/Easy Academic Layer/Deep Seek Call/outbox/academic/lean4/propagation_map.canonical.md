# Theophysics: Propagation Analysis of Substrate Ontologies

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic propagation analysis of six candidate ontological substrates—Matter, Mind, Information, Mathematical Structure, Relational Structure, and Brute Primitive—through a twelve-level decision tree (Q0–Q12) derived from first principles. Each substrate is evaluated on its capacity to answer successive questions without internal contradiction, circularity, or reliance on extraneous explanatory frameworks. The analysis demonstrates that only the Information substrate survives the full propagation sequence, yielding eight boundary conditions (BC1–BC8) that collectively converge upon a specific theological identification. Published secular theories are embedded at each node to establish correspondence between open questions in contemporary physics and the structural requirements of the surviving substrate. The propagation principle is formalized, and the failure modes—blank, death, strain, and terminal—are defined with operational criteria.

---

## 1. The Propagation Principle: Formal Definition

Let \( S \) denote an ontological substrate selected at question \( Q_2 \). The propagation principle requires that \( S \) must provide a non-contradictory answer to every subsequent question \( Q_n \) for \( n \in \{3, 4, \ldots, 12\} \). Three failure modes are defined:

1. **Blank**: \( S \) cannot generate an answer to \( Q_n \).
2. **Death**: \( S \) yields a self-contradictory answer at \( Q_n \).
3. **Strain**: \( S \) answers \( Q_n \) only by importing vocabulary or explanatory resources from another substrate \( S' \neq S \).

The accumulated cost of strain positions renders a substrate unsustainable, typically by \( Q_6 \)–\( Q_8 \). At each node, published secular theories are embedded to demonstrate correspondence between empirically established results and the structural requirements imposed by the propagation sequence.

| Substrate | Verdict | Justification |

**Open question:** *What is a distinction?* Physical thing? Mental event? Information? This forces \( Q_2 \).

### 2.3 Q2: What Is a Distinction? — The Fork

| Substrate | Verdict | Justification |
|-----------|---------|---------------|
| Matter | ⚠ CIRCULAR | Distinction = property of matter. But matter has not been established. Presupposes what it needs to explain. |

| Information | ✓ PASS | Definitional identity. Distinction \( \equiv \) 1 bit. Shannon formalized this. No circularity. |

| Relational | ⚠ COLLAPSES | Relations without relata = \( Q_2 \)-D (dead). Relations with content = information. Ladyman and French (2003) structural realism either collapses or reduces. |

**Published theories:** Wheeler (1990), "Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links," in *Complexity, Entropy, and the Physics of Information*: "It from Bit"—physical reality derives from binary distinctions. Landauer (1961), "Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process," *IBM Journal of Research and Development*, 5: 183–191: information erasure costs \( kT \ln 2 \). Bekenstein (1973), "Black Holes and Entropy," *Physical Review D*, 7: 2333–2346; Hawking (1975), "Particle Creation by Black Holes," *Communications in Mathematical Physics*, 43: 199–220: holographic principle, \( S = A/4\ell_P^2 \).

**Open questions:**
- Wheeler: *Where does the bit come from?* What instantiates it?
- Landauer: *Why does information cost energy?* What is the deeper coupling?
- Holographic: *Why is geometry derived from information* rather than the reverse?

All three questions point to: *the substrate that carries information.*

**Open questions (boundary-condition-shaped):**

## 4. Conclusion

The propagation analysis demonstrates that of the six candidate ontological substrates, only the Information substrate survives the full twelve-question sequence without circularity, contradiction, or explanatory collapse.
