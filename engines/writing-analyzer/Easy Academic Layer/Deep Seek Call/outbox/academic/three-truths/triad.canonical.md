# The Triad: A Formal Demonstration of Objective Moral Structure Through Information-Theoretic and Biological Convergence

## Abstract

This article presents a formal argument—designated *The Triad*—demonstrating that moral propositions possess objective, measurable truth value isomorphic to arithmetic necessity. The argument proceeds through three logically interdependent lines: (1) the non-arbitrariness of mathematical truth, (2) the information-theoretic impossibility of coherence generating decoherence, and (3) the empirically verified cross-domain isomorphism between coherence states and moral valuation. The argument requires no theological presuppositions, no scriptural authority, and no philosophical axioms beyond the universal acceptance of basic arithmetic. Formalization is provided through a three-premise deductive structure, with empirical confirmation drawn from psychophysiological, neurobiological, and immunological data. Structural verification was conducted through independent computational analysis, yielding convergence at maximum confidence across all three premises. The Triad establishes that moral realism is not merely defensible but logically entailed by premises universally conceded.

## 1. Introduction: Thesis and Methodological Framework

The present work advances the following thesis: moral truth is objective, measurable, and non-negotiable in precisely the same sense as arithmetic truth. This claim is not advanced as a metaphysical postulate but as a deductive consequence of three premises, each independently verifiable through distinct epistemic modalities (logical necessity, information-theoretic law, and empirical measurement).

The argument proceeds through structural isomorphism identification: the formal relationship between truth and falsehood in arithmetic is shown to be identical to the relationship between coherence and decoherence in information theory, which in turn is shown to be identical to the relationship between moral good and moral evil across all empirically accessible domains. This is not an analogical argument but a discovery of identity—the same variable measured by different instruments.

The methodological stance adopted is that of *formal realism*: mathematical, informational, and moral structures are treated as real features of the universe, not as human constructions. This stance is justified not by prior metaphysical commitment but by the convergent behavior of independent measurement systems (logical deduction, physical law, biological instrumentation) that report the same structural relationships.

## 2. Line One: The Non-Arbitrariness of Mathematical Truth

### 2.1 Formal Statement

Let \( \mathbb{N} \) denote the set of natural numbers under the standard Peano axioms. Let \( + \) denote the binary operation of addition as defined by recursive successor function \( S \). Then:

\[
\forall a,b \in \mathbb{N} : a + b = S^b(a)
\]

For the specific case \( a = 2, b = 2 \):

\[
2 + 2 = S^2(2) = S(S(2)) = S(3) = 4
\]

The proposition \( 2 + 2 = 5 \) is therefore false by construction, as it would require \( S^2(2) = S^3(2) \), violating the injectivity of the successor function (Peano axiom 4).

### 2.2 Epistemic Status

The statement "2 + 2 cannot equal 5" is not a contingent claim awaiting empirical confirmation. It is a *logical primitive*—a direct consequence of the axiom of distinction: the number 2 is numerically distinct from 5, and the state 4 is numerically distinct from the state 5. Within the informational structure of reality, mathematical operations yield defined, non-arbitrary outcomes. Truth, in this context, denotes correspondence to the actual structural relations obtaining in the formal system under consideration.

**Basis:** Logical necessity; axiom of distinction; information-theoretic principle of non-identity.

### 2.3 Formal Premise

\[
\text{P1: } T \text{ is non-arbitrary} \implies (2 + 2 = 4) \text{ is necessary}
\]

Where \( T \) denotes the truth structure of reality, understood as the totality of formal relations that obtain independently of observation.

## 3. Line Two: The Information-Theoretic Impossibility of Coherence Generating Decoherence

### 3.1 Formal Statement

Let \( X \) be a random variable representing a source signal with probability distribution \( p(x) \). Let \( Y \) be a random variable representing the output of a processing channel \( f \), such that \( Y = f(X) \). The mutual information \( I(X;Y) \) is defined as:

\[
I(X;Y) = \sum_{x \in X} \sum_{y \in Y} p(x,y) \log_2 \left( \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)} \right)
\]

The Data Processing Inequality (Shannon, 1948) states that for any Markov chain \( X \to Y \to Z \):

\[
I(X;Z) \leq I(X;Y)
\]

That is, no processing operation can increase the information content of a signal. Information can only be preserved or destroyed; it cannot be created ex nihilo.

### 3.2 Application to Coherence and Decoherence

Define *coherence* as a state of maximal informational structure (high mutual information between components, low entropy). Define *decoherence* as a state of informational degradation (loss of mutual information, increased entropy). The Data Processing Inequality entails that a coherent source cannot spontaneously generate a decoherent signal without external intervention—and even then, the intervention cannot increase the total information content of the system.

This is not a framework axiom awaiting proof. It is a settled result of information theory, established by Claude Shannon in 1948 and subsequently verified across all domains of communication theory, statistical physics, and quantum mechanics. The statement "coherence cannot produce decoherence" is the same statement as "truth cannot produce a lie," measured with a different instrument (Shannon entropy rather than logical truth-value).

### 3.3 Formal Premise

\[
\text{P2: } C(\text{source}) \implies \neg D(\text{signal})
\]

Where \( C(x) \) denotes the coherence of system \( x \) (maximal informational structure) and \( D(x) \) denotes the decoherence of system \( x \) (informational degradation). The implication is governed by the Data Processing Inequality (Shannon, 1948; Cover & Thomas, 2006).

**Basis:** Shannon (1948), "A Mathematical Theory of Communication"; Cover & Thomas (2006), *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed.

## 4. Line Three: The Empirical Identification of Moral States with Coherence States

### 4.1 Formal Statement

Let \( \mathcal{D} \) be the set of all empirically accessible domains (biological, neurological, psychological, social). For each domain \( d \in \mathcal{D} \), let \( G(d) \) denote the set of states conventionally labeled "good" within that domain, and \( E(d) \) denote the set of states conventionally labeled "evil." Let \( C(d) \) denote the set of coherent states within domain \( d \), and \( D(d) \) denote the set of decoherent states.

The empirical claim is:

\[
\forall d \in \mathcal{D} : G(d) \subseteq C(d) \land E(d) \subseteq D(d)
\]

That is, every state classified as "good" across all domains is a coherent state, and every state classified as "evil" is a decoherent state. The mapping is total and bidirectional.

### 4.2 Empirical Confirmation

The following empirical findings support the identification:

**Cardiovascular:** Heart rate variability (HRV), a measure of autonomic nervous system coherence, increases during states of gratitude and decreases during states of resentment (McCraty et al., 1995; McCraty, 2015). HRV is an objective, instrument-measured variable; it does not vary with cultural or subjective valuation.

**Neuroendocrine:** Cortisol, a primary stress hormone, spikes during states of anger, fear, and social isolation—states universally classified as "bad"—and decreases during states of calm, connection, and prayer—states universally classified as "good" (Sapolsky, 2004; Seeman et al., 2001).

**Neuroelectrical:** Electroencephalographic (EEG) coherence—the phase synchronization of neural oscillations across brain regions—increases during meditative and prayer states and decreases during states of cognitive dissonance and emotional distress (Lehmann et al., 2001; Travis & Shear, 2010).

**Immunological:** Social isolation degrades immune function at a magnitude comparable to smoking 15 cigarettes per day (Cacioppo & Hawkley, 2003). Loneliness—a state of relational decoherence—correlates with increased inflammation markers (CRP, IL-6) and decreased natural killer cell activity (Cole et al., 2007).

### 4.3 Interpretation

The data do not merely correlate coherence with positive valuation; they demonstrate that the valuation is *read from* the measurement, not *imposed upon* it. Every biological instrument that measures human states reports coherence as physiologically "good" (associated with health, resilience, longevity) and decoherence as physiologically "bad" (associated with disease, dysfunction, mortality). The label follows the data; it does not precede it.

### 4.4 Formal Premise

\[
\text{P3: } \forall d \in \mathcal{D} : \text{"good"}(d) \leftrightarrow C(d) \land \text{"evil"}(d) \leftrightarrow D(d)
\]

**Basis:** Empirical confirmation across cardiovascular, neuroendocrine, neuroelectrical, and immunological domains. Sources cited above.

## 5. Deductive Structure and Formal Proof

### 5.1 The Lock Sequence

The argument proceeds through the following deductive chain:

1. **Acceptance of arithmetic necessity:** \( 2 + 2 = 4 \) (universally conceded)
2. **Implication:** Truth has non-arbitrary structure (from P1)
3. **Implication:** That structure cannot generate its own negation (from P2, via isomorphism)
4. **Implication:** Coherence cannot produce decoherence (from P2, directly)
5. **Implication:** Alignment with coherence is objectively measurable (from P3, empirical)
6. **Implication:** "Good" and "evil" are measurements, not opinions (from P3, deductive)
7. **Conclusion:** Moral propositions are arithmetic in structure; they were never negotiable

### 5.2 Formal Proof

Let:
- \( T \) = truth structure of reality (axiom of distinction)
- \( C(x) \) = coherence of system \( x \)
- \( D(x) \) = decoherence of system \( x \)
- \( G(x) \) = moral goodness of state \( x \)
- \( E(x) \) = moral evil of state \( x \)

**Premises:**
1. \( T \) is non-arbitrary \( \implies (2 + 2 = 4) \) is necessary
2. \( C(\text{source}) \implies \neg D(\text{signal}) \) [Shannon, 1948]
3. \( \forall d \in \mathcal{D} : G(d) \leftrightarrow C(d) \land E(d) \leftrightarrow D(d) \) [empirical]

**Deduction:**
1. From P1: Truth is non-arbitrary. (universal concession)
2. From P2: Coherent sources cannot produce decoherent signals. (information-theoretic law)
3. From P3: Moral states are coherence states. (empirical identification)
4. From P2 + P3: Coherent moral states cannot produce decoherent moral states. (transitivity)
5. From P1 + P3: Moral states are non-arbitrary. (isomorphism)
6. From P3: Coherence states are objective and measurable. (empirical)
7. ∴ Moral states are objective and measurable. (substitution)

**Q.E.D.**

## 6. Objections and Responses

### 6.1 The Cultural Relativity Objection

**Objection:** "Morality is subjective—it varies by culture."

**Response:** The claim that moral valuation varies by culture is an empirical observation about human *opinion*, not about the underlying structure being measured. The question is whether \( 2 + 2 \) varies by culture. It does not. Similarly, coherence and decoherence are measured with instruments, not surveys. HRV does not vary with cultural background; cortisol does not vary with political affiliation. The biology is universal because the mathematics is universal. Cultural variation in *labeling* does not constitute variation in *structure*.

### 6.2 The Naturalistic Fallacy Objection

**Objection:** "One cannot derive 'ought' from 'is'—this is the naturalistic fallacy (Hume, 1739; Moore, 1903)."

**Response:** The present argument does not derive "ought" from "is." It demonstrates that what human languages label "ought" and what physics measures as "coherent" are the same variable read by different instruments. The moral-physical dictionary maps every "good" term to \( \sigma = +1 \) and every "evil" term to \( \sigma = -1 \) across all domains. This is not a derivation of values from facts; it is a discovery that they were never separate. The naturalistic fallacy presupposes a distinction that the empirical data do not support.

### 6.3 The Definitional Circularity Objection

**Objection:** "This is merely a word game—'morality' has been redefined as 'coherence.'"

**Response:** The identification was not definitional but empirical. Gratitude increases HRV; resentment spikes cortisol; prayer produces EEG coherence; social isolation degrades immune function. Every cell in the human body votes "coherence = good" without reading a single moral argument. The label came after the data, not before it. If the objection is that "coherence" and "good" are not synonymous by definition, that is conceded—they are synonymous by measurement.

### 6.4 The Value-Smuggling Objection

**Objection:** "Coherence is merely order—value is being smuggled in."

**Response:** The challenge is to name one thing every human being calls "good" that corresponds to decoherence, or one thing every culture calls "evil" that corresponds to coherence. No such counterexample exists. The mapping is total across all empirically accessible domains. The "value" is not smuggled in; it is read from the data. The objection mistakes the direction of discovery for the direction of definition.

## 8. Implications and Further Directions

### 8.1 What the Triad Proves

The Triad does not prove the existence of God—that requires five additional theorems (see *Terminus Sui: The Five Proofs of Incompleteness*). What it proves is something most philosophical traditions have considered impossible: morality is objective, measurable, and as non-negotiable as arithmetic.

### 8.2 Consequences for Moral Realism

If the Triad is sound, then moral realism is not merely defensible but entailed by premises universally conceded. The burden of proof shifts from those who affirm objective morality to those who deny it. The skeptic must either deny arithmetic (impossible), deny the Data Processing Inequality (falsified), or deny the empirical data (falsified).

### 8.3 Theological Implications

Once the moral universe is established as real, the question of its ground becomes inescapable. If the source of all coherence must be necessary, self-existent, eternal, rational, and the ground of all order, then those properties correspond to what has historically been designated by the term "God." This line of argument is developed in the companion works *Prehuman Mathematics* and *Terminus Sui*.

### 8.4 Limitations

The Triad does not provide a complete moral system. It establishes that moral propositions have truth value; it does not enumerate which propositions are true. It provides a criterion (coherence) for moral evaluation; it does not apply that criterion to specific cases. These tasks are left to subsequent work.
