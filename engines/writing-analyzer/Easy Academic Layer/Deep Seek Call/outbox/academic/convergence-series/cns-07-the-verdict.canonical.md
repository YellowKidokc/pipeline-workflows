# A Formal Academic Evaluation of Theistic and Naturalistic Explanatory Frameworks: Scoring, Weaknesses, and Falsification Conditions

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic comparative analysis of the explanatory power of theistic and naturalistic worldviews across ten empirically and philosophically significant phenomena. Employing a transparent, reproducible scoring methodology (1–5 scale), the analysis yields aggregate scores of 39 for theism and 21 for naturalism. The methodology, including all scoring criteria and potential weighting modifications, is fully disclosed to facilitate independent replication and critique. The theistic framework's principal weakness—the problem of suffering—is explicitly acknowledged and examined through the lens of the free will defense and *privatio boni* doctrine. Five specific falsification conditions ("kill conditions") are formally articulated, each representing a discrete empirical or theoretical demonstration that would necessitate retraction of the central claims. The analysis concludes that the directional signal across multiple independent domains—biblical predictive accuracy, structural gaps in naturalistic explanation, and convergence of properties across mathematics, morality, and theology—supports the theistic hypothesis without claiming deductive proof.

---

## 1. Introduction: Methodological Framework and Epistemic Grounding

The present investigation proceeds from the methodological premise established in prior work (Chapter One of this series): that both theistic and naturalistic worldviews operate within circular epistemic frameworks, each resting upon unprovable axiomatic foundations. This symmetry is not asserted as a weakness unique to either position but rather as a structural feature of all comprehensive metaphysical systems. The analysis therefore eschews claims of foundational certainty in favor of comparative explanatory power—the capacity of each worldview to account for observed phenomena with maximal coherence and minimal ad hoc postulation.

The scoring methodology employs a 1–5 ordinal scale applied to ten phenomena selected for their intrinsic difficulty rather than their amenability to either worldview. Each phenomenon is scored independently for explanatory power: the degree to which the worldview in question provides a coherent, non-arbitrary, and empirically grounded account of the observed data. Scores are assigned without weighting; however, the methodology permits re-analysis with alternative weighting schemes, and all raw data are provided for independent evaluation.

### 2.2 Detailed Rationale for Selected Scores

**Fine-Tuning of Physical Constants (Naturalism: 2, Theism: 4).** The observed precision of fundamental physical constants—including the cosmological constant, the strong nuclear force coupling constant, and the mass-energy density of the early universe—exceeds probabilistic thresholds that render chance explanation untenable under standard Bayesian analysis (Barrow & Tipler, 1986; Rees, 2000). Naturalistic explanations appeal either to brute fact (epistemically unsatisfying) or to multiverse hypotheses (empirically unfalsifiable). Theism posits intentional design by a transcendent agent, which, while not empirically demonstrable, provides a coherent causal account consistent with the observed fine-structure.

**Hard Problem of Consciousness (Naturalism: 1, Theism: 4).** The explanatory gap between neural correlates of consciousness and subjective phenomenal experience (Chalmers, 1995) remains unresolved within materialist frameworks. Naturalism can map correlations but cannot account for *why* subjective experience exists at all. Theism posits consciousness as a reflection of a conscious Creator, thereby grounding phenomenal experience in a metaphysically prior reality.

**Effectiveness of Mathematics (Naturalism: 2, Theism: 5).** Wigner's (1960) "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics" poses a foundational puzzle: why do abstract mathematical structures, developed independently of empirical observation, successfully describe physical reality with extraordinary precision? Theism resolves this puzzle by positing a unified Author of both mind and matter, such that mathematical reasoning and physical law share a common source. This explanation achieves maximal parsimony relative to alternative accounts.

**Evil and Suffering (Naturalism: 4, Theism: 2).** Naturalism treats suffering as a morally neutral outcome of natural processes, achieving logical consistency at the cost of existential adequacy. Theism's free will defense (Plantinga, 1974) provides structural coherence—genuine moral agency requires the possibility of harm—but fails to account for the magnitude and apparent gratuitousness of suffering. This represents the theistic framework's most significant explanatory weakness.

### 2.3 Aggregate Results and Interpretive Caveats

The aggregate scores (Theism: 39, Naturalism: 21) indicate a directional advantage for the theistic framework across the selected phenomena. However, several caveats warrant explicit acknowledgment:

1. The scoring is ordinal, not cardinal; a score of 4 does not represent twice the explanatory power of a score of 2.
2. Alternative weighting schemes could alter aggregate results; the raw data are provided to facilitate such re-analysis.
3. The selection of phenomena, while intended to be neutral, may reflect implicit biases; independent replication with alternative phenomenon sets is encouraged.

## 3. Acknowledged Weakness: The Problem of Suffering

The theistic framework's score of 2/5 on the problem of suffering requires extended methodological commentary. The free will defense (Plantinga, 1974) demonstrates logical consistency: a world containing free creatures capable of moral evil is compatible with the existence of an omnipotent, omnibenevolent God. However, logical consistency does not entail existential adequacy.

The *privatio boni* (privation of good) doctrine—which characterizes evil not as a created substance but as the absence of due good (Augustine, *Confessions* VII.12)—provides a metaphysical framework for understanding evil's ontological status. Yet this framework does not address the *magnitude* of suffering: the apparent disproportion between moral goods and the suffering they enable, the suffering of non-human animals, and the suffering of human beings who lack the cognitive capacity for moral agency.

The author acknowledges that no extant theodicy fully resolves these difficulties. The structural explanation for *how* evil entered creation (free will operating in a genuine moral space) is coherent; the explanation for *why* the permitted evil reaches its observed magnitude remains incomplete. This weakness is stated without evasion, and the scoring reflects this honest assessment.

## 4. Falsification Conditions: Formal Kill Criteria

The following five conditions, if empirically or theoretically demonstrated, would necessitate retraction of the central claims advanced in this series. Each condition is specified with sufficient precision to permit experimental or logical testing.

### 4.1 Retrodiction of Biblical Predictions

**Condition:** Demonstration that the biblical texts cited as predictive of later scientific discoveries were written or substantially modified *after* the corresponding scientific discoveries were made. Manuscript evidence—particularly from the Dead Sea Scrolls and other pre-discovery textual witnesses—would be required to establish post-dating. If the predictive passages are shown to be retrodictive, the argument from predictive accuracy collapses.

### 4.2 Successful Abiogenesis Model

**Condition:** Production of a working, empirically demonstrated model of abiogenesis in which functional, self-replicating information arises from purely chemical processes without intelligent intervention. A theoretical framework is insufficient; a laboratory demonstration is required. If information can arise from non-information without design, the argument that information requires an intelligent source is falsified.

### 4.3 Materialist Solution to the Hard Problem of Consciousness

**Condition:** Provision of a purely materialist explanation for the existence of subjective phenomenal experience—not merely neural correlates or functional models, but an account of *why* subjective experience exists at all. If consciousness is fully explicable within physicalist ontology, the argument that consciousness reflects a conscious Creator loses its evidential force.

### 4.4 Complete Evolutionary Account of Moral Qualia

**Condition:** Demonstration that the full range of human moral evaluation—including the qualia of guilt, pre-verbal moral evaluation in infants (as documented by the Yale Baby Lab; Hamlin, Wynn, & Bloom, 2007), and self-sacrificial behavior with no reproductive payoff—is fully accounted for by evolutionary survival advantage. If moral firmware is entirely explicable through natural selection, the judgment layer argument reduces to analogy.

### 4.5 Convergence Break

**Condition:** Identification of a single property among the 24 shared properties of mathematics, morality, and God (as catalogued in prior work) that genuinely belongs to only one domain and not the others. If the convergence is broken by even one legitimate counterexample, the structural identity claim fails.

## 5. Conclusion: Directional Signal and Epistemic Humility

The present analysis yields a directional signal: across ten independently selected phenomena, the theistic framework demonstrates superior explanatory power relative to naturalism, with aggregate scores of 39 and 21 respectively. This result is presented not as deductive proof but as cumulative evidence warranting further investigation.

The analysis is distinguished by three methodological commitments: (1) transparency of scoring, with all criteria and data publicly available for challenge and re-analysis; (2) explicit acknowledgment of weakness, particularly regarding the problem of suffering; and (3) articulation of specific falsification conditions, each representing a discrete empirical or theoretical demonstration that would necessitate retraction.

The convergence of evidence across multiple independent domains—biblical predictive accuracy (fifteen predictions with an average lead of approximately 2,800 years), structural gaps in naturalistic explanation (ten phenomena resistant to materialist account), and shared properties across mathematics, morality, and theology (twenty-four convergence points)—suggests a unified source. The hypothesis that best accounts for this convergence, with minimal ad hoc postulation, is that the universe was created by a transcendent intelligence whose nature is reflected in the structure of reality itself.
