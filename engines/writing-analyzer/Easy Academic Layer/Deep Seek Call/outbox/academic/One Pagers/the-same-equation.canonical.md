# A Formal Presentation of Theophysical Laws: Cross-Domain Isomorphisms Between Physical Fields and Spiritual Realities

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic framework identifying structural isomorphisms between fundamental physical laws and corresponding spiritual principles. Through term-by-term comparison of ten physical equations with their spiritual analogues, a consistent pattern emerges: each spiritual equation preserves the mathematical structure of its physical counterpart while incorporating a single additional term encoding agent choice or receptivity. The framework includes a Master Equation integrating all ten laws into a unified coherence functional, with formal verification conducted via Wolfram Mathematica 14.3.0 and JAX 0.7.2. The claim advanced is not metaphorical but structural: the equations are identical under variable substitution, with the sole systematic difference being the presence of a free-will asymmetry term in the spiritual domain.

## 1. Introduction

The relationship between physical law and theological principle has historically been approached through analogy, metaphor, or apologetic argument. The present work proposes a more rigorous methodology: direct structural comparison of mathematical formulations across domains. By treating physical equations as formal structures and examining whether identical structures appear in spiritual contexts under consistent variable substitution, one may evaluate whether the correspondence is coincidental or architectonic.

The framework comprises ten laws, each pairing a well-established physical equation with a spiritual analogue. For each pair, three elements are identified: (1) the substitution map mapping physical variables to spiritual variables, (2) the additional term present in the spiritual version but absent from the physical version, and (3) the theological or philosophical significance of that asymmetry.

### 2.1 Physical Formulation

Newton's law of universal gravitation states:

\[
F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
\]

where \(F\) is the gravitational force (dimensions: MLT⁻²), \(G\) is Newton's gravitational constant (dimensions: M⁻¹L³T⁻²), \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) are the interacting masses (dimensions: M), and \(r\) is the spatial separation (dimensions: L).

\]

where \(F_n\) denotes the grace force, \(G_s\) is the grace constant (analogous to \(G\)), \(\psi_1\) and \(\psi_2\) represent soul weights or spiritual mass (dimensions analogous to M), \(d\) is relational distance (dimensions analogous to L), and \(R\) is a dimensionless resistance parameter ranging from 0 to 1.

### 2.3 Structural Comparison

| Physical Variable | Spiritual Variable | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| \(F\) (gravitational force) | \(F_n\) (grace force) | Both represent attractive influence |
| \(G\) (Newton's constant) | \(G_s\) (grace constant) | Both set field strength |

**Asymmetry term:** \((1 - R)\), where \(R\) represents resistance to grace. When \(R = 0\), the equations are formally identical. When \(R = 1\), the grace force vanishes entirely, corresponding to complete resistance.

### 2.4 Significance

Gravitational attraction operates universally and involuntarily: no massive object can resist gravitational pull. The spiritual analogue introduces a degree of freedom absent in physics: the capacity to resist. This asymmetry—agent choice—appears consistently across all ten laws.
