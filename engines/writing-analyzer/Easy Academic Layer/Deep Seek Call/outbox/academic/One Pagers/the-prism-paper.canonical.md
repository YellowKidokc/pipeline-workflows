## 7. The Open System Argument

### 7.1 The Objection

The most common objection to the LLC is that it does not conserve energy. This objection requires careful examination.

### 7.2 Response

1. All canonical Lagrangians conserve energy in isolation. This follows from Noether's theorem and time-translation invariance. It is not a discovery about reality but a consequence of the assumption of closure.

2. When an external source is added, no Lagrangian conserves energy. Driven simple harmonic oscillators, sourced Klein-Gordon equations, sourced φ⁴ theory, and Λ-sourced Friedmann equations all exhibit energy change. This indicates an open system, not a defective equation.

3. The LLC contains a built-in source: \(\xi \cdot R \cdot \chi\) (grace). It never claimed to describe a closed system. The energy change formalizes the proposition that creation is open to its Creator.

4. The appropriate test is not "does it conserve energy?" but "does the energy change track the source?" If yes, the system is as well-posed as electromagnetism with charges.

5. Penrose's calculation of the probability of initial conditions (approximately \(1 \text{ in } 10^{10^{123}}\)) indicates that a closed-system Lagrangian cannot explain its own beginning. An open-system Lagrangian can—if the source is external.
