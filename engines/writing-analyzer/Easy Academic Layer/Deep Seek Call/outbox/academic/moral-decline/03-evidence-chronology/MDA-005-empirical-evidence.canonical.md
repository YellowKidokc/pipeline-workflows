# Empirical Evidence for Social Coherence as an Order Parameter: A Four-Test Falsification Analysis

## Abstract

This article presents a formal empirical investigation of the hypothesis that social coherence (χ) functions as a genuine order parameter within a constraint-pressure (P) thermodynamic framework. Drawing upon longitudinal data spanning 1940–2024 across seven institutional domains in the United States, four distinct falsifiable predictions derived from the phase-transition model are tested. Results demonstrate: (1) cross-domain pairwise correlations yielding a mean coefficient of R̄ = 0.986 (Fisher z = 7.44, p << 0.0001), indicating that the seven domains constitute repeated measurements of a single latent variable; (2) structural break synchronization, with six of seven domains exhibiting maximum decline within the 1968–1973 window (p < 10⁻⁸ under the null hypothesis of uniform temporal distribution); (3) threshold behavior characterized by a decline rate 4.5 times greater during the critical window (1.92 points/year) relative to the preceding period (0.43 points/year) and 2.5 times greater relative to the subsequent period (0.76 points/year); and (4) control group divergence, wherein Amish communities—which rejected the constraint-removal events of the 1968–1973 period—maintained stable coherence metrics across all measured indicators. These findings collectively confirm the hypothesis that the decline in American social coherence between 1968 and 1973 constituted a phase transition driven by cumulative constraint removal, rather than gradual cultural drift or generational preference shift.

## 1. Introduction and Theoretical Framework

The present investigation proceeds from the postulate that social coherence (χ) constitutes a measurable order parameter governed by constraint pressure (P), where P represents the aggregate of institutional, normative, and structural constraints maintaining social order. Within this framework, the relationship between χ and P is hypothesized to exhibit the characteristics of a thermodynamic phase transition: a critical threshold Pc exists such that when P falls below Pc, the system undergoes a discontinuous state change from a coherent to a decoherent phase.

This isomorphism between social systems and thermodynamic phase transitions was identified through structural comparison of the mathematical properties of social coherence indices with those of established order parameters in condensed matter physics. Specifically, the model predicts that χ should exhibit: (a) cross-domain correlation, as all domains are coupled through the shared constraint architecture; (b) structural synchronization, wherein phase transitions occur simultaneously across domains; (c) threshold behavior, characterized by discontinuous rather than gradual decline; and (d) control group divergence, wherein populations maintaining constraint pressure above Pc retain coherence.

The present study tests these four predictions against longitudinal data from the United States (1940–2024), employing the Moral Chronology dataset (MDA_ALL_EXCEL_COMBINED.xlsx; Moral_Chronology.xlsx) and the Moral America Consolidated dataset (MORAL_AMERICA_CONSOLIDATED.xlsx), with supplementary data from the National Center for Education Statistics, Federal Reserve, Bureau of Labor Statistics, Federal Bureau of Investigation Uniform Crime Reports, General Social Survey, American National Election Studies, and National Center for Health Statistics.

## 2. Test 1: Cross-Domain Correlation

### 2.1 Methodological Framework

The null hypothesis (H₀) posits that the seven institutional domains—Family, Religious, Institutional, Education, Media, Economic, and Social—constitute independent phenomena, yielding an expected mean pairwise correlation of R̄ ≈ 0. The alternative hypothesis (H₁) posits that these domains represent repeated measurements of a single latent variable (χ), yielding an expected mean pairwise correlation of R̄ ≫ 0.

Pairwise Pearson correlation coefficients were computed across all seven domain indices for the full measurement period (1940–2024, n = 11 time points). The Fisher z-transformation was applied to assess the statistical significance of the observed mean correlation.

### 2.2 Results

**Table 1: Pairwise Pearson Correlation Matrix — Seven Domains, 1940–2024**

| Domain | Family | Religious | Institutional | Education | Media | Economic | Social |
|--------|--------|-----------|---------------|-----------|-------|----------|--------|
| Family | 1.000 | — | — | — | — | — | — |
| Religious | 0.984 | 1.000 | — | — | — | — | — |
| Institutional | 0.996 | 0.989 | 1.000 | — | — | — | — |
| Education | 0.994 | 0.995 | 0.990 | 1.000 | — | — | — |
| Media | 0.993 | 0.967 | 0.991 | 0.978 | 1.000 | — | — |
| Economic | 0.970 | 0.996 | 0.976 | 0.989 | 0.945 | 1.000 | — |
| Social | 0.998 | 0.989 | 0.996 | 0.995 | 0.991 | 0.976 | 1.000 |

*Source: Moral America Consolidated dataset (MORAL_AMERICA_CONSOLIDATED.xlsx, Core_Metrics, J8).*

The mean pairwise correlation across all 21 domain pairs is R̄ = 0.986. The Fisher z-test yields z = 7.44, corresponding to p << 0.0001. All 21 pairs exceed the critical threshold of r = 0.602 (Bonferroni-corrected for multiple comparisons at α = 0.05).

### 2.3 Interpretation

The null hypothesis of domain independence is decisively rejected. The observed mean correlation of 0.986 indicates that the seven domains are not independent phenomena but rather constitute repeated measurements of a single latent variable. This finding is consistent with the hypothesis that social coherence (χ) functions as a real order parameter governing all seven domains simultaneously.

## 3. Test 2: Structural Break Synchronization

### 3.1 Methodological Framework

If the seven domains are independent, the periods of maximum decline for each domain should be distributed randomly across the 84-year measurement period (1940–2024). Conversely, if the domains are coupled through a shared order parameter undergoing a phase transition, the periods of maximum decline should cluster within a narrow temporal window corresponding to the critical transition.

The period of maximum single-period decline was identified independently for each domain using the Moral Chronology dataset.

Six of seven domains exhibit maximum decline within the 1968–1973 window. The Economic domain shows maximum decline in 1980, representing a delayed response consistent with the lagged transmission of constraint removal to monetary systems following the 1971 closure of the gold window.

p < 10⁻⁸

### 3.3 Interpretation

The null hypothesis of random temporal distribution is rejected at a significance level far below conventional thresholds. The clustering of maximum decline periods within the 1968–1973 window constitutes evidence of a phase transition occurring simultaneously across domains, consistent with the hypothesis that a single order parameter (χ) governs all seven domains.

## 4. Test 3: Threshold Behavior

### 4.1 Methodological Framework

If the decline in social coherence represents a true phase transition, the rate of decline should be discontinuous—significantly faster during the critical window than during either the preceding or subsequent periods. The rate of decline (points per year) was computed for three distinct periods: pre-transition (1940–1960), critical window (1960–1973), and post-transition (1973–2024).

### 4.2 Results

**Table 3: Decline Rate by Period**

| Period | Duration | Average Decline | Rate (points/year) |
|--------|----------|----------------|--------------------|
| 1940–1960 | 20 years | 8.6 points | 0.43 |
| **1960–1973 (Critical Window)** | **13 years** | **25.0 points** | **1.92** |
| 1973–2024 | 51 years | 38.9 points | 0.76 |

*Sources: Pre-transition period (MORAL_AMERICA_CONSOLIDATED.xlsx, Core_Metrics, L18; FBI UCR); Critical window (MORAL_AMERICA_CONSOLIDATED.xlsx, Core_Metrics, N8, K8; Legal Records); Post-transition period (MORAL_AMERICA_CONSOLIDATED.xlsx, Core_Metrics, N14; Gallup).*

The decline rate during the critical window (1.92 points/year) is 4.5 times greater than the pre-transition rate (0.43 points/year) and 2.5 times greater than the post-transition rate (0.76 points/year).

### 4.3 Interpretation

The observed discontinuity in decline rate is characteristic of a critical transition. Gradual erosion would produce a constant or smoothly accelerating rate of decline; instead, the data exhibit a sharp acceleration during the 1960–1973 window followed by a deceleration to a rate intermediate between the pre-transition and critical-window rates. This pattern is consistent with the signature of a phase transition in which the system crosses a critical threshold (Pc) and undergoes a rapid state change.

## 5. Test 4: Control Group Divergence

### 5.1 Methodological Framework

If coherence collapse is caused by constraint removal, then populations that rejected the constraint-removal events of the 1968–1973 period should maintain stable coherence. The Amish constitute a natural experiment in this regard, as they explicitly rejected the following constraint-removal events: no-fault divorce, fiat currency dependence, mass media saturation, the contraceptive revolution, and the erosion of religious authority structures.

The prediction is that χ_Amish(t) ≈ constant while χ_America(t) → 0 over the measurement period.

The finding confirms the prediction that constraint pressure (P) is the governing variable for social coherence (χ).
