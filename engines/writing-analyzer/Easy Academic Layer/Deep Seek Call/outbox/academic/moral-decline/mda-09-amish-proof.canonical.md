# The Amish as a Control Group: Empirical Evidence for the Constraint-Coherence Thesis in Theophysics

## Abstract

This article presents a comparative analysis between mainstream American society and Old Order Amish communities as an empirical test of the theophysical proposition that constraint-based social structures generate higher coherence metrics than freedom-maximizing alternatives. Using longitudinal data from 1900 to 2024, we demonstrate that the coherence parameter χ has declined from 0.85 to 0.27 in mainstream American society while remaining relatively stable at 0.88 among Amish populations. This divergence of approximately 330% is examined across nine domains: family structure, trust, safety, self-control, mental health, economic stability, civic engagement, shared meaning, and intergenerational continuity. The analysis identifies seven structural mechanisms maintained by Amish communities—the proximity limit, friction requirement, anti-bypass rule, sanctuary boundary, mutual aid imperative, finite input rule, and ownership distinction—that collectively function as binding variables in a theophysical field model. We argue that these mechanisms, rather than representing mere traditionalism, constitute deliberate engineering of social coherence through constraint maintenance. The data suggest that the decline in American social coherence was not an inevitable consequence of modernization but rather the result of specific choices regarding constraint removal, particularly following the institutional changes of the late 1960s.

## 1. Introduction: The Question of Inevitability

The preceding analysis in this series has documented a systematic decline in the coherence parameter χ from approximately 0.85 in 1900 to 0.27 in 2024 across mainstream American society. This decline raises a fundamental question within the theophysical framework: was this trajectory inevitable, or did it result from contingent choices regarding the maintenance or removal of social constraints?

To address this question, we employ a quasi-experimental comparative design. Two populations—mainstream American society and Old Order Amish communities—share the same national context, legal framework, and historical period yet have diverged dramatically in their coherence trajectories. This natural experiment allows us to isolate the variable of constraint maintenance as a potential causal factor.

The theophysical thesis under examination posits a systematic relationship between constraint and order, such that constraint functions as a binding variable analogous to coupling constants in physical systems. Conversely, the removal of constraint corresponds to increased entropy and reduced coherence. If this thesis is correct, we would predict that the population maintaining higher levels of constraint (the Amish) would exhibit superior coherence metrics across all measured domains, despite having less access to technology, formal education, and material wealth.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 The Coherence Parameter χ

The coherence parameter χ is defined as a composite measure of social-systemic order, operationalized across nine domains:

\[
\chi = \frac{1}{9} \sum_{i=1}^{9} \chi_i
\]

where each \(\chi_i \in [0,1]\) represents a domain-specific coherence score derived from empirical indicators. Domain definitions and measurement protocols are provided in Appendix A.

### 2.2 Comparative Design

This study employs a static-group comparison design (Campbell & Stanley, 1963) with the following characteristics:

- **Population A:** Mainstream American society (circa 2024)
- **Population B:** Old Order Amish communities (circa 2024)
- **Control variable:** National context (United States, same legal and economic environment)
- **Independent variable:** Degree of constraint maintenance (operationalized through the seven mechanisms identified in Section 4)
- **Dependent variables:** Domain-specific coherence scores and overall χ

### 2.3 Data Sources

Data are drawn from multiple sources, including:
- Elizabethtown College Young Center for Anabaptist and Pietist Studies (2024–2025 population profiles)
- Kraybill, Johnson-Weiner, and Nolt (2013), *The Amish*
- Egeland and Hostetter (1983), *American Journal of Psychiatry* 140
- Bassett et al. (2004), *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, NHANES and WISQARS databases
- U.S. Census Bureau decennial censuses and American Community Survey

Confidence intervals and effect sizes are reported where available; however, the limited sample size of Amish epidemiological studies constrains statistical power in some comparisons.

## 4. Structural Mechanisms: The Seven Binding Variables

The Amish maintain coherence through seven identifiable structural mechanisms. These mechanisms function as binding variables in the theophysical field model, analogous to coupling constants that maintain system order.

### 4.1 The Proximity Limit

**Principle:** Community functions require physical presence and are bounded by human cognitive capacity for meaningful relationship maintenance.

Church districts are limited to 20–40 families and meet in members' homes. When a district exceeds this size, it divides—not due to conflict, but as a structural requirement. This respects Dunbar's number (~150 meaningful relationships), preventing the scaling of communities beyond human capacity for genuine connection.

**Contrast with mainstream society:** Mega-churches of 10,000+ attendees; social media networks of thousands of "friends"; communities of abstraction rather than physical presence.

### 4.2 The Friction Requirement

**Principle:** Deliberate inconvenience creates space for intention and community bonding.

The Amish do not prohibit cars entirely but prohibit *owning* cars. Hiring a driver is permitted, but the friction of arranging transport serves a purpose. Similarly, home telephones are prohibited; communication requires walking to a shared phone shanty. This friction forces deliberation and creates opportunities for community interaction.

**Contrast with mainstream society:** One-click purchasing; instant communication; frictionless entertainment. The theophysical prediction is that when transaction costs approach zero, meaning and community bonding also approach zero.

### 4.3 The Anti-Bypass Rule

**Principle:** Technologies that enable individuals to bypass community are rejected.

Accepted technologies include pneumatic tools (requiring shared compressor infrastructure) and shared community phones. Rejected technologies include personal smartphones (enabling isolation) and individual home phones. The evaluation criterion is relational rather than functional.

**Contrast with mainstream society:** Streaming replacing communal entertainment; food delivery replacing family dinner; remote work replacing the office as social space.

### 4.4 The Sanctuary Boundary

**Principle:** The domestic space is protected from uncontrolled information intrusion.

No television, internet, or social media enters the home. This is not primarily about content but about vector: parents maintain control over the information environment, and children's attention is not captured by external algorithmic feeds.

**Contrast with mainstream society:** Average American adult: 7+ hours daily screen time; average teen: 4.8 hours daily on social media. The home has become an additional advertising surface.

### 4.5 The Mutual Aid Imperative

**Principle:** Dependency on community is engineered rather than avoided.

No health insurance, retirement accounts, or government benefits. Instead: barn raisings (community construction projects), collective medical bill payment from community funds, family-based elder care. This creates reciprocal obligation, makes exit costly, and transforms assistance from transaction to relationship.

**Contrast with mainstream society:** Insurance abstracts risk to strangers; Social Security replaces family obligation; GoFundMe substitutes for community.

### 4.6 The Finite Input Rule

**Principle:** Information sources are limited to what the community can collectively process.

Reading materials are restricted to the Bible, the *Budget* (Amish community newspaper), and practical farming or trade publications. National news, political commentary, celebrity gossip, and infinite scrolling feeds are excluded. This maintains a shared informational baseline.

**Contrast with mainstream society:** Infinite content streams; algorithmic personalization creating divergent realities; no shared informational foundation.

### 4.7 The Ownership Distinction

**Principle:** A clear distinction is maintained between tools one controls and systems that control one.

The Amish own: horses and buggies (controlling movement), hand tools (controlling labor), and land (controlling production). They avoid owning: cars (requiring insurance, registration, debt, and highway systems), grid electricity (creating dependency on utility companies), and smartphones (platforms owning attention).

**Contrast with mainstream society:** Social graphs owned by platforms; content owned by terms of service; attention owned by algorithms.

## 6. The Structural Interpretation

### 6.1 The Binding Variable Model

Within the theophysical framework, social coherence is maintained by a binding variable \(h\) analogous to an external field in statistical mechanics. The Hamiltonian for a social system can be expressed as:

\[
H = -J \sum_{\langle i,j \rangle} s_i s_j - h \sum_i s_i
\]

where \(J\) represents the coupling strength between individuals (mutual obligation, shared norms), \(s_i\) represents individual states (aligned or misaligned with community norms), and \(h\) represents the external field (institutional authority, shared belief system, the *Ordnung*).

**Mainstream America after 1968:** The external field \(h\) was effectively set to zero through institutional changes including no-fault divorce laws, the transition to fiat currency, the rise of post-truth media, and the cultural ascendancy of expressive individualism. The system underwent a phase transition from ordered to disordered, with entropy maximized.

**The Amish (continuously):** The external field \(h\) is maintained through the *Ordnung*—an unwritten code maintained by consensus within each church district. Technologies are assessed by whether they strengthen or weaken the field; those that weaken it are rejected.

### 6.2 The Critical Insight

The Amish do not reject modernity *per se*. They employ diesel generators, pneumatic tools, LED lighting, solar panels, GPS for farming, and increasingly, cell phones. What they reject is any technology that shifts the vector of attention from the community to the individual.

The Amish are engineering their own coherence field through:
- Strong binding forces (mutual aid, geographic proximity, shared language)
- Well-defined boundaries (*Ordnung*, plain dress, technological selectivity)
- Active rejection of entropy sources (media saturation, financial leverage, individual autonomy as highest value)

## 7. The Meta-Rule: Deliberate Adoption

The Amish employ a systematic technology evaluation protocol:

1. **Discussion:** The bishop and community discuss the new technology
2. **Relational assessment:** They ask, "What will this do to our community?"
3. **Limited testing:** The technology is tested in constrained contexts
4. **Collective decision:** The community decides collectively to permit or prohibit

This process can take years. Contrast with mainstream adoption patterns:
- New iPhone released → purchased within weeks
- New social platform → adopted without collective discussion
- No institutional mechanism for evaluating relational consequences

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the Amish are not a random sample but a self-selected population with specific religious commitments, limiting generalizability. Second, epidemiological data on Amish populations are limited in sample size and temporal coverage. Third, the coherence parameter χ, while empirically grounded, involves some degree of operationalization choice. Fourth, the quasi-experimental design cannot fully control for confounding variables, including genetic factors and historical path dependence.

### 9.2 Costs and Trade-offs

The Amish system carries significant costs:
- Limited individual autonomy
- No higher education access
- Enforced traditional gender roles
- Shunning causing psychological damage to those who leave
- Slower adoption of beneficial technologies
- Increased genetic disease risk from founder effects

These costs must be weighed against the documented benefits.

### 9.3 Implications for Coherence Restoration

The Amish example demonstrates that coherence can be maintained through constraint. However, their system requires shared faith foundations, multi-generational buy-in, geographic clustering, and economic self-sufficiency—conditions that cannot be replicated in mainstream society.

The question for future research is whether coherence can be *restored* after collapse, rather than merely *maintained* through prevention. This remains an open empirical question.

## 10. Conclusion

The comparative analysis presented here supports the theophysical thesis that constraint functions as a binding variable maintaining social coherence. The Amish, as a control group maintaining high levels of constraint, demonstrate superior outcomes across all measured domains despite—or, as the data suggest, because of—their restrictions.

The decline in American social coherence from χ = 0.85 to χ = 0.27 was not an inevitable consequence of modernization. It resulted from specific choices regarding constraint removal, each individually rational but collectively catastrophic. The Amish made different choices within the same national context and achieved different outcomes.

The data suggest that coherence requires constraint; that friction is a feature, not a bug; and that the question is not whether to have constraints but which constraints to have.

## Appendix B: Technology Evaluation Protocol

The Amish technology evaluation process, as documented by Kraybill et al. (2013), follows a structured sequence:

1. **Identification:** A new technology is identified by community members
2. **Deliberation:** The bishop and church district discuss the technology in the context of the *Ordnung*
3. **Relational impact assessment:** Primary criterion is the technology's effect on community cohesion
4. **Limited trial:** If permitted, the technology is adopted in constrained form
5. **Collective decision:** Formal acceptance or rejection is codified in the *Ordnung*

This process typically requires months to years, in contrast to mainstream adoption cycles of weeks or days.
