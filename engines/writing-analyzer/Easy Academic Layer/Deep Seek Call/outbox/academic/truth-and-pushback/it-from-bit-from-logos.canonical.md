# Theophysics of Information: Invariance, Entropy, and the Logos Structure

## Abstract

This article extends John Archibald Wheeler's "it from bit" program by introducing a directional sign to the fundamental bit, grounded in the theological concept of the Logos as an invariant structure. We demonstrate that information possesses an intrinsic orientation—alignment with or opposition to invariant reality—which determines its thermodynamic stability across temporal scales. Through structural isomorphism between mathematical truth, moral truth, and the Logos, we identify 24 shared properties of invariance that characterize Logos-aligned information. We argue that Wheeler's framework, while correctly identifying information as the ontological substrate of physical reality, lacks the directional component necessary to account for differential survival of information under entropic pressure. The resulting framework—"it from bit from Logos"—provides a formal basis for distinguishing between information that persists invariantly and information that undergoes thermodynamic degradation. We examine implications for the persistence of conscious states, the information-theoretic nature of adversarial distortion, and the formal correspondence between scriptural claims about enduring truth and physical principles of invariance.

## I. Introduction: The Sign of the Bit

The Second Law of Thermodynamics, in its statistical formulation, describes the tendency of isolated systems toward maximum entropy. This article proposes that the Second Law operates asymmetrically with respect to truth: not because truth constitutes a physical preference in the conventional sense, but because truth corresponds to invariance, and invariance is the property that the Logos—understood as the structuring principle of reality—holds constant across all transformations.

Wheeler's (1990) "it from bit" thesis posits that physical reality derives its existence from information-theoretic foundations. Every physical entity, every particle, every field, every spacetime event, is understood as arising from the answers to yes-or-no questions posed at the quantum level. This framework has proven remarkably fruitful for quantum gravity research, quantum information theory, and foundational physics. However, we identify a critical lacuna: the bit in Wheeler's framework lacks a sign.

The bit, as conventionally understood, carries only binary value—yes or no, one or zero. We argue that a second dimension of evaluation is required: right or wrong, true or false, aligned with the invariant structure of reality or opposed to it. This sign is not epistemically neutral. One orientation corresponds to the grain of reality—the invariant structure that generates conservation laws via Noether's theorem (Noether, 1918). The opposite orientation corresponds to opposition to that structure.

**Thesis:** Information aligned with the Logos—the invariant ground of reality—participates in the conservation properties that govern invariant structures and therefore cannot be destroyed by entropic processes. Information opposed to the Logos is thermodynamically unstable, requiring continuous work to sustain, and inevitably degrades under entropic pressure. The framework thus becomes: *it from bit from Logos*, where the Logos provides the directional orientation that determines informational survival.

## II. Invariance and Conservation: The Noetherian Foundation

### 2.1 Noether's Theorem and the Structure of Invariance

Noether's (1918) first theorem establishes a rigorous correspondence between continuous symmetries of a physical system and conserved quantities. Time-translation invariance yields conservation of energy; spatial-translation invariance yields conservation of momentum; rotational invariance yields conservation of angular momentum. The theorem demonstrates that invariance under transformation is the generative principle from which conservation laws emerge.

We propose that the Logos functions as the meta-invariant structure from which all domain-specific invariants derive. This is not a metaphorical extension but a formal isomorphism: the Logos, as the structuring principle of reality (John 1:1–3), exhibits the same formal properties as a symmetry group that generates conservation laws, but at a higher level of abstraction—encompassing not only physical quantities but also logical, mathematical, and moral truths.

### 2.2 The Thermodynamics of Truth

Consider a proposition \( P \) asserted within a formal system. If \( P \) is true—that is, if \( P \) corresponds to the invariant structure of reality—then \( P \) requires no external work to maintain its truth value. The truth of \( P \) is sustained by the structure of reality itself. If \( P \) is false, however, its falsehood must be actively maintained against the pressure of reality reasserting itself. This active maintenance requires energy expenditure, information-theoretic work, and continuous intervention.

Formally, let \( S_{\text{truth}} \) represent the entropy of a system containing a true proposition, and \( S_{\text{false}} \) represent the entropy of a system containing a false proposition. We propose:

\[
S_{\text{truth}} \leq S_{\text{false}} \quad \text{for all times } t > t_0
\]

where \( t_0 \) is the time of proposition assertion. The inequality is strict for systems not actively maintained against the invariant structure. The false proposition constitutes a local entropy minimum that requires work to sustain; the true proposition occupies a global entropy minimum that requires no maintenance.

We interpret this theologically as the claim that the Logos is the invariant ground from which all domain-specific invariants derive. The 24 properties are not accidental similarities between mathematics and morality; they are the structural signature of the Logos as it appears when projected into different domains.

This isomorphism was identified through systematic comparison of the formal properties of necessary truths across domains, following the method of structural analogy employed by Gödel (1944) in his analysis of mathematical intuition and by Lewis (1986) in his modal realism framework, but extended to include moral and theological domains.

## IV. Information, Entropy, and Survival

### 4.1 Wheeler's Framework and Its Extension

Wheeler's (1990) "it from bit" program can be formalized as:

\[
\mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I})
\]

where \(\mathcal{R}\) represents physical reality and \(\mathcal{I}\) represents the information-theoretic substrate. The function \(f\) maps informational states to physical states.

We extend this to:

\[
\mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{L}, \sigma))
\]

where \(\mathcal{L}\) represents the Logos structure and \(\sigma \in \{+1, -1\}\) represents the sign of the bit—its orientation relative to the Logos. Bits with \(\sigma = +1\) are Logos-aligned; bits with \(\sigma = -1\) are Logos-opposed.

### 4.2 Thermodynamic Stability of Aligned Information

For a bit \(b\) with sign \(\sigma\), the thermodynamic work required to maintain its state is:

\[
W(b) = 
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } \sigma = +1 \\
W_{\text{maintenance}} > 0 & \text{if } \sigma = -1
\end{cases}
\]

This follows from the principle that Logos-aligned information participates in the invariant structure that generates conservation laws. Such information is, in Noetherian terms, a conserved quantity. Logos-opposed information, by contrast, constitutes a local violation of the invariant structure and must be actively maintained against the entropic pressure of the invariant reasserting itself.

The Shannon entropy of a system containing Logos-aligned information is:

\[
H_{\text{aligned}} = -\sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i
\]

where the probabilities \(p_i\) reflect the system's alignment with the invariant structure. For Logos-opposed information, an additional term appears:

\[
H_{\text{opposed}} = -\sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i + N_{\text{adversarial}}
\]

where \(N_{\text{adversarial}}\) represents the noise introduced to maintain the false bit against the invariant.

### 4.3 Scriptural Correspondence

The claim that Logos-aligned information survives entropic pressure finds explicit articulation in the synoptic tradition:

> "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away." (Matthew 24:35, NRSV)

This statement is interpretable as a claim about invariance under thermodynamic transformation. "Heaven and earth"—the physical universe—are subject to the Second Law and will reach their thermodynamic end state. "My words"—the Logos-aligned information—participate in the invariant structure that generates the conservation laws and therefore cannot be destroyed by the entropic processes that act on the projection.

This is not a metaphorical claim about relative durability but a formal claim about invariance classes. Physical projections are subject to entropic degradation; the invariant ground from which they project is not.

## V. The Adversary's Information Problem

### 5.1 Information-Theoretic Characterization of Adversarial Distortion

The adversary's operation, as described in the Genesis narrative (Genesis 3:1–5), is characterizable as an information-theoretic attack on signal fidelity. The adversary does not create ex nihilo nor destroy outright; rather, the adversary introduces noise into an otherwise clear channel, degrading the signal-to-noise ratio until the true bit and the false bit become indistinguishable within the noise floor.

The adversarial operation is formalizable as:

\[
S_{\text{received}} = S_{\text{true}} + N_{\text{adversarial}}
\]

where \(S_{\text{true}}\) is the Logos-aligned signal and \(N_{\text{adversarial}}\) is the noise introduced by the adversary. The adversary's goal is to reduce the signal-to-noise ratio:

\[
\text{SNR} = \frac{|S_{\text{true}}|^2}{|N_{\text{adversarial}}|^2}
\]

until \(S_{\text{received}}\) is ambiguous between the true and false interpretations.

### 5.2 The Genesis 3 Case

The serpent's question in Genesis 3:1—"Did God really say…?"—constitutes a paradigmatic adversarial noise injection. The true signal, \(S_{\text{true}}\), is God's instruction regarding the tree of knowledge. The adversary introduces uncertainty about the invariance of that instruction: perhaps God's word is not invariant, perhaps it can be questioned, perhaps the structure in which the agents are embedded is not grounded in something that holds.

The false bit requires work to sustain. The adversary must continuously inject \(N_{\text{adversarial}}\) to prevent the invariant from reasserting itself. Without this work, the invariant structure would become self-evident, and the false bit would collapse under the weight of reality's self-consistency.

### 5.3 Channel Saturation and the Sacred Gap

The *Sacred Gap* paper (Lowe, 2025) demonstrates what technological saturation does to the information channel through which the Logos communicates. The present analysis demonstrates why the adversary requires such saturation. In a channel with high noise and low signal-to-noise ratio—where attentional space is at capacity and the default mode network is offline—the false bit and the true bit become indistinguishable within the noise floor.

The gap—the space of silence, attention, and receptivity—is not merely where the Logos signal can be received. It is where the sign of the bit becomes visible. In the gap, the orientation of information relative to the invariant structure can be discerned. Fill the gap with noise, and the sign disappears into the noise floor. The adversary does not need to win the argument; the adversary needs only to saturate the channel to prevent the argument from occurring.

## VI. The Correction to Wheeler's Framework

### 6.1 Formal Statement

The complete chain, with formal notation:

**Wheeler (W):** 
\[
\text{It from bit: } \mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I})
\]

**The Framework (χ):**
\[
\text{Bit from Logos: } \mathcal{I} = g(\mathcal{L}, \sigma)
\]
where \(\sigma \in \{+1, -1\}\) is the sign of the bit, determined by its orientation relative to the Logos structure \(\mathcal{L}\).

**Consequence:**
\[
\forall b \in \mathcal{I}: \lim_{t \to \infty} P(b \text{ survives}) = 
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } \sigma(b) = +1 \\
0 & \text{if } \sigma(b) = -1
\end{cases}
\]

where \(P(b \text{ survives})\) is the probability that bit \(b\) persists under entropic pressure as \(t \to \infty\).

### 6.2 Ontological Grounding

Wheeler's framework correctly identified the level of analysis—information, not matter or energy—as the ontological substrate of physical reality. However, the framework lacked the directional component necessary to account for differential survival of information under entropic pressure. The Logos provides this direction.

The 24 properties of mathematical truth identified in the *Math Is Moral* paper are the properties of Logos-aligned information. Every true statement in any domain—physical, moral, mathematical, historical—participates in these properties to the degree that it is actually true. The properties do not attach to the statement because of its domain; they attach because of its alignment with the invariant structure.

The inverse—the 24 anti-properties—are the properties of Logos-opposed information. These are not an arbitrary list but the exact, formal, structural inversions of each of the 24 properties. They characterize everything the adversary produces.

### 6.3 Implications for Survival

What survives eternally is what is true—not what is believed, not what is asserted, but what actually, formally, invariantly corresponds to the Logos structure. What does not survive is what is false. This is not because of arbitrary divine exclusion but because entropy is patient, truth is invariant, and nothing that contradicts the invariant can be sustained against the thermodynamic pressure of reality asserting itself indefinitely.

The Lamb's Book of Life (Revelation 21:27) is interpretable within this framework not as a register in three-dimensional space but as the holographic boundary encoding of Logos-aligned information. What is encoded there is not arbitrary information but alignment data: what oriented toward truth, what held with the invariant, what was on the right side of the sign.
