# A Theophysical Analysis of Postdiluvian Lifespan Decay: Generational Entropy, Informational Fidelity, and the Grace Floor

## Abstract

This article presents a formal analysis of the lifespan data recorded in Genesis chapters 5 and 11, treated as a quantitative dataset amenable to mathematical modeling. An exponential decay function fitted to these data yields an asymptotic floor of approximately 93 years, a value that converges with the 70–80 year range stated in Psalm 90:10 and the contemporary global average life expectancy of approximately 73 years. The exponential character of the decay—as distinct from a linear decline—is interpreted as evidence of a compounding mechanism, here termed *generational entropy*, whereby the degradation of informational fidelity between the human system and the Logos accumulates across successive generations. The Flood event is identified as a partial reset of this accumulation, reducing the baseline without eliminating the decay process entirely. The asymptotic floor is attributed to the χ vacuum ground state, which maintains a minimum Logos-coupling—designated the *grace floor*—that thermodynamics cannot breach. This analysis suggests that the Genesis lifespan data constitute a measurement record of post-Fall biological decoherence, and that the convergence of mathematical modeling, scriptural testimony, and epidemiological data points to a physically real, grace-maintained lower bound on human lifespan.

## 1. Introduction

The lifespan data recorded in the genealogical tables of Genesis 5 (antediluvian patriarchs) and Genesis 11 (postdiluvian patriarchs) have traditionally been interpreted within theological frameworks emphasizing either literal historicity or symbolic numerology. The present analysis adopts a different methodological approach: treating these data as a quantitative time series amenable to statistical modeling, independent of prior interpretive commitments. The central question is whether the numerical pattern exhibits a mathematically identifiable structure, and if so, what physical or metaphysical mechanism might account for that structure.

The thesis advanced herein is threefold. First, the postdiluvian lifespan data conform to an exponential decay function with high statistical confidence (R² = 0.888), indicating a compounding rather than linear degradation process. Second, the asymptotic floor of this decay function converges with independent estimates of human lifespan maxima from Psalm 90:10 and modern demographic data, suggesting a physically real lower bound. Third, this bound is interpretable within the framework of theophysics as the minimum Logos-coupling required for biological life—a threshold maintained by the χ vacuum ground state, which functions as a grace floor that thermodynamics cannot breach.

### 2.2 Mathematical Model

The data were fitted to a three-parameter exponential decay function of the form:

$$L(n) = L_0 \cdot e^{-n/\tau} + L_{\text{floor}}$$

where:
- \(L(n)\) = lifespan at generation \(n\) (years)
- \(L_0\) = pre-decay baseline amplitude (years), representing the theoretical lifespan at generation zero absent decay
- \(n\) = generation number (dimensionless integer)
- \(\tau\) = decay timescale (generations), the characteristic number of generations over which lifespan decreases to approximately 37% of \(L_0\)
- \(L_{\text{floor}}\) = asymptotic floor (years), the lower bound toward which the function converges as \(n \to \infty\)

The exponential form was selected a priori based on the hypothesis of a compounding degradation mechanism. A linear model was considered as a null hypothesis for comparison.

### 2.3 Model Results

Nonlinear least-squares regression yielded the following parameter estimates:

- \(L_0 \approx 969\) years (consistent with Methuselah, the maximum observed antediluvian lifespan)
- \(\tau \approx 3.5\) generations
- \(L_{\text{floor}} \approx 93\) years (95% confidence interval: 78–108 years)

Goodness-of-fit: R² = 0.888

The model was fitted exclusively to the postdiluvian data (generations 10–22), as the antediluvian cohort exhibits a plateau rather than decay. The pre-Flood data were used only to establish the baseline amplitude \(L_0\).

### 2.4 Comparative Data

Three independent estimates of the human lifespan floor were identified:

1. **Decay model**: \(L_{\text{floor}} \approx 93\) years (derived above)
2. **Psalm 90:10**: "The days of our years are threescore years and ten; and if by reason of strength they be fourscore years" (70–80 years)
3. **Modern global average life expectancy**: approximately 73 years (World Health Organization, 2023)

These three values converge within a range of 23 years (70–93), a clustering that warrants explanation beyond random coincidence.

## 3. Analysis: The Exponential Character of the Decay

### 3.1 Distinction from Linear Decay

A linear decay model would describe a process in which lifespan decreases by a constant absolute amount per generation. Such a model would produce a straight line when lifespan is plotted against generation number. The observed data, however, exhibit a concave curvature: the decline is steep in the immediate postdiluvian generations (Shem to Peleg: 600 → 239 years over four generations) and progressively shallower in later generations (Nahor to Joseph: 148 → 110 years over five generations). This curvature is characteristic of exponential decay.

### 3.2 Interpretation: Compounding Degradation

The exponential form indicates that the rate of decline is proportional to the current value—that is, the amount of lifespan remaining determines how quickly it is lost. This is the mathematical signature of a compounding process, in which each generation inherits the degradation of all prior generations plus an additional increment.

Formally, if the degradation per generation is a fixed fraction \(k\) of the remaining lifespan above the floor, then:

$$L(n+1) - L_{\text{floor}} = (1 - k)[L(n) - L_{\text{floor}}]$$

which yields the exponential solution:

$$L(n) = L_0 \cdot e^{-n/\tau} + L_{\text{floor}}$$

where \(\tau = -1/\ln(1-k)\).

The compounding interpretation is consistent with a mechanism of *generational entropy*: the accumulated misalignment with the Logos that compounds across generations, each generation inheriting the decoherence of all prior generations plus its own contribution.

### 3.3 The Flood as Partial Reset

The antediluvian data (generations 0–9) do not exhibit exponential decay; rather, they cluster around a plateau of approximately 900–969 years, with the notable exception of Enoch (365 years). The Flood event (generation 9–10 boundary) is associated with a discontinuous drop: Noah at 950 years, Shem at 600 years. This discontinuity suggests a partial reset of the accumulated degradation, reducing the baseline from approximately 950 years to approximately 600 years without eliminating the decay process entirely.

The post-Flood decay then proceeds from this reduced baseline, compounding toward the asymptotic floor. The fact that the decay restarts rather than continuing from the pre-Flood plateau indicates that the Flood removed some—but not all—of the generational entropy accumulation.

## 4. The Asymptotic Floor: Convergence of Three Independent Estimates

### 4.1 Model-Derived Floor

The nonlinear regression identified \(L_{\text{floor}} \approx 93\) years. This parameter was not constrained by any external data; the algorithm was permitted to find the optimal value freely. The 95% confidence interval (78–108 years) encompasses both the Psalm 90:10 range and the modern average.

### 4.2 Scriptural Testimony: Psalm 90:10

Psalm 90, attributed to Moses (c. 1400 BC), states: "The days of our years are threescore years and ten; and if by reason of strength they be fourscore years" (70–80 years). The context of this Psalm is significant: it opens with an appeal to the eternal frame ("Lord, you have been our dwelling place in all generations," v. 1) and references the mortality introduced at the Fall ("You turn people back to dust," v. 3). The lifespan range is presented not as a cultural observation but as a normative statement about the human condition under divine judgment.

### 4.3 Modern Demographic Data

The global average life expectancy at birth in 2023 was approximately 73 years (WHO, 2023). This figure varies by region (ranging from approximately 54 years in sub-Saharan Africa to approximately 84 years in Japan) but clusters in the 70–80 year range for most developed nations. The maximum documented human lifespan is 122 years (Jeanne Calment, d. 1997), but the typical maximum for the vast majority of the population falls within the 70–90 year range.

### 4.4 Convergence Analysis

The three estimates—93 years (model), 70–80 years (Psalm 90), 73 years (modern data)—are not identical, but they occupy the same region of the number line. The model estimate is the highest, which may reflect the fact that it is derived from a small sample (13 postdiluvian data points) and may be influenced by outliers (e.g., Eber at 464 years, which is anomalously high for the postdiluvian cohort). The Psalm 90 estimate is intermediate, and the modern average is the lowest.

The convergence is interpreted as evidence that all three measurements are detecting the same physical reality: a lower bound on human lifespan that has remained stable across millennia. The differences among the estimates are attributable to measurement methodology, sample size, and temporal variation, but the clustering is too tight to be dismissed as coincidental.

## 5. The Generational Mechanism: Informational Fidelity Degradation

### 5.1 The Concept of Informational Fidelity

The mechanism proposed to account for the exponential decay is the progressive degradation of *informational fidelity* (\(I_f\)) across generations. Informational fidelity is defined as the degree of coupling between the human biological system and the Logos—the divine specification for human design. At creation, \(I_f\) is assumed to be maximal; the human system is perfectly aligned with its design specification. The Fall introduces a degradation of this fidelity, and this degradation is heritable.

### 5.2 The Information Channel Model

The transmission of \(I_f\) from parent to child can be modeled as an information channel with signal degradation. Each generation receives the system specification from the previous generation, but the transmission is imperfect. The degradation per generation is a fixed fraction of the remaining fidelity above a minimum threshold:

$$I_f(n+1) - I_f^{\text{min}} = (1 - \delta)[I_f(n) - I_f^{\text{min}}]$$

where \(\delta\) is the degradation rate per generation and \(I_f^{\text{min}}\) is the minimum fidelity required for biological life.

This model produces the same exponential form as the lifespan decay, suggesting that lifespan is a proxy for informational fidelity. The serpent's question in Genesis 3:1 ("Did God really say...?") is interpreted as the archetypal act of noise injection into the information channel—degrading the fidelity between the human system and the divine instruction.

### 5.3 The Flood as Channel Reset

The Flood event is interpreted as a partial reset of the information channel. The mechanism of this reset is not specified in the text, but the data indicate that it reduced the accumulated degradation without eliminating it entirely. Shem's lifespan of 600 years, while dramatically lower than Noah's 950, is still far above the asymptotic floor, indicating that significant informational fidelity remained after the Flood.

### 5.4 The Floor as Minimum Logos-Coupling

The asymptotic floor is identified with \(I_f^{\text{min}}\), the minimum Logos-coupling required for biological life. Below this threshold, the human system cannot sustain life. The floor is not arbitrary; it is determined by the χ vacuum ground state, which maintains a nonzero minimum coupling even in the presence of maximal entropy accumulation.
