## II. The Double-Slit Experiment: Information as Physical Parameter

The double-slit experiment, described by Richard Feynman as containing "the only mystery" of quantum mechanics, demonstrates a phenomenon that remains inadequately incorporated into classical ontology. When electrons are fired one at a time toward a barrier containing two slits, with no detector present to determine which slit each electron traverses, the accumulated hits on the detection screen form an interference pattern characteristic of wave-like behavior. When a which-path detector is installed, the interference pattern vanishes, and the screen displays a particle-like distribution consistent with classical ballistic trajectories.

Crucially, the which-path detector need not physically disturb the particle in any classical sense. The information can be extracted with arbitrarily small physical impact. What destroys the interference pattern is not the mechanical perturbation of measurement but the *possibility of knowing* which path the particle took. The pattern collapses the moment which-path information becomes available—even if no conscious entity ever reads that information.

The standard decoherence account of this phenomenon is technically correct but operationally incomplete. Decoherence describes the mechanism by which quantum superpositions become classical mixtures through interaction with a large environment. However, decoherence does not explain *why measurements have definite outcomes at all*—the measurement problem remains open. The decoherence account describes the propagation of *knowability* without addressing the question of whose knowing counts or what degree of coherence the knower possesses.

## III. The Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research Program

### III.1 Experimental Design and Methodology

The Princeton Engineering Anomalies Research (PEAR) laboratory, operating from 1979 to 2007 at Princeton University, conducted what remains one of the most statistically rigorous investigations of intention-related anomalies ever undertaken by a major research institution. The experimental apparatus consisted of a Random Event Generator (REG)—an electronic circuit producing random bits at a steady rate using quantum noise as its randomness source. In baseline operation, the REG produced binary outcomes with a probability of 0.5 each, converging to this mean over millions of bits to many decimal places.

Human operators were instructed to mentally influence the REG output according to a pre-registered protocol block—to push the output high, push it low, or maintain baseline. No physical contact or electrical connection existed between operator and apparatus. Operators held focused intention for several minutes per trial.

### III.2 Statistical Results

Over 28 years and approximately 2.5 million experimental trials, the cumulative result showed a deviation from chance expectation of approximately six standard deviations (6σ). The per-trial effect magnitude was small—mean shifts on the order of \(10^{-4}\) in the bit probability—but the dataset was sufficiently large and the signature was directionally consistent with operator intention.

In particle physics, five standard deviations (5σ) constitutes the threshold for discovery. The Higgs boson was announced at 5σ with a substantially smaller dataset. PEAR's cumulative result exceeds this threshold by an order of magnitude in statistical significance.

### III.3 Critical Assessment

The PEAR dataset is not without legitimate criticism. Effect sizes declined over the 28-year period. Independent replications have produced mixed results. Critiques regarding selection effects and optional stopping have been raised and only partially addressed. The file-drawer problem—the possibility that null results remain unpublished—constitutes a fair question.

However, the following assessment is warranted: PEAR has not been refuted. It has been *unincorporated*. The statistics are real, the dataset is large, the protocols were pre-registered, and the result—while contested—has not been cleanly overturned by methodological critique. The absence of consciousness-coupling from physics curricula is attributable not to decisive refutation but to the subject having been deemed unworthy of serious engagement.

## IV. The Global Consciousness Project: Independent Confirmation at Scale

### IV.1 Experimental Design

Roger Nelson, PEAR's long-time laboratory manager, founded the Global Consciousness Project (GCP) in 1998 to investigate whether collective coherent attention produces measurable effects on a distributed network of REGs. The experimental design deployed dozens of REGs worldwide, running continuously and generating random bits twenty-four hours per day. When pre-registered global events occurred—events attracting simultaneous emotional focus from large populations—the hypothesis predicted correlated, non-random signatures in the distributed REG network.

Events were selected in advance according to a fixed, publicly documented protocol. No post-hoc cherry-picking was permitted.

### IV.2 Statistical Results

The September 11, 2001 attacks produced one of the largest deviations in GCP history, with anomalous signatures beginning in the hours surrounding the event. By 2015, after analyzing over 500 pre-registered global events, the cumulative Z-score had reached approximately six standard deviations.

The GCP thus provides independent confirmation of the general direction observed in PEAR. Different methodology, different scale, different investigators—yet converging on a similar sigma threshold in the same direction. Two independent datasets pointing toward the same conclusion constitute stronger evidence than either alone.

### IV.3 Critical Assessment

The GCP faces legitimate methodological criticism. The event selection protocol, while principled, is not infinitely tight. The analytic pipeline contains researcher degrees of freedom. Over 500 events, small biases can accumulate. The debate remains live.

Nevertheless, the result has been on the table for two decades without refutation. The mainstream response has been closer to avoidance than engagement. A per-trial effect too small to affect daily life, combined with a cumulative signature too strong to dismiss, describes precisely the shape of a real but subtle phenomenon.

**Response:** True in part. Independent replication has produced mixed results. This lowers confidence in the effect without eliminating it. A 28-year single-laboratory result with mixed external replication is not the same epistemic object as a 28-year single-laboratory result with clean external replication. ### VIII.2 Objection: GCP Has Too Many Researcher Degrees of Freedom

**Response:** Partly true. The event selection protocol is principled but not infinitely tight; the analytic pipeline contains choices; over 500 events, small biases can accumulate. Independent re-analysis of the GCP corpus is precisely what the field should undertake and has largely refused to do. If the effect dissolves under re-analysis, that constitutes information, and the author will update accordingly.

### VIII.3 Objection: Decoherence Already Explains the Observer Effect

**Response:** Technically correct and operationally incomplete. Decoherence explains the mechanism by which quantum superpositions become classical-looking mixtures. It does not explain why measurements have definite outcomes at all. The measurement problem remains open. The framework's claim—that observer coherence is one input to which outcome becomes definite—is compatible with decoherence but not addressed by it.

### VIII.4 Objection: The Genesis Reading Is Eisegetical

**Response:** This is the most serious objection. The author acknowledges being a believer, and believers have a long history of finding their theology reflected in whatever science is current. The best response is that the sevenfold *ra'ah* structure is in the text regardless of the author's presence, and the three-operation decomposition of the Born Rule is in the physics regardless of a theologian's presence.
