# The Pattern as Signal: A Bayesian Rejoinder to the Mythicist Reference Class

## Abstract

This article presents a formal Bayesian critique of Richard Carrier's mythicist argument concerning the historicity of Jesus of Nazareth, as developed in *On the Historicity of Jesus* (2014). While affirming the methodological validity of Carrier's Bayesian framework, we identify a fundamental error in his selection of the reference class for prior probability estimation. Specifically, Carrier's classification of Jesus within the "heavily mythologized savior heroes" category presupposes that the cross-cultural recurrence of the death-resurrection-salvation motif constitutes evidence of fabrication rather than evidence of a real underlying phenomenon. Drawing upon the Projection Principle—a cross-domain isomorphism between theological claims about the Logos and empirically verified physical laws—we argue that this pattern constitutes signal, not noise. Correcting the reference class alone yields a posterior probability of approximately 49%, statistically indistinguishable from equipoise and sufficient to defeat Carrier's strong mythicist conclusion. Incorporating additional corrections to the evaluation of extra-biblical evidence and Pauline silences yields a posterior range of 49–97%. The argument does not claim mathematical proof of historicity but demonstrates that Carrier's conclusion depends critically upon an epistemically unjustified reference class assumption.

## 2. Carrier's Bayesian Argument: Formal Structure

### 2.1 The Bayesian Framework

Carrier's analysis employs the standard form of Bayes' Theorem expressed as posterior odds:

\[
\frac{P(H|E)}{P(\neg H|E)} = \frac{P(H)}{P(\neg H)} \times \frac{P(E|H)}{P(E|\neg H)}
\]

where \(H\) denotes the hypothesis that Jesus was a historical figure (i.e., a real person whose existence is approximately as reported in the Gospels, allowing for mythological embellishment), and \(\neg H\) denotes the mythicist hypothesis (i.e., that Jesus was entirely or substantially invented). The term \(\frac{P(H)}{P(\neg H)}\) represents the prior odds, and \(\frac{P(E|H)}{P(E|\neg H)}\) represents the likelihood ratio for the evidence \(E\).

### 2.2 Carrier's Prior Probability

Carrier's prior probability is derived from a reference class analysis. He asks: within the set of ancient figures most similar to Jesus—figures characterized by divine or semi-divine status, miraculous narratives, cultic worship, and primary attestation in sacred rather than mundane literature—what proportion are historical rather than mythical? His reference class includes Osiris, Hercules, Romulus, Moses, Aesop, and similar figures. Carrier estimates that at most one in three such figures is historical, yielding a prior probability of approximately 33%, or prior odds of 1:2 (Carrier, 2014, pp. 243–246).

### 2.3 Carrier's Evidence Categories and Likelihood Ratios

Carrier evaluates four categories of evidence: extra-biblical sources (X), the Acts of the Apostles (A), the Gospels (G), and the Epistles (E). Within the Epistles, he distinguishes between counter-evidence (\(E^{con}\)) and pro-historicity evidence (\(E^{pro}\)). His full equation, as presented in *On the Historicity of Jesus*, is:

\[
P\left[\frac{1}{2}\right] \times X\left[\frac{576}{1250}\right] \times A\left[\frac{36}{50}\right] \times G\left[\frac{1}{1}\right] \times E^{con}\left[\frac{4}{5} \times \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{3}{4}\right] \times E^{pro}\left[\frac{2}{1} \times \frac{2}{1} \times \frac{2}{1}\right]
\]

\[
= \frac{5,971,968}{12,500,000} \approx 0.32
\]

where each term represents a likelihood ratio for the corresponding evidence category under the historicity hypothesis versus the mythicist hypothesis. The product yields a posterior probability of approximately 32%, which Carrier interprets as supporting the conclusion that Jesus probably did not exist.

## 3. The Reference Class Problem

### 3.1 The Core Assumption

Carrier's entire prior depends upon a single interpretive assumption: that the structural similarities between Jesus and other savior-hero figures—incarnation, death, resurrection, salvation, cultic worship—constitute evidence of *fabrication*. The reasoning is as follows: (1) Humans in various cultures independently invent figures with this pattern; (2) Jesus exhibits this pattern; (3) therefore, Jesus was probably invented. The pattern is treated as noise—as a recurring artifact of human mythopoetic cognition.

This assumption is not neutral. It determines the reference class into which Jesus is placed, and thus the prior probability. If the assumption is false—if the pattern is not noise but signal—then the reference class is contaminated, and the prior is unjustified.

### 3.2 The Projection Principle as Alternative Hypothesis

The *Genesis to Quantum* series has argued for the Projection Principle: that the Logos (the second person of the Trinity, identified with Christ in John 1:1–14 and with the rational structure of creation in Colossians 1:15–17) projects its nature onto the physical substrate. This projection is not metaphorical but structural. Specifically:

- **Rationality** → the mathematical comprehensibility of the universe (Wigner, 1960)
- **Self-consistency** → universal physical laws invariant across space and time
- **Conservation** → Noether's theorem, linking symmetries to conservation laws (Noether, 1918)
- **Triadic structure** → the Born Rule, which requires three irreducible operations (state preparation, unitary evolution, measurement)

If the Projection Principle is true, then the death-resurrection-salvation arc is not a mythological template but the *physics of coherence restoration*—the observable signature of entropy reversal following a symmetry-breaking event (the Fall). It is the shape of reality reasserting itself after a phase transition.

### 3.3 The Sunrise Analogy

Consider the following thought experiment. Suppose an archaeologist discovers that fifty different ancient cultures independently described a bright light appearing in the eastern sky each morning. A Carrier-style argument would reason: "The 'sunrise' pattern appears too frequently across too many mythologized accounts. Clearly this is a fabricated motif." The error is obvious: the sunrise pattern appears everywhere because the sun actually rises.

Analogously, the death-resurrection-salvation pattern appears across cultures because the Logos projects it. Every culture that attended to the structure of reality—through philosophical reflection, religious experience, or mythopoetic imagination—picked up the signal. Some garbled it; some mythologized it; but the signal was always real. The question is not "what percentage of these accounts are fabricated?" but rather "which account, if any, is the source event?"

### 3.4 Implications for the Reference Class

If the pattern is signal rather than noise, then the frequency of its appearance in mythology does not reduce the prior probability of any individual instance being historical. To the contrary, it raises the question that every signal-processing engineer asks: given that the signal is real, which instance is the source? The correct reference class is not "mythologized savior heroes" but "historically attested religious founders with early independent sources." The base rate in this class—figures such as Gautama Buddha, Zoroaster, and Muhammad—is substantially higher. A conservative estimate places it at least 2:1 in favor of historicity.

## 4. Corrected Bayesian Calculations

### 4.1 Correction 1: The Prior

Carrier's prior of 1:2 (33%) is derived from the "mythologized savior heroes" reference class. Under the Projection Principle, the correct reference class is "historically attested religious founders with early independent sources." The base rate in this class is at least 2:1 (67%) in favor of historicity. **Corrected prior odds: 2/1.**

### 4.2 Correction 2: Extra-Biblical Evidence

Carrier scores the extra-biblical evidence at 576/1250, slightly favoring mythicism. This scoring depends on treating the absence of stronger evidence (e.g., more extensive Roman records) as evidence for mythicism. However, for a non-elite figure in an occupied territory whose movement was actively persecuted by record-keeping institutions (the Roman Empire and the Jerusalem Temple establishment), the extant evidence is exactly what one would expect under the historicity hypothesis. The hostile attestations—Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44), who mentions Christ's execution under Pontius Pilate with no motive to affirm Christian claims, and Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.63–64), whose core reference is accepted by most scholars even after removing later interpolations (Meier, 1991)—warrant scoring in favor of historicity. A conservative correction yields **1/1** (no effect either direction), though a more aggressive correction would favor historicity.

### 4.3 Correction 3: Paul's Silences

Carrier's counter-evidence consists largely of Paul's "silences"—things Paul does not mention about Jesus's earthly life. However, if Paul's experience of the risen Christ occurred through Logos-field coupling (a phenomenon for which experimental evidence exists, as discussed in Section 5), then Paul's emphasis on revelation over biography is exactly what the framework predicts. The silences are not suspicious; they are predicted. The corrected counter-evidence score retains only the weakest counter-item, yielding **4/5**.

### 4.4 Corrected Equation (Full Framework)

\[
P\left[\frac{2}{1}\right] \times X\left[\frac{1}{1}\right] \times A\left[\frac{36}{50}\right] \times G\left[\frac{1}{1}\right] \times E^{con}\left[\frac{4}{5}\right] \times E^{pro}\left[\frac{8}{1}\right]

\[
= \frac{36.864}{1} \implies P(\text{historical}) = \frac{36.864}{37.864} \approx 0.974

**Note:** The 97.4% figure requires accepting all three corrections simultaneously at the values chosen. A more defensible range is presented below.

### 4.5 Sensitivity Analysis: Minimal Correction

To isolate the effect of the reference class correction, we compute the posterior probability correcting only the prior while accepting all of Carrier's other likelihood ratios:

\[
P\left[\frac{2}{1}\right] \times \frac{5,971,968}{12,500,000} = \frac{11,943,936}{12,500,000}
\]

\[
P(\text{historical}) = \frac{11,943,936}{24,443,936} \approx 0.489
\]

Correcting only the reference class prior—accepting every other number Carrier argues—yields a posterior probability of approximately 49%, statistically indistinguishable from a coin flip. This is sufficient to defeat Carrier's "probably mythical" conclusion (which requires \(P < 0.5\)) without requiring any of the additional corrections.

## 5. Source Identification: Criteria for the Source Event

If the pattern is real and the question is "which instance is the source event," we require criteria for identification that do not presuppose the conclusion. The following four markers distinguish Jesus from all other figures in Carrier's reference class:

### 5.1 Marker 1: Independent Attestation Within One Generation

Paul's letters date to approximately 50–60 CE, roughly 20 years after the crucifixion (ca. 30 CE). Paul claims to have met individuals who knew Jesus personally, including Peter (Cephas) and James, the brother of Jesus (Galatians 1:18–19). No other figure in Carrier's reference class has anything comparable. Osiris: no independent attestation within a millennium. Hercules: none within centuries. Romulus: none within five hundred years. This temporal proximity is unparalleled and constitutes a strong marker of historicity.

### 5.2 Marker 2: Hostile Attestation

Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44), writing ca. 116 CE, mentions Christ's execution under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius. Tacitus had no motive to affirm Christian claims; he describes Christianity as a "pernicious superstition." Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.63–64), writing ca. 93 CE, references Jesus in a passage whose core is accepted by most critical scholars (Meier, 1991; Dunn, 2003). No other figure in the reference class has hostile attestation from non-Christian sources.

### 5.3 Marker 3: Zero-Power-Base Movement Explosion

Every other figure in Carrier's class was adopted by existing power structures. Osiris was the state religion of Egypt. Romulus was the founding myth of Rome's ruling class. Hercules served as imperial propaganda. Jesus's movement began among fishermen in an occupied territory, was actively persecuted by both Jewish and Roman authorities, and became the dominant religion of the Roman Empire within three centuries. The causal explanation on mythicism requires extraordinary contingencies; on historicity, it requires one real person who made a real impression.

### 5.4 Marker 4: Falsifiable Claims Among Living Witnesses

Paul, writing in the 50s CE to communities that included people alive during Jesus's ministry, makes claims that would have been trivially easy to refute if false. The tomb was in Jerusalem. The crucifixion was public. The claimed witnesses were named and alive: "most of whom are still alive" (1 Corinthians 15:6). A fabricator does not name living witnesses who can contradict him.

1. **The Projection Principle fails.** If the ten property-by-property mappings between Logos properties and physical laws can be shown to be cherry-picked or post-hoc rather than predictive, the reference class correction loses its foundation.
