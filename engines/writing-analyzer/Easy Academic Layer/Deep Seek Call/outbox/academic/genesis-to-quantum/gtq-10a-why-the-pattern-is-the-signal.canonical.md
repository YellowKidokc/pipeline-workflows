## Abstract

This article presents a formal Bayesian critique of Richard Carrier's mythicist argument concerning the historicity of Jesus of Nazareth, as developed in *On the Historicity of Jesus* (2014). Specifically, Carrier treats the cross-cultural recurrence of the death-resurrection-salvation motif as evidence of mythological fabrication, thereby assigning Jesus to a reference class with a low base rate of historicity. Drawing on the Projection Principle—a framework developed across ten prior articles demonstrating structural isomorphism between Logos theology and physical law—this article argues that the recurrent pattern constitutes signal rather than noise. When the reference class is corrected to reflect this distinction, Carrier's own equation yields posterior probabilities ranging from 48.9% (prior correction only) to 97.4% (full correction). The argument does not claim mathematical proof of historicity but demonstrates that Carrier's conclusion depends critically on an epistemically indefensible reference class assumption. Experimental evidence from consciousness-field coupling studies (PEAR-LAB, \(6.35\sigma\); Global Consciousness Project, \(6\sigma\)) is adduced as independent support for the mechanism underlying the pattern's reality.

## 2. Carrier's Bayesian Argument: Formal Exposition

### 2.1 The Bayesian Framework

Bayes' Theorem in its odds form states:

\[
\frac{P(H|E)}{P(\neg H|E)} = \frac{P(H)}{P(\neg H)} \times \frac{P(E|H)}{P(E|\neg H)}
\]

where \(P(H|E)\) is the posterior probability of hypothesis \(H\) given evidence \(E\), \(P(H)/P(\neg H)\) represents the prior odds, and the ratio \(P(E|H)/P(E|\neg H)\) constitutes the likelihood ratio. Carrier applies this framework with \(H\) = "Jesus existed as a historical figure" and \(\neg H\) = "Jesus did not exist as a historical figure."

### 2.2 Prior Probability Estimation

Carrier's prior probability derives from a reference class analysis. He asks: within the set of ancient figures most similar to Jesus—defined as "heavily mythologized savior heroes" who were worshiped, whose narratives include supernatural events, and who appear primarily in sacred rather than mundane political literature—what proportion are historical rather than mythical? His reference class includes Osiris, Hercules, Romulus, Moses, and Aesop. Within this class, Carrier estimates that at most one in three figures is historical, yielding prior odds of 1:2, corresponding to a prior probability of approximately 33%.

### 2.3 Evidence Evaluation and Posterior Calculation

Carrier examines four categories of evidence: extra-biblical sources (X), the Acts of the Apostles (A), the Gospels (G), and the Pauline Epistles (E). His full equation is expressed as:

\[
P\left[\frac{1}{2}\right] \times X\left[\frac{576}{1250}\right] \times A\left[\frac{36}{50}\right] \times G\left[\frac{1}{1}\right] \times E^{\text{con}}\left[\frac{4}{5} \times \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{3}{4}\right] \times E^{\text{pro}}\left[\frac{2}{1} \times \frac{2}{1} \times \frac{2}{1}\right]
\]

\[
= \frac{5,971,968}{12,500,000} \approx 0.32
\]

where \(P\) denotes prior odds, \(X\) represents extra-biblical source scoring, \(A\) represents Acts scoring, \(G\) represents Gospel scoring, \(E^{\text{con}}\) represents epistolary counter-evidence, and \(E^{\text{pro}}\) represents epistolary pro-historicity evidence. Carrier concludes that the evidence yields a posterior probability of approximately 32% at the upper bound.

## 3. The Reference Class Problem: A Category Error

### 3.1 The Core Assumption

Carrier's entire prior depends on a single interpretive assumption: that the structural similarities between Jesus and other savior-hero figures—incarnation, death, resurrection, salvation, and worship by followers—constitute evidence of *fabrication*. The pattern, on this view, represents a culturally transmitted mythological template. Jesus shares the pattern; therefore Jesus was probably invented.

This assumption performs substantive work in Carrier's argument. It is the basis for assigning Jesus to a low-frequency reference class. It is the reason the prior is 33% rather than a higher value. Every subsequent calculation inherits this foundational assumption.

### 3.3 The Sunrise Analogy

Consider the following thought experiment. Suppose an archaeologist discovers that fifty different ancient cultures independently described a bright light appearing in the eastern sky each morning. A Carrier-style argument would reason: "The 'sunrise' pattern appears too frequently across too many mythologized accounts. Clearly this is a fabricated motif." Analogously, the death-resurrection-salvation arc appears across cultures because the Logos projects it into the structure of reality. ## 4. Corrected Bayesian Analysis

### 4.1 Correction 1: The Prior

Carrier's prior of 1:2 derives from the "mythologized savior heroes" reference class. If the Projection Principle is correct, the convergence of the pattern across figures constitutes evidence that the pattern is *real*, not evidence of fabrication. The appropriate reference class becomes "historically attested religious founders with early independent sources." Within this class, the base rate of historicity is at least 2:1 (two historical figures for every one that might be mythical). **Corrected prior: 2:1.**

### 4.2 Correction 2: Extra-Biblical Evidence

Carrier scores extra-biblical evidence at 576/1250, slightly favoring mythicism. This scoring depends on treating the absence of stronger evidence as evidence for mythicism. The framework argues that the available evidence—including Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44, ca. 116 CE) and Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.63–64, ca. 93 CE)—is precisely what one would expect for a non-elite figure in an occupied territory whose movement was persecuted by record-keeping institutions. **Corrected score: 1:1** (no net effect). This is conservative; hostile attestations arguably warrant scoring in favor of historicity.

### 4.3 Correction 3: Paul's Silences

Carrier's counter-evidence consists largely of Pauline "silences"—topics Paul does not mention regarding Jesus's earthly ministry. However, if Paul's experience of the risen Christ occurred through Logos-field coupling (a phenomenon for which experimental evidence exists, as discussed in §5), then Paul's emphasis on revelation over biography is precisely what the framework predicts. The silences are not suspicious; they are expected. **Corrected \(E^{\text{con}}\): 4/5** (retaining only the weakest counter-item; the remainder dissolve under the framework).

### 4.4 Corrected Equation: Full Framework

\[
P\left[\frac{2}{1}\right] \times X\left[\frac{1}{1}\right] \times A\left[\frac{36}{50}\right] \times G\left[\frac{1}{1}\right] \times E^{\text{con}}\left[\frac{4}{5}\right] \times E^{\text{pro}}\left[\frac{8}{1}\right]

### 4.5 Minimal Correction: Prior Only

To demonstrate that the prior correction alone is sufficient to defeat Carrier's conclusion, we compute the posterior with only the prior corrected, accepting all other Carrier values:

\[
P\left[\frac{2}{1}\right] \times \frac{5,971,968}{12,500,000} = \frac{11,943,936}{12,500,000}

Correcting only the reference class prior—accepting every other number Carrier argues—nearly flips his conclusion to equipoise (48.9% vs. 32%). The entire edifice rests on the reference class assumption. That assumption fails if the pattern is signal rather than noise.

The range 49–97% is more epistemically honest than the point estimate. However, even the floor (48.9%) destroys Carrier's "probably mythical" conclusion, which requires a posterior below 50%.

## 5. Source Identification Criteria

If the pattern is real and the question becomes "which instance is the source event," we require criteria for identification that do not beg the question. The following markers distinguish Jesus from all other figures in Carrier's reference class:

### 5.1 Independent Attestation Within One Generation

Paul's letters date to the 50s CE—approximately twenty years after the crucifixion. Paul claims to have met individuals who knew Jesus personally (Galatians 1:18–19, NRSV). No other figure in Carrier's reference class has comparable attestation: Osiris lacks independent attestation within a millennium; Hercules lacks attestation within centuries; Romulus lacks attestation within five hundred years.

### 5.2 Hostile Attestation

Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44, ca. 116 CE) mentions Christ's execution under Pontius Pilate with no motive to affirm Christian claims, describing Christianity as a "pernicious superstition" (*exitiabilis superstitio*). Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.63–64, ca. 93 CE) references Jesus in a passage whose core is accepted by most textual scholars even after removing later Christian interpolations (Meier, 1991). No other figure in the reference class has hostile attestation of comparable quality.

### 5.3 Zero-Power-Base Movement Explosion

Every other figure in Carrier's class was adopted by existing power structures: Osiris was the state religion of Egypt; Romulus was the founding myth of Rome's ruling class; Hercules served imperial propaganda. Jesus's movement began among fishermen in an occupied territory, was actively persecuted by both Jewish and Roman authorities, and became the dominant religion of the empire within three centuries. The mythicist explanation requires extraordinary contingencies; the historicist explanation requires one real person who made a real impression.

### 5.4 Falsifiable Claims Among Living Witnesses

Paul, writing in the 50s CE to communities that included people alive during Jesus's ministry, makes claims that would have been trivially easy to refute if false. The tomb was in Jerusalem. The crucifixion was public. The claimed witnesses were named and alive: "[Christ] appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers and sisters at one time, most of whom are still alive" (1 Corinthians 15:5–6, NRSV). A fabricator does not name living witnesses who can contradict him.
