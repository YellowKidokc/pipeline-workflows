# Genesis to Quantum: A Formal Theophysics Framework

## Abstract

This article series presents a systematic structural isomorphism between the Genesis 2–3 narrative and fundamental quantum mechanical processes, proposing that information constitutes the ontologically primitive substrate from which matter, energy, and spacetime emerge. The framework is formalized through a master coherence equation comprising ten super-factors: Gravity (G), Mass (M), Entropy (E), Space (S), Time (T), Knowledge (K), Redemption (R), Observer (Q), Faith (F), and Coherence (C). Each of the nine constituent articles examines a specific cross-domain mapping, ranging from the Fall as wavefunction collapse to the Cross as retrocausal repair mechanism. The series maintains epistemic transparency through explicit falsification criteria, self-audit protocols, and classification of claims according to demonstrated isomorphism versus proposed identity. This introductory document establishes the methodological foundation, presents the master equation, and provides the structural architecture within which subsequent articles operate.

**Keywords:** quantum theology, wavefunction collapse, retrocausality, information ontology, structural isomorphism, coherence equation, Genesis hermeneutics

## 1. Introduction: The Thesis and Its Epistemic Boundaries

The central thesis of this series may be stated as follows: **Information is ontologically primitive—neither matter, nor energy, nor spacetime constitutes the fundamental substrate of reality.** This proposition, while speculative within contemporary physics, finds structural resonance with quantum information theory (Wheeler, 1990; Zeilinger, 1999) and provides a bridge domain through which theological and physical descriptions of reality may be systematically compared.

The series comprises nine articles, each examining a specific structural correspondence between quantum mechanical formalism and the Genesis 2–3 narrative, interpreted as a quantum measurement protocol. The analysis proceeds through ten super-factors integrated within a single master equation:

\[
\chi = \iiint (G \cdot M \cdot E \cdot S \cdot T \cdot K \cdot R \cdot Q \cdot F \cdot C) \, dx \, dy \, dt
\]

where \(\chi\) represents the coherence integral over spatial dimensions \(x, y\) and temporal dimension \(t\). Each super-factor is defined operationally within both physical and theological domains, with dimensional analysis provided in subsequent articles.

### 1.1 Epistemic Caveats

The authors wish to establish, at the outset, the precise epistemic status of the claims advanced herein. Structural isomorphism between quantum mechanical and theological frameworks does not entail ontological identity. Where a mapping has been demonstrated through formal correspondence, this is explicitly stated. Where identity is proposed, this is marked as speculative and accompanied by the relevant falsification criteria. Where the nature of the correspondence—whether identity, causation, or coincidence—remains undetermined, this uncertainty is preserved rather than collapsed for rhetorical convenience.

The framework incorporates five concrete falsification conditions, designated "kill conditions," which are enumerated within each article. These conditions represent observable phenomena that, if confirmed, would invalidate the proposed isomorphism. The inclusion of such conditions constitutes a methodological commitment to Popperian falsifiability (Popper, 1959) as a standard of scientific rigor.

### 2.1 Variable Definitions

Each super-factor is defined as follows:

- **G (Gravity):** Gravitational coupling strength, operationalized as spacetime curvature in the physical domain and as divine sovereignty in the theological domain.
- **M (Mass):** Inertial and gravitational mass in the physical domain; creaturely substance or embodiment in the theological domain.
- **E (Entropy):** Thermodynamic disorder in the physical domain; moral or spiritual corruption in the theological domain.
- **S (Space):** Spatial extension in the physical domain; created order or "firmament" in the theological domain.
- **T (Time):** Temporal duration in the physical domain; eschatological or eternal dimension in the theological domain.
- **K (Knowledge):** Information content in the physical domain; gnostic or revealed knowledge in the theological domain.
- **R (Redemption):** Not directly defined in standard physics; operationalized as retrocausal information repair in the quantum domain.
- **Q (Observer):** Measurement apparatus or conscious observer in the physical domain; divine or creaturely agency in the theological domain.
- **F (Faith):** Not directly defined in standard physics; operationalized as trust in unobserved quantum states or as theological fidelity.
- **C (Coherence):** Quantum phase coherence in the physical domain; covenantal or narrative coherence in the theological domain.

**Article 05 (GTQ-05): Why Reality Needs Three** decomposes the Born Rule into a tripartite structure isomorphic to the Trinity. The mapping identifies the Father as possibility space, the Son as measurement basis, and the Holy Spirit as actualization operator. **Article 06 (GTQ-06): Why Reality Needs Three** provides a standalone treatment of the tripartite structure introduced in Article 05, examining why two-valued logic underdetermines measurement outcomes and where the third axis enters the formalism.

**Article 08 (GTQ-08): The Eraser and the Cross** maps the delayed-choice quantum eraser experiment onto the atonement theology of the Cross. The article achieves the highest time-eternity score (9.52) and the highest wisdom score (2.41) in the series.

**Article 09 (GTQ-09): The Bible as Error-Correction Code** examines coherence across the biblical narrative, proposing that the canonical structure exhibits error-correcting code properties. The article contains 15 axioms (the most in the series) and 16 cross-domain bridges.

**Article 10 (GTQ-10): The Counter-Move** identifies seven adversarial moves and seven divine counter-moves within the biblical narrative. This article contains only 2 equations, as the argument is structural rather than formal, and achieves the best wisdom-to-knowledge ratio (0.86).
