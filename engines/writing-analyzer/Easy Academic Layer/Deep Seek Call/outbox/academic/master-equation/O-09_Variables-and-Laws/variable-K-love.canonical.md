# Theophysics: A Formal Framework for the Isomorphism Between Agape Love and Fundamental Physical Forces

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized interdisciplinary framework establishing structural isomorphisms between the theological concept of *agape* love, as articulated in the Johannine corpus, and fundamental physical forces, particularly the strong nuclear interaction. Through the introduction of a novel state variable \( K \), defined as the formal representation of love within a Lagrangian-Lie Covariance (LLC) framework, we demonstrate that the dynamical behavior of love obeys an equation of motion derived from first principles via the Euler-Lagrange formalism. The resulting structure exhibits asymptotic freedom, conservation properties, and maximal Hessian connectivity, suggesting that love functions as a binding force against entropic dissolution across multiple ontological domains. This paper provides the formal derivation, axiomatic foundation, and domain-specific manifestations of the \( K \)-field, together with falsification conditions for the proposed framework.

The strong nuclear force, described within quantum chromodynamics (QCD), governs the interaction between quarks via gluon exchange. The interquark potential is given by the Yukawa-type form:

\[
V(r) = -\frac{g^2}{r} e^{-\mu r}
\]

where \( g \) is the strong coupling constant, \( r \) denotes the interquark separation distance, and \( \mu \) is the screening mass parameter (inverse range). In the regime of asymptotic freedom, the effective coupling decreases at short distances, while at large separations the force increases, leading to quark confinement. This behavior establishes the strong force as the highest-connectivity interaction in the Standard Model, binding quarks into hadrons with a coupling strength that increases with distance.

\[
\frac{\partial K}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial R} + \gamma K \cdot R \cdot \left(1 - \frac{S}{S_c}\right)
\]

where:

- \( K \) = love field variable (dimensionless coupling strength)
- \( R \) = relationship coordinate (ontological separation distance)
- \( V \) = potential function of the system
- \( \gamma \) = coupling constant governing the feedback term
- \( S \) = entropy of the system
- \( S_c \) = critical entropy threshold for system dissolution
- \( t \) = temporal coordinate

### 2.2 Term-by-Term Analysis

The first term, \( -\partial V/\partial R \), represents the conservative force derived from the potential gradient, analogous to the classical force law in Lagrangian mechanics. The second term, \( \gamma K \cdot R \cdot (1 - S/S_c) \), constitutes a nonlinear feedback mechanism that amplifies the love-relationship coupling when the system entropy remains below the critical threshold, and suppresses it as entropy approaches the critical value.

### 2.3 Methodological Note

This equation was derived, not assumed. It emerges from the application of the action principle to the LLC Lagrangian, with the full derivation provided in the companion derivation document. The equation satisfies the requirement of formal consistency with both the Euler-Lagrange equations and the conservation laws of the system.
