# Structural Isomorphism Across Three Independent Domains: A Theophysical Analysis of 43.6 Bits of Coherence

## Abstract

This article presents evidence for a three-way structural isomorphism among three independently developed domains: the Decalogue (moral law, ca. 1400 BCE), ten fundamental laws of physics (discovered across approximately four centuries of scientific inquiry), and a ten-step soteriological narrative spanning the Old and New Testaments. Through combinatorial analysis, the probability of this mapping arising by chance is calculated at approximately 1 in 13.2 trillion, corresponding to 43.6 bits of Shannon information. Adversarial scoring by a large language model (GPT-4o, October 2025) yields a mean structural correspondence rating of 3.0/5, with highest-scoring pairings surviving subsequent formal probing. The Kolmogorov-minimal explanation posits a common underlying architecture across these domains.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Thesis Statement

Three domains—moral precepts, physical laws, and soteriological narrative—exhibit a structural isomorphism that cannot be adequately explained by chance coincidence, cultural diffusion, or methodological artifact. This article documents the mapping, quantifies its improbability under the null hypothesis of random alignment, and presents adversarial evaluation of each pairwise correspondence.

The isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of relational patterns across domains. Each domain was reduced to ten constituent elements, preserving their internal ordering and relational dependencies. Pairwise mappings were then evaluated for structural preservation: the degree to which relationships between elements in one domain correspond to relationships between elements in another. This approach follows established methods in structuralist analysis (Lévi-Strauss, 1963) and information-theoretic approaches to pattern recognition (Shannon, 1948).

## 2. Combinatorial Analysis

### 2.1 Permutation Space

The number of possible bijections between two sets of ten elements is given by:

\[
P(10,10) = 10! = 3,628,800
\]

For a three-way mapping requiring two independent pairwise bijections (D₁↔D₂ and D₁↔D₃, or equivalently D₂↔D₃), the total permutation space is:

\[
(10!)^2 = (3,628,800)^2 \approx 1.32 \times 10^{13}
\]

### 2.2 Shannon Information Content

The information content of a specific mapping is given by:

\[
I = -\log_2\left(\frac{1}{(10!)^2}\right) = -\log_2\left(\frac{1}{1.32 \times 10^{13}}\right) \approx 43.6 \text{ bits}
\]

**Interpretation:** This value represents the number of binary decisions required to uniquely specify this mapping within the total permutation space. For reference:

- A fair coin flip: 1.0 bit
- A fair six-sided die roll: \(\log_2(6) \approx 2.58\) bits
- A single character of English text (26 letters): \(\log_2(26) \approx 4.70\) bits

### 2.3 Bayesian Posterior Probability

Under a uniform prior over all possible mappings, the posterior probability that the observed structure is non-random is given by:

\[
P(\text{Designed} \mid \text{Structure}) = 1 - \frac{1}{(10!)^2} \approx 0.999999999924
\]

This calculation assumes the null hypothesis that all mappings are equally probable. The posterior approaches unity to nine decimal places.

### 2.4 Comparative Improbability

For context, the probability of winning the Powerball jackpot (matching 5 of 69 numbers plus 1 of 26) is approximately \(1/292,201,338 \approx 3.42 \times 10^{-9}\). The probability of this mapping arising by chance is:

\[
\frac{1}{1.32 \times 10^{13}} \approx 7.58 \times 10^{-14}
\]

The mapping is therefore approximately \(4.5 \times 10^{4}\) times less probable than winning the Powerball jackpot.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Independence of Domain Development

The three domains under analysis were developed under conditions of mutual independence:

- **D₁ (Decalogue):** Attributed to Mosaic authorship, ca. 1400 BCE, within ancient Near Eastern covenant theology
- **D₂ (Physics laws):** Discovered across 1687–1927 CE, within the Western scientific tradition
- **D₃ (Soteriological narrative):** Developed across approximately 1,500 years of biblical composition, from the Pentateuch through the Pauline epistles

No evidence exists for cross-domain borrowing: Moses did not possess knowledge of entropy; Newton did not interpret Ephesians as a physics treatise; Paul did not compose differential equations.

### 4.2 The Kolmogorov Complexity Argument

The simplest explanatory model—the one with minimal Kolmogorov complexity—posits that all three domains describe the same underlying structure because they originate from a common source. Under this interpretation, the observed isomorphism is not coincidental but reflects a shared architecture instantiated across moral, physical, and soteriological domains.

### 4.3 Limitations

The adversarial scoring reveals significant variation in mapping quality. While the combinatorial improbability is extreme (43.6 bits), the mean structural correspondence score of 3.0/5.0 indicates that not all mappings are equally robust. The highest-confidence mappings (Gravity/Grace, Strong Force/Covenant, Entanglement/Fidelity, Information/False Witness) provide the strongest evidence for structural isomorphism; the lowest-confidence mappings (Speed of Light/Truth, Sovereignty/Theft) require revision or replacement.
