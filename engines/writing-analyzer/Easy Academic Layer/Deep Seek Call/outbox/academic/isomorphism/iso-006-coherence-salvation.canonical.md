## Abstract

This article presents a formally identified structural isomorphism between the Lowe Coherence Equation, a logistic-type ordinary differential equation governing coherence dynamics in physical systems, and the theological framework of salvation and sanctification as articulated in the New Testament epistles. Through systematic variable mapping, three distinct phase regimes are identified—autonomous decay, performance mode, and full surrender—each corresponding to empirically observable spiritual states with independent neuroimaging and clinical support. The analysis demonstrates that the Calvinist-Arminian theological debate, historically framed as mutually exclusive, is resolved within the equation's two-variable product structure O·G, wherein both positions identify necessary but insufficient factors of a single multiplicative term. Empirical validation is provided via Cochrane review data (N = 10,565), positron emission tomography evidence of D2 receptor downregulation, functional magnetic resonance imaging of anterior cingulate cortex hyperactivity, and ventromedial prefrontal cortex activation during contemplative prayer. Four separator tests—structural preservation, non-arbitrariness, constraint, and bidirectionality—are satisfied, warranting classification as a Level 3 structural isomorphism.

## 2. The Lowe Coherence Equation: Formal Definition

### 2.1 Governing Equation

The time evolution of coherence \( C \) is governed by the following ordinary differential equation:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G (1 - C) - S \cdot C
\]

where:

- \( C \in [0,1] \) represents coherence, a dimensionless state variable normalized to the unit interval
- \( O \in [0,1] \) represents openness or observer coupling, a dimensionless parameter quantifying the degree of receptivity
- \( G > 0 \) represents the grace field strength, an external input parameter with dimensions of inverse time \([T^{-1}]\)
- \( S > 0 \) represents entropy or noise, a dissipation parameter with dimensions of inverse time \([T^{-1}]\)
- \( (1 - C) \) represents the capacity term, which introduces logistic saturation as coherence approaches unity

### 2.2 Equilibrium Analysis

The system admits a single stable equilibrium obtained by setting \( dC/dt = 0 \):

\[
C_{eq} = \frac{O \cdot G}{O \cdot G + S}
\]

This equilibrium is globally stable for all positive parameter values, as the logistic structure ensures monotonic approach from any initial condition \( C(0) \in [0,1] \).

### 2.3 Phase Regimes

The coupling parameter \( s \in \{-1, 0, +1\} \) determines three distinct dynamical regimes:

**Regime 1: Autonomous Decay (\( s = -1 \))**

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = -S \cdot C
\]

In this regime, the coupling product \( O \cdot G \) vanishes entirely, leaving only exponential decay. The system evolves according to \( C(t) = C(0)e^{-St} \), asymptotically approaching zero with no lower bound beyond the state variable's domain.

**Regime 2: Performance Mode (\( s \approx 0 \))**

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G (1 - C) - S \cdot C, \quad O \cdot G \ll S
\]

Partial coupling yields a low equilibrium value, approximately \( C_{eq} \approx 1/3 \) under typical parameter ratios. The system exhibits slow convergence with high sensitivity to initial conditions.

**Regime 3: Full Surrender (\( s = +1 \))**

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = G(1 - C) - S \cdot C
\]

Full coupling to the external grace field produces growth toward a high equilibrium, with the capacity term \( (1 - C) \) providing natural saturation as coherence approaches unity.

## 3. Theological Domain Specification

### 3.1 Salvation and Sanctification Dynamics

The New Testament epistles describe a process by which human beings transition from spiritual death toward spiritual coherence with God. This process involves two distinct but inseparable components: (1) an initial act of divine grace that enables the possibility of transformation, and (2) an ongoing human response of openness, obedience, and surrender.

The historical theological tradition has bifurcated into two principal camps:

- **Calvinism** emphasizes the sovereignty of grace (\( G \)), asserting that salvation is entirely God's work and cannot be earned or initiated by human effort.
- **Arminianism** emphasizes free will (\( O \)), asserting that human response and openness are necessary conditions for salvation and that grace can be resisted.

### 3.2 Scriptural Foundation

The primary scriptural reference for the isomorphism is Philippians 2:12–13 (Nestle-Aland 28th edition):

> "Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure."

The structural claim is that the comma separating "work out your salvation" from "for it is God who works in you" encodes the multiplicative coupling between human agency (\( O \)) and divine grace (\( G \)).

## 6. Empirical Validation

### 6.1 Cochrane Review 2020: Alcoholics Anonymous vs. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy

A Cochrane systematic review (Kelly et al., 2020; N = 10,565) comparing Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for addiction recovery found that AA was at least as effective as CBT, with some measures showing superiority for AA.

**Interpretation within the isomorphism:** AA changes the surrender coefficient \( s \) (the coupling parameter), whereas CBT optimizes \( O \) (openness) at a fixed \( s \). The data indicate that changing \( s \) is categorically more powerful than optimizing \( O \) at fixed \( s \), consistent with the equation's prediction that the coupling parameter dominates the equilibrium value.

### 6.2 Neuroimaging Evidence

**D2 receptor downregulation in addiction:** Positron emission tomography studies (Volkow et al., 2001) demonstrate that chronic substance use produces exponential downregulation of D2 dopamine receptors, mapping precisely to the autonomous decay regime (\( s = -1 \)). The dopaminergic system exhibits exponential collapse when decoupled from external coherence sources.

**Anterior cingulate cortex hyperactivity:** Functional magnetic resonance imaging studies (Proudfit et al., 2015) show that anterior cingulate cortex hyperactivity correlates with performance-based religious practice, mapping to the \( s \approx 0 \) regime. This represents the neural signature of "trying harder" without surrender—effortful striving at a fixed low coupling.

**Ventromedial prefrontal cortex activation:** Neuroimaging studies of contemplative prayer (Newberg et al., 2003) demonstrate ventromedial prefrontal cortex activation during deep prayer and meditation, mapping to the \( s = +1 \) regime. This represents the neural signature of surrender.

## 9. Falsification Criteria

This isomorphism carries six active kill conditions. Demonstration of any one of the following would result in demotion or destruction of the isomorphism:

1. **Collapse of \( O \) and \( G \) to one variable:** If openness and grace are shown to be the same variable, the equation reduces and the Calvinist-Arminian resolution fails.
2. **Consistent superiority of CBT over surrender-based approaches:** If recovery data consistently show CBT outperforming AA and similar surrender-based programs, the \( s \)-change model fails.
3. **Reachability of \( C = 0 \) with \( G > 0 \):** If a system with \( S > 0 \) and \( G > 0 \) can reach \( C = 0 \), the grace floor claim fails. The equation mathematically forbids this.
4. **Unobserved predicted behavior:** If the equation predicts a behavior not observed in any spiritual tradition, the mapping suffers empirical falsification.
5. **Failure of Augustine's four states to map:** If Augustine's four states of the will do not map to four parameter regions of the equation, historical theological validation fails.
6. **Neuroimaging contradiction of three-regime prediction:** If brain imaging data contradict the three predicted regimes (decay, performance, surrender), biological falsification occurs.
