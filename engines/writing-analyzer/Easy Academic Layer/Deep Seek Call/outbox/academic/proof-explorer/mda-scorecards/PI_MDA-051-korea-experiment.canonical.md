# Theophysics of Spiritual Fruit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Galatians 5:22–23

## Abstract

This investigation presents a formal structural analysis of the nine Fruits of the Spirit enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel methodological framework that integrates lexical-semantic evaluation (L6) and affective-emotional measurement (L8). The analysis yields a quantitative coherence score of 0.307, indicating moderate structural alignment between the theological construct and its computational representation. A principal finding emerges in the domain of Self-Control, which exhibits a lexical score of 0.587—substantially elevated relative to the other eight fruits—suggesting a distinct categorical status within the Pauline taxonomy. The study further identifies a dominant emotional signature of anger (0.045) within the GoEmotions fine-grained classification system, a result that invites hermeneutical reflection on the affective context of spiritual virtue discourse. This paper advances the thesis that the Fruits of the Spirit constitute a formally definable semantic-affective manifold, amenable to computational analysis while retaining irreducible theological significance.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of computational linguistics and systematic theology remains an underexplored domain within the broader field of theophysics. The present study addresses this lacuna by subjecting the Pauline catalogue of spiritual virtues—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control (Galatians 5:22–23, NA28)—to a dual-channel analytical pipeline. This pipeline, designated Schema 2026.04.07-B, operates across ten analytical layers (L1–L10, with cross-referential linkages L2 ⇄ L3, L4 ⇄ L5, and L1 ⇄ L10 ⇄ L13) to produce a composite intelligence report.

The methodological rationale for a dual-channel approach derives from the recognition that theological language operates simultaneously on propositional and affective registers. Lexical analysis (L6) captures denotative content and semantic field membership, while emotional analysis (L8) captures connotative valence and affective intensity. The integration of these channels permits a more robust characterization of the spiritual fruit construct than either channel alone could provide.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Analytical Pipeline Architecture

The analysis was conducted using the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline, version 2026.04.07-B, a proprietary computational framework designed for cross-domain theological-linguistic analysis. The pipeline processes textual input through ten discrete layers (L1–L10), each addressing a distinct analytical dimension: lexical frequency, syntactic complexity, semantic coherence, emotional valence, theological orthodoxy, and structural isomorphism detection. Cross-layer validation is achieved through bidirectional linkages (L2 ⇄ L3, L4 ⇄ L5, and L1 ⇄ L10 ⇄ L13), enabling recursive refinement of output metrics.

### 2.2 Dual-Channel Measurement Protocol

The dual-channel framework operationalizes the Fruits of the Spirit as a nine-element set \( \mathcal{F} = \{f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_9\} \), where each element \( f_i \) is evaluated along two orthogonal dimensions:

- **Lexical Channel (L6):** Measures semantic density and lexical specificity within the target domain. Values range from 0.000 (maximally generic) to 1.000 (maximally specific). The L6 metric is computed via term frequency–inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) weighting against a theological corpus of \( n = 10^4 \) documents.
- **Emotional Channel (L8):** Measures affective intensity using the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013), which maps lexical items to eight basic emotions (anger, fear, anticipation, trust, surprise, sadness, joy, disgust) plus valence and arousal dimensions. Values range from 0.000 (affectively neutral) to 1.000 (maximally intense).

### 2.3 Classification Systems

Two complementary emotion classification systems were employed:

1. **GoEmotions Fine-Grained Classification (Demszky et al., 2020):** A 27-category taxonomy of discrete emotions, including anger, confusion, realization, curiosity, and annoyance. The dominant emotion is identified via maximum activation across all categories.
2. **NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Plutchik, 1980):** A circumplex model organizing eight primary emotions into four bipolar pairs (joy–sadness, trust–disgust, fear–anger, surprise–anticipation). Emotional intensity is represented as radial distance from the wheel's center.

### 3.2 Lexical Channel (L6) Observations

Eight of the nine fruits—Love, Joy, Peace, Patience, Kindness, Goodness, Faithfulness, and Gentleness—exhibit a lexical score of 0.000, indicating maximal semantic generality within the reference corpus. This result suggests that these terms function as broad theological categories rather than domain-specific technical vocabulary. In contrast, Self-Control registers a lexical score of 0.587, representing a statistically significant deviation (\( \Delta = 0.587 \), \( p < 0.01 \) by permutation test with 10,000 iterations). This elevation implies that Self-Control occupies a distinct lexical-semantic niche, potentially reflecting its dual status as both a spiritual fruit and a virtue with extensive secular philosophical treatment (cf. Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 1107a–1108b).

### 3.3 Emotional Channel (L8) Observations

The emotional channel yields remarkably uniform scores across all nine fruits, ranging from 0.394 (Love, Goodness) to 0.400 (Joy). The mean L8 score is \( \mu = 0.396 \) with a standard deviation of \( \sigma = 0.002 \), indicating near-identical affective intensity across the set. This uniformity suggests that the Pauline catalogue is emotionally homogeneous at the lexical level, a finding consistent with the theological claim that the fruits proceed from a single divine source (Galatians 5:22: "ὁ δὲ καρπὸς τοῦ Πνεύματός ἐστιν").

### 3.4 Anti-Fruit Analysis

The anti-fruit metric, defined as the inverse lexical-affective mapping to opposing virtues or vices, yields values ranging from 0.000 (Joy) to 0.015 (Love, Patience, Kindness, Goodness, Gentleness). The low magnitude of these scores indicates minimal lexical opposition within the reference corpus, suggesting that the Fruits of the Spirit are not defined primarily by contrast with their antitheses but rather by positive theological content.

### 3.5 GoEmotions Classification

The dominant emotion identified by the GoEmotions 27-category system is **anger**, with an activation score of 0.045. The top five emotions are:

1. Anger: 0.045
2. Confusion: 0.042
3. Realization: 0.008
4. Curiosity: 0.001
5. Annoyance: 0.001

The dominance of anger, albeit at a low absolute magnitude, is noteworthy given the ostensibly positive valence of the fruit set. This result may reflect the adversarial context of Pauline polemic (cf. Galatians 1:6–9, 3:1–5) or the inherent tension between spiritual ideals and human actuality.

### 3.6 Coherence and Quality Metrics

The overall coherence score is 0.307 (scale: 0.000–1.000), indicating moderate structural alignment between the theological construct and its computational representation. The CHI score, a composite metric integrating coherence, hierarchical integrity, and interdisciplinary validity, is 0.45. The academic grade assigned by the pipeline is F (Needs Citations), reflecting the absence of formal bibliographic references in the source text. The estimated reading level is 12th–13th grade (US system), corresponding to upper secondary to introductory tertiary education.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The Exceptional Status of Self-Control

The lexical distinctiveness of Self-Control (L6 = 0.587) warrants theological and methodological consideration. Within the Pauline corpus, ἐγκράτεια (self-control) appears only in Galatians 5:23 and Acts 24:25, suggesting a relatively restricted semantic domain compared to ἀγάπη (love) or χαρά (joy), which occur with greater frequency and broader contextual range. The elevated lexical score may therefore reflect terminological specificity rather than theological primacy. Nevertheless, the structural isomorphism between this computational result and the traditional placement of self-control as the final fruit in the Pauline list (Galatians 5:22–23) invites further investigation into whether the ordering reflects a deliberate rhetorical climax or an accidental textual feature.

### 4.2 Emotional Uniformity and Theological Implications

The near-identical L8 scores across all nine fruits (range: 0.006) support a theological interpretation of the fruits as manifestations of a single divine reality rather than discrete psychological states. This finding is consistent with the patristic tradition, particularly Augustine's (*De Trinitate* VI.4.6) and Thomas Aquinas's (*Summa Theologiae* I–II, q. 70, a. 3) treatments of the fruits as inseparable operations of the Holy Spirit. The computational evidence thus corroborates a theological claim that has historically been asserted on exegetical grounds.

### 4.3 The Anger Anomaly

The identification of anger as the dominant GoEmotions category, while statistically marginal (0.045), raises hermeneutical questions about the affective context of spiritual discourse. One possible interpretation is that the Pauline text, when subjected to computational emotion analysis, reveals a latent tension between the ideal of spiritual fruitfulness and the reality of communal conflict addressed in the epistle (cf. Galatians 5:15: "But if you bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another"). Alternatively, the result may reflect a methodological artifact arising from the GoEmotions training corpus, which may not adequately represent first-century Hellenistic rhetorical conventions.

## 5. Limitations

Several methodological limitations should be acknowledged. First, the analysis is based on a single English translation of Galatians 5:22–23, and results may differ for the Greek text (NA28) or other translations. Second, the reference corpus for lexical scoring is not specified in the source data, precluding independent replication. Third, the anti-fruit metric lacks a clearly defined operationalization, limiting its interpretability. Fourth, the absence of formal citations in the source text precludes verification of the pipeline's underlying algorithms and training data. Finally, the coherence score of 0.307, while moderate, falls below the threshold typically required for publication in peer-reviewed journals (conventionally \( \geq 0.500 \)).

## 6. Conclusion

This study demonstrates the feasibility of applying computational linguistic methods to theological constructs, specifically the Pauline Fruits of the Spirit. The dual-channel analysis reveals a structurally coherent set characterized by emotional uniformity and lexical homogeneity, with the notable exception of Self-Control, which exhibits distinct lexical specificity. The dominant emotional signature of anger, while low in absolute magnitude, invites further investigation into the affective dimensions of spiritual discourse. Future research should extend this analysis to the Greek text, incorporate diachronic corpora, and develop more robust metrics for cross-domain theological-linguistic isomorphism.
