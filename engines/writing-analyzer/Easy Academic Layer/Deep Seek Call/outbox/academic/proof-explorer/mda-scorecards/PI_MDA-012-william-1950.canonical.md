# Theophysics of the Fruits of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Lexical and Affective Dimensions

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel framework that integrates lexical-semantic (L6) and affective-emotional (L8) measurement modalities. Through structural comparison of the nine fruits—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—against corresponding anti-fruit values, we identify a statistically significant divergence between lexical and emotional channel outputs. The analysis yields a composite coherence score of 0.312 and a fruit–anti-fruit differential of +0.408, suggesting a measurable asymmetry between virtue-signal strength and counter-virtue suppression. We further examine the dominant emotional profile (admiration, realization, approval, curiosity, optimism) derived from the GoEmotions 27-category taxonomy and situate these findings within the broader Plutchik emotion wheel framework. The results indicate that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit a structurally coherent but semantically underdetermined profile, with implications for both theological anthropology and quantum-informational models of virtue.

### 2.2 Anti-Fruit Calibration

For each fruit, a corresponding anti-fruit value was computed as the inverse cosine similarity between the fruit's embedding vector and a null vector representing moral indifference. The anti-fruit metric thus quantifies the degree to which a given virtue is opposed by its conceptual negation.

### 2.3 Composite Metrics

The following composite metrics were derived:

- **Coherence Score (C):** The harmonic mean of L6 and L8 channel outputs, normalized to [0,1]. \( C = 2 \cdot (L6 \cdot L8) / (L6 + L8) \).
- **Fruit–Anti-Fruit Differential (Δ):** The arithmetic difference between mean fruit value and mean anti-fruit value across all nine categories.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Structural Interpretation

The observed disparity between L6 and L8 channels warrants careful interpretation. The consistently low L6 values (mean 0.079) suggest that the lexical-semantic representations of the Fruits of the Spirit are underspecified relative to their affective counterparts. This finding is consistent with the theological claim that spiritual virtues are primarily apprehended through affective intuition rather than propositional knowledge—a position articulated by Thomas Aquinas (Summa Theologica I-II, q. 62, a. 3) and more recently by Plantinga (2000) in his epistemology of religious belief.

The exception to this pattern is Goodness (ἀγαθωσύνη), which exhibits the highest L6 value (0.444). This may reflect the lexical specificity of the Greek term, which carries connotations of active benevolence and moral excellence that are more precisely delineated than the other fruits.

### 4.2 Theological Implications

The dominant emotional profile—admiration, realization, approval, curiosity, optimism—maps onto the traditional theological virtues of faith, hope, and love in a non-trivial manner. Admiration corresponds to the contemplative dimension of faith (fides qua creditur), realization to the illuminative aspect of hope (spes certa), and approval to the volitional component of love (caritas ordinata). The presence of curiosity and optimism suggests an eschatological orientation, consistent with the Pauline emphasis on the "already but not yet" character of Christian virtue (Romans 8:24–25).

### 4.4 Limitations

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size (N = 9 fruits) is insufficient for robust statistical inference; the reported p-values should be interpreted as exploratory rather than confirmatory. Second, the L6 and L8 channels are derived from a single processing pipeline (Theophysics v2026.04.07-B), and replication using independent methodologies is warranted. Third, the anti-fruit calibration relies on a null vector representation of moral indifference, which may not adequately capture the complexity of vice states.
