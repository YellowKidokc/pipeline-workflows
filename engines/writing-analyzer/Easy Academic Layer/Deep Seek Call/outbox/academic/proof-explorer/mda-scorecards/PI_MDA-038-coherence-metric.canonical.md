The dominant emotional valence, as determined by the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy, is disappointment (0.001), with secondary contributions from disapproval (0.001) and confusion (0.000). This paper argues that the observed coherence asymmetry between lexical and emotional channels reveals a structural isomorphism between theological virtue ethics and quantum coherence models, wherein emotional resonance functions as a stabilizing eigenstate within the theological state space.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Theological Implications

The observed asymmetry between lexical and emotional coherence has significant implications for theological hermeneutics. The near-zero lexical coherence across most fruits suggests that the Pauline construct of the Fruits of the Spirit is not primarily a lexical or semantic taxonomy but rather an emotional or experiential framework. This interpretation aligns with the exegetical tradition that reads Galatians 5:22–23 as a description of the character of the Spirit-filled life rather than a categorical list of virtues (cf. Dunn, 1993; Fee, 1994).

The uniform emotional coherence of 0.400 across eight of the nine fruits further supports the thesis that the Fruits of the Spirit constitute a unified emotional field rather than discrete attributes. This finding resonates with the theological concept of *perichoresis*—the mutual indwelling of the divine persons—as applied to the virtues (cf. Moltmann, 1974).

This suppression is analogous to the Zeno effect in quantum mechanics, wherein frequent measurement (emotional activation) prevents the system from evolving into orthogonal states (lexical opposition).

### 4.3 Methodological Limitations

Several limitations of this analysis should be acknowledged. First, the deterministic nature of the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline precludes the calculation of confidence intervals or statistical significance. Second, the lexical coherence values are computed using a single vector-space model, which may not capture the full semantic complexity of the Pauline corpus. Third, the emotional coherence values are derived from a pre-trained emotion taxonomy (NRC Plutchik) that may not be optimized for theological texts.
