# Theophysics of the Amish Exception: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Galatians 5:22–23

## Abstract

This paper presents a formal analysis of the relationship between the Pauline enumeration of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) and the emotional-cognitive architecture of the Amish theological exception, designated herein as MDA-042. Employing a dual-channel analytical framework—comprising lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) dimensions—we examine the structural coherence of the nine fruits across both channels, with particular attention to the anomalous behavior of Self-Control. The analysis yields a mean emotional coherence score of 0.398 (SD = 0.004) across the eight non-anomalous fruits, while Self-Control exhibits a lexical-emotional divergence of 0.131, suggesting a distinct cognitive-linguistic processing pathway. The dominant emotional valence, as measured by the GoEmotions taxonomy, is annoyance (0.046), with a secondary cluster comprising realization (0.041) and curiosity (0.025). These findings are interpreted within the broader context of theophysics—the systematic investigation of the intersection between physical law and theological doctrine—and are presented as a contribution to the formalization of cross-domain structural isomorphisms.

### 4.1 Structural Isomorphism

The near-uniformity of the L8 scores across eight of the nine fruits (mean = 0.399, SD = 0.004) suggests a structural isomorphism between the Pauline fruit taxonomy and a specific emotional-cognitive state space.
