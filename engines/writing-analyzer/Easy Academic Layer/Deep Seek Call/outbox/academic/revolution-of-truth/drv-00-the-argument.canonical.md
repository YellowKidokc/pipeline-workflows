## 2. Empirical Foundation: Structure Before Culture

Before the formal derivation proceeds, two bodies of empirical evidence establish that mathematical and moral structure are not cultural inventions but pre-loaded features of cognition—features that appear before language, before formal education, and before socialization can plausibly explain them. This evidence closes the most common dismissal of the framework—namely, that mathematics and morality are human constructs—before the formal derivation begins.

### 2.1 Mathematical Structure Precedes Language

The Approximate Number System (ANS) is present and measurable in preverbal infants. Behavioral and neural studies demonstrate that infants as young as 3–5 months discriminate numerosity—distinguishing 8 dots from 16 dots, for example—even when total area, density, and other low-level visual features are controlled (Xu & Spelke, 2000; Lipton & Spelke, 2003). Discrimination follows Weber's law, depending on the ratio between quantities rather than absolute difference. This is not pattern recognition in the general sense; it is numerical structure detection.

Longitudinal evidence indicates that ANS acuity measured at 6 months predicts standardized mathematics scores at 3.5 years (Starr, Libertus, & Brannon, 2013). Twin studies at 5 months demonstrate that numerosity sensitivity is partially heritable (Tosto et al., 2014). Electroencephalography (EEG) work reveals that 3–4-month-old infants display distinct neural signatures when hand-opening actions are numerically congruent versus incongruent with dot arrays—linking number representation to action representation before the infant can crawl, let alone count (Bulf, de Hevia, & Macchi Cassia, 2016).

**Interpretation.** These results indicate that human cognitive systems do not invent number. They encounter and approximate a structure that is already operative. The most parsimonious reading is that mathematical structure is prior to and independent of specific material configurations and developmental epochs—which is precisely what Axioms A1–A7 (Section 3) formalize. The receiver did not create the signal; the signal was already there.

### 2.2 Moral Evaluation Precedes Socialization

The helper-versus-hinderer experiments (Hamlin, Wynn, & Bloom, 2007) demonstrate that 6- and 10-month-old infants preferentially reach for prosocial agents over antisocial ones in simple climbing scenarios. At 3 and 6 months, infants look significantly longer at helpers than hinderers (Hamlin, Wynn, & Bloom, 2010). Follow-up studies demonstrate that infants distinguish helpers from hinderers even when a neutral character is present—they are not merely tracking novelty but evaluating moral valence.

By 4–5 years, children not only prefer helpers but call them "nicer," allocate punishment disproportionately to hinderers, and justify those judgments verbally (Hamlin et al., 2007). With simplified procedures, 3-year-olds show the same patterns (Sloane, Baillargeon, & Premack, 2012).

**Interpretation.** This is not learned behavior in any straightforward sense. It is moral evaluation emerging from cognitive architecture that precedes the cultural and linguistic systems that supposedly "construct" morality. The moral signal is not invented by society; it unfolds from structure—which is exactly what Book III formalizes as the visceral response argument and what Axiom A11 (Section 3) derives from information theory.

### 2.3 Significance for the Proof

These two empirical findings—pre-linguistic numeracy and pre-socialized moral evaluation—are not decorative. They are load-bearing. They establish empirically what the formal proof establishes logically: that mathematical truth and moral normativity are not human inventions projected onto an indifferent universe, but features of reality that human cognition is tuned to receive.

## 3. Formal Derivation: Twenty Axioms from Information Theory

### 3.1 Axioms A1–A7: Mathematical Truth and Necessity

**Axiom A1 (Existence of Mathematical Truth).** There exist propositions that are true independently of any finite agent's knowledge of them. *Justification:* The Pythagorean theorem was true before any human discovered it; its truth does not depend on the existence of human mathematicians.

**Axiom A2 (Necessity of Mathematical Truth).** Mathematical truths are necessarily true—they could not have been otherwise. *Justification:* The proposition \(2 + 2 = 4\) is not contingently true; it is true in all possible worlds.

**Axiom A3 (Universality of Mathematical Truth).** Mathematical truths are invariant across cultures, epochs, and physical contexts. *Justification:* The developmental evidence (Section 2.1) demonstrates convergence across independent cognitive systems before cultural intervention.

**Axiom A4 (Cross-Domain Applicability).** Mathematical structures developed for pure abstraction routinely and precisely describe physical reality (Wigner, 1960). *Justification:* The "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics" in the natural sciences is a well-documented phenomenon requiring explanation.

**Axiom A5 (Gödelian Incompleteness).** Any consistent formal system capable of expressing arithmetic contains true propositions that are not provable within that system (Gödel, 1931). *Justification:* This is a proven theorem with the same certainty as any mathematical result.

**Axiom A6 (Chaitin's Incompleteness).** The Kolmogorov complexity of a string is not computable in general; there exist strings whose complexity cannot be proven to be above a given threshold (Chaitin, 1974). *Justification:* This is a proven theorem.

**Axiom A7 (Shannon's Entropy as Fundamental).** Information content is measurable via Shannon entropy: \(H = -\sum p_i \log_2 p_i\), where \(p_i\) are probabilities of discrete states (Shannon, 1948). *Justification:* This is a foundational result of information theory.

### 3.2 Axioms A8–A14: Information, Thermodynamics, and Morality

**Axiom A8 (Landauer's Principle).** Information has irreducible physical cost: erasing one bit of information in a computational system dissipates at least \(k_B T \ln 2\) energy (Landauer, 1961). *Justification:* This is a proven result of thermodynamics.

**Axiom A9 (Second Law of Thermodynamics).** In any closed system, entropy never decreases: \(dS/dt \geq 0\). *Justification:* This is a fundamental law of physics with extensive empirical confirmation.

**Axiom A10 (Kolmogorov Complexity and Randomness).** A string is random if its Kolmogorov complexity \(K(x)\) is approximately equal to its length; randomness is mathematically undecidable in general (Kolmogorov, 1965). *Justification:* This is a proven theorem.

**Axiom A11 (Non-Deception of Mathematical Truth).** Mathematical truth is non-deceptive—it does not systematically mislead rational agents who correctly apply its methods. *Justification:* If mathematics were systematically deceptive, the entire scientific enterprise would be undermined. This is a moral property of mathematical truth.

**Axiom A12 (Moral Normativity as Objective).** Moral judgments admit of truth values independent of individual or cultural preference. *Justification:* The developmental evidence (Section 2.2) demonstrates pre-socialized moral evaluation; cross-cultural moral universals (Haidt, 2012) converge on core moral intuitions.

**Axiom A13 (Is-Ought Bridge).** There exists a non-vacuous derivation from descriptive facts to normative obligations. *Justification:* Axiom A11 provides the bridge: if mathematical truth is non-deceptive, then agents ought to align their reasoning with it.

**Axiom A14 (Coherence as Precondition).** Coherence is a necessary condition for any entity to exist, persist, or possess meaning. *Justification:* An incoherent system cannot sustain stable identity over time.

### 3.3 Axioms A15–A20: The Soteriological Limit

**Axiom A15 (Finite Systems Are Closed).** Any system whose grounding is entirely within finite, spatiotemporal agents is a closed finite system. *Justification:* This follows from the definition of "finite system."

**Axiom A16 (Gödelian Boundary on Finite Systems).** A closed finite system cannot prove its own consistency. *Justification:* This follows from Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem applied to the system's formal representation.

**Axiom A17 (Chaitin Boundary on Finite Systems).** A closed finite system cannot decide the Kolmogorov complexity of all its internal states. *Justification:* This follows from Chaitin's Incompleteness Theorem.

In philosophy of mathematics, there is no consensus that numbers or mathematical structures are ontologically prior to the physical universe. Prominent anti-Platonist positions—nominalism (no abstract objects exist), fictionalism (mathematics is useful fiction), psychologism (numbers are mental constructs), and physicalism (mathematics describes physical configurations)—explicitly deny the existence of timeless abstract structures and instead treat mathematics as a human-devised language or a description of concrete systems (Field, 1980; Balaguer, 1998; Maddy, 1990).

The conclusion is precise: "mathematics is man-made" is only internally consistent if one is willing to surrender three features that mathematics actually exhibits—and that the entire scientific apparatus depends on:

1. **Necessity.** Mathematical truths do not depend on who discovers them or when. The Pythagorean theorem was not "less true" before Pythagoras. If mathematics is grounded in finite agents, its truths are contingent on those agents—but we treat them as necessary.
2. **Universality.** Mathematics works the same in every culture, every epoch, every physical context. If it were genuinely a product of specific brains or cultures, there is no non-miraculous reason it should converge across independent civilizations—yet it does, and the developmental evidence (Section 2.1) shows it converges even before culture can intervene.
3. **Cross-domain applicability.** Mathematics developed for pure abstraction routinely and precisely describes physical reality—Wigner's (1960) "unreasonable effectiveness." If mathematics is merely a human language, this is an extraordinary coincidence with no explanation. If mathematics describes a prior structure that both minds and physical systems participate in, applicability is inevitable.

### 4.4 Anti-Platonist Options and Their Implicit Dependencies

**Nominalism** relies on robust, stable inferential practices and logical norms that themselves behave like the invariants requiring an external ground (Field, 1980). The norms of inference are not themselves nominalizable without circularity.

**Fictionalism** treats mathematics as a "story," but the unreasonable success of that story in predicting reality reintroduces the external constraint as "applicability" (Balaguer, 1998). A fiction that systematically predicts empirical outcomes is not merely a fiction.

**Psychologism** grounds mathematics in brains, but then cannot explain why different brains in different centuries converge on the same truths (Mill, 1843). The convergence requires explanation that psychologism cannot provide.

**Physicalism** grounds mathematics in matter, but then cannot explain why mathematics describes possible physical systems that do not and may never exist (Maddy, 1990). The modal scope of mathematics exceeds the actual physical universe.

## 10. Conclusion: Prediction and Confirmation, Not Retrofit

He derived the law from first principles and then the predictions followed. If the predictions had failed, the law would have been wrong.

Books I–III derive requirements from information theory, thermodynamics, Gödel, Chaitin, Shannon, and Kolmogorov. At no point does any theological premise appear. The axioms are not designed to point at Christianity; they are designed to characterize mathematical truth. Anyone who disagrees with the axioms can attack them on their own terms—as information theory, not theology.

The empirical evidence—pre-linguistic numeracy at 3 months, pre-socialized moral evaluation at 6 months—confirms that these are features of reality encountered by cognition, not invented by it. The developmental data was not gathered to support this argument; it was gathered to understand cognitive development, and it supports this argument as a side effect.

The "mathematics is man-made" escape hatch is formally closed: any position that grounds mathematical truth entirely in finite agents must surrender necessity, universality, and cross-domain applicability—features our scientific practice depends on and cannot abandon.

Book IV checks which worldview satisfies all requirements. Book V shows the physical and spiritual share identical mathematical structure—with one degree of freedom added: the freedom to choose. Book VI shows why institutional corruption is thermodynamically inevitable and why incarnation is the predicted solution.

The proof generates the lock before you go looking for the key. That eliminates curve-fitting. That is prediction and confirmation, not retrofit.
