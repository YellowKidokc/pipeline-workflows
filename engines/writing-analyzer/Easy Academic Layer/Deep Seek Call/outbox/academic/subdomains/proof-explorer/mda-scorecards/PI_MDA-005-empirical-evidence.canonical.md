This paper presents a computational linguistic investigation into the Pauline concept of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) as operationalized through dual-channel measurement across lexical (L6) and emotional (L8) dimensions. Employing a multi-layer analytical pipeline incorporating the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) and the NRC Plutchik emotion wheel framework (Mohammad & Turney, 2013), the study examines nine virtue constructs—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—for their respective lexical and affective signatures. Notably, the construct of self-control demonstrates a unique lexical activation (L6 = 0.138) absent in the other eight virtues, suggesting a distinct cognitive-linguistic profile. ## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The Self-Control Anomaly

The unique lexical activation of self-control (L6 = 0.138) warrants particular attention. Within the Pauline theological framework, self-control (*ἐγκράτεια*, *enkrateia*) occupies a distinctive position as the final enumerated fruit, often interpreted as the virtue that enables the cultivation of the preceding eight (cf. Barclay, 2002). The present computational findings provide empirical support for this theological interpretation: self-control exhibits a lexical profile that is qualitatively distinct from the other virtues, suggesting that it functions as a meta-virtue or enabling condition rather than a parallel construct.

### 4.2 Emotional Uniformity and Theological Implications

The near-uniform L8 values across constructs (mean = 0.405, SD = 0.003) raise important questions regarding the emotional specificity of the Fruits of the Spirit. From a theological perspective, this uniformity may reflect the Pauline conception of the fruits as manifestations of a single divine source (the Holy Spirit) rather than discrete psychological states. Alternatively, the uniformity may be an artifact of the measurement instrument, which may lack the granularity to distinguish between closely related positive emotions.

### 4.3 Methodological Limitations

Several limitations of the present analysis should be acknowledged. First, the zero lexical activation for eight constructs may reflect corpus limitations rather than genuine absence of lexical representation. Second, the GoEmotions taxonomy, while validated for general emotional classification, has not been specifically validated for theological or religious textual domains. Third, the absence of confidence intervals for the reported values precludes formal statistical inference regarding the significance of observed differences.

## 5. Conclusion

This study provides preliminary empirical evidence for the dual-channel structure of the Fruits of the Spirit as measurable through computational linguistic analysis. The identification of self-control as lexically distinct from the other eight virtues supports its theological interpretation as a meta-virtue. The uniformity of emotional activation across constructs suggests a shared affective foundation consistent with the Pauline doctrine of the Holy Spirit as the unitary source of all virtues. Future research should expand the corpus size, incorporate confidence interval estimation, and validate the measurement framework against theological expert annotations.
