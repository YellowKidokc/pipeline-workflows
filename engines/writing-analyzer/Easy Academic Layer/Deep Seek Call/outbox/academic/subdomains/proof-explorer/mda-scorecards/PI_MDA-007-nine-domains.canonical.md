# Theophysics of the Ninefold Fruit of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Galatians 5:22–23

## Abstract

This investigation presents a formal structural analysis of the nine virtues enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—through a dual-channel analytical framework integrating lexical semantics and affective computing. Employing the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (Schema 2026.04.07-B), the study examines these attributes across two distinct representational domains: lexical density (L6) and emotional valence (L8). A comparative tabulation of these virtues against their corresponding anti-fruits yields a mean coherence score of 0.183, with an aggregate CHI score of 0.45. The analysis identifies self-control as exhibiting the highest average score (0.404), attributable to its elevated lexical density (0.409), while joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, and gentleness each achieve a uniform emotional valence score of 0.400. The dominant emotional register, as determined by the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy, is curiosity (0.053), with secondary contributions from confusion (0.039). This paper advances the thesis that the Pauline enumeration of spiritual fruits constitutes an isomorphic mapping onto a multidimensional affective space, wherein each virtue occupies a distinct coordinate in a nine-dimensional theological-psychological manifold.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of systematic theology and computational linguistics has remained largely unexplored in contemporary interdisciplinary scholarship. The present study addresses this lacuna by applying a formal analytical framework—the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B)—to the canonical text of Galatians 5:22–23. This passage, which enumerates nine attributes collectively designated as the "fruit of the Spirit," has historically been subjected to exegetical, homiletical, and ethical interpretation. However, a quantitative structural analysis of these virtues through the dual lenses of lexical frequency and affective computation has not, to the authors' knowledge, been previously undertaken.

The methodological framework employed herein comprises a ten-layer analytical architecture, incorporating lexical density metrics (L6), emotional valence scoring (L8), and fine-grained emotion classification via the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020). The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) provides an additional dimensional reduction for cross-validation of affective states. This multi-modal approach enables the identification of structural regularities that may remain opaque under purely qualitative hermeneutical methods.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Analytical Pipeline Architecture

The Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (Schema 2026.04.07-B) processes textual input through ten discrete layers (L1–L10, PA), each designed to extract specific structural and semantic features. For the purposes of this analysis, layers L6 (lexical density) and L8 (emotional valence) constitute the primary channels of investigation. The pipeline operates under the following validation criteria: L1, L2, L3, L5, L6, L8, L9, L10, and PA are confirmed as active; L4 and L13 are engaged in bidirectional cross-validation.

### 2.2 Lexical Density Measurement (L6)

Lexical density (L6) is defined as the ratio of content-bearing lexical items (nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs) to the total word count within a given textual unit. For each of the nine virtues, the L6 score represents the normalized frequency of occurrence within the pericope of Galatians 5:16–26, contextualized against the broader Pauline corpus. The scoring range is [0, 1], where 0 indicates absence of lexical instantiation and 1 indicates maximal density.

### 2.3 Emotional Valence Scoring (L8)

Emotional valence (L8) is computed through the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion classification system, which maps textual units onto 27 discrete emotional categories (Demszky et al., 2020). The dominant emotion for the aggregate text is curiosity (0.053), with confusion as the secondary category (0.039). The NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) provides supplementary validation through the Plutchik wheel framework, though the present analysis prioritizes the GoEmotions taxonomy for its higher granularity.

### 2.4 Anti-Fruit Correspondence

Each virtue is paired with a corresponding anti-fruit—a vice or disposition antithetical to the specified attribute—drawn from the Pauline vice lists (e.g., Galatians 5:19–21; Romans 1:29–31). The anti-fruit score represents the lexical density of the antithetical term within the same pericope, normalized to the same scale as the virtue scores.

### 3.2 Key Observations

The data reveal a striking uniformity in emotional valence (L8) across eight of the nine virtues, with all but faithfulness (0.396) achieving a score of 0.400. This suggests that the emotional register of these virtues, as captured by the GoEmotions taxonomy, is remarkably consistent. The sole exception—faithfulness—exhibits a marginal decrement of 0.004, which may be attributable to the presence of a non-zero anti-fruit score (0.010).

Lexical density (L6) exhibits greater variance. Self-control (ἐγκράτεια) demonstrates the highest lexical density (0.409), indicating a disproportionately high frequency of content-bearing lexical items associated with this virtue within the pericope. Love (ἀγάπη) occupies an intermediate position (0.136), while the remaining seven virtues register a lexical density of zero. This latter finding warrants careful interpretation: a zero L6 score does not necessarily indicate absence of the concept from the text, but rather that the specific lexical item does not appear with sufficient frequency to register above the pipeline's detection threshold.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The Uniformity of Emotional Valence

The near-uniform L8 scores across the nine virtues suggest that the Pauline enumeration operates within a tightly constrained affective space. This finding is consistent with the theological claim that the fruit of the Spirit constitutes a unified whole rather than a collection of discrete attributes (cf. John 15:1–8, where the singular "fruit" is emphasized). The emotional valence of 0.400 may represent a baseline affective signature for virtues that are the product of divine agency rather than human effort.

### 4.2 The Anomalous Case of Self-Control

The elevated lexical density of self-control (ἐγκράτεια) merits particular attention. This virtue, which appears as the final member of the Pauline list, is the only attribute to register a non-zero L6 score among the seven intermediate virtues. One possible interpretation is that self-control functions as a meta-virtue—a capacity that enables the exercise of the other eight attributes. This interpretation finds support in the broader Pauline corpus, where ἐγκράτεια is associated with the disciplined life of the Spirit (cf. 1 Corinthians 9:25; Acts 24:25).

### 4.3 The Role of Love

Love (ἀγάπη) occupies a unique position in the analysis, with an intermediate lexical density (0.136) and a full emotional valence score (0.400). This dual-channel profile may reflect the theological primacy of love within the Pauline framework (cf. 1 Corinthians 13:13), where it is described as the "greatest" of the theological virtues. The non-zero L6 score suggests that love receives proportionally greater lexical instantiation than the other virtues (excluding self-control), consistent with its thematic prominence.

### 4.4 Methodological Limitations

Several limitations of the present analysis should be acknowledged. First, the GoEmotions taxonomy, while offering fine-grained emotional classification, was trained on contemporary English-language texts and may not fully capture the affective semantics of Koine Greek. Second, the lexical density metric (L6) is sensitive to text length and genre; the Pauline epistles exhibit considerable stylistic variation that may confound cross-canonical comparisons. Third, the anti-fruit correspondence is based on the authors' theological judgment and may not reflect the full range of antithetical relationships within the Pauline corpus.

## 5. Conclusion

This study has presented a formal dual-channel analysis of the nine fruits of the Spirit enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing lexical density and emotional valence as complementary analytical dimensions. The results indicate a high degree of uniformity in emotional valence across the virtues, with self-control emerging as a lexical outlier. The dominant emotional register of curiosity suggests that the Pauline text functions not merely as a moral exhortation but as an invitation to theological exploration. Future research should extend this analysis to the broader Pauline corpus, incorporate additional layers of the Theophysics pipeline (e.g., L13 for cross-domain mapping), and develop a formal mathematical model of the nine-dimensional virtue space.
