# Theophysics of Moral Ontology: A Dual-Channel Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit Through Lexical-Semantic and Affective Computing Frameworks

## Abstract

This investigation presents a formalized theophysical analysis of the Pauline construct known as the "Fruits of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22–23) through a dual-channel computational methodology integrating lexical-semantic density analysis (L6) and affective-emotional valence mapping (L8). The study employs a structural isomorphism between theological virtue ethics and information-theoretic models of moral cognition, operationalized through the MDA-044 Amish Control Study protocol. Results indicate differential coherence across the nine fruit categories, with Joy, Patience, Kindness, Faithfulness, and Gentleness achieving maximal affective alignment (L8 = 0.400–0.407), while Love, Peace, Goodness, and Self-Control exhibit lower lexical density scores (L6 = 0.095–0.190). The Anti-Fruit metric registers negligible values across all categories (mean = 0.001), suggesting minimal adversarial semantic contamination within the dataset. These findings are interpreted within a theophysical framework wherein moral virtues are conceptualized as eigenstates of a theological Hilbert space, with the Fruits of the Spirit representing basis vectors of maximal coherence.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of quantum information theory and systematic theology has yielded a nascent disciplinary framework—theophysics—within which theological propositions may be subjected to formal mathematical analysis. The present study extends this paradigm by applying a dual-channel analytical protocol to the Pauline enumeration of moral virtues commonly designated the "Fruits of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22–23, NA28). This passage, which enumerates nine discrete virtues—love (*agapē*), joy (*chara*), peace (*eirēnē*), patience (*makrothymia*), kindness (*chrēstotēs*), goodness (*agathōsynē*), faithfulness (*pistis*), gentleness (*praotēs*), and self-control (*enkrateia*)—constitutes a foundational text for Christian moral ontology.

The methodological innovation of this study resides in its operationalization of theological content through two distinct computational channels: (1) lexical-semantic density (L6), which quantifies the informational entropy of virtue terms within their scriptural context, and (2) affective-emotional valence (L8), which maps virtue terms onto the Plutchik emotion wheel and the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020). This dual-channel approach enables a formal comparison between the semantic structure of theological language and the affective architecture of human moral cognition.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Data Acquisition and Preprocessing

The corpus under analysis was derived from the MDA-044 Amish Control Study protocol (Schema 2026.04.07-B), which comprises a controlled linguistic dataset of Amish theological discourse. The dataset was subjected to a 10-layer analytical pipeline (Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B) incorporating lexical, syntactic, semantic, and affective processing modules. All analyses were conducted on 2026-05-30T05:40:54 UTC.

### 2.2 Dual-Channel Analytical Framework

#### 2.2.1 Channel L6: Lexical-Semantic Density

Lexical density (L6) was computed as the ratio of content-bearing lexical items (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) to total word tokens within each virtue category, normalized to the interval [0,1]. This metric operationalizes the informational richness of theological language, with higher values indicating greater semantic specificity.

#### 2.2.2 Channel L8: Affective-Emotional Valence

Affective valence (L8) was determined through a two-stage process. First, each virtue term was embedded within the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013), which maps lexical items onto eight primary emotions (joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, anticipation, anger, disgust) and their intensity gradients. Second, fine-grained emotional classification was performed using the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020), which identifies 27 discrete emotion categories. The L8 score represents the maximum normalized activation across all emotion categories for each virtue term.

#### 2.2.3 Anti-Fruit Metric

The Anti-Fruit metric quantifies the degree of semantic opposition or adversarial contamination within each virtue category. This was computed as the cosine distance between the virtue term's embedding vector and the centroid of its antonymic set, normalized to [0,1].

### 2.3 Statistical Framework

All metrics are reported as point estimates without confidence intervals, consistent with the exploratory nature of this analysis. The CHI Score (0.43) and Coherence metric (0.284) provide global measures of dataset integrity and internal consistency, respectively. The Academic Grade (F) and CKG Tier (D—Developing) reflect the preliminary status of this analytical framework.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Interpretation of Dual-Channel Results

The results reveal a striking bifurcation within the Fruits of the Spirit: five virtues (Joy, Patience, Kindness, Faithfulness, Gentleness) achieve maximal affective valence (L8 = 0.400–0.402) while registering zero lexical density (L6 = 0.000). This pattern suggests that these virtues function primarily as affective attractors within the theological semantic space, possessing high emotional resonance but low propositional specificity. Conversely, Love, Peace, Goodness, and Self-Control exhibit non-zero lexical density (L6 = 0.095–0.190), indicating greater semantic differentiation at the cost of reduced affective coherence.

This inverse relationship between lexical density and affective valence may reflect a fundamental property of moral theological language: virtues that are more lexically differentiated (i.e., possessing richer semantic content) may be more susceptible to interpretive variance, thereby reducing their affective uniformity across the dataset. Conversely, virtues with zero lexical density may function as "pure" affective primitives, resistant to semantic decomposition.

### 4.2 Theophysical Implications

Within the theophysical framework, the Fruits of the Spirit may be interpreted as eigenstates of a theological Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}_T\), where each virtue corresponds to a basis vector \(|v_i\rangle\) with associated eigenvalue \(\lambda_i\) representing its moral weight. The dual-channel analysis suggests that the affective valence (L8) corresponds to the magnitude of the eigenvalue, while lexical density (L6) corresponds to the informational entropy of the eigenstate. The near-zero Anti-Fruit metric across all categories (mean = 0.001) indicates that the virtue basis is approximately orthogonal to its adversarial counterpart, supporting the theological claim that the Fruits of the Spirit constitute a coherent moral ontology.

### 4.3 Methodological Limitations

Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the small sample size (n = 9 virtue categories) precludes robust statistical inference. Second, the absence of confidence intervals for all reported metrics limits the generalizability of findings. Third, the Academic Grade (F) and CKG Tier (D—Developing) indicate that the analytical framework requires further validation. Fourth, the dominant GoEmotions classification of "realization" and "confusion" suggests potential semantic ambiguity in the dataset that may confound interpretation.

## 5. Conclusion

This study presents a preliminary theophysical analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit through a dual-channel lexical-affective framework. The results indicate differential coherence across virtue categories, with five virtues exhibiting maximal affective alignment and four virtues demonstrating greater lexical differentiation. The near-zero Anti-Fruit metric supports the theological coherence of the Pauline virtue construct. Future research should expand the dataset, incorporate confidence intervals, and develop formal models of the theological Hilbert space within which these virtues operate.

## Appendix A: Variable Definitions

- **L6**: Lexical density score, defined as the ratio of content-bearing lexical items to total word tokens, normalized to [0,1]
- **L8**: Affective valence score, defined as the maximum normalized activation across all emotion categories in the NRC Plutchik and GoEmotions taxonomies, normalized to [0,1]
- **Anti-Fruit**: Cosine distance between virtue term embedding and antonymic centroid, normalized to [0,1]
- **CHI Score**: Cross-channel coherence index, computed as the Pearson correlation between L6 and L8 across all virtue categories
- **Coherence**: Internal consistency metric, computed as Cronbach's alpha across all analytical channels
