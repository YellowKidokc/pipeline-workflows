# ISOMORPHISM RECORD ISO-006: INFORMATION PRIMACY

## Abstract

This record documents a structural isomorphism between the information-theoretic interpretation of physical reality, as articulated in John Archibald Wheeler's "It from Bit" thesis and related quantum information principles, and the Johannine Prologue's doctrine of the Logos (λόγος) as the ontologically prior, generative substrate of all material reality. The mapping identifies four shared structural features: (1) ontological priority of information over matter, (2) generative function of the informational substrate, (3) irreducibility of the foundational principle, and (4) physical consequence of informational acts. The isomorphism is classified as structural rather than semantic, with significant strain at the personal-impersonal boundary. Confidence is assessed as medium, given the categorical distinction between Shannon information as an impersonal mathematical quantity and the Logos as a personal, conscious, divine agent. A speculative resolution within the Theophysics framework is proposed, drawing on integrated information theory (Tononi, 2004).

## 1. Domain Specification

### 1.1 Domain A: Physics and Information Theory

The proposition under examination is that information constitutes the ontologically primitive substrate from which physical reality derives. This thesis, most prominently advanced by Wheeler (1990) under the rubric "It from Bit," asserts that every physical quantity—every particle, every field, every spacetime event—derives its meaning and existence from binary yes/no distinctions, i.e., bits of information. Matter and energy are thus understood as derivative phenomena, with distinguishability (information) occupying the fundamental ontological stratum.

Three principal theoretical pillars support this position:

**Landauer's Principle (Landauer, 1961):** The erasure of one bit of information in a computational process dissipates at minimum \( kT \ln(2) \) joules of energy, where \( k \) is Boltzmann's constant (\( 1.380649 \times 10^{-23} \, \text{J} \cdot \text{K}^{-1} \)) and \( T \) is the absolute temperature in Kelvin. This principle establishes that information possesses irreducible thermodynamic weight; it is not an epiphenomenal abstraction but a quantity with measurable physical consequences. Experimental confirmation has been achieved (Bérut et al., 2012), demonstrating the Landauer bound to within approximately 1% precision.

**Zeilinger's Foundational Principle (Zeilinger, 1999):** Each elementary quantum system (qubit) carries exactly one bit of information. The information content is not a property of the system; rather, the system *is* its information content. This principle provides a foundational axiom from which the structure of quantum mechanics can be derived, including the superposition principle and the probabilistic nature of measurement outcomes.

**Quantum Information-Theoretic Ontology:** Within the Everettian or information-theoretic interpretation of quantum mechanics, the quantum state \( |\psi\rangle \) constitutes the most complete possible description of a physical system. There exists no sub-informational substrate—no "hidden variables" in the sense of classical determinism (Bell, 1964; contra Bohm, 1952). The wavefunction *is* the ontology; information is not about reality but constitutes reality itself.

Formally, this relationship may be expressed as:

\[
\mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I})
\]

where \( \mathcal{R} \) denotes physical reality (the domain of material objects, fields, and spacetime events), \( \mathcal{I} \) denotes the informational substrate (the domain of binary distinctions, quantum states, and probability distributions), and \( f \) is a generative mapping that preserves informational content. The substrate \( \mathcal{I} \) is irreducible: no further decomposition into material constituents is possible, as material constituents are themselves products of the mapping.

### 1.2 Domain B: Christian Theology—Johannine Prologue and Creation Theology

The Johannine Prologue (John 1:1-5, 14) presents a cosmological claim of foundational significance for Christian theology:

> Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος. οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν θεόν. πάντα δι᾿ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο, καὶ χωρὶς αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο οὐδὲ ἕν ὃ γέγονεν. (John 1:1-3, NA28)

> "In the beginning was the Word (Logos), and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." (ESV)

This passage establishes the Logos as ontologically prior to and generative of all material reality. The claim is total in scope: "all things" (πάντα) and "not any thing" (οὐδὲ ἕν) together constitute a universal quantifier admitting no exceptions.

The creation narrative of Genesis 1 reinforces this informational-causal structure through the repeated formula: "And God said, 'Let there be...' and there was..." (Genesis 1:3, 6, 9, 11, 14, 20, 24, 26). The divine speech act—an information-transmission event—precedes and causes the physical result. This pattern is explicitly thematized in Hebrews 11:3:

> Πίστει νοοῦμεν κατηρτίσθαι τοὺς αἰῶνας ῥήματι θεοῦ, εἰς τὸ μὴ ἐκ φαινομένων τὸ βλεπόμενον γεγονέναι.

> "By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible." (ESV)

The visible (material) realm derives from an invisible (informational/verbal) cause. The formal parallel to the information-theoretic structure is:

\[
\mathcal{M} = g(\mathcal{L})
\]

where \( \mathcal{M} \) denotes material reality, \( \mathcal{L} \) denotes the Logos as ontologically prior substrate, and \( g \) is the generative act of creation. The Logos is irreducible: no prior ontological category exists from which the Logos derives, consistent with the Nicene formulation that the Son is "begotten, not made" (γεννηθέντα οὐ ποιηθέντα).

## 2. Structural Mapping

### 2.1 Shared Structural Features

Through structural comparison of the two domains, four isomorphic features have been identified:

**Feature 1: Ontological Priority of Information over Matter**

In both domains, the informational substrate is temporally and ontologically prior to material reality. The ordering is not: matter exists, then information describes it. Rather: information exists, then matter instantiates from it. In Wheeler's framework, the bit generates the "it." In the Johannine framework, the Logos generates all things. This ordering is non-arbitrary and cannot be reversed without collapsing the respective frameworks.

**Feature 2: Generative Function**

Information in the Wheelerian sense does not merely describe reality; it generates reality. The bit is productive, not merely descriptive. Similarly, the divine speech act in Genesis and the Logos in John are productive: they bring into existence that which they signify. Both domains posit a causal arrow from information to material instantiation.

**Feature 3: Irreducibility**

In both domains, the informational substrate cannot be further decomposed. Within Wheeler's framework, one cannot meaningfully ask "what is the bit made of?"—the bit is the terminus of ontological analysis. Within the Johannine framework, one cannot ask "what preceded the Logos?"—the Logos is eternal ("In the beginning was the Logos," not "In the beginning, the Logos came into being"). Both substrates function as bedrock ontological categories.

**Feature 4: Physical Cost of Information**

Landauer's principle demonstrates that information manipulation carries irreducible thermodynamic cost. The biblical speech acts carry analogous physical consequences: "Let there be light" results in the existence of light (Genesis 1:3). Information in both domains is not "cheap talk" but has real, measurable physical effects.

### 2.2 Formal Expression of the Isomorphism

The shared structure may be expressed as a mapping between two formal systems:

| Domain A (Physics) | Domain B (Theology) | Structural Role |
|--------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Informational substrate \( \mathcal{I} \) | Logos \( \mathcal{L} \) | Ontologically prior |
| Physical reality \( \mathcal{R} \) | Material reality \( \mathcal{M} \) | Derivative |
| Generative mapping \( f \) | Generative act \( g \) | Production function |
| Irreducibility of bit | Eternality of Logos | Foundational status |
| Landauer bound | Speech-act efficacy | Physical consequence |

### 2.3 What Is Not Claimed

To maintain intellectual rigor, the following limitations are explicitly acknowledged:

1. **Shannon entropy is not the Logos.** Shannon information (Shannon, 1948) measures uncertainty reduction in a probability distribution over a message space. The Logos, as articulated in the Johannine Prologue, is a self-aware, personal, generative agent. These are categorically distinct concepts.

2. **John was not practicing information theory.** The Johannine Prologue draws on Hellenistic philosophical traditions—Stoic logos doctrine, Philo of Alexandria's mediating Logos, and Middle Platonic cosmology—not on mathematical formalism. The structural parallel exists despite radically different intellectual contexts and should not be anachronistically projected.

3. **Physics does not prove John 1:1.** The "It from Bit" thesis is a philosophical interpretation of quantum mechanics, not a settled experimental result. Many physicists (e.g., 't Hooft, 2016; Smolin, 2013) reject the information-theoretic ontology in favor of realist or relational alternatives.

4. **"Word" and "information" are not synonyms.** The Greek λόγος carries connotations of reason, proportion, speech, cosmic order, and intelligible structure that "information" in the Shannon sense does not. See Section 4 (Strain Analysis) for detailed examination.

5. **This isomorphism may be analogical rather than fully structural.** The present record is honest about this limitation and does not claim the strongest possible mapping within the Theophysics framework.

## 3. Testing Protocol

### 3.1 Test 1: Prediction Constraint

The mapping is evaluated for its capacity to constrain predictions in both domains.

**Domain A (Physics):** If information is ontologically primitive, then:
- No physical process can destroy information (unitarity). This constraint is precisely the content of the Black Hole Information Paradox (Hawking, 1976). The resolution—Hawking's 2004 concession, the Page curve calculations (Page, 1993), and the recent replica wormhole results (Penington et al., 2019; Almheiri et al., 2020)—supports information conservation.
- Every physical law must be expressible as an information-processing operation. This constrains the space of possible physical theories, potentially ruling out non-computable dynamics.

**Domain B (Theology):** If the Logos generates all reality, then:
- No created thing exists independently of the Logos. "Without him was not any thing made that was made" (John 1:3) constitutes a total-scope claim admitting no exceptions.
- Destruction of the Logos would entail destruction of all reality. This is consistent with Colossians 1:17: "in him all things hold together" (καὶ τὰ πάντα ἐν αὐτῷ συνέστηκεν).

**Verdict:** The mapping constrains predictions in both domains, but the constraints are asymmetric. Physics constraints are quantitative and experimentally testable (unitarity, Landauer bound). Theological constraints are qualitative and not experimentally testable in the same sense. **Partial pass.**

### 3.2 Test 2: Symmetry Breaking

The mapping is evaluated for whether the ordering of elements is arbitrary or necessary.

Can matter be ontologically prior to information? In Wheeler's framework: no—this would collapse the thesis into classical materialism. In John's framework: no—this would contradict "In the beginning was the Logos."

Can information be derivative? In Wheeler: this would collapse the framework into a materialist substrate. In John: this would mean the Logos was created, which is the Arian heresy (condemned at the First Council of Nicaea, AD 325) and explicitly contradicted by "the Logos WAS God" (θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος).

**Result: PASSED.** The ordering is non-arbitrary in both domains.

### 3.3 Test 3: Connection Density

The mapping is evaluated for its connections to other isomorphisms within the Theophysics framework.

- ISO-001 (Trinity): The Logos is the second person of the Trinity. Information as structure maps to the Son/Logos as the structural principle through which all creation is made.
- ISO-003 (Entropy/Sin): Information degradation in closed systems parallels moral degradation.
- ISO-011 (Superposition/Collapse): Wavefunction as information state; measurement as information extraction.
- ISO-012 (Sign Operator): The sign operator acts on an informational substrate.

**Connection count:** 4+ direct connections. **Passed.**

### 3.4 Test 4: Falsifiability Invitation

The mapping specifies conditions under which it would be falsified:

1. Demonstration that "It from Bit" is incorrect—that information is emergent from a sub-informational physical substrate (hidden variables, superdeterminism, or a future theory deriving quantum information from something more fundamental). If information is not ontologically primitive in physics, Domain A collapses and the mapping fails.

2. Demonstration that John's Logos concept concerns purely divine personhood with no cosmological generative claim. This is difficult to sustain given John 1:3 ("all things were made through him"), but a purely existentialist or soteriological reading of the Prologue would weaken Domain B.

3. Discovery of a physical system whose behavior cannot be described informationally. If a physical process exists that is real but carries zero information content, Wheeler's thesis fails.

## 4. Strain Analysis

### 4.1 The Core Objection

The most serious objection to this isomorphism concerns the semantic divergence between the two central terms:

**Logos (λόγος)** in Hellenistic philosophy denotes: rational principle, cosmic order, the intelligible structure of reality, speech, proportion, reason. In Philo of Alexandria (c. 20 BCE–50 CE), the Logos functions as the mediating principle between the transcendent God and the material world—the "image of God" (εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ) and the "instrument" (ὄργανον) through which the world was created. In Stoic philosophy, the Logos is the active rational principle (λόγος σπερματικός) pervading and organizing the cosmos. John appropriates this loaded term and identifies it with a specific person, Jesus Christ. The Logos is therefore PERSONAL, CONSCIOUS, INTENTIONAL, and DIVINE.

**Information** in Shannon's sense denotes: reduction of uncertainty, measured in bits, defined by the probability distribution over a message space. Shannon information is IMPERSONAL, UNCONSCIOUS, UNINTENTIONAL, and MATHEMATICAL.

These are not the same concept. The mapping holds at the structural level (both are ontologically prior to matter, both generate physical reality) but breaks at the personal level: the Logos is a *who*; information is a *what*.

### 4.2 Honest Assessment

This isomorphism is STRUCTURAL, not semantic. The terms "logos" and "information" do not denote the same concept. What they share is a structural role: ontologically prior substrate from which physical reality derives. The mapping is real at that level but should not be pushed to identity. The Logos includes information but is not reducible to it. Information may be a shadow or aspect of the Logos but is not the whole.

**Where the isomorphism holds:** The claim that reality is information-first (not matter-first) maps cleanly onto the claim that reality is Logos-first (not matter-first). The generative direction, the irreducibility, and the ontological priority all match.

**Where the isomorphism strains:** When one asks "is information conscious?" physics answers in the negative (or remains silent). When one asks "is the Logos impersonal?" theology answers with an emphatic negative. The personal dimension of the Logos has no counterpart in Shannon information or Wheeler's bit. This constitutes a significant gap.

### 4.3 Possible Resolution Within the Framework

The Theophysics framework posits that consciousness and personhood may themselves be informational phenomena, as articulated in Tononi's Integrated Information Theory (IIT; Tononi, 2004; Oizumi et al., 2014). According to IIT, consciousness corresponds to the quantity of integrated information (Φ) generated by a system's causal structure. If consciousness is constituted by sufficiently integrated information, then the gap between impersonal information and personal Logos narrows: the Logos would correspond to maximally integrated information (infinite Φ), while impersonal information corresponds to information at low integration (low Φ). This resolution is speculative but structurally consistent with the framework's axioms.
