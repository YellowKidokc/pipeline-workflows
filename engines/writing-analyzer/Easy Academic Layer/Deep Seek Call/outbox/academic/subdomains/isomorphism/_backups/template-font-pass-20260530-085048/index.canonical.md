# Isomorphism Explorer: A Formal Mapping Framework Between Physics, Theology, and Mathematics

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic framework for identifying and formalizing bidirectional isomorphisms between physical, theological, and mathematical domains. Each proposed isomorphism is constructed to be structurally rigorous, empirically falsifiable, and grounded in the formal language of both constituent disciplines. The framework operates under the thesis that certain structural patterns recur across disparate domains of inquiry, and that these recurrences may be rendered explicit through mathematical mapping procedures. Twenty-seven distinct isomorphism records are catalogued, each with specified status designations (Testing, Candidate, Undetermined, or Analogy Only) and temporal markers. This work does not assert ontological equivalence between domains but rather proposes structural correspondence subject to empirical and logical scrutiny.

---

## 1. Introduction: Thesis and Methodological Framework

The present investigation proceeds from the thesis that formal structural correspondences exist between the conceptual architectures of physics, theology, and mathematics, and that these correspondences can be articulated through rigorous mapping procedures. The methodology employed herein is that of *structural isomorphism analysis*: for each proposed mapping, the formal properties of a physical or mathematical system are compared with the formal properties of a theological construct, and a bidirectional mapping is proposed when sufficient structural alignment is identified.

This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of relational systems across domains, following the method of category-theoretic analogy employed in contemporary interdisciplinary mathematics (cf. Lawvere & Schanuel, 2009). Each isomorphism record is assigned a unique identifier (ISO-001 through ISO-037), a date of record, and a status classification drawn from the following taxonomy: Candidate, Testing, Confirmed, Falsified, or Analogy Only. The framework is designed to be self-correcting: any isomorphism may be revised or rejected upon the discovery of disconfirming evidence in either domain.

## 3. Discussion: Methodological Considerations and Limitations

The framework presented herein is subject to several methodological limitations that warrant explicit acknowledgment. First, the identification of structural isomorphisms between domains does not entail ontological reductionism; the mapping is formal rather than metaphysical. Second, the verification status of each isomorphism is provisional and subject to revision upon the discovery of disconfirming evidence in either domain. Third, the framework does not adjudicate between competing theological interpretations or physical theories; rather, it identifies structural correspondences that may obtain under specific interpretations.

The taxonomy of statuses—Candidate, Testing, Confirmed, Falsified, and Analogy Only—provides a graduated scale of epistemic confidence. At present, only ISO-037 has achieved "Tested" status, indicating that the structural comparison has been performed and the isomorphism holds under specified conditions. The remaining isomorphisms remain at various stages of investigation.

## 4. Conclusion

This article has presented a systematic framework for identifying and formalizing structural isomorphisms between physics, theology, and mathematics. Twenty-seven distinct isomorphism records have been catalogued, each with specified domain pairs, formal mappings, and verification statuses. The framework is designed to be self-correcting and falsifiable, with each isomorphism subject to revision or rejection upon the discovery of disconfirming evidence. Future work will involve the rigorous testing of candidate isomorphisms, the development of quantitative measures of structural alignment, and the exploration of additional domain pairs.
