# PEER REVIEW: ISO-033_Pharmacology_Grace_Mechanics

## A. CITATION QUALITY

**A1: 3 — Source Existence & Verifiability | Comment:** The paper cites Hill (1910) and Clark (1933) but provides no page numbers, edition information, or DOI/URLs. Biblical citations lack translation, manuscript tradition, or scholarly commentary references. The "Evidence Bundles" section references entire empirical literatures without a single specific study, making verification impossible.

**A2: 4 — Source Authority & Relevance | Comment:** Hill and Clark are foundational to pharmacology, and the biblical citations are relevant. However, the paper cites no contemporary pharmacological research (post-1950), no theological scholarship on grace mechanics, and no peer-reviewed work on interdisciplinary isomorphism methodology. The authority is narrow and dated.

**A3: 2 — Citation Formatting | Comment:** No consistent citation style is used. Biblical citations appear as bare book-chapter-verse references with no version specified. Scientific citations lack journal volume, page numbers, or access information. The "Related Papers" section is not a proper bibliography.

**A4: 1 — Citation Density & Distribution | Comment:** The paper has approximately 12 citations total for a document of ~3000 words. The first 2000 words contain zero citations. The distribution is heavily skewed toward the final sections. No citations appear in the mathematical formalism or mapping sections where they are most needed.

## B. FACTUAL ACCURACY

**B1: 7 — Empirical Claim Accuracy | Comment:** The pharmacological claims (Hill equation behavior, competitive inhibition, tolerance, receptor regulation) are factually correct and well-established. The theological claims about grace mechanics are consistent with mainstream Protestant and Catholic soteriology, though presented without nuance.

**B2: 6 — Historical/Contextual Accuracy | Comment:** The paper's treatment of Hebrews 6:4-6 as describing "grace-tolerance" is a plausible but contested interpretation. The claim that "conversion narratives show threshold behavior" is asserted without evidence. The historical examples (Augustine, Wesley, Lewis) are named but not analyzed.

**B3: 8 — Mathematical/Formal Accuracy | Comment:** The Hill equation is correctly stated. The competitive inhibition modification is accurate. The substitution of theological variables preserves the mathematical structure. The connection to the Heaviside step function as n→∞ is mathematically sound.

**B4: 6 — Claim-Evidence Alignment | Comment:** The 11 "independent correspondences" are presented as evidence but are actually restatements of the mapping itself. The probability calculation (p < 0.05^11) is mathematically incoherent—these are not independent statistical tests. The evidence does not support the confidence level claimed.

## C. LOGICAL RIGOR

**C1: 5 — Deductive Validity | Comment:** The core deduction—"if both domains share the same mathematical structure, then predictions in one constrain the other"—is valid. However, the paper never establishes that the domains share the same structure rather than being mapped onto it by selective analogy.

**C2: 4 — Inductive Strength | Comment:** The inductive argument from 11 correspondences is weak because the correspondences were selected to fit the mapping, not discovered independently. No negative evidence is presented. The sample of correspondences is not random.

**C3: 3 — Absence of Fallacies | Comment:** Multiple fallacies present: confirmation bias (only supporting examples cited), false analogy (the "isomorphism" claim is asserted, not demonstrated), special pleading (the excretion asymmetry is treated as "informative" rather than as evidence the mapping is incomplete), and circularity (the mapping is used to validate itself).

**C4: 5 — Argument Chain Integrity | Comment:** The argument chain is: (1) both domains have sigmoidal response → (2) therefore they share structure → (3) therefore predictions transfer. Step (2) is the weakest link—sigmoidal response is a common pattern found in hundreds of domains, not evidence of structural identity.

## D. EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY

**D1: 3 — Claim Strength vs Evidence Weight | Comment:** The paper makes strong claims ("Structural Isomorphism," "Confidence: High") but provides no systematic evidence. The 11 correspondences are asserted without empirical support from either domain. No theological texts are cited to support the grace mechanics model.

**D2: 2 — Counter-Evidence Engagement | Comment:** The paper acknowledges "what is NOT claimed" but does not engage with actual counterarguments. The excretion asymmetry is mentioned but dismissed as "informative" rather than treated as a genuine disconfirmation. No theological traditions that reject this model (e.g., Eastern Orthodox theosis, process theology) are addressed.

**D3: 1 — Replication & Corroboration | Comment:** No replication is possible because no specific empirical predictions are tested. The "Evidence Bundles" reference entire literatures without citing a single study. The paper provides no methodology for testing its claims.

**D4: 2 — Statistical Rigor | Comment:** The probability calculation (p < 0.05^11 ≈ 5 × 10^-15) is statistically illiterate. The 11 correspondences are not independent tests. No correction for multiple comparisons. No effect sizes. No confidence intervals. This is pseudostatistics.

## E. SEMANTIC PRECISION

**E1: 6 — Term Definition Consistency | Comment:** "Grace," "faith," "sin," and "receptor" are defined operationally within the mapping and used consistently. However, the definitions are stipulative and may not correspond to how these terms are used in theology or pharmacology.

**E2: 5 — Equivocation Detection | Comment:** The paper equivocates between "isomorphism" (structural identity) and "analogy" (structural similarity). The term "isomorphism" in mathematics requires a bijective structure-preserving map; the paper provides no proof of bijectivity or full structure preservation.

**E3: 4 — Domain Translation Fidelity | Comment:** The translation of theological concepts into pharmacological terms is creative but loses theological nuance. "Faith" as "receptor density" collapses a complex theological concept into a single parameter. "Sin" as "competitive antagonist" ignores moral and relational dimensions.

**E4: 7 — Hedging Calibration | Comment:** The "What Is NOT Claimed" section is appropriately cautious. However, this caution is contradicted by the "Confidence: High" classification and the strong claims about "structural isomorphism" elsewhere.

**F2: 1 — Methodology Transparency | Comment:** No methodology is described. How were the correspondences identified? What criteria determine an isomorphism vs. an analogy? What search strategy was used? What inclusion/exclusion criteria? The paper provides no way to evaluate its methods.

**F3: 5 — Structure & Flow | Comment:** The document has a clear structure (Domains, Mapping, Tests, Classification, Cross-Reference) but the flow is disrupted by the extensive "What Is NOT Claimed" section and the mathematical formalism appearing before the mapping is fully motivated.

**H1: 4 — Isomorphism vs Analogy Distinction | Comment:** The paper claims "Structural Isomorphism" but provides evidence only for analogy. An isomorphism requires a bijective mapping that preserves all structural relations. The paper acknowledges at least one structural asymmetry (excretion) and does not demonstrate bijectivity.

**H2: 5 — Domain Boundary Respect | Comment:** The paper acknowledges that grace is not a chemical substance and faith is not a physical receptor. However, it then proceeds to treat them as if they were, making predictions about theology based on pharmacological principles without establishing that the boundary conditions are equivalent.

**H3: 3 — Mapping Rigor | Comment:** The mapping is presented as a simple variable substitution (E → alpha, [D] → [G], EC_50 → GT_50, n → faith-receptor density). No justification is given for why these specific variables map to each other. No alternative mappings are considered. No mapping validation criteria are provided.

**H4: 4 — Prediction Transfer | Comment:** The predictions in Domain B (theology) are plausible but not uniquely derived from the mapping. The prediction that "conversion events should cluster bimodally" could be derived from many threshold models. The prediction that "community faith density should correlate with conversion threshold steepness" is not operationalized.

## 1. THREE MOST CRITICAL FAILURES

**Failure 1: Pseudostatistical Evidence (Section: Tests, Test 3)**
> "11 independent correspondences. At p < 0.05 per correspondence, the probability of 11 independent chance matches is < 0.05^11 ≈ 5 x 10^-15."

This is a catastrophic methodological error. The 11 correspondences are not independent statistical tests—they are selected features of a single mapping. The probability calculation is mathematically incoherent and would be rejected by any peer reviewer. It suggests the author does not understand basic statistics.

**Failure 2: No Methodology (Section: Entire Paper)**
The paper provides no description of how the mapping was derived, what criteria were used to select correspondences, what alternative mappings were considered and rejected, or how the isomorphism claim could be validated. Without methodology, the paper is an assertion, not an argument.

**Failure 3: Isomorphism Claim Without Proof (Section: Classification)**
> "Type: Structural Isomorphism | Confidence: High"

The paper provides no proof of isomorphism (bijective structure-preserving map). It provides evidence for analogy (structural similarity). The claim of isomorphism is unsupported and likely false given the acknowledged asymmetry (excretion). This overclaiming undermines the entire paper.

## 2. THREE SPECIFIC REVISIONS

**Revision 1 (highest impact): Remove the pseudostatistical probability calculation and replace with honest qualitative assessment.**
The claim of p < 0.05^11 is indefensible and would cause immediate rejection. Replace with: "These 11 correspondences are not independent statistical tests, so no probability can be calculated. However, the density and specificity of the mapping exceeds what would be expected from a casual analogy." This revision would raise scores on D4 (Statistical Rigor) from 2 to 6 and C3 (Absence of Fallacies) from 3 to 5.

**Revision 2 (medium impact): Add a methodology section describing how the mapping was derived and validated.**
Include: (a) search strategy for identifying correspondences, (b) criteria for inclusion/exclusion, (c) alternative mappings considered and rejected, (d) validation criteria for isomorphism vs. analogy. This revision would raise scores on F2 (Methodology Transparency) from 1 to 6 and H3 (Mapping Rigor) from 3 to 6.

**Revision 3 (lower impact): Change "Structural Isomorphism" to "Structural Analogy" throughout and adjust confidence claims accordingly.**
The evidence supports analogy, not isomorphism. Acknowledging this would make the paper more honest and defensible. This revision would raise scores on H1 (Isomorphism vs Analogy Distinction) from 4 to 8 and E2 (Equivocation Detection) from 5 to 7.
