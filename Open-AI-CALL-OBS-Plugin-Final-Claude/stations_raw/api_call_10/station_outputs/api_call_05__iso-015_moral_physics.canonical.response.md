The relevance of decoherence theory to moral measurement is asserted, not argued.

The physics citations are decades old; the social science citations are vague.

## B. FACTUAL ACCURACY

**B1: 5 — Empirical Claim Accuracy | Comment:** The claim that "high-trust societies outperform low-trust ones on virtually every metric" is broadly supported by social science literature, but the paper provides no specific data points, effect sizes, or confidence intervals. The claim about "Rome, Weimar" civilizational decline is historically contested.

**B2: 4 — Historical/Contextual Accuracy | Comment:** The reference to "Rome, Weimar" as examples of civilizational coherence decline is oversimplified. The paper treats complex historical phenomena as straightforward illustrations of a physical principle, ignoring historiographic debates about causality.

**B3: 6 — Mathematical/Formal Accuracy | Comment:** The coherence functional C[chi] = integral sqrt(-g) [kinetic - V(chi) + L_int] d^4x is presented as if it were a standard physics expression, but it appears to be a custom construction. The notation is internally consistent but its physical meaning is unclear.

**B4: 4 — Claim-Evidence Alignment | Comment:** The central claim (morality is empirically measurable) is supported only by a structural analogy and vague references to social survey data. The evidence does not match the strength of the claim by several orders of magnitude.

## C. LOGICAL RIGOR

**C1: 5 — Deductive Validity | Comment:** The paper attempts deductive reasoning from axioms (coherence as measurable moral quantity) to claims, but the axioms themselves are unsubstantiated. The logical chain from "coherence is measurable in physics" to "morality is measurable via coherence metrics" contains hidden premises.

**C2: 4 — Inductive Strength | Comment:** The inductive leap from "some societies with moral decline show measurable decay" to "all moral violations produce decoherence signatures" is extremely weak. The sample of historical cases is small and cherry-picked.

**C3: 3 — Absence of Fallacies | Comment:** The paper commits the analogy fallacy (treating structural similarity as evidence of causal connection), the false equivalence (equating physical coherence with moral goodness), and confirmation bias (selecting only evidence that supports the framework).

**C4: 4 — Argument Chain Integrity | Comment:** The argument chain from Axiom → Claim → Evidence → Bridge is structurally clear, but each link is weak. The weakest link (operationalization gap) is acknowledged but not addressed.

## D. EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY

**D1: 3 — Claim Strength vs Evidence Weight | Comment:** The claim "morality is empirically measurable via coherence metrics" is extraordinarily strong, but the evidence is limited to one survey dataset and historical anecdotes. This is a catastrophic mismatch.

**D2: 2 — Counter-Evidence Engagement | Comment:** The paper acknowledges that "a criminal organization can be internally 'coherent'" but dismisses this as "local self-consistency" rather than "cosmic coherence." This is an ad hoc distinction that immunizes the framework from falsification.

**D3: 3 — Replication & Corroboration | Comment:** No replication studies are cited. The World Values Survey data is mentioned but not analyzed. The paper provides no evidence that any other researcher has found similar patterns.

**D4: 2 — Statistical Rigor | Comment:** No statistical analysis is presented. The paper mentions "trust indices" but provides no correlation coefficients, significance tests, or effect sizes. The claim of measurability is entirely non-quantitative.

**E2: 3 — Equivocation Detection | Comment:** The paper systematically equivocates between "coherence" as a precise physical quantity and "coherence" as a vague moral/social concept. This is the central rhetorical strategy and a major logical flaw.

**E3: 5 — Domain Translation Fidelity | Comment:** The mapping from physics to theology is structurally explicit, but the translation loses precision. "Decoherence rate" in physics has a specific mathematical definition; "moral decoherence" has none.

**E4: 4 — Hedging Calibration | Comment:** The "What Is NOT Claimed" section shows awareness of limitations, but the hedging is inconsistent. The paper oscillates between bold claims ("morality is empirically measurable") and cautious qualifications ("this is not a fatal flaw").

## 1. THREE MOST CRITICAL FAILURES

**Failure 1: Equivocation on "Coherence"**
The paper uses "coherence" in three distinct senses (physical, social, moral) without distinguishing them. The central argument depends on treating them as equivalent. Quote: "Coherence C[chi] as a measurable quantity of organized information density" is presented as if it applies equally to quantum systems and moral actions. This is the paper's foundational logical error.

**Failure 2: Catastrophic Claim-Evidence Mismatch**
The paper claims "morality is empirically measurable via coherence metrics" but provides no measurement protocol, no unit of moral coherence, no instrument, and no calibration standard. Quote: "The framework uses C[chi] but does not specify its moral-domain units." The paper acknowledges this as a weakness but does not resolve it, leaving the central claim unsupported.

**Failure 3: Absence of Methodology**
No methodology is described for how the World Values Survey data was analyzed, what coherence metrics were computed, or what statistical tests were performed. Quote: "What device measures moral coherence? Brain scans? Social surveys? Economic indicators? The framework suggests 'coherence metrics' but does not specify the measurement protocol." Without methodology, the paper is not reproducible.
