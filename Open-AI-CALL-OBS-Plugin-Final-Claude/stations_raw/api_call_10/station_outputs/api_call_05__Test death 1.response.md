**Thesis:** God can be proven only when the deepest law of the universe meets someone it has no right to bind.

**Method:** The argument builds a logical case by distinguishing between a mere rule-breaking event and a "rightful exception"—one that the law itself recognizes as just.

**Key Finding:** The resurrection of Jesus is strongest as proof of God not because it shows power over death, but because death had no legal claim on Him, making His release a verdict of justice rather than a display of force.

**Significance:** This reframes the search for God from looking for supernatural power to looking for moral authority over the final constraint of reality.

---

The document argues that proving God requires more than showing a miracle. A demon, a magician, or an advanced alien could all break a natural rule. What matters is a *rightful exception*—an event where the deepest law of the universe, death, encounters someone it cannot lawfully hold. The author calls this "proof by jurisdiction." Jurisdiction means legal authority or rightful claim. Death has jurisdiction over everything that is sinful or corruptible. But if someone is innocent and voluntarily enters death, death has no claim. Reality itself must release that person.

The evidence presented is entirely logical and theological. The author does not cite historical records, scientific data, or philosophical sources. Instead, the argument rests on a chain of reasoning: sin gives death its claim; Christ had no sin; therefore death had no claim on Him. The resurrection is not just a supernatural event but a structural verdict—the system's final constraint testifying against itself. The author contrasts this with ordinary miracles, which prove only power, not moral authority.

The conclusion is that a true proof of God would not be a rule being broken, but the deepest rule meeting the one person it had no right to bind. This makes the resurrection a matter of justice, not force.

**What the document does well:** It presents a clear, original framework for thinking about proof of God that avoids common pitfalls like relying on unexplained power or vague spirituality. The distinction between a "rightful exception" and a "hacked rule" is sharp and memorable.

**What remains unresolved:** The document offers no evidence that Jesus was actually sinless or that death is a legal rather than a biological reality. It also does not address how a reader would verify that this specific exception happened in history. The argument is logically elegant but rests entirely on assumptions that are asserted, not demonstrated.
