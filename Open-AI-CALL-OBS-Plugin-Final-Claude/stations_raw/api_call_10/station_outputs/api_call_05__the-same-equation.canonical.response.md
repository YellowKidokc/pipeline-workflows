## B. FACTUAL ACCURACY

**B1: 3 — Empirical Claim Accuracy** | Comment: The paper correctly reproduces several standard physics equations (Newton's law, E=mc², Maxwell's equations, Schrödinger equation, Fermi decay formula) in their physical form, but makes unsubstantiated empirical claims about spiritual analogues that have no empirical basis.

**B2: 2 — Historical/Contextual Accuracy** | Comment: The paper presents Fermi's 192π³ denominator as a striking coincidence without acknowledging that this constant arises from specific phase space integrals in weak interaction theory—it is not a free parameter that could "appear" in a spiritual equation by mathematical necessity.

**B3: 4 — Mathematical/Formal Accuracy** | Comment: The physical equations are correctly transcribed, but the spiritual analogues introduce ad hoc multiplicative factors (1-R, I, A, 1-B, Pwill) that are mathematically undefined—no domain, no range, no units, no operationalization.

## C. LOGICAL RIGOR

**C1: 2 — Deductive Validity** | Comment: The paper commits a fundamental category error: treating typographic similarity between equations as deductive proof of structural identity. The argument "same letters, same structure, therefore same law" is formally invalid.

**C2: 3 — Inductive Strength** | Comment: The inductive argument from ten examples to a universal claim about the structure of reality is extremely weak—no base rate is established, no null hypothesis is tested, and no alternative explanations (e.g., confirmation bias, pattern matching) are considered.

**C3: 1 — Absence of Fallacies** | Comment: The paper is built on multiple fallacies: false analogy (physical forces and spiritual concepts are not comparable domains), equivocation (using "force," "energy," "entropy" in both physical and metaphorical senses), and confirmation bias (only showing mappings that work).

**C4: 2 — Argument Chain Integrity** | Comment: The argument chain has a critical broken link: the paper asserts that because equations look similar, they describe the same underlying reality, but never establishes that the spiritual terms (grace, sin, faith) are well-defined quantities that can enter into mathematical relationships.
