The key move is to remove "special pleading"—the unfair habit of letting one side play by easier rules. For example, science demands physical proof for religious claims, but it cannot prove its own most basic rules, like cause and effect or the reliability of our senses. Those are taken on trust. Meanwhile, theology often claims to have direct knowledge of God, but when pressed, it leans on logic and observations about the natural world—tools borrowed from science. The audit reveals that each side quietly relies on the other.
