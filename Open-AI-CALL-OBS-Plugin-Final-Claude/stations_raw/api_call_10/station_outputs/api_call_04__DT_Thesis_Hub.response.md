Mathematics comes before matter. That is the central claim of this doctoral thesis framework: mathematical structure exists first, and physical reality emerges from it. The entire project, called Theophysics, builds on this single starting point to connect math, physics, theology, and human consciousness into one unified system. At the heart of everything is something called the Master Equation, a single formula that ties together all the pieces.

The thesis is organized into eight units that build on each other. The first three units lay the foundation: they prove that math is not neutral but actually moral, and that the way quantum particles behave requires exactly three basic operations—which matches the Christian idea of the Trinity. The next two units examine problems in how systems work, like why rule-based systems can become rigid and self-destructive, and why reality doesn't just fall apart into an endless chain of questions. The final three units apply these ideas to real-world situations, including a method where multiple artificial intelligences reach the same truth independently, an analysis of what makes constitutions strong or weak, and a study of how civilizations decay and recover over four-hundred-year cycles.

What makes this framework unusual is that it treats mathematics, physics, and theology as different views of the same underlying reality. Each unit is a complete paper on its own, but together they form a single argument: that the universe has a coherent structure, that this structure has moral weight, and that human societies follow the same patterns of breakdown and renewal that appear in physics. The project has already produced several complete papers and is working on the rest.
