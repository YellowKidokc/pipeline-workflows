## B. FACTUAL ACCURACY

**B1: 6** — Empirical Claim Accuracy | Comment: The paper's empirical claims about scientific practice (6-sigma standards, peer review, paradigm shifts) are broadly accurate but oversimplified. The claim that "the demarcation problem is unsolved" is a contested philosophical position presented as settled fact.

**B2: 7** — Historical/Contextual Accuracy | Comment: Historical references (Galileo, heliocentrism, slavery abolition, Big Bang cosmology) are accurate in broad strokes. The claim that "traditions that engage with science... have shown greater intellectual durability" is asserted without historical evidence.

**B3: 5** — Mathematical/Formal Accuracy | Comment: The 6-sigma standard (p < 3.4 × 10⁻⁶) is correctly stated. However, the paper presents no mathematical formalism for its own claims about "chi field detection" or "consciousness thresholds," making the mathematical accuracy of these claims unassessable.

**B4: 6** — Claim-Evidence Alignment | Comment: The central claim (shared epistemic structure) is supported by the mapping table and swap test. However, the paper's own "Honest Assessment" section undermines its evidence by admitting the mapping may be "trivially true" and "too general."

## C. LOGICAL RIGOR

**C1: 5** — Deductive Validity | Comment: The paper's deductive structure is weak. It claims an isomorphism but then provides extensive caveats that the mapping fails at the quantitative level. The conclusion does not follow from the premises—the paper argues for a structural isomorphism while providing evidence it is merely analogical.

**C2: 6** — Inductive Strength | Comment: The inductive argument from examples (geocentrism→heliocentrism, slavery→abolition) is suggestive but not strong. Two historical examples do not constitute robust inductive support for a general epistemological claim.

**C3: 4** — Absence of Fallacies | Comment: Multiple fallacies present: false analogy (comparing scientific method to theological epistemology without establishing commensurability), straw man (presenting "strict sola scriptura" as the foil without engaging sophisticated theological epistemology), and appeal to authority (6-sigma standard borrowed from physics without justification).

**C4: 5** — Argument Chain Integrity | Comment: The argument chain has broken links. The paper moves from "science has falsifiability" to "theology has something similar" to "therefore they share epistemic structure" without establishing that the similarities are non-trivial or that the differences are not fatal.

## D. EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY

**D1: 4** — Claim Strength vs Evidence Weight | Comment: The claim is ambitious (structural isomorphism between science and theology) but the evidence is thin: a mapping table, a swap test, and two historical examples. The paper's own admission that the mapping may be "trivially true" indicates evidence insufficiency.

**D2: 6** — Counter-Evidence Engagement | Comment: The paper does engage counter-evidence in its "Falsification" section, listing four ways the mapping could fail. However, it does not resolve these objections—it merely acknowledges them and proceeds as if they were addressed.

**D3: 3** — Replication & Corroboration | Comment: No replication or corroboration is provided. The paper cites no empirical studies of theological epistemology, no comparative analyses of knowledge systems, and no experimental tests of its claims.

**D4: 2** — Statistical Rigor | Comment: Despite invoking 6-sigma standards, the paper provides zero statistical analysis. Claims about "chi field detection" and "consciousness thresholds" are made without any quantitative framework, let one at the 6-sigma level.

## E. SEMANTIC PRECISION

**E1: 5** — Term Definition Consistency | Comment: Key terms shift meaning: "falsifiability" is used in Popper's technical sense for science but in a loose, metaphorical sense for theology. "Testable" means different things in the two domains (empirical experiment vs. rational scrutiny).

**E2: 4** — Equivocation Detection | Comment: The paper equivocates on "faith" (scientific trust in method vs. religious faith in God), "test" (experimental vs. experiential), and "evidence" (empirical data vs. historical testimony). It acknowledges some equivocations but does not resolve them.

**E3: 6** — Domain Translation Fidelity | Comment: The mapping table attempts careful translation between domains, identifying structural roles. **E4: 5** — Hedging Calibration | Comment: The paper oscillates between strong claims ("the framework adopts a 6-sigma standard") and extreme hedging ("the mapping is REAL but may be TOO GENERAL"). ## 1. THREE Most Critical Failures

**Failure 1: The paper claims "isomorphism" but provides evidence only for "analogy," and its own "Honest Assessment" admits the mapping may be "trivially true" and "too general."** This is a fatal self-contradiction: the paper's central thesis is undermined by its own analysis. The "Swap Test" reveals that the quantitative apparatus does not transfer, which should disqualify the mapping as an isomorphism. The paper acknowledges this but does not adjust its classification.

**Failure 2: The paper invokes 6-sigma standards (p < 3.4 × 10⁻⁶) for its empirical predictions but provides no quantitative framework, no statistical analysis, and no operationalized tests.** The "Honest Assessment" admits that "many of the framework's predictions... are not currently testable at any sigma level, let alone six." Claiming 6-sigma standards for untestable predictions is not just aspirational—it is misleading and undermines the paper's credibility.

**Failure 3: The paper equivocates on the central term "falsifiability," using it in Popper's technical sense for science but in a loose, metaphorical sense for theology.** The paper acknowledges that "theological claims are not the type of thing that can be tested at 6-sigma" but then proceeds to claim that theology should adopt falsifiability standards. This equivocation is never resolved, and the paper never provides criteria for what counts as "falsification" in theology.

## 2. THREE Specific Revisions (Ranked by Impact)

**Revision 1 (highest impact): Rename the paper from "Isomorphism" to "Analogy" and restructure the argument accordingly.** The paper's own evidence supports analogy, not isomorphism. Changing the classification would resolve the self-contradiction, align the paper with its own "Honest Assessment," and allow the author to claim the more defensible position that science and theology share analogical features without claiming structural identity. This would raise scores on C1 (deductive validity), C4 (argument chain integrity), and H1 (isomorphism vs analogy distinction).

**Revision 2 (medium impact): Remove or substantially revise the 6-sigma claim.** Either provide an actual quantitative framework for the paper's predictions (operationalizing "chi field detection" and "consciousness thresholds" with testable metrics) or acknowledge that the 6-sigma standard is aspirational and not currently operational. The current claim is indefensible and undermines the paper's credibility. This would raise scores on B3 (mathematical accuracy), D4 (statistical rigor), and I4 (testable prediction specificity).

**Revision 3 (lower impact): Add a methodology section that defines criteria for isomorphism vs. analogy and explains how the mapping was constructed and tested.** The paper currently lacks transparency about its method. A methodology section would clarify what counts as evidence for the claimed relationship, how the mapping was derived, and what would constitute disconfirmation. This would raise scores on F2 (methodology transparency), H3 (mapping rigor), and I1 (falsification criteria stated).

## 3. One Thing the Paper Does Surprisingly Well

The paper's "What Is NOT Claimed" section is a model of intellectual honesty and precision. It explicitly lists six things the paper is NOT claiming, including that science and theology use the same method, that theology should adopt 6-sigma standards, that science is a form of faith, and that all theological claims are testable. This section demonstrates careful thinking about the limits of the argument and anticipates likely objections. It is the most rigorous part of the paper and suggests the author has a more nuanced understanding than the main argument displays.
