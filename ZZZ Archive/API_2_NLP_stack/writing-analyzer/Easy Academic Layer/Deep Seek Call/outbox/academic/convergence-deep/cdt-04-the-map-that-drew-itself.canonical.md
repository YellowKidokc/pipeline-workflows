## 2. Methodological Framework: The Four-Test Protocol

The distinction between analogy and isomorphism is foundational to this analysis. An analogy asserts similarity without structural constraint; an isomorphism requires exact correspondence of relational architecture. The framework employs a Four-Test Protocol to distinguish genuine structural identity from rhetorical resemblance:

**T1: Prediction Constraint.** The mapping must generate bidirectional predictions. Knowledge of the physical domain must yield specific, testable implications for the theological domain, and vice versa.

**T2: Symmetric Breaking.** If the physical law is broken (e.g., entropy reverses spontaneously), the theological mapping must break in the corresponding manner (e.g., the doctrine of sin loses coherence). Failure of symmetric breaking invalidates the isomorphism.

**T3: Connection Density.** The mapping must exhibit multiple independent correspondences. One match constitutes noise; three or more constitute a pattern; seven or more become statistically improbable as coincidence.

**T4: Falsifiability Invitation.** The mapping must specify conditions under which it would be destroyed. An isomorphism that cannot be falsified is not an isomorphism but a belief.

Throughout this article, the term *isomorphism* is reserved for mappings that pass all four tests. Mappings that fail one or more tests are designated *parallels* or *analogies*, with correspondingly reduced epistemic weight.

**Property 1: Maximum Bond Strength.** The energy required to separate two quarks within a proton is approximately \(E_{\text{sep}} \sim 1\) GeV/fm. At this energy scale, the vacuum spontaneously generates new quark-antiquark pairs, preventing separation. The bond creates new reality rather than breaking.

**Property 2: Asymptotic Freedom.** At short range (\(r < 0.5\) fm), the strong coupling constant decreases logarithmically, permitting quarks to move freely. The strongest force in the universe is invisible at the scale of its operation.

**Property 3: Confinement.** As interquark distance increases, the gluon flux tube between them stores energy linearly with separation (\(V(r) \approx \sigma r\), where \(\sigma \approx 0.9\) GeV/fm). The force increases with distance rather than decreasing. The bond would rather create new particle-antiparticle pairs than permit separation.

**Theological Mapping:** The strongest bond in theology is covenant love—not emotional attachment but structural binding. ### 4.2 The Negative Eigenstate Proof: Salvation as State-Vector Transformation

In quantum mechanics, a system is described by a state vector \(|\psi\rangle\) in a Hilbert space. Application of an operator \(\hat{O}\) yields eigenvalues \(\lambda\) satisfying \(\hat{O}|\psi\rangle = \lambda|\psi\rangle\). The framework identifies a moral sign operator \(\hat{M}\) such that:

\[
\hat{M}|\psi\rangle = \pm 1|\psi\rangle
\]

where \(+1\) corresponds to alignment (saved) and \(-1\) to misalignment (unsaved).

**Theorem (Eigenspace Invariance):** An operator \(\hat{A}\) that acts entirely within an eigenspace of \(\hat{M}\) cannot change the eigenvalue. Formally, if \(\hat{M}|\psi\rangle = -1|\psi\rangle\) and \([\hat{M}, \hat{A}] = 0\) (i.e., \(\hat{A}\) commutes with \(\hat{M}\) within the eigenspace), then \(\hat{M}(\hat{A}|\psi\rangle) = -1(\hat{A}|\psi\rangle)\).

**Corollary 1:** Works—defined as operators within the \(-1\) eigenspace—can modify the state vector within that eigenspace but cannot flip the sign. Good deeds, moral effort, and religious performance produce a better \(-1\) but cannot produce \(+1\).

**Corollary 2:** The only operator capable of flipping the sign is an external, non-unitary operator \(\hat{G}\) that acts from outside the eigenspace—i.e., \([\hat{M}, \hat{G}] \neq 0\) and \(\hat{G}\) is not representable as a linear combination of operators within the eigenspace.

**Theological Interpretation:** The impossibility of self-rescue is not a theological assertion but a theorem in linear algebra. Salvation requires an external operator (grace) because internal operators (works) are structurally incapable of producing the required eigenvalue transformation.

**Four-Test Verification:**

- **T1 (Prediction Constraint):** If the quantum structure holds, works cannot save; if works cannot save, the quantum structure should hold.
- **T2 (Symmetric Breaking):** If eigenvalues could be changed internally, both the mathematics and the theology would collapse.
- **T3 (Connection Density):** Eigenvalue → salvation state; operator type → works versus grace; eigenspace → moral domain; dimensionality → transcendence.
- **T4 (Falsifiability):** Find a method to flip an eigenvalue from inside the eigenspace, and the isomorphism fails.
