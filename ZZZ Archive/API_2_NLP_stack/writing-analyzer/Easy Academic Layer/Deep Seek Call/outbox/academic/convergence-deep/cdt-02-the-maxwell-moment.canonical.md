## 2. Maxwell's Equations: Formal Presentation

### 2.1 The Four Equations

Maxwell's equations in differential form, expressed in SI units, are as follows:

**Equation 1—Gauss's Law for Electricity:**
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$$

where $\mathbf{E}$ is the electric field (V/m), $\rho$ is the electric charge density (C/m³), and $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space (F/m). This equation establishes electric charges as sources of electric fields.

**Equation 2—Faraday's Law of Induction:**
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

where $\mathbf{B}$ is the magnetic field (T) and $t$ is time (s). A time-varying magnetic field generates a curling electric field.

**Equation 3—Gauss's Law for Magnetism:**
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$

This equation asserts the absence of magnetic monopoles; magnetic field lines form closed loops.

**Equation 4—Ampère's Law with Maxwell's Addition:**
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \epsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space (H/m) and $\mathbf{J}$ is the current density (A/m²). The term $\mu_0 \epsilon_0 \partial \mathbf{E}/\partial t$ is the displacement current, Maxwell's critical addition.

### 2.2 The Displacement Current and Wave Emergence

Prior to Maxwell, three of the four equations were known. The displacement current term was required for internal consistency: without it, the continuity equation $\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0$ fails, violating charge conservation. Combining Equations 2 and 4 yields the electromagnetic wave equation:

$$\frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \nabla^2 \mathbf{E}$$

where $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}$ is the speed of light in vacuum (approximately $2.998 \times 10^8$ m/s). The wave equation emerges necessarily from the coupled PDE structure; it is not an additional postulate.
