# The Ontological Decision Tree: A Formal Theophysical Argument from First Principles

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized decision-theoretic framework for evaluating foundational ontological commitments, proceeding from the minimal presupposition of existence to the derivation of necessary conditions for a complete explanatory system. The method employs an exhaustive branching structure in which every possible answer to each successive question is enumerated, and each non-viable branch is eliminated through explicit criteria: self-refutation, infinite regress, empirical contradiction, or logical incoherence. The analysis demonstrates that secular physicalist and theophysical frameworks traverse an identical decision tree through the first three levels, diverging at the question of what grounds the observed structural order of reality. The theophysical branch, proceeding through self-grounding substrate logic, yields eight necessary boundary conditions whose simultaneous satisfaction identifies a terminal observer possessing properties traditionally attributed to the divine. A comparative evaluation of major world religious systems against these boundary conditions is provided, with Christianity achieving the highest correspondence score. The article concludes with an honest enumeration of unresolved questions in both frameworks.

## 1. Methodology: Four Question Types and Four Death Conditions

The argument proceeds through a structured decision tree in which each node corresponds to one of four question types, and each branch is evaluated against one of four death conditions.

### 1.1 Question Types

**Type 1: "Does X hold?"** — Binary with escape routes. Maximum four branches: Yes, No, Meaningless, Illusory.

**Type 2: "What IS X?"** — Ontological identity. Every philosophical category is run: material, mental, informational, relational, mathematical, brute, composite.

**Type 3: "What grounds X?"** — Explanatory origin. Branches: nothing (brute), external (regress risk), itself (self-grounding), illusory, necessary.

**Type 4: "What must X be?"** — Constraint derivation. Not branching but deduction: given survivors, derive necessary properties.

### 1.2 Death Conditions

**Self-Refutation (SR):** The claim destroys itself when stated.

**Infinite Regress (IR):** The answer pushes the question back without resolving it.

**Empirical Contradiction (EC):** The claim contradicts what is observed.

**Logical Incoherence (LI):** The claim contradicts something already established in the tree.

## 2. Level 0 — Bedrock: Type 1 Question

**Q0: Does anything exist?**

This question is self-justifying: the act of asking presupposes a questioner.

**Q0-A: No, nothing exists.**
*Death condition: Self-Refutation.* The denial requires a denier. To assert "nothing exists" is to instantiate a claim, which is itself something. The proposition destroys itself upon utterance.

**Q0-B: Yes, something exists.**
*Undeniable.* Even radical skepticism (cf. Descartes' *cogito ergo sum*) terminates here. The act of doubting is itself something. This branch forces Q1.

**Q0-C: The question is meaningless.**
*Death condition: Self-Refutation.* Declaring meaninglessness requires a meaning-bearing entity to make the declaration. The proposition presupposes what it denies.

**Q0-D: Existence is an illusion.**
*Collapses to Q0-B.* An illusion requires something for which the illusion exists. The illusion itself exists as illusion. This routes back to "something exists."

**System convergence:** Both secular physics (SYS-A) and theophysics (SYS-B) agree: something exists rather than nothing. No divergence at this level.

*Worldview survival tracking:* 18 accept, 2 partial, 0 eliminated.

## 3. Level 1 — Distinction: Type 1 Question

**Q1: Can that which exists be distinguished from that which does not?**

Forced by Q0-B. If "something" exists, the term implies it is not "nothing." The question is whether this distinction is real.

**Q1-A: No — being and non-being are indistinguishable.**
*Death condition: Logical Incoherence.* Contradicts Q0-B. If existence and non-existence are indistinguishable, "something exists" has no content. The starting point is collapsed.

**Q1-B: Yes — the distinction between being and non-being is real.**
*This is what "existence" means.* To exist is to be distinguishable from not-existing. This branch forces Q2.

**Q1-C: The distinction is real at one level but illusory at another.**
*Recursion cost.* Advaita Vedanta resides here: empirical distinction is real, ultimate distinction is illusory. However, stating this requires distinguishing the two levels — recursion is present and carries cost forward.

**System convergence:** Both systems agree: existence requires distinguishability. Observables in physics are defined by distinguishability.

*Worldview survival tracking:* 15 accept, 2 partial, 1 rejects.

## 4. Level 2 — The Information Fork: Type 2 Question

**Q2: What IS a distinction?**

Forced by Q1-B. Distinction is real. What is its ontological status? This constitutes the first major kill zone, where six worldviews are eliminated simultaneously.

**Q2-A: A property of matter — physical objects differ.**
*Death condition: Circular.* Presupposes matter, which has not been established. Only "something exists" and "distinction is real" are available. What is matter, if not a pattern of distinctions?

**Q2-B: A property of mind — observers create difference.**
*Death condition: Circular.* Presupposes observers, which have not been established. What distinguishes the observer from the non-observer?

**Q2-C: A distinction IS information — the minimum unit of difference is the bit.**
*Definitional.* Shannon (1948) formalized precisely this: information is the reduction of uncertainty between distinguishable states. One distinction equals one bit. No additional primitives are required. This branch forces Q3.

**Q2-D: A brute, unanalyzable primitive.**
*Collapses.* A "brute distinction" carrying no informational content is indistinguishable from no distinction (violates Q1-B). If it does carry content, Q2-C is restated.

**Q2-E: A mathematical relation.**
*Variant of Q2-C.* Mathematical relations are informational structures. This is Q2-C in different language — not wrong, but not independent.

**Q2-F: A structural relation (Ontic Structural Realism — Ladyman and French, 2003).**
*Collapses.* "Relations all the way down." If relata are eliminated, relations without content reduce to Q2-D (dead). If relata carry content, "structure" equals "pattern of distinctions," reducing to Q2-C. Not an independent branch.

**System divergence:** SYS-B adopts A1.3: "Distinguishability IS information; information is ontologically primitive." SYS-A operates in Q2-A territory — functional but logically circular at this level.

*Worldview survival tracking:* 2 fully accept, 9 partial, 7 reject.

## 5. Level 3 — Order: Type 1 Question

**Q3: Does information require structure, or can it exist as pure randomness?**

Forced by Q2-C. Pure noise equals maximum Shannon entropy, yielding zero usable content.

**Q3-A: Information can be purely random and still ground reality.**
*Death condition: Empirical Contradiction.* Random information carries no structure. A reality grounded in pure noise has no laws, no regularities, no physics. This contradicts observed reality.

**Q3-B: Information must have structure — pattern, regularity, compression.**
*Both systems agree.* Reality has structure. The question is what explains it. This branch forces Q4.

**Q3-C: Structure is observer-imposed, not inherent.**
*Deferred.* Presupposes observers. For the information itself, it is random until observed, reducing to Q3-A. The question is kicked forward.

**System convergence:** Both systems agree that order is required. SYS-B holds that coherence is built into the substrate; SYS-A takes structure as given — "the laws just are."

## 6. Level 4 — The Great Fork: Type 3 Question

**Q4: What grounds the structure?**

This is the primary divergence point. Secular physics and theophysics take different branches. Both are logically valid, but one terminates and one continues.

**Q4-A: Nothing — the substrate is a brute fact.**
*Explanatory Terminal.* This is where secular physics resides. "The laws are what they are." Logically coherent. Epistemically closed. No further questions are answerable. This is not a contradiction but a boundary.

**Q4-B: Another structure grounds it — turtles all the way down.**
*Death condition: Infinite Regress.* Every "ground" requires a deeper ground. No explanation is ever complete; no foundation is ever reached.

**Q4-C: The substrate grounds itself — self-consistent and self-referencing.**
*Self-grounding.* The "why" and the "what" are identical at the deepest level. The system could not fail to exist — mathematical necessity. This branch forces Q5 (property derivation).

**Q4-D: A multiverse generates all possible substrates — anthropic selection.**
*Death condition: Unfalsifiable + Regress.* (a) No observable test distinguishes this from Q4-A. (b) What grounds the multiverse mechanism? The question is pushed back. (c) Violates parsimony.

**Q4-E: The question is illegitimate — "why" has no answer.**
*Collapses to Q4-A.* Saying "no answer exists" is accepting brute fact. Same position, philosophical hat.

**System divergence:** SYS-B adopts A2.2: self-grounding substrate, leading to D2.1 (Logos Field χ(x,t)). SYS-A adopts Q4-A: an honest stopping point. Physics describes; it does not explain ground.

*Worldview survival tracking:* 5 accept self-grounding, 6 partial, 7 at brute fact terminal.

## 7. Level 5 — Properties of Ground: Type 4 Question

**Q5: What are the necessary properties of a self-grounding substrate?**

This is not branching but deduction. Self-consistency constrains what the ground can be. These are logical constraints, not theological claims.

### Derived Properties

**Self-consistent:** A self-contradictory ground destroys itself (A2.2).

**Contains its own explanation:** Incomplete self-reference cannot close the loop, falling to Q4-B.

**Non-material:** A specific material configuration is contingent, not necessary (G1.3).

**Non-temporal:** If the ground depends on time, time requires grounding, leading to regress (G1.2).

**Maximally coherent:** Incoherent self-grounding fragments and destroys itself (A3.1, D3.1).

**Non-deceptive:** Deception — maintaining P and ¬P toward subsystems — constitutes internal incoherence, which is coherence failure. This follows from maximal coherence, not self-consistency alone (G2.4).

**Generative:** Must produce the world we observe. An inert ground explains nothing (D3.3, E3.1).

**System divergence:** SYS-B properties match — universal, eternal, immaterial, coherent, non-deceptive, generative. SYS-A cannot derive these, having stopped at Q4-A.

## 8. Level 6 — Observer Requirement: Type 1 Question

**Q6: Does the ground require actualization — does information become actual on its own?**

This addresses the measurement problem: potential versus actual. Superposition persists until observation.

**Q6-A: Information self-actualizes — no observer needed.**
*Problematic.* Contradicts quantum mechanics. If information self-actualizes, why does superposition exist at all? The measurement problem is precisely the question of how potential becomes actual.

**Q6-B: Information requires observation to become actual.**
*Participatory universe.* Wheeler's delayed-choice experiments, the Born rule. Potential requires actualization; actualization requires an observer. This branch forces Q7.

**Q6-C: Decoherence handles it — spontaneous collapse.**
*Incomplete.* Explains the appearance of collapse, not the thing itself. Wigner's friend: where does the observer chain end?

**Q6-D: No potential/actual distinction — everything is always actual.**
*Death condition: Empirical Contradiction.* Superposition is experimentally verified. Interference patterns require it.

**System divergence:** SYS-B adopts A5.1: observer required for actualization; A5.2: observers participate. SYS-A is split — Many-Worlds (Q6-A), Copenhagen (Q6-B), Decoherence (Q6-C). No consensus.

### Four Modes Activate

At this level, the first finite agent enters the tree:

**M1: Agentic** — Selective attention; the observer chooses what to attend to.

**M2: Entropic** — Finite bandwidth; the observer cannot observe everything; structural blind spots exist.

**M3: Adversarial** — Manipulable perception; adversarial agents can distort inputs.

**M4: Grace Attenuation** — Clarity depends on coupling to the coherence field. Low coupling yields distortion.

**Q7-D: The chain dissolves — consciousness has no bottom level.**
*Incomplete.* Functionalism resides here. It does not resolve measurement but declares it irrelevant. Superposition remains unexplained.

At Level 4, the frameworks diverge: secular physics adopts the brute fact terminal, while theophysics pursues self-grounding substrate logic. The latter path yields eight necessary boundary conditions whose simultaneous satisfaction identifies a terminal observer with properties traditionally attributed to the divine. A comparative evaluation of major world religious systems against these boundary conditions indicates that Christianity achieves the highest correspondence score (8/8). Both frameworks retain unresolved questions, which are enumerated in the interest of intellectual honesty. The argument does not claim deductive proof but rather abductive inference: the theophysical framework provides explanatory closure where secular physics acknowledges explanatory limits.
