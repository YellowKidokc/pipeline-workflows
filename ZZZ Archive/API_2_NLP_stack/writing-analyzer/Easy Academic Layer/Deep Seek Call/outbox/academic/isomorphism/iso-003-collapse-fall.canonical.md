## Wavefunction Collapse ↔ The Fall: A Five-Property Structural Isomorphism Between Quantum Measurement and Genesis 3

### Abstract

This article establishes a formal five-property structural isomorphism between the quantum mechanical phenomenon of wavefunction collapse and the theological event described in Genesis 3:6-7, commonly termed the Fall. Through systematic comparison of defining properties—instantaneity, irreversibility, non-locality, qualitative regime change, and information-freedom trade-off—we demonstrate that both domains exhibit an identical five-parameter architecture. This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of the formal properties of quantum measurement theory with the narrative and theological structure of the Genesis account. The mapping satisfies separability, reducibility, constraint, and bidirectionality tests, distinguishing it from mere analogy or metaphor. We present falsification criteria and predictive consequences for both domains.

**Thesis:** The instantaneous, irreversible, non-local, regime-changing, freedom-destroying event that physics denominates wavefunction collapse maps one-to-one onto what the Genesis narrative denominates the Fall. This constitutes a structural isomorphism—not analogy, not metaphor—governed by five shared properties.

## 3. Domain Specifications

### 3.1 Domain A — Physics: Wavefunction Collapse

Wavefunction collapse denotes the instantaneous, irreversible transition from superposition to eigenstate upon measurement. The system evolves unitarily under the Schrödinger equation:

\[
i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\Psi(t)\rangle = \hat{H}|\Psi(t)\rangle
\]

until a measurement interaction occurs, at which point the wavefunction projects onto a single eigenstate \(|\phi_i\rangle\) with probability \(|\langle\phi_i|\Psi\rangle|^2\) (Born Rule). Key properties:

1. **Instantaneous:** No gradual transition; the projection is temporally unextended.
2. **Irreversible:** The original superposition cannot be recovered; the measurement record is permanent.
3. **Non-local:** Entangled subsystems collapse simultaneously, as confirmed by Bell test experiments (Aspect et al., 1982).
4. **Qualitative regime change:** Transition from unitary Schrödinger evolution (deterministic, linear) to Born Rule probabilities (stochastic, non-linear projection).
5. **Information-freedom trade-off:** A definite eigenvalue is obtained (information gain), but the superposition's degrees of freedom are destroyed (freedom loss).

### 3.2 Domain B — Theology: The Fall (Genesis 3:6-7)

The Fall, as narrated in Genesis 3:6-7, constitutes the collapse event of the moral universe. The text reads: "When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom, she took some and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it. Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they realized they were naked" (NIV). Key properties:

1. **Instantaneous:** The Hebrew verb וַיִּפָּקְחוּ (*vayyippāqəḥû*) marks a discrete state transition—a punctiliar event, not a gradual process.
2. **Irreversible:** Genesis 3:24—cherubim with a flaming sword bar return to Eden; the prior state is inaccessible.
3. **Non-local:** "The eyes of **both** were opened"—simultaneous experience via federal headship (cf. Romans 5:12: "sin entered the world through one man").
4. **Qualitative regime change:** Transition from direct communion with God to mediated access; from life trajectory to death trajectory; from toil-free existence to labor, pain, and mortality (Genesis 3:16-19).
5. **Knowledge-freedom trade-off:** "They knew that they were naked"—knowledge of good and evil is gained, but innocence (the moral superposition) is destroyed.
