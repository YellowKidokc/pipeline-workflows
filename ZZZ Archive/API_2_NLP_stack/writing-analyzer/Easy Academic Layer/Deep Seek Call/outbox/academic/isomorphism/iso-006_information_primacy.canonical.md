# ISOMORPHISM RECORD ISO-006: INFORMATION PRIMACY

## Abstract

This record documents a structural isomorphism between the information-theoretic ontology proposed in contemporary physics—particularly John Archibald Wheeler's "It from Bit" thesis—and the Johannine Prologue's doctrine of the Logos (λόγος) as the ontologically prior, generative principle of all material reality. The mapping identifies four shared structural features: (1) ontological priority of the informational substrate over material instantiation, (2) generative function of information rather than merely descriptive capacity, (3) irreducibility of the foundational substrate, and (4) physical consequence of informational operations. Significant strain is acknowledged at the personal-impersonal boundary, as Shannon information and Wheeler's bit lack the conscious, intentional, and personal attributes ascribed to the Logos in Christian theology. The isomorphism is classified as structural with medium confidence, passing three of four formal tests (symmetry breaking, connection density, and falsifiability invitation) while receiving a partial pass on prediction constraint due to asymmetric testability across domains.

## 1. Domain Specification

### 1.1 Domain A: Physics and Information Theory

The proposition under examination is that information constitutes the ontologically primitive substrate from which physical reality derives. This position, most prominently articulated by Wheeler (1990) as the "It from Bit" thesis, holds that every physical quantity—every particle, every field, every spacetime event—derives its meaning and existence from binary yes/no distinctions, i.e., bits of information. Matter and energy are understood as derivative phenomena; distinguishability, which is information, is fundamental.

Three supporting pillars warrant explicit articulation:

**Landauer's Principle (Landauer, 1961):** The erasure of one bit of information dissipates at minimum \( kT \ln(2) \) joules of energy, where \( k \) is Boltzmann's constant (\( 1.380649 \times 10^{-23} \, \text{J} \cdot \text{K}^{-1} \)) and \( T \) is the absolute temperature of the thermal reservoir in kelvins. This establishes that information has irreducible thermodynamic weight; it is not epiphenomenal but carries physical consequence. Experimental confirmation has been provided by Bérut et al. (2012), who demonstrated the Landauer bound in a colloidal particle system.

**Zeilinger's Foundational Principle (Zeilinger, 1999):** Each elementary system (qubit) carries exactly one bit of information. The information content is the system; there is no substrate beneath the information. This principle has been proposed as a foundational axiom from which the formalism of quantum mechanics can be derived.

**Quantum Information-Theoretic Ontology:** On the Everettian or information-theoretic reading of quantum mechanics, the quantum state \( |\psi\rangle \) constitutes the most complete description of a system. There exists no hidden-variable substrate beneath the wavefunction; the wavefunction itself is the ontology. The quantum state is information, and information is the state.

Formally, let \( \mathcal{R} \) denote physical reality and \( \mathcal{I} \) denote the informational substrate. The relationship is expressed as:

\[
\mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I})
\]

where \( f \) is a generative function mapping informational states to physical states, and \( \mathcal{I} \) is irreducible (cannot be decomposed into non-informational constituents) while \( \mathcal{R} \) is derivative.

### 1.2 Domain B: Christian Theology—Johannine Prologue and Creation Theology

The Johannine Prologue (John 1:1-5, 14) presents the Logos (λόγος) as the rational, structuring principle that precedes and generates all material reality. The critical passage reads:

> Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος. οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν θεόν. πάντα δι᾿ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο, καὶ χωρὶς αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο οὐδὲ ἕν ὃ γέγονεν. (John 1:1-3, NA28)

> "In the beginning was the Word (Logos), and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." (John 1:1-3, ESV)

This theological claim is reinforced by the Genesis creation narrative, in which creation occurs through divine speech acts: "And God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light" (Genesis 1:3, ESV). The speech act—an information transmission event—precedes and causes the physical result.

The author of Hebrews provides an explicit statement of the ontological priority of the informational cause:

> Πίστει νοοῦμεν κατηρτίσθαι τοὺς αἰῶνας ῥήματι θεοῦ, εἰς τὸ μὴ ἐκ φαινομένων τὸ βλεπόμενον γεγονέναι. (Hebrews 11:3, NA28)

> "By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible." (Hebrews 11:3, ESV)

The visible, material reality derives from an invisible, informational/verbal cause. The formal parallel may be expressed as:

\[
\mathcal{M} = g(\mathcal{L})
\]

where \( \mathcal{M} \) denotes material reality, \( \mathcal{L} \) denotes the Logos, and \( g \) is the generative function of divine creative agency. \( \mathcal{L} \) is ontologically prior, and \( \mathcal{M} \) is derivative.

## 2. Structural Mapping

The isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of the ontological commitments in each domain. Four shared structural features emerge:

### 2.1 Ontological Priority of Information over Matter

In both domains, the informational/verbal substrate is first, and matter/energy is derived. The ordering is not: matter exists, then information describes it. The ordering is: information exists, then matter instantiates from it. In Wheeler's framework, the bit generates the "it." In the Johannine framework, the Logos generates all things. Both domains reject the materialist ordering in favor of an informational-first ontology.

### 2.2 Generative Function

Information in Wheeler's framework does not merely describe reality; it generates reality. The bit is productive, not merely descriptive. Similarly, the divine speech act in Genesis and the Logos in John are productive: "Let there be light" does not describe light but brings light into existence. Both domains posit a generative function from informational substrate to physical instantiation.

### 2.3 Irreducibility

In both domains, the informational substrate cannot be reduced further. Within Wheeler's framework, one cannot meaningfully ask "what is the bit made of?"—the bit is the terminus of analysis. Within the Johannine framework, one cannot ask "what preceded the Logos?"—the Logos is eternal ("In the beginning was the Logos," not "In the beginning, the Logos came into being"). Both domains treat the informational substrate as bedrock.

### 2.4 Physical Cost of Information

Landauer's principle demonstrates that information operations have real physical consequences: erasing one bit dissipates \( kT \ln(2) \) joules. Biblical speech acts similarly have real physical consequences: "Let there be light" results in the existence of light. In neither domain is information "cheap talk" without physical import.

## 3. Formal Test Protocol

### 3.1 Test 1: Prediction Constraint

The mapping constrains predictions in both domains, though asymmetrically.

**Domain A constraints:** If information is ontologically primitive, then:
- No physical process can destroy information (unitarity). This is testable and constitutes the content of the Black Hole Information Paradox. Recent theoretical developments—including Hawking's (2004) concession, Page curve calculations (Page, 1993), and replica wormhole results (Almheiri et al., 2020)—support information conservation.
- Every physical law must be expressible as an information-processing operation, constraining the space of possible physical theories.

**Domain B constraints:** If the Logos generates all reality, then:
- No created thing exists independently of the Logos: "Without him was not any thing made that was made" (John 1:3). This is a total-scope claim admitting no exceptions.
- Destruction of the Logos would entail destruction of all reality, consistent with Colossians 1:17: "in him all things hold together."

**Verdict: Partial pass.** The physics constraints are quantitative and experimentally testable (unitarity, Landauer bound). The theological constraints are qualitative and not experimentally testable in the same sense.

### 3.2 Test 2: Symmetry Breaking

Can the ordering of the mapping be reversed? In Wheeler's framework, matter cannot be ontologically prior to information—such a reversal would collapse the framework into classical materialism. In the Johannine framework, the Logos cannot be derivative—such a claim would constitute the Arian heresy, explicitly contradicted by "the Logos was God" (John 1:1).

**Verdict: Passed.** The ordering is non-arbitrary in both domains.

### 3.3 Test 3: Connection Density

This isomorphism connects to at least four other records within the framework:
- ISO-001 (Trinity): The Logos is the second person of the Trinity. Information as structure maps to the Son/Logos as the structural principle through which all things are made.
- ISO-003 (Entropy/Sin): Information degradation in closed systems parallels moral degradation.
- ISO-011 (Superposition/Collapse): Wavefunction as information state, measurement as information extraction.
- ISO-012 (Sign Operator): The sign operator acts on an informational substrate.

**Verdict: Passed.** Connection count: 4+ direct connections.

### 3.4 Test 4: Falsifiability Invitation

The mapping would be broken by any of the following:
1. Demonstration that "It from Bit" is incorrect—that information is emergent from a sub-informational physical substrate (hidden variables, superdeterminism, or a future theory deriving quantum information from something more fundamental).
2. Demonstration that John's Logos is not about information primacy but purely about divine personhood with no cosmological generative claim. This is difficult to sustain given John 1:3 ("all things were made through him"), though a purely existentialist reading of the Prologue would weaken Domain B.
3. Discovery of a physical system whose behavior cannot be described informationally—a real physical process carrying zero information content.

**Verdict: Passed.** Falsifiability conditions are specified.

## 4. Strain Analysis

### 4.1 The Core Objection

The most serious objection to this isomorphism concerns the semantic divergence between "Logos" (λόγος) in the Johannine Prologue and "information" (bit) in Wheeler's framework. These terms denote categorically different concepts.

**Logos (λόγος)** in Hellenistic philosophy carries a constellation of meanings: rational principle, cosmic order, the intelligible structure of reality, speech, proportion, reason. In Philo of Alexandria (c. 20 BCE–50 CE), the Logos functions as the mediating principle between the transcendent God and the material world. In Stoic philosophy, the Logos is the active rational principle pervading the cosmos. John appropriates this loaded term and identifies it with a specific person, Jesus Christ. The Logos is therefore personal, conscious, intentional, and divine.

**Information** in Shannon's (1948) sense means reduction of uncertainty, measured in bits, defined by the probability distribution over a message space. Shannon information is impersonal, unconscious, unintentional, and mathematical. Wheeler's "bit" inherits these properties.

These are not the same concept. The mapping holds at the structural level (both are ontologically prior to matter, both generate physical reality) but breaks at the personal level: the Logos is a *who*; information is a *what*.

### 4.2 Honest Assessment

This isomorphism is structural, not semantic. The terms "Logos" and "information" do not denote the same thing. What they share is a structural role: ontologically prior substrate from which physical reality derives. The mapping is real at that level but should not be pushed to identity. The Logos includes information but is not reducible to it. Information may be a shadow or aspect of the Logos but is not the whole.

**Where the isomorphism holds:** The claim that reality is information-first (not matter-first) maps cleanly onto the claim that reality is Logos-first (not matter-first). The generative direction, the irreducibility, and the ontological priority all match.

**Where the isomorphism strains:** When one asks "is information conscious?" physics answers negatively (or remains silent). When one asks "is the Logos impersonal?" theology answers absolutely not. The personal dimension of the Logos has no counterpart in Shannon information or Wheeler's bit. This constitutes a significant gap.

### 4.3 Possible Resolution within the Framework

The Theophysics framework posits that consciousness and personhood are themselves informational phenomena, following the integrated information theory (IIT) of Tononi (2004). If consciousness is sufficiently integrated information (quantified by the measure Φ), then the gap narrows: the Logos would correspond to maximally integrated information (infinite Φ), while impersonal information corresponds to Logos at low integration (low Φ). This resolution is speculative but structurally consistent with the framework's axioms.
