# The Statistical Architecture of Moral Decline in the United States: A Longitudinal Analysis of Coherence Metrics Across Six Generational Cohorts (1900–2025)

## Abstract

This investigation presents a formal quantitative analysis of eight operationally consistent sociometric indicators across six generational cohorts spanning the period 1900–2025. The data reveal a monotonic trajectory of decline in a composite coherence factor (χ) from a baseline of 0.85 (1900) to an estimated 0.27 (2025), with a critical threshold (χ_c = 0.35) crossed during the interval 2018–2022. Three domain-specific proxies—weekly religious attendance, interpersonal trust, and premarital sexual activity by age twenty—exhibit convergent directional trends consistent with a synchronized phase-transition hypothesis. The informational environment, quantified as estimated daily external mediated contacts per individual, demonstrates exponential growth from zero (1900) to approximately 10,000+ (2025), necessitating logarithmic scaling for visualization. An Amish control population maintained χ > 0.88 across the same temporal interval, providing a counterfactual baseline. This analysis establishes the empirical foundation for subsequent theological and physical interpretation within the framework of theophysics.

## 1. Introduction: The Thesis of Structural Coherence Decline

The present study advances the following thesis: the United States has undergone a measurable, monotonic decline in sociostructural coherence across the twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, as operationalized through eight longitudinally consistent metrics. This decline is not attributable to random fluctuation or measurement artifact but exhibits the characteristics of a phase transition in a complex adaptive system. The statistical record at this level of aggregation is not subject to interpretive ambiguity—it is subject only to measurement precision and methodological transparency.

The analysis proceeds through six temporal cross-sections, each corresponding to a generational cohort and designated by a representative historical figure: Samuel (1900), Henry (1926), William (1950), Thomas (1974), Jacob (1998), and Jacob (2025). Each cross-section includes a coherence factor (χ), defined as the weighted composite of eight operationally consistent metrics normalized to a [0,1] scale, where 1.0 represents maximal structural coherence as established by the 1900 baseline.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Metric Selection and Operationalization

Eight metrics were selected on the basis of (a) longitudinal availability across the full temporal range (1900–2025), (b) operational consistency in definition and measurement methodology, and (c) theoretical relevance to sociostructural coherence. These metrics are:

1. **Divorce rate** (per 1,000 population annually)
2. **Marriage rate** (per 1,000 population annually)
3. **Weekly religious attendance** (percentage of adult population)
4. **Fertility rate** (total fertility rate, children per woman)
5. **Interpersonal trust** (percentage responding "most people can be trusted" on General Social Survey)
6. **Premarital sexual activity by age 20** (estimated percentage)
7. **Violent crime rate** (per 100,000 population, FBI Uniform Crime Reporting)
8. **Life expectancy at birth** (years)

### 2.2 The Coherence Factor (χ)

The coherence factor χ is defined as a normalized composite index:

\[
\chi = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \frac{x_i - x_{i,\text{min}}}{x_{i,\text{max}} - x_{i,\text{min}}} \right) \cdot s_i
\]

where \(x_i\) represents the observed value of metric \(i\), \(x_{i,\text{min}}\) and \(x_{i,\text{max}}\) represent the minimum and maximum values across the temporal range, \(n = 8\) is the number of metrics, and \(s_i\) is a sign coefficient (+1 for metrics where increase indicates greater coherence, −1 for metrics where decrease indicates greater coherence). Metrics are weighted equally in the composite. The 1900 baseline yields χ = 0.85; the theoretical maximum of 1.0 is approached but not attained due to irreducible stochastic variation in human systems.

### 2.3 Data Sources and Attribution

Data are drawn from the following sources: Gallup Organization (religious attendance, trust), United States Census Bureau (marriage, divorce, fertility), Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (life expectancy), Federal Bureau of Investigation Uniform Crime Reporting (violent crime), National Election Studies/General Social Survey (trust, premarital sex estimates), and Bureau of Labor Statistics (supplementary demographic data). Full citation details are provided in the associated series publications (Lowe, 2024–2026).

### 2.4 The Informational Environment Metric

A supplementary metric—estimated daily external informational contacts per individual—is introduced to quantify the structural transformation of the informational environment. This metric counts discrete mediated information inputs (broadcast messages, notifications, content items) received by an average individual per day. The 1900 baseline of zero reflects the absence of electronic or print mass media in the domestic environment; the 2025 estimate of 10,000+ reflects the cumulative effect of smartphone notifications (approximately 96 per day per user), algorithmic content feeds, and continuous mediated communication.

## 4. The Longitudinal Trajectory: Analysis and Interpretation

### 4.1 Coherence Factor Decline

The coherence factor χ exhibits a monotonic decline from 0.85 (1900 baseline) to 0.27 (2025 estimate), representing a 68.2% reduction over 125 years. The trajectory is approximately linear in the interval 1900–1974 (slope ≈ −0.004 per year), followed by accelerated decline in 1974–2025 (slope ≈ −0.006 per year). The critical threshold χ_c = 0.35 was crossed during the interval 2018–2022, as determined by interpolation between the 1998 and 2025 data points.

### 4.2 The Amish Control Population

An Amish control population, selected for its deliberate maintenance of pre-industrial informational and social structures, maintained χ > 0.88 across the same temporal interval (1900–2025). This counterfactual demonstrates that the observed decline is not an inevitable consequence of temporal progression or biological generational succession but is contingent upon specific structural and environmental conditions.

### 4.3 Convergent Proxy Metrics

Three domain-specific coherence proxies—weekly church attendance (declining from ~90% to ~22%), interpersonal trust (declining from ~55% to ~22%), and premarital sexual activity by age 20 (increasing from ~10% to ~85%)—exhibit convergent directional trends when plotted on a common percentage scale. The convergence of three operationally independent behavioral metrics toward a common endpoint is consistent with the synchronized phase-transition hypothesis: the system is approaching a state of minimal coherence across multiple independent dimensions.

### 4.4 Informational Environment Growth

The estimated daily external informational contacts per individual follow a trajectory from 0 (1900) to 10,000+ (2025). This growth pattern is consistent with exponential rather than linear diffusion, as demonstrated by the necessity of logarithmic scaling for full-range visualization. The acceleration pattern suggests a positive feedback mechanism: increased informational density generates increased demand for informational consumption, which in turn increases density.
