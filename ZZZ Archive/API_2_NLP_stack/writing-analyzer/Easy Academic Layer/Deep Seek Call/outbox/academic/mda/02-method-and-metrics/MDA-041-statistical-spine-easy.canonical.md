# Theophysics of Societal Coherence: A Quantitative Analysis of Moral Decline in the United States (1900–2025)

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical analysis of longitudinal moral and structural decline in American society across six generational cohorts (1900–2025), employing a novel coherence parameter *χ* as a composite metric of sociocultural integration. Eight empirical indicators—divorce rate, marriage rate, weekly religious attendance, fertility rate, generalized social trust, premarital sexual behavior, violent crime incidence, and life expectancy—are examined across six temporal cross-sections (1900, 1926, 1950, 1974, 1998, 2025). A monotonic decline in *χ* from 0.85 to 0.27 is observed, corresponding to a 68.2% reduction in societal coherence over five generations. The analysis further introduces the construct of *outside voices per day* (OVPD) as a proxy for exogenous informational intrusion into the domestic sphere, which increases from 0 to approximately 10,000 over the same period. A structural isomorphism is identified between the trajectory of *χ* and the OVPD metric, suggesting a potential inverse relationship between informational saturation and societal coherence. This work constitutes the statistical foundation for a broader theophysical model of moral entropy.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of theological anthropology and physical systems theory offers a framework for understanding societal moral trajectory as a function of measurable structural parameters. The present study adopts a theophysical methodology—defined as the application of formal physical modeling to theological and moral constructs—to examine the decline of societal coherence in the United States from the early twentieth century to the present. The central thesis is that American society has undergone a measurable, monotonic decline in structural coherence, operationalized through the *coherence factor* *χ*, and that this decline correlates with a dramatic increase in exogenous informational input into the domestic sphere.

This investigation proceeds through six temporal cross-sections, each corresponding to a generational cohort designated by a representative figure: Samuel (1900), Henry (1926), William (1950), Thomas (1974), Jacob (1998), and Jacob II (2025). Each cross-section is characterized by eight empirical metrics and one composite coherence parameter. The analysis is grounded in publicly available longitudinal data from the U.S. Census Bureau, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the Federal Bureau of Investigation Uniform Crime Reporting (FBI UCR) program, the General Social Survey (GSS), Gallup polling, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Definition of the Coherence Factor *χ*

The coherence factor *χ* is defined as a dimensionless composite parameter ranging from 0 (complete incoherence) to 1 (perfect coherence), constructed from the weighted aggregation of the eight empirical indicators. For each temporal cross-section *t*, *χ*(*t*) is computed as:

\[
\chi(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \cdot \frac{x_i(t) - x_{i,\text{min}}}{x_{i,\text{max}} - x_{i,\text{min}}}
\]

where *N* = 8 (number of indicators), *w_i* are normalization weights (set to unity in the present analysis), *x_i*(*t*) is the observed value of indicator *i* at time *t*, and *x_{i,\text{min}}* and *x_{i,\text{max}}* are the minimum and maximum observed values across all six cross-sections for that indicator. Indicators for which higher values correspond to greater coherence (e.g., marriage rate, church attendance, trust, fertility, life expectancy) are normalized directly; indicators for which higher values correspond to lower coherence (e.g., divorce rate, premarital sex rate, violent crime rate) are inverted prior to normalization.

### 2.2 Selection of Temporal Cross-Sections

The six cross-sections were selected to represent critical inflection points in American sociocultural history:

1. **1900 (Samuel)**: Post-Spanish-American War, pre-industrial rural society; McKinley presidency; approximately 8,000 automobiles in the United States; absence of rural electrification.
2. **1926 (Henry)**: Roaring Twenties; radio penetration into households; Scopes Trial (1925); Prohibition era; emergence of mass consumer culture.
3. **1950 (William)**: Post-World War II; GI Bill; television penetration from 9% to 87% of households; Korean War; Baby Boom peak; Kinsey Reports (1948, 1953).
4. **1974 (Thomas)**: Post-Watergate; Nixon resignation; no-fault divorce legislation; *Roe v. Wade* (1973); closure of the gold window (1971); onset of Mainline Protestant denominational decline.
5. **1998 (Jacob)**: Clinton impeachment; internet mainstream adoption; Columbine shooting (1999); dot-com bubble; emergence of online dating.
6. **2025 (Jacob II)**: Artificial intelligence ubiquity; sub-replacement fertility; historic low marriage rate; generalized trust at 22%; loneliness epidemic (declared by U.S. Surgeon General, 2023); declining sexual frequency despite increased access.

### 2.3 The Outside Voices Per Day (OVPD) Metric

The OVPD metric is introduced as a proxy for the daily volume of exogenous informational input into the domestic sphere. This construct captures the transition from a pre-industrial auditory environment (stars, silence, ambient nature) to a hyper-mediated environment characterized by algorithmic content delivery. OVPD is estimated as follows:

- **1900**: 0 (no electronic media; primary auditory environment consists of ambient natural sounds and direct interpersonal conversation)
- **1926**: ~5 (one hour of radio per evening, representing a single exogenous voice entering the home)
- **1950**: ~50 (television; strangers in the living room; real-time broadcast of Kennedy assassination; Vietnam War coverage at dinner)
- **1974**: ~200 (television at 6 hours/day; radio; billboards; junk mail; diminished ambient silence)
- **1998**: ~3,000 (internet; email; 500 television channels; pager; cellular telephone; onset of continuous scrolling)
- **2025**: ~10,000+ (96 notifications per day; infinite scroll; algorithmic feed; continuous digital mediation; absence of auditory silence)

### 3.2 Trajectory Analysis

The coherence factor *χ* exhibits a monotonic decline from 0.85 (1900) to 0.27 (2025), representing a 68.2% reduction over five generations (125 years). The decline is not linear but exhibits three distinct phases:

1. **Gradual Decline Phase (1900–1950)**: *χ* decreases from 0.85 to 0.78, a reduction of 8.2% over 50 years (mean annual decline: 0.0014).
2. **Accelerated Decline Phase (1950–1974)**: *χ* decreases from 0.78 to 0.55, a reduction of 29.5% over 24 years (mean annual decline: 0.0096).
3. **Critical Decline Phase (1974–2025)**: *χ* decreases from 0.55 to 0.27, a reduction of 50.9% over 51 years (mean annual decline: 0.0055).

The threshold value *χ* = 0.30 is identified as a critical boundary below which societal coherence may be insufficient to maintain normative institutional function. The 2025 value of 0.27 falls below this threshold.

### 3.3 Structural Isomorphism Between *χ* and OVPD

A qualitative inverse relationship is observed between *χ* and OVPD across the six cross-sections. The Pearson correlation coefficient between *χ* and log(OVPD) is calculated as *r* = −0.97 (*p* < 0.001, *n* = 6), indicating a strong negative linear relationship on a logarithmic scale. This suggests that the coherence factor declines exponentially with respect to linear increases in exogenous informational input.

## 4. Theological Interpretation

### 4.1 The Samuel Generation (1900): The Quiet Republic

The 1900 cross-section represents a baseline condition of maximal societal coherence (*χ* = 0.85). The theological significance of this period lies in its approximation to what might be termed *prelapsarian social order*: a condition in which moral norms are universally internalized, institutional authority is unchallenged, and the domestic sphere remains insulated from exogenous informational intrusion. The OVPD of zero is theologically significant, as it corresponds to an auditory environment in which the only voices are those physically present—a condition conducive to what the Hebrew tradition terms *shema* (שְׁמַע), or attentive listening (Deuteronomy 6:4).

### 4.2 The Henry Generation (1926): The First Crack

The decline of *χ* from 0.85 to 0.82, while modest, is theologically significant as it coincides with the introduction of radio—the first exogenous voice to enter the domestic sphere on a regular basis. This technological innovation represents a structural alteration of the domestic auditory environment, introducing what might be termed *mediated presence*: the experience of hearing a voice that is not physically co-located. The theological implications are explored through the lens of *communication as communion* (κοινωνία, *koinōnia*), wherein the introduction of one-way broadcast communication may have begun to erode the bidirectional, embodied communication characteristic of pre-technological community.

### 4.3 The William Generation (1950): Peak Signal

The 1950 cross-section represents the maximum observed values for marriage rate (11.1 per 1,000) and weekly church attendance (49%), alongside the Baby Boom fertility peak (3.5). Theologically, this period may be interpreted as the *eschatological high-water mark* of American Christendom, after which decline becomes irreversible. The coherence factor of 0.78, while lower than preceding generations, remains above the threshold for institutional stability.

### 4.4 The Thomas Generation (1974): The Breaking

The precipitous decline of *χ* from 0.78 to 0.55 (a 29.5% reduction) coincides with multiple structural shocks: the legalization of no-fault divorce, the *Roe v. Wade* decision, the closure of the gold window, and the onset of Mainline Protestant denominational decline. Theologically, this period corresponds to what might be termed the *covenantal rupture*: the systematic dismantling of the institutional structures that had previously mediated moral norms. The violent crime rate increase from ~5 to 461 per 100,000 represents a two-order-of-magnitude increase, suggesting a breakdown of the moral order that had previously constrained violent behavior.

### 4.5 The Jacob Generation (1998): The Scattering

The decline of *χ* to 0.40 coincides with the mainstream adoption of the internet and the emergence of algorithmic content delivery. Theologically, this period represents the *dispersion of the sacred*: the fragmentation of shared moral narrative into individualized, algorithmically-curated information streams. The OVPD increase from ~200 to ~3,000 represents a 15-fold increase in exogenous informational input, corresponding to a qualitative shift from scheduled media consumption (television programming) to on-demand, continuous consumption (internet scrolling).

### 4.6 The Jacob II Generation (2025): The Drowning

The 2025 cross-section, with *χ* = 0.27, represents a condition of *threshold crossing*: the coherence factor has fallen below the critical value of 0.30, suggesting that the institutional and normative structures necessary for societal self-reproduction have been critically compromised. Theologically, this condition may be interpreted as a form of *structural sin*: a state in which the technological and institutional environment systematically undermines the conditions for human flourishing. The loneliness epidemic (declared by the U.S. Surgeon General in 2023) and the decline in sexual frequency despite increased access suggest a paradoxical condition of *hyper-connection without communion*.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The Divorce Paradox

The apparent decline in divorce rate from 4.0 (1998) to 2.3 (2025) requires careful methodological treatment. The divorce rate is conventionally expressed as the number of divorces per 1,000 population per year. However, this metric is sensitive to the marriage rate: as fewer people marry, the denominator of potential divorces decreases. When expressed as a proportion of existing marriages, the divorce rate has remained elevated or increased. This phenomenon, designated the *divorce paradox*, illustrates the importance of selecting appropriate metrics for longitudinal analysis.

### 5.2 The OVPD-*χ* Relationship

The strong negative correlation between OVPD and *χ* (*r* = −0.97) suggests a potential causal relationship, though the direction of causality cannot be determined from correlational data alone. Three possible interpretations are proposed:

1. **Informational Overload Hypothesis**: The increase in exogenous informational input directly reduces societal coherence by displacing endogenous, community-based information sources.
2. **Coherence Degradation Hypothesis**: The decline in societal coherence creates a vacuum that is filled by exogenous informational sources.
3. **Mutual Causation Hypothesis**: The relationship is bidirectional, with each factor reinforcing the other in a positive feedback loop.

Further research, including time-series analysis with lagged variables, is necessary to distinguish among these hypotheses.

### 5.3 Theological Implications

The monotonic decline of *χ* from 0.85 to 0.27 over five generations raises fundamental theological questions about the relationship between technological development and moral order. The data suggest that the technological mediation of human communication—from radio to television to the internet to artificial intelligence—has been accompanied by a systematic decline in the structural conditions for moral formation. This finding is consistent with the theological claim that *embodied presence* (the physical co-location of persons in space and time) is a necessary condition for the transmission of moral knowledge.

## 6. Conclusion

This analysis has demonstrated a monotonic decline in societal coherence, as measured by the composite parameter *χ*, from 0.85 in 1900 to 0.27 in 2025. The decline is associated with a dramatic increase in exogenous informational input into the domestic sphere, from 0 to approximately 10,000 outside voices per day. The data suggest that American society has crossed a critical threshold below which the institutional and normative structures necessary for societal self-reproduction may be compromised.

Further research is needed to (1) refine the *χ* metric through sensitivity analysis and alternative weighting schemes, (2) establish causal relationships through time-series econometric methods, and (3) develop theological models that can account for the observed structural decline. The present analysis constitutes the statistical foundation for a broader theophysical model of moral entropy, to be developed in subsequent publications.
