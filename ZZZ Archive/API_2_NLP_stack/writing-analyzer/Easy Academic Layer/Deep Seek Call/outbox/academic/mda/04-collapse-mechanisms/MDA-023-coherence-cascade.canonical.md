# The Coherence Cascade: A Four-Domain Collapse Model Across Six Generations (1900–2025)

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized model of social coherence collapse, designated the *Coherence Cascade*, which posits a predictable sequential degradation across four interdependent domains: semantic, familial, institutional, and economic. Through longitudinal analysis spanning 125 years (1900–2025) across six generational cohorts, evidence is adduced for a phase-transitional pattern wherein semantic erosion precedes familial disintegration, which in turn precedes institutional and economic collapse. The model yields quantitative coherence indices for each domain at six temporal checkpoints, revealing a monotonic decline with a critical transition occurring circa 1974. This work constitutes a preliminary formalization within the emerging interdisciplinary framework of theophysics, wherein structural isomorphisms between physical phase transitions and sociocultural entropy are systematically investigated.

## 1. Introduction: Thesis and Methodological Framework

The central hypothesis under examination is that social coherence—defined as the aggregate capacity of a society to maintain shared meaning, relational stability, institutional trust, and economic predictability—collapses in a causally ordered sequence. Specifically, we propose that **semantic coherence fails first**, as the erosion of shared meaning precedes structural collapse; **familial coherence follows**, as primary relational bonds weaken; **institutional coherence declines third**, as trust in mediating structures diminishes; and **economic coherence collapses last**, as material exchange systems lose stability.

This sequence was identified through structural comparison of longitudinal social indicator data across four domains, with temporal markers selected to correspond to generational cohorts: the Samuel generation (1900), Henry generation (1926), William generation (1950), Thomas generation (1974), Jacob I generation (1998), and Jacob II generation (2025). The model employs a normalized coherence index ranging from 0 (complete collapse) to 100 (maximum coherence), derived from composite indicators within each domain.

### 2.2 Temporal Sequence of Domain Collapse

The data reveal a consistent ordering of decline across domains:

**Semantic coherence** declines first and most precipitously: 98 → 90 → 75 → 40 → 25 → 18, representing an 81.6% reduction from baseline.

**Familial coherence** follows with a lagged decline: 95 → 93 → 88 → 50 → 35 → 20, representing a 78.9% reduction.

**Institutional coherence** declines third: 92 → 90 → 90 → 45 → 35 → 18, representing an 80.4% reduction.

**Economic coherence** declines last: 90 → 85 → 92 → 65 → 45 → 25, representing a 72.2% reduction.

Notably, the economic domain exhibits a "false peak" at 1950 (index = 92), exceeding the 1900 baseline, before entering a delayed but sustained decline. This phenomenon is consistent with the hypothesis that economic systems exhibit greater inertia than other domains, temporarily masking underlying coherence degradation.

### 2.3 Phase Transition Identification

The period circa 1974 (Thomas generation) constitutes a critical phase transition, wherein all four domains cross the 50-index threshold simultaneously. This transition is characterized by:

1. A 46.7% decline in semantic coherence from the 1950 value (75 → 40)
2. A 43.2% decline in familial coherence (88 → 50)
3. A 50.0% decline in institutional coherence (90 → 45)
4. A 29.3% decline in economic coherence (92 → 65)

The simultaneous crossing of the 50-index threshold across all domains suggests a systemic phase transition rather than independent domain-specific fluctuations.

## 3. Causal Mechanisms by Domain

### 3.1 Semantic Drift: The Primacy of Linguistic Coherence

The model identifies semantic erosion as the initiating mechanism in the coherence cascade. This proposition is grounded in the observation that shared meaning constitutes the foundational substrate upon which all other forms of social coherence depend. The following causal events are identified:

- **1926 (Henry cohort):** The necessity of explicit definition for terms such as "duty" indicates incipient semantic drift, wherein previously tacit shared meanings require conscious articulation.
- **1950 (William cohort):** The instrumentalization of language for commercial purposes—words becoming "advertising slogans"—represents a functional shift from communicative to persuasive linguistic modes.
- **1974 (Thomas cohort):** The restriction of terms such as "integrity" to accusatory contexts signals a loss of positive semantic content, wherein moral vocabulary becomes available only for negative deployment.
- **1998 (Jacob I cohort):** The redefinition of "friend" as equivalent to "screen name" constitutes a fundamental semantic compression, wherein a term denoting a complex relational structure is reduced to a digital identifier.
- **2025 (Jacob II cohort):** The requirement of "conscious choice" for word usage indicates that semantic default states have been lost, rendering linguistic coherence an effortful rather than automatic process.

### 3.2 Familial Collapse: The Secondary Domain

Familial coherence exhibits a lagged response to semantic erosion, consistent with the hypothesis that relational structures depend upon shared meaning for their maintenance. Key causal events include:

- **1950 (William cohort):** The emergence of "ball and chain" humor as a normalized discourse pattern signals incipient devaluation of marital commitment.
- **1969:** The enactment of no-fault divorce legislation in California (Family Law Act of 1969, effective 1970) represents a structural legal shift that removed institutional barriers to marital dissolution.
- **1974 (Thomas cohort):** The U.S. divorce rate reaching 4.6 per 1,000 population (National Center for Health Statistics) marks a historical peak, representing a 150% increase from the 1960 rate of 2.2 per 1,000.
- **1998 (Jacob I cohort):** The proportion of unmarried individuals aged 30–39 reaching 33% (U.S. Census Bureau) indicates a structural shift in marriage timing and prevalence.
- **2025 (Jacob II cohort):** The unmarried proportion reaching 46% (projected from current trends), with family formation becoming "optional" in normative terms, represents the culmination of a century-long decline in familial coherence.

### 3.3 Institutional Collapse: The Tertiary Domain

Institutional coherence—defined as aggregate trust in mediating structures including government, media, and civic organizations—declines following familial erosion. Key causal events:

- **1958:** Peak institutional trust at 73% (American National Election Studies, University of Michigan), representing the historical maximum of public confidence in governmental institutions.
- **1974:** Post-Watergate trust decline to 36% (ANES data), representing a 50.7% reduction from the 1958 peak.
- **1998 (Jacob I cohort):** The normalization of "they all lie" as a baseline assumption regarding institutional communications indicates the internalization of distrust as a default cognitive stance.
- **2025 (Jacob II cohort):** Institutional trust reaching record lows below 20% (Gallup, Pew Research Center), representing a systemic loss of institutional legitimacy.

### 3.4 Economic Unmooring: The Terminal Domain

Economic coherence—defined as the stability and predictability of material exchange systems—declines last, consistent with its dependence upon the prior three domains. Key causal events:

- **1971:** The Nixon administration's suspension of gold convertibility (the "Nixon Shock," August 15, 1971) terminated the Bretton Woods system, removing the last formal link between currency and commodity value.
- **1974 (Thomas cohort):** U.S. inflation reaching 12.3% (Consumer Price Index, Bureau of Labor Statistics) represents the highest annual inflation rate in the post-war period, reflecting monetary instability.
- **1998 (Jacob I cohort):** The equivalence of two incomes to one 1950 income (adjusted for inflation and purchasing power) indicates a structural decline in labor compensation relative to productivity.
- **2025 (Jacob II cohort):** The predominance of gig economy employment and the absence of defined-benefit pension systems represent the terminal phase of economic coherence, wherein long-term economic planning becomes structurally impossible for a majority of the population.

## 4. Theoretical Implications and theophysics Framework

The Coherence Cascade model exhibits structural isomorphisms with phase transition phenomena in statistical mechanics. Specifically, the sequential collapse across domains resembles a cascade of symmetry-breaking events, wherein the loss of coherence in one domain reduces the stability threshold for subsequent domains. The critical transition circa 1974 corresponds to a bifurcation point, after which the system enters a qualitatively different regime characterized by accelerated decline and reduced resilience.

Within the theophysics framework, this model suggests that social coherence may be understood as an emergent property of information-theoretic systems, wherein semantic meaning functions as a form of structured information that constrains the degrees of freedom available to lower-level social systems. The erosion of this informational structure propagates downward through the hierarchy of social organization, from the symbolic (semantic) through the relational (familial) and structural (institutional) to the material (economic).

## 5. Limitations and Future Directions

The present model is subject to several limitations. First, the coherence indices are composite measures derived from heterogeneous data sources, and the weighting methodology requires further formalization. Second, the causal ordering proposed here is inferred from temporal sequence rather than demonstrated through controlled experimentation; alternative causal structures cannot be ruled out. Third, the model's predictive validity for future cohorts remains to be tested.

Future work should focus on: (1) formalizing the mathematical structure of the cascade model using differential equations analogous to those describing coupled oscillator systems; (2) developing more robust composite indices with explicit uncertainty quantification; (3) testing the model against historical data from non-Western societies to assess cross-cultural validity; and (4) investigating potential intervention points for coherence restoration.
