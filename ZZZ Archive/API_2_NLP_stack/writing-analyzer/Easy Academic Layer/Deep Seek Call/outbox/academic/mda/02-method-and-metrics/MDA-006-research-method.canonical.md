# Theophysics: A Formal Framework for Cross-Domain Analysis of Moral Decline in America (1900–Present)

## Abstract

This article presents a methodological framework for investigating the observed correlation between measurable indicators of moral decline in American society from the early twentieth century to the present day and underlying structural principles that may be isomorphic across physical and theological domains. The investigation proceeds from the premise that certain patterns of systemic degradation—whether in thermodynamic, informational, or moral systems—may exhibit formal structural similarities that warrant interdisciplinary examination. A five-page cognitive architecture is proposed for conducting human-led, AI-assisted research that preserves epistemic rigor while enabling rapid cross-domain mapping. The framework is designed to compress research time without compressing analytical depth, and to surface contradictions as sites of potential insight rather than obstacles to resolution.

## 1. Introduction: The Research Question and Its Epistemic Status

The central research question motivating this investigation is: *Why is there a ubiquitous, measurable change in moral decline in America from the early 1900s to the present day?* This question, as formulated, spans multiple disciplinary domains—sociology, theology, economics, family structure, institutional trust, and philosophy—and cannot be adequately addressed within the methodological boundaries of any single discipline. The question is therefore classified as a *cross-domain inquiry*, requiring a methodological framework capable of holding complexity without collapsing it into disciplinary reductionism.

The researcher acknowledges a personal stake in this question, rooted in the conviction that contemporary economic, social, and financial systems are approaching a state of critical instability. This personal dimension is noted not as a methodological liability but as a *positional acknowledgment*: the researcher's pre-theoretical intuitions are documented prior to engagement with AI-assisted analysis, thereby preserving the epistemic boundary between initial framing and subsequent investigation. This practice aligns with established protocols in reflexive sociology and qualitative research methodology, wherein researcher positionality is rendered explicit rather than suppressed (Bourdieu & Wacquant, 1992).

## 2. Methodological Framework: The Five-Page Cognitive Architecture

The research methodology employed in this investigation is structured around five distinct cognitive functions, each instantiated as a separate document. This architecture is not arbitrary but is designed to enforce specific epistemic operations that are frequently elided in conventional research workflows.

### 2.1 Inquiry Seed: Question and Orientation

The Inquiry Seed document serves to fix the research question in its precise formulation and to capture the researcher's initial intuition prior to any AI-mediated analysis. This step is epistemologically load-bearing: if the AI system is permitted to frame the research question, the researcher cedes authorship of the inquiry's foundational assumptions. The Inquiry Seed thus functions as a *pre-theoretical anchor*, ensuring that subsequent analysis remains tethered to the researcher's original epistemic commitment.

### 2.2 AI Prompt Engine: Mapping Intellectual Terrain

The AI Prompt Engine documents the exact prompts submitted to AI models, the models consulted, and the raw outputs received—without evaluative judgment at this stage. The function of this document is to map the intellectual terrain: competing interpretations, key figures, major fault lines, and suggested search domains. The AI output is treated as *provisional cartography*, not as authoritative conclusion. This distinction is critical: the AI maps the terrain; the researcher traverses it.

### 2.3 Research Workspace: Evidence Accumulation and Verification

The Research Workspace functions as the living document where primary-source evidence accumulates. This includes data links, study citations, statistics with dates and sourcing, and annotations. The workspace is where AI-generated suggestions undergo stress-testing against primary literature. Not every AI claim survives contact with primary sources; the workspace is the site where that collision occurs and is documented.

### 2.4 Contradictions and Resolution: Sense-Making Through Irresolution

The Contradictions and Resolution document is identified as the most epistemologically significant component of the framework. Its function is to force the researcher to sit with irresolution—to identify points where sources disagree, where AI output contradicts primary literature, and where the researcher's intuition conflicts with data. The methodological principle underlying this document is that truth is most frequently located in contradictions that resist clean resolution, rather than in consensus that resolves too easily. This principle finds formal support in dialectical epistemology and in the philosophy of science literature on anomaly-driven theory change (Kuhn, 1962; Lakatos, 1970).

### 2.5 Quotes, Domains, and Links: Cumulative Reference Architecture

The Quotes, Domains, and Links document serves as a shared reference library that accumulates across all inquiries. It contains key quotes with attribution and date, domain categories surfaced during research, and external links with contextual annotations. This document is cumulative and cross-referential, building the raw material from which publications are drawn.

## 3. Epistemic Status of AI in This Framework

The methodological framework distinguishes sharply between the functions for which AI is employed and those for which it is not. This distinction is formalized below.

### 3.1 Permitted AI Functions

AI is used to:
1. Map intellectual terrain quickly across multiple disciplines
2. Surface contradictions between competing interpretations
3. Organize competing frameworks side by side for comparison
4. Suggest where deeper primary-source study is needed
5. Stress-test early conclusions against counterarguments

### 3.2 Prohibited AI Functions

AI is not used to:
1. Decide what is true
2. Replace primary-source verification
3. Frame the initial question before the researcher does
4. Select which evidence counts as significant
5. Resolve contradictions that require human judgment

The rationale for these restrictions is epistemic: AI systems, as currently constituted, lack the capacity for genuine judgment, contextual understanding, and normative evaluation that are required for truth-determination in complex cross-domain inquiries. AI outputs that are incomplete or erroneous are treated not as failures of the method but as *signals*—indicators of genuinely contested intellectual terrain, manufactured consensus, or areas requiring deeper primary-source investigation.

## 4. Theoretical Underpinnings: Theophysics as Cross-Domain Structural Analysis

The term "theophysics" is employed in this framework to denote the systematic investigation of structural isomorphisms between physical and theological domains. This is not a claim of identity between these domains but a methodological hypothesis: that certain formal patterns—particularly those involving systemic degradation, entropy, information loss, and moral decay—may exhibit structural similarities that are accessible to cross-domain analysis.

The hypothesis is grounded in the observation that both physical systems (as described by thermodynamics and information theory) and moral systems (as described by theological and ethical frameworks) exhibit characteristic patterns of degradation when subjected to certain conditions. In thermodynamics, the second law describes the inevitable increase in entropy in isolated systems. In information theory, the Shannon-Hartley theorem describes the relationship between channel capacity, bandwidth, and signal-to-noise ratio, where information loss is a function of noise. In theological ethics, moral decline is often described in terms of increasing distance from a normative standard, with corresponding loss of coherence, integrity, and functional order.

The formal hypothesis under investigation is that these patterns of degradation may be governed by structurally analogous principles—that the *form* of moral decline may exhibit mathematical or logical properties isomorphic to those of thermodynamic or informational degradation. This hypothesis is not asserted as proven but is proposed as a research program requiring systematic investigation.

## 5. Workflow Sequence and Cognitive Architecture

The research workflow proceeds through the following sequential steps:

1. **Inquiry Seed**: Formulate the research question and document initial intuition *before* running any AI prompt.
2. **AI Prompt Engine**: Execute AI prompts; collect outputs without evaluative judgment.
3. **Research Workspace**: Follow AI-generated leads into primary sources; cite everything; record what the data actually says.
4. **Contradictions and Resolution**: Force disagreements into the open; sit with irresolution; do not paper over contradictions.
5. **Quotes, Domains, and Links**: Maintain the cumulative reference library; add to it with each session.

This sequence is not optional and is not decorative. Each step performs a distinct cognitive function that cannot be collapsed into the others. The structure constitutes the cognitive architecture that prevents AI-assisted research from collapsing into AI-generated opinion.

## 6. Conclusion: The Epistemic Imperative

The research presented here exists because the researcher felt the weight of the question and determined that feeling alone was insufficient. The methodological framework described above is designed to enable rigorous investigation of cross-domain questions that resist disciplinary containment. It is a commitment to looking, to going where the data is uncomfortable, and to letting contradictions speak before reaching for resolution.

The aphorism that frames this work—"Truth is rarely found where everyone agrees. It is found where disagreement is sharpest and still survivable"—is not merely decorative. It expresses a methodological principle: that the most productive sites of inquiry are those where competing frameworks collide, where evidence resists easy synthesis, and where the researcher must hold multiple incompatible accounts in productive tension.

This is what this project is. This is what this method is for.
