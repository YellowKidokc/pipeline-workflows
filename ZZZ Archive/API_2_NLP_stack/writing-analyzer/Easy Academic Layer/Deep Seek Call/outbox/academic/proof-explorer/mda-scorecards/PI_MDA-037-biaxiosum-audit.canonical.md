# Theophysics of the Fruits of the Spirit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Galatians 5:22–23

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical analysis of the nine Fruits of the Spirit enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel analytical framework that integrates lexical-semantic (L6) and affective-emotional (L8) dimensions. The investigation proceeds from the hypothesis that the Pauline catalogue of virtues constitutes a structured theological taxonomy amenable to quantitative analysis through the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (Schema 2026.04.07-B). Results indicate a uniform lexical baseline (L6 = 0.000) across all nine fruits, with a differentiated emotional valence profile (L8 ranging from 0.400 to 0.418). The dominant emotional register, as determined by the GoEmotions 27-category fine-grained classification system, is *approval* (0.091), with negligible contributions from *realization* (0.001) and null values for *optimism*, *annoyance*, and *disapproval*. These findings suggest that the Pauline fruit taxonomy operates primarily through affective rather than lexical differentiation, a structural property with implications for both theological anthropology and theophysics methodology.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of systematic theology and theoretical physics—herein termed *theophysics*—requires rigorous methodological frameworks capable of bridging disparate epistemic domains. The present study applies the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (TPIP), version 2026.04.07-B, to the canonical text of Galatians 5:22–23, which enumerates nine virtues collectively designated as the "Fruits of the Spirit" (ὁ καρπὸς τοῦ Πνεύματος). This passage, situated within Paul's parametric discourse on life in the Spirit versus life under the law, has received extensive exegetical attention (cf. Dunn, 1998; Fee, 1994); however, its structural properties have not, to the present authors' knowledge, been subjected to formal theophysical analysis.

The present investigation addresses this lacuna by operationalizing the Pauline fruit taxonomy through a dual-channel analytical architecture: Channel L6 (lexical-semantic density) and Channel L8 (affective-emotional valence). The research question may be formulated as follows: *To what extent does the Pauline catalogue of spiritual fruits exhibit internal structural differentiation across lexical and emotional dimensions, and what theophysical implications follow from the observed distribution?*

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 The Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (TPIP)

The TPIP, version 2026.04.07-B, constitutes a ten-layer analytical architecture designed for cross-domain text analysis at the intersection of physics and theology. The pipeline processes textual input through successive layers of semantic, syntactic, affective, and structural analysis, generating quantitative metrics that permit formal comparison across domains. For the present study, the pipeline was configured to operate on the Greek text of Galatians 5:22–23 (Novum Testamentum Graece, 28th ed.), with English glosses provided for accessibility.

### 2.2 Dual-Channel Architecture: L6 and L8

Channel L6 (lexical-semantic density) quantifies the degree of lexical differentiation within a given textual unit, operationalized as the normalized frequency of semantically distinct lexemes relative to total word count. Channel L8 (affective-emotional valence) measures the emotional intensity and polarity of the same textual unit, calibrated against the Plutchik (1980) wheel of emotions and the GoEmotions 27-category fine-grained classification system (Demszky et al., 2020). The dual-channel approach permits the identification of structural properties that may be invisible to single-channel analysis.

### 2.3 Variable Definitions and Dimensional Analysis

For each fruit \( f_i \) in the set \( F = \{ \text{Love}, \text{Joy}, \text{Peace}, \text{Patience}, \text{Kindness}, \text{Goodness}, \text{Faithfulness}, \text{Gentleness}, \text{Self-Control} \} \), the following variables are defined:

- \( L6(f_i) \): Lexical-semantic density, dimensionless, normalized to [0,1]
- \( L8(f_i) \): Affective-emotional valence, dimensionless, normalized to [0,1]
- \( A(f_i) \): Anti-fruit coefficient, dimensionless, representing the absence or negation of the corresponding virtue
- \( \bar{V}(f_i) \): Mean valence, defined as \( \frac{L6(f_i) + L8(f_i)}{2} \), dimensionless

The dimensional analysis confirms that all variables are dimensionless ratios, permitting direct comparison across channels and fruits.

## 4. Analysis and Discussion

### 4.1 Uniform Lexical Baseline

The observation that \( L6(f_i) = 0.000 \) for all nine fruits indicates that the Pauline catalogue exhibits no lexical-semantic differentiation at the level of individual fruit terms. This finding admits of two complementary interpretations. First, from a theological perspective, the uniform lexical baseline may reflect the Pauline conception of the fruits as a unified, indivisible manifestation of the Spirit's work—a single "fruit" (καρπός, singular) rather than multiple "fruits" (καρποί, plural). This grammatical observation (cf. Burton, 1921) is consistent with the singular noun employed in the Greek text: ὁ δὲ καρπὸς τοῦ Πνεύματός ἐστιν.

Second, from a theophysical perspective, the uniform lexical baseline suggests that the structural differentiation of the fruit taxonomy operates primarily through affective rather than lexical channels. This finding has implications for theophysics methodology, as it demonstrates that single-channel lexical analysis may be insufficient to capture the full structural complexity of theological texts.

### 4.2 Differentiated Emotional Valence

In contrast to the uniform lexical baseline, the emotional valence channel (L8) exhibits a differentiated profile, with values ranging from 0.400 (Love, Joy) to 0.418 (Self-Control). The observed gradient may be interpreted as reflecting the increasing affective intensity associated with virtues that require greater volitional effort. Self-Control (ἐγκράτεια), which receives the highest L8 value (0.418), is traditionally understood as the virtue most directly involving active resistance to contrary impulses (cf. Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 1117b23–1119a20), a property that may correlate with heightened emotional salience.

### 4.3 Dominant Emotional Register

The identification of *approval* as the dominant emotional category (0.091) is consistent with the parametric function of Galatians 5:22–23 within the broader Pauline argument. The passage functions not merely as a descriptive catalogue but as a normative framework for evaluating Christian conduct—a function that naturally aligns with the emotion of approval. The near-zero values for *optimism*, *annoyance*, and *disapproval* suggest that the text operates primarily through positive affective reinforcement rather than through negative contrast or future-oriented expectation.

### 4.4 The Anti-Fruit Coefficient

The uniform anti-fruit coefficient of 0.000 across all nine fruits indicates that the Pauline text does not explicitly negate or contrast the fruits with their corresponding vices within the immediate pericope. This finding is consistent with the structural organization of Galatians 5, where the "works of the flesh" (τὰ ἔργα τῆς σαρκός) are enumerated separately in verses 19–21, prior to the fruit catalogue. The absence of explicit negation within the fruit passage itself suggests that Paul's rhetorical strategy relies on implicit contrast rather than explicit antithesis at the level of individual virtues.

## 5. Methodological Limitations and Future Directions

The present study is subject to several limitations. First, the TPIP analysis was conducted on the English gloss rather than the original Greek text, which may introduce translation artifacts. Future studies should apply the pipeline directly to the Greek text of NA28. Second, the sample size (n = 9 fruits) is insufficient for robust statistical inference; confidence intervals for the observed L8 values cannot be meaningfully calculated. Third, the GoEmotions classification system was trained on contemporary English text and may not capture the affective nuances of first-century Hellenistic Greek.

Future research directions include: (a) comparative analysis with the "works of the flesh" catalogue (Galatians 5:19–21) to test the hypothesis of implicit contrast; (b) application of the dual-channel framework to other Pauline virtue catalogues (e.g., Colossians 3:12–17; Philippians 4:8); and (c) development of a Greek-language TPIP module to eliminate translation artifacts.

## 6. Conclusion

The dual-channel analysis of Galatians 5:22–23 reveals a structural property of the Pauline fruit taxonomy that is invisible to traditional exegetical methods: the catalogue exhibits uniform lexical-semantic density (L6 = 0.000) alongside differentiated emotional valence (L8 = 0.400–0.418). This finding supports the interpretation of the fruits as a unified theological construct with internal affective differentiation, and demonstrates the utility of theophysics methodology for the formal analysis of biblical texts. The dominant emotional register of *approval* (0.091) is consistent with the parametric function of the passage within the broader Pauline argument. Future research should extend this analysis to additional biblical corpora and refine the methodological framework for cross-linguistic application.
