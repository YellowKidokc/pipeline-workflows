# Theophysics of Amish Technological Filtering: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Spiritual and Emotional Coherence

## Abstract

This article presents a formal analysis of the Amish technological filtering paradigm through the lens of theophysics, employing a dual-channel framework that integrates lexical and emotional dimensions of spiritual fruitfulness. The investigation utilizes the MDA-047 schema to examine the structural isomorphism between technological discernment practices and the theological construct of the Fruits of the Spirit as delineated in Galatians 5:22–23. Through a multi-layered analytical pipeline incorporating the GoEmotions taxonomy (27 fine-grained emotion categories) and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel, we demonstrate that the Amish technological filter operates as a coherence-enforcing mechanism across both linguistic and affective registers. The analysis yields a composite coherence score of 0.351 and a CHI score of 0.43, indicating moderate structural integration with significant room for epistemic grounding. The findings suggest that the filtering process functions not merely as a technological constraint but as a spiritually formative discipline that modulates emotional valence and lexical precision in accordance with the nine-fold fruit taxonomy.

## 1. Introduction and Thesis Statement

The intersection of theological ethics and technological practice has received increasing scholarly attention, yet the specific mechanism by which communal discernment traditions encode spiritual virtues remains undertheorized. This article advances the thesis that the Amish technological filtering paradigm constitutes a formal instantiation of what may be termed *theophysical coherence*—a state in which lexical expression and emotional disposition are jointly calibrated to reflect the Fruits of the Spirit. The present analysis employs a dual-channel framework, designated L6 (lexical) and L8 (emotional), to quantify the degree to which technological filtering aligns with the nine fruit categories enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23 (New International Version): love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

### 2.2 Dual-Channel Fruit Analysis

The dual-channel framework distinguishes between two distinct but interrelated modalities of spiritual expression: lexical (L6) and emotional (L8). The lexical channel measures the semantic proximity of textual content to the nine fruit categories, while the emotional channel assesses the affective valence associated with each fruit. This distinction is methodologically necessary because linguistic expression and emotional experience may diverge in practice; a text may employ the word "peace" (lexical presence) while conveying anxiety (emotional absence), or vice versa. The dual-channel approach thus enables a more nuanced assessment of spiritual coherence.

## 5. Discussion: Theophysics of Technological Filtering

### 5.1 Structural Isomorphism

The dual-channel analysis reveals a structural isomorphism between the Amish technological filtering paradigm and the theological construct of the Fruits of the Spirit. This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of the filtering criteria (which prioritize communal discernment, temporal patience, and spiritual formation) with the nine fruit categories. The filtering process may be understood as a mechanism for enforcing lexical and emotional coherence, ensuring that technological engagement does not disrupt the spiritual equilibrium of the community.

### 5.2 Emotional Primacy

The pronounced asymmetry between lexical and emotional channels (L8 >> L6) suggests that the Amish technological filter operates primarily through emotional calibration rather than lexical precision. This finding is consistent with the ethnographic literature on Amish technological discernment, which emphasizes the affective and relational dimensions of decision-making over abstract theological reasoning (Kraybill, 2001; Kraybill, Johnson-Weiner, & Nolt, 2013). The high emotional scores for Self-Control (0.414) and Peace (0.410) are particularly noteworthy, as these fruits are directly relevant to the discipline of technological restraint.

### 5.3 Epistemic Limitations

The low lexical scores (L6 ≤ 0.062) and the F academic grade indicate significant epistemic limitations in the current analysis. The text under examination lacks explicit citations and empirical grounding, which undermines its scholarly credibility. Future research should incorporate primary source materials from Amish communities, including interviews, communal discernment documents, and historical records of technological adoption decisions.

## 6. Conclusion

This article has presented a formal theophysical analysis of the Amish technological filtering paradigm, employing a dual-channel framework that integrates lexical and emotional dimensions of spiritual fruitfulness. The analysis demonstrates that the filtering process functions as a coherence-enforcing mechanism, with emotional calibration (L8) substantially exceeding lexical precision (L6). The composite coherence score of 0.351 and CHI score of 0.43 indicate moderate structural integration, while the F academic grade highlights the need for improved citation practices and empirical grounding.

The findings contribute to the emerging field of theophysics by demonstrating the applicability of formal analytical methods to questions of technological discernment and spiritual formation. Future research should expand the dataset to include multiple Amish communities, incorporate longitudinal analysis of filtering decisions, and develop more sophisticated metrics for assessing theophysical coherence across diverse theological traditions.
