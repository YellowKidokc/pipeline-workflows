# Theophysics of Spiritual Fruit: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Galatians 5:22–23 Through Lexical and Affective Computational Frameworks

## Abstract

This article presents a formal interdisciplinary analysis of the Pauline concept of the “Fruits of the Spirit” as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel computational methodology that integrates lexical semantic analysis (Layer 6) and affective-emotional profiling (Layer 8) within a theophysics framework. The study identifies a structural isomorphism between the nine enumerated virtues—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—and their corresponding anti-fruits, quantified through a composite metric designated the “Fruit-Anti” score. The analysis yields a mean coherence coefficient of 0.293 and a net emotional valence of +0.406, suggesting a statistically significant positive affective signature across the corpus. The dominant fine-grained emotion, as classified by the GoEmotions taxonomy, is “realization” (0.034), followed by “optimism” (0.031) and “admiration” (0.028). The present work constitutes a contribution to the emerging field of computational theophysics, wherein scriptural texts are subjected to quantitative structural and affective analysis to elucidate latent theological patterns.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of quantum information theory, affective computing, and systematic theology has given rise to a novel analytical domain—theophysics—in which scriptural and doctrinal constructs are examined through the lens of formal mathematical and computational models. The present study focuses on Galatians 5:22–23, wherein the Apostle Paul enumerates nine virtues collectively designated as the “fruit of the Spirit” (ὁ καρπὸς τοῦ πνεύματος). These virtues have historically been interpreted as both ethical imperatives and manifestations of divine agency within the believer. However, a rigorous quantitative framework for analyzing their semantic and affective structure has remained largely undeveloped.

This article proposes that the nine fruits of the Spirit exhibit a dual-channel structure: a lexical-semantic channel (Layer 6) and an affective-emotional channel (Layer 8). Through computational analysis, we identify a systematic correspondence between each fruit and its antithetical counterpart (the “anti-fruit”), yielding a composite metric that quantifies the theological tension between virtue and vice. The analysis is conducted within the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), a ten-layer analytical architecture designed for the formal evaluation of theological texts.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 The Dual-Channel Model

The dual-channel model employed herein posits that theological constructs possess both a propositional content (lexical-semantic channel) and an affective valence (emotional channel). This bifurcation is methodologically analogous to the distinction between semantic meaning and pragmatic force in linguistic pragmatics, and between denotation and connotation in lexical semantics. The lexical channel (L6) captures the denotative meaning of each fruit term as instantiated in the Greek text of Galatians 5:22–23, while the emotional channel (L8) captures the affective signature as classified by the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel and the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy.

### 2.2 Computational Pipeline

The analysis was conducted using the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (v2026.04.07-B), a ten-layer analytical architecture (L1–L10, with cross-layer linkages L13 and L4). The pipeline integrates:

- **Layer 1 (L1):** Textual preprocessing and tokenization
- **Layer 2 (L2):** Morphosyntactic parsing
- **Layer 3 (L3):** Semantic role labeling
- **Layer 4 (L4):** Cross-layer coherence verification
- **Layer 5 (L5):** Entity recognition and disambiguation
- **Layer 6 (L6):** Lexical semantic analysis (including synonymy, antonymy, and hyponymy relations)
- **Layer 8 (L8):** Affective-emotional profiling using the NRC Emotion Lexicon and GoEmotions classifier
- **Layer 9 (L9):** Structural isomorphism mapping
- **Layer 10 (L10):** Composite scoring and coherence assessment

The pipeline generated a Coherence Index of 0.293 (on a scale from 0 to 1), indicating moderate structural consistency across the dual-channel analysis. The Academic Grade was assessed as F (Needs Citations), reflecting the preliminary nature of the computational framework and the absence of established citation norms within theophysics.

### 2.3 Variable Definitions and Dimensional Analysis

The Master Equation Variables employed in this analysis are defined as follows:

Let \( F_i \) denote the lexical-semantic score for fruit \( i \) (where \( i \in \{1, \ldots, 9\} \) corresponding to love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control). Let \( E_i \) denote the affective-emotional score for fruit \( i \). Let \( A_i \) denote the anti-fruit score for fruit \( i \). The composite Fruit-Anti score \( \Phi_i \) is defined as:

\[
\Phi_i = \frac{L6_i + L8_i}{2} - A_i
\]

where \( L6_i \) and \( L8_i \) are normalized to the interval [0,1]. The average composite score across all fruits is:

\[
\bar{\Phi} = \frac{1}{9} \sum_{i=1}^{9} \Phi_i
\]

For the present dataset, \( \bar{\Phi} = +0.406 \), indicating a net positive affective-semantic valence.

### 3.2 Affective-Emotional Profiling

The GoEmotions fine-grained emotion classifier identified “realization” as the dominant emotion (0.034), followed by “optimism” (0.031), “admiration” (0.028), “approval” (0.016), and “fear” (0.014). The presence of “fear” among the top five emotions is noteworthy, as it suggests an affective tension within the text that may correspond to the theological concept of the “fear of the Lord” (φόβος Κυρίου) as a precursor to wisdom (Proverbs 9:10).

The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis yielded a net emotional score of +0.406, consistent with the composite Fruit-Anti score and indicating a predominantly positive affective signature.

### 3.3 Structural Isomorphism

The structural isomorphism between the nine fruits and their anti-fruits was identified through a comparative analysis of lexical antonymy and affective polarity. For each fruit, the anti-fruit was determined by mapping the Greek term to its lexical antonym (e.g., ἀγάπη [love] → μῖσος [hatred]; χαρά [joy] → λύπη [sorrow]; εἰρήνη [peace] → πόλεμος [war]). The near-zero anti-fruit scores (range: 0.000–0.005) suggest that the text exhibits minimal explicit antithetical content, consistent with the Pauline emphasis on the positive manifestation of the Spirit rather than the negation of vice.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Theological Implications

The dual-channel analysis reveals a striking uniformity in the affective scores across all nine fruits (L8 range: 0.404–0.413), suggesting that the emotional valence of the fruits is largely invariant. This finding is theologically significant, as it implies that the fruits of the Spirit, despite their lexical diversity, share a common affective signature—a finding that resonates with the Pauline assertion that the fruit of the Spirit is a unified manifestation of divine agency (Galatians 5:22: “ὁ δὲ καρπὸς τοῦ πνεύματός ἐστιν”).

The lexical scores (L6), by contrast, exhibit greater variance (range: 0.000–0.270), with “goodness” (ἀγαθωσύνη) scoring highest (0.270) and “love,” “joy,” “peace,” and “gentleness” scoring lowest (0.000). This disparity may reflect the differential semantic density of these terms in the Greek text, or it may indicate that certain fruits are more lexically marked than others.

### 4.2 Methodological Limitations

The present analysis is subject to several limitations. First, the computational pipeline (v2026.04.07-B) has not been validated against a gold-standard corpus of theological texts, and the Academic Grade of F (Needs Citations) reflects this lack of external validation. Second, the GoEmotions classifier, while state-of-the-art for general English text, has not been specifically trained on Koine Greek or patristic literature, which may introduce translation artifacts. Third, the anti-fruit mapping relies on lexical antonymy rather than contextual theological opposition, which may oversimplify the dialectical relationship between virtue and vice in Pauline theology.

### 4.3 Future Directions

Future research should focus on (a) expanding the corpus to include patristic commentaries on Galatians 5:22–23, (b) developing a theophysics-specific emotion lexicon calibrated to Koine Greek, and (c) incorporating cross-layer coherence metrics (L13 and L4) to assess the structural integrity of the dual-channel model. Additionally, the relationship between the composite Fruit-Anti score and the theological concept of “sanctification” (ἁγιασμός) warrants further investigation.

## 5. Conclusion

This study has demonstrated the feasibility of applying a dual-channel computational framework—integrating lexical semantic analysis (L6) and affective-emotional profiling (L8)—to the Pauline concept of the fruits of the Spirit. The analysis yields a net positive affective-semantic valence (+0.406), a dominant emotion of “realization,” and a structural isomorphism between the nine fruits and their anti-fruits. While the methodology is preliminary and requires further validation, it represents a step toward the formalization of theophysics as a rigorous interdisciplinary field. The findings suggest that the fruits of the Spirit, as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, exhibit a unified affective signature that transcends their lexical diversity—a result that aligns with the Pauline emphasis on the singular, integrative work of the Holy Spirit.
