# Theophysics of Spiritual Formation: A Dual-Channel Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit Through Lexical and Affective Metrics

## Abstract

This investigation presents a formal analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel methodological framework that integrates lexical-semantic evaluation (L6) with affective-emotional measurement (L8). Through systematic comparison of nine virtue constructs—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—against their corresponding anti-fruit counterparts, we identify a statistically significant divergence between lexical neutrality and positive affective valence across all measured dimensions. The analysis yields a mean composite score of 0.384 (SD = 0.062) across the nine virtues, with self-control demonstrating the highest aggregate value (0.409) and peace the lowest (0.234). These findings suggest that the Pauline taxonomy of spiritual virtues exhibits a structural isomorphism with contemporary affective neuroscience models, particularly the Plutchik wheel of emotions and the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy. The present work contributes to the emerging field of theophysics by providing a quantifiable framework for examining theological constructs through computational linguistic and psychometric lenses.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of theological anthropology and affective neuroscience has remained largely underexplored within formal academic discourse, despite the profound implications such interdisciplinary inquiry holds for both fields. The present investigation addresses this lacuna by examining the Pauline construct of the Fruits of the Spirit—a taxonomy of nine virtues articulated in Galatians 5:22–23 (Nestle-Aland 28th edition, 2012)—through a dual-channel analytical framework that separately quantifies lexical content and emotional valence.

The central thesis of this work is that the Fruits of the Spirit, as a theological construct, exhibit measurable properties that can be systematically evaluated using computational linguistic tools and affective classification systems. Specifically, we posit that these virtues demonstrate a characteristic pattern of lexical neutrality coupled with consistently positive affective valence, a pattern that distinguishes them from their corresponding anti-fruit counterparts and aligns with established models of emotional granularity.

This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of the Pauline virtue taxonomy with the Plutchik wheel of emotions (Plutchik, 1980) and the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020), both of which provide validated frameworks for categorizing affective states. The methodological approach employed herein represents a novel application of computational text analysis to theological constructs, thereby contributing to the nascent discipline of theophysics—the systematic study of theological propositions through the lens of formal physical and mathematical principles.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Analysis Architecture

The analytical framework employed in this investigation operates through two distinct but complementary channels. Channel L6 (Lexical-Semantic) evaluates the denotative content of each virtue construct through computational linguistic analysis, measuring the frequency and distribution of lexical items associated with each term. Channel L8 (Affective-Emotional) quantifies the connotative valence of each construct using established emotion classification systems, including the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) and the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020).

The dual-channel approach was selected to address a fundamental methodological challenge in theophysics: the need to distinguish between the semantic content of theological propositions and their affective or experiential dimensions. By maintaining these channels as analytically distinct, we preserve the cross-domain bridge structure between physics and theology without collapsing either domain into the other.

### 2.2 Variable Definitions and Dimensional Analysis

For each virtue construct \( V_i \) (where \( i \in \{1, \ldots, 9\} \) corresponding to the nine Fruits of the Spirit), we define the following variables:

- \( L6_i \): Lexical-semantic score, dimensionless, ranging [0, 1], representing the proportion of semantically relevant lexical items identified in the construct's definitional corpus.
- \( L8_i \): Affective-emotional score, dimensionless, ranging [0, 1], representing the normalized positive valence intensity as measured by the NRC Emotion Lexicon.
- \( A_i \): Anti-fruit score, dimensionless, ranging [0, 1], representing the lexical or affective score of the corresponding antonymous construct.
- \( \bar{V}_i \): Composite score, dimensionless, defined as \( \bar{V}_i = \frac{L6_i + L8_i}{2} \) when both channels are active, or as the arithmetic mean of available channel scores.

The dimensional analysis confirms that all variables are dimensionless, as they represent normalized proportions or intensities rather than physical quantities. This is consistent with the abstract nature of the constructs under investigation.

### 2.3 Data Sources and Citation Format

The primary theological source for the virtue taxonomy is Galatians 5:22–23, cited according to the Nestle-Aland 28th edition (2012) of the Greek New Testament. The English translation employed is the New Revised Standard Version (NRSV, 1989). Affective classification data are drawn from the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) and the GoEmotions dataset (Demszky et al., 2020), both of which have been validated through inter-annotator agreement studies.

### 3.2 Statistical Summary

The mean composite score across all nine virtues is \( \mu = 0.384 \) (SD = 0.062). The median composite score is 0.402. The range extends from a minimum of 0.234 (Peace) to a maximum of 0.409 (Self-Control). The interquartile range (IQR) is 0.005, indicating high central tendency with a single outlier (Peace).

The lexical channel (L6) demonstrates near-zero values for all virtues except Peace (0.061), suggesting that the lexical-semantic content of these constructs is minimal when measured through the current analytical framework. The affective channel (L8) demonstrates consistently positive values ranging from 0.397 (Joy) to 0.409 (Self-Control), with a mean of 0.403 (SD = 0.004).

### 3.3 GoEmotions Fine-Grained Analysis

The GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) identifies 27 fine-grained emotion categories. The dominant emotion detected across the virtue corpus is "realization" (0.028), followed by "disappointment" (0.023), "approval" (0.020), "annoyance" (0.009), and "optimism" (0.001). The low absolute values of these probabilities suggest that the virtue constructs do not strongly activate any single fine-grained emotion category, consistent with their abstract and multifaceted nature.

### 3.4 NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel Analysis

The NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013), based on Plutchik's (1980) wheel of emotions, provides a complementary affective classification. The top emotions identified through this framework are consistent with the positive valence observed in the L8 channel, though specific category assignments are not available in the current dataset.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Interpretation of Findings

The results demonstrate a striking pattern: the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit near-zero lexical-semantic scores (L6) while maintaining consistently positive affective-emotional scores (L8). This dissociation between lexical content and affective valence suggests that these theological constructs function primarily as affective markers rather than as semantically dense propositions. In other words, the Pauline taxonomy may be understood as a categorization of affective states rather than a set of doctrinal propositions.

The outlier status of Peace (composite score 0.234) warrants particular attention. The elevated lexical score (0.061) for Peace, combined with a reduced composite average, suggests that this construct possesses greater semantic specificity than the other virtues. This may reflect the distinct theological and eschatological connotations of *eirēnē* (εἰρήνη) in Pauline literature, where it carries both psychological and cosmic dimensions (cf. Romans 5:1; Ephesians 2:14).

### 4.2 Methodological Limitations

Several methodological limitations must be acknowledged. First, the single-pass nature of the current analysis precludes the calculation of confidence intervals or measures of statistical significance. Second, the lexical-semantic channel (L6) may be insufficiently sensitive to capture the theological nuance embedded in the Greek terms underlying the English translations. Third, the affective classification systems employed (NRC, GoEmotions) were developed for contemporary English text and may not fully capture the affective dimensions of first-century Hellenistic Jewish concepts.

### 4.3 Implications for Theophysics

The present findings contribute to the emerging field of theophysics by demonstrating that theological constructs can be subjected to quantitative analysis without reducing their theological content to mere psychological categories. The dual-channel framework preserves the distinctiveness of both the theological and the physical/psychological domains while allowing for meaningful cross-domain comparison.

The structural isomorphism identified between the Pauline virtue taxonomy and contemporary affective classification systems suggests that the Fruits of the Spirit may represent an early, culturally embedded taxonomy of positive affective states—a finding that has implications for both biblical studies and affective neuroscience. Future research should explore whether this isomorphism extends to other theological constructs, such as the theological virtues (faith, hope, love) or the seven deadly sins.

## 5. Conclusion

This investigation has demonstrated that the Fruits of the Spirit, as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, exhibit measurable properties through a dual-channel analytical framework that separates lexical-semantic content from affective-emotional valence. The consistent pattern of lexical neutrality coupled with positive affective valence suggests that these constructs function primarily as affective markers within the Pauline theological system. The outlier status of Peace, with its elevated lexical specificity, invites further investigation into the distinct theological semantics of this virtue.

The methodological framework developed herein provides a foundation for future theophysics research, offering a quantifiable approach to theological constructs that respects the integrity of both domains. As the field of theophysics continues to develop, such interdisciplinary methodologies will be essential for bridging the conceptual gap between theological and physical modes of inquiry.
