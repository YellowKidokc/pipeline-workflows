## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Structural Implications of the Patience Anomaly

The lexical differentiation of patience (\( L6 = 0.964 \)) relative to the other eight fruits suggests that patience possesses a unique structural property within the Pauline virtue taxonomy. This finding is consistent with the theological interpretation of patience (*μακροθυμία*) as a temporally extended virtue—one that involves endurance over time rather than instantaneous affective response. The lexical channel may be capturing this temporal dimensionality, which is absent in the other fruits that are more immediately affective in nature.

From a theophysical perspective, this differential activation may correspond to a higher-order structural coupling between the lexical-semantic field and the temporal dimension of virtue. Patience, unlike love or joy, requires a sustained state over an interval \( \Delta t > 0 \), which may manifest as increased lexical complexity in its scriptural encoding.

### 4.2 Uniform Emotional Baseline

The constancy of the emotional channel coefficient (\( L8 = 0.400 \)) across all nine fruits indicates that the emotional valence of these virtues is treated uniformly within the Pauline corpus. This uniformity may reflect a theological presupposition that all Fruits of the Spirit share an equal positive affective status, irrespective of their lexical or structural differentiation.

### 4.3 Methodological Limitations

The present analysis is limited by the absence of confidence intervals for the channel coefficients, as the underlying data are derived from a single-pass pipeline without bootstrap resampling or cross-validation. Additionally, the anti-fruit null state is defined as uniformly zero, which may not capture the full semantic range of virtue negation in the original Greek text.
