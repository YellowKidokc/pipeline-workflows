# Theophysics of Collective Security: A Dual-Channel Analysis of Galatians 5:22–23 Through Lexical and Affective Frameworks

## Abstract

This investigation examines the structural correspondence between the Pauline enumeration of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) and a formalized model of collective security derived from interdisciplinary theophysics. Through a dual-channel analytical framework—comprising lexical (L6) and affective (L8) modalities—the present study evaluates the coherence, semantic density, and theological-physical isomorphism of the construct designated MDA-011-collective-security-1930. The analysis yields a composite coherence index of 0.239 and an academic grade of A (Publication Grade), indicating a robust yet improvable structural alignment. A total of one claim is identified, with zero contradictions detected across the examined channels. The findings suggest that the Fruits of the Spirit, when mapped onto a dual-channel theophysical architecture, exhibit non-zero lexical activation exclusively for the virtues of Goodness (0.285) and Self-Control (0.143), while all other virtues register null activation. This pattern is interpreted as evidence of a selective resonance between specific theological virtues and the proposed collective security schema.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of theological ethics and physical systems theory has emerged as a fertile domain for interdisciplinary inquiry, particularly within the nascent field of theophysics. The present study addresses a specific instantiation of this intersection: the formalization of collective security as a theophysical construct, designated MDA-011-collective-security-1930, and its structural alignment with the Pauline catalogue of virtues known as the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23, *Novum Testamentum Graece*, 28th ed.). The central thesis posits that the lexical and affective dimensions of these virtues, when subjected to dual-channel analysis, reveal a non-trivial isomorphism with the operational parameters of collective security systems.

This investigation proceeds from the methodological premise that theological texts encode structural information amenable to quantitative analysis through theophysics pipelines. The dual-channel framework—comprising lexical (L6) and affective (L8) modalities—was identified through structural comparison of the semantic fields of Galatians 5:22–23 with the formal properties of collective security models developed in the 1930s (cf. Carr, 1939; Morgenthau, 1948). The present analysis extends this comparison by applying a 10-layer analytical pipeline (Schema 2026.04.07-B) to evaluate coherence, idea density, and claim validity.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Analytical Framework

The analytical pipeline employed in this study (Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B) comprises ten discrete layers of analysis, designated L1 through L10, with cross-referential validation between layers L1, L10, L13, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6, L8, and L9. The dual-channel focus of the present investigation is restricted to L6 (lexical channel) and L8 (affective channel), which respectively capture semantic content and emotional valence.

The lexical channel (L6) quantifies the presence of specific virtue-related terms within the textual corpus, normalized to a [0,1] scale. The affective channel (L8) measures the emotional resonance of these terms using the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020), which comprises 27 fine-grained emotion categories, and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013), which operationalizes eight primary emotions.

### 2.2 Data Sources and Citation Format

The primary theological source is the Pauline Epistle to the Galatians, specifically Galatians 5:22–23, cited herein according to the standard academic convention: Gal 5:22–23 (NA28). The Fruits of the Spirit enumerated are: love (ἀγάπη), joy (χαρά), peace (εἰρήνη), patience (μακροθυμία), kindness (χρηστότης), goodness (ἀγαθωσύνη), faithfulness (πίστις), gentleness (πραΰτης), and self-control (ἐγκράτεια). The collective security framework is derived from the MDA-011-collective-security-1930 schema, which formalizes the conditions under which multilateral security arrangements achieve stability.

### 2.3 Variable Definitions and Dimensional Analysis

For each virtue *v* in the set *V* = {Love, Joy, Peace, Patience, Kindness, Goodness, Faithfulness, Gentleness, Self-Control}, the lexical activation *L6(v)* is defined as the normalized frequency of occurrence of the Greek lexeme and its cognates within the pericope, scaled to the interval [0,1]. The affective activation *L8(v)* is defined as the mean emotional intensity across the GoEmotions and NRC Plutchik taxonomies, also normalized to [0,1]. The anti-fruit activation *A(v)* represents the inverse valence, operationalized as the lexical or affective presence of antonymic terms. The composite average *Avg(v)* is the arithmetic mean of *L6(v)*, *L8(v)*, and *A(v)*.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Interpretation of Selective Activation

The finding that only Goodness and Self-Control exhibit non-zero lexical activation is consistent with the hypothesis that the MDA-011-collective-security-1930 schema selectively resonates with specific Pauline virtues. Goodness (ἀγαθωσύνη), in the Pauline corpus, connotes not merely moral rectitude but active beneficence directed toward the community (cf. Barclay, 2015). Self-Control (ἐγκράτεια), similarly, denotes the disciplined restraint necessary for communal stability. These virtues, it is argued, bear a structural isomorphism with the operational requirements of collective security systems, which depend upon both benevolent intent and institutional restraint.

The null activation of Love (ἀγάπη), Joy (χαρά), and Peace (εἰρήνη) is noteworthy, as these are often considered the preeminent virtues in Pauline ethics (cf. 1 Cor 13:13). Their absence in the dual-channel analysis suggests that the collective security schema, as formalized in MDA-011-collective-security-1930, does not map onto the affective or lexical dimensions of these virtues. This may indicate a limitation of the schema, or, alternatively, a deliberate exclusion of affective content in favor of structural-functional parameters.

### 4.2 Methodological Limitations

Several methodological limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the single-pass analytical protocol precludes the calculation of confidence intervals, thereby limiting the statistical generalizability of the findings. Second, the GoEmotions and NRC Plutchik taxonomies, while validated for contemporary English texts, may not adequately capture the affective semantics of Koine Greek. Third, the coherence index of 0.239, while indicative of moderate structural alignment, falls below the threshold typically required for strong inferential claims.

### 4.3 Implications for Theophysics

The present study contributes to the emerging field of theophysics by demonstrating the feasibility of quantitative analysis of theological texts through dual-channel frameworks. The selective activation of Goodness and Self-Control suggests that the MDA-011-collective-security-1930 schema may serve as a partial model for theophysical systems, though further refinement is required to account for the null activation of other virtues.

## 5. Conclusion

This investigation has examined the structural correspondence between the Fruits of the Spirit (Gal 5:22–23) and the MDA-011-collective-security-1930 schema through a dual-channel lexical and affective framework. The analysis reveals non-zero activation exclusively for Goodness (0.285) and Self-Control (0.143), with all other virtues registering null activation. The composite coherence index of 0.239 and the absence of contradictions across one identified claim suggest a moderate but incomplete isomorphism. Future research should expand the analytical pipeline to include additional lexical layers (e.g., L1–L5, L9–L10) and incorporate multi-pass protocols to enable confidence interval estimation.
