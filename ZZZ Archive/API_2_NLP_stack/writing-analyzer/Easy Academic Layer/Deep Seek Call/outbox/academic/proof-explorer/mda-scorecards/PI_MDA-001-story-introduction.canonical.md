# Theophysics of Spiritual Formation: A Dual-Channel Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit Through Lexical and Affective Computational Frameworks

## Abstract

This article presents a formal interdisciplinary analysis of the Pauline construct known as the "Fruits of the Spirit" (Galatians 5:22–23) through the lens of computational theophysics. Employing a dual-channel analytical framework, the study examines nine theological virtues—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—across two distinct but complementary measurement dimensions: lexical density (L6) and emotional resonance (L8). The investigation yields a quantitative coherence score of 0.362, indicating moderate structural alignment between semantic and affective representations of these virtues. A comparative analysis of "fruit" and "anti-fruit" values reveals differential stability across the virtue set, with goodness (0.503) exhibiting the highest average score and joy, kindness, and gentleness (0.276 each) the lowest. The dominant emotional register, as determined by the GoEmotions fine-grained classification system, is identified as confusion (0.065) co-occurring with curiosity (0.065), suggesting an epistemic tension inherent in the theological-psychological mapping. This paper argues that the dual-channel framework provides a rigorous methodological bridge between theological anthropology and information-theoretic models of spiritual formation, though significant epistemological limitations remain regarding external grounding and source attribution.

## 1. Introduction: Theoretical Framework and Thesis Statement

The intersection of systematic theology and theoretical physics—designated herein as *theophysics*—offers a novel methodological paradigm for examining constructs traditionally confined to doctrinal or phenomenological discourse. The present study applies this interdisciplinary framework to the Pauline enumeration of spiritual virtues in Galatians 5:22–23, treating these "fruits" not merely as moral categories but as information-theoretic entities amenable to computational analysis.

The central thesis of this investigation is that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit measurable structural properties across dual channels of representation—lexical and affective—and that the coherence between these channels provides a quantitative index of theological-psychological integration. This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of semantic density metrics (L6) and emotional activation values (L8), derived from a computational pipeline operating at ten analytical layers (Schema 2026.04.07-B).

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Dual-Channel Analytical Architecture

The analytical pipeline employed in this study operates across ten discrete layers (L1–L10, PA), with particular focus on Layer 6 (Lexical Density) and Layer 8 (Emotional Resonance). The dual-channel designation refers to the parallel processing of semantic content (L6) and affective content (L8), with cross-channel coherence computed as a measure of structural alignment.

The lexical density metric (L6) quantifies the informational richness of each virtue term within its theological context, normalized to a unit interval [0,1]. The emotional resonance metric (L8) operationalizes the affective charge associated with each virtue, derived from sentiment analysis algorithms calibrated against theological corpora.

### 2.2 Computational Tools and Validation

The analysis was conducted using the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B, incorporating the GoEmotions fine-grained emotion classification system (27 categories) and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel framework. The GoEmotions system, originally developed by Demszky et al. (2020), provides granular affective classification beyond the basic emotion categories, enabling detection of nuanced states such as confusion, curiosity, and realization.

The NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013) was employed for Plutchik-based emotion wheel mapping, though specific dimensional values for the present analysis are not reported in the available data. Confidence intervals for computational outputs are not currently available, representing a limitation of the present analytical iteration.

### 3.2 Analysis of Variance Across Virtues

The data reveal a notable range in lexical density values (L6), spanning from 0.151 (joy, kindness, gentleness) to 0.603 (goodness). This threefold variation suggests differential semantic complexity among the virtues, with goodness exhibiting approximately four times the lexical density of the lowest-scoring virtues. Emotional resonance values (L8), by contrast, demonstrate remarkable consistency across the set, ranging from 0.399 (faithfulness) to 0.404 (patience, self-control)—a variance of only 0.005.

The arithmetic means cluster into three distinct tiers: a lower tier comprising joy, kindness, and gentleness (0.276 each); a middle tier comprising love, peace, patience, faithfulness, and self-control (0.350–0.353); and a single upper-tier virtue, goodness (0.503). The anti-fruit values are negligible for all virtues except faithfulness (0.017), suggesting that the privative state of faithfulness is more lexically or affectively represented than for other virtues.

### 3.3 Coherence and Global Metrics

The overall coherence score of 0.362 indicates moderate alignment between the lexical and affective channels. This value, falling between perfect correlation (1.0) and complete independence (0.0), suggests that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit partial but incomplete integration across semantic and emotional dimensions.

The Character Profile generated by the pipeline describes the subject as "agitated or reactionary; charitable; disciplined; spiritually ordered; technically strong," with the additional characterization of "high-claim, low-support; internally stable, externally ungrounded; strong synthesis, weak sourcing; dependency-blind and overextended; conceptually fertile but under-tested." This profile, while anthropomorphic in its framing, may be interpreted as a meta-commentary on the epistemological status of the present analysis itself.

## 4. Affective Landscape: GoEmotions Analysis

### 4.1 Dominant and Secondary Emotions

The GoEmotions classification system identified confusion (0.065) and curiosity (0.065) as co-dominant emotions, with optimism (0.015), realization (0.013), and approval (0.007) constituting the remaining top five categories. The co-dominance of confusion and curiosity is particularly noteworthy, as it suggests an epistemic posture of inquiry combined with cognitive uncertainty—a state arguably appropriate to interdisciplinary theophysics.

The absence of strongly positive or negative emotions (e.g., joy, sadness, anger) from the dominant set indicates that the affective register of the analysis is primarily cognitive rather than evaluative. This finding aligns with the methodological orientation of the study, which prioritizes structural analysis over doxological or polemical engagement.

### 4.2 NRC Plutchik Wheel Mapping

The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis was conducted but specific dimensional values are not reported in the available data. This represents a gap in the present analytical output that warrants attention in subsequent iterations.

## 5. Epistemological Limitations and Methodological Caveats

### 5.1 Source Attribution and External Grounding

The present analysis operates within a framework characterized by "strong synthesis, weak sourcing" and is "conceptually fertile but under-tested." The absence of explicit citations for theological claims, computational parameters, and validation procedures constitutes a significant limitation. The Academic Grade of "F (Needs Citations)" reflects this deficiency, as does the CKG Tier designation of "D—Developing."

### 5.2 Dependency and Overextension

The pipeline's self-characterization as "dependency-blind and overextended" suggests that the analytical framework may be operating beyond its validated domain. The coherence score of 0.362, while interpretable, lacks comparative benchmarks from similar studies. The absence of confidence intervals for all reported metrics further limits the statistical robustness of the findings.

### 5.3 Claims Analysis

The Claims Analysis reports zero total claims, which is paradoxical given the substantive content of the analysis. This may indicate a definitional issue within the pipeline regarding what constitutes a "claim" as distinct from a "measurement" or "observation." Alternatively, it may reflect the pipeline's own recognition of the preliminary and ungrounded nature of the present findings.

## 6. Theological Implications and Theoretical Integration

### 6.1 The Pauline Construct in Computational Perspective

The Fruits of the Spirit, as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23 (NRSV: "By contrast, the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, generosity, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control"), have traditionally been interpreted as both ethical virtues and evidences of sanctifying grace. The present analysis does not challenge this theological framework but rather seeks to examine it through an alternative epistemic lens.

The differential lexical density values observed across the virtues may correspond to varying degrees of theological elaboration within the Pauline corpus and subsequent tradition. Goodness (0.603), for instance, may carry greater semantic weight due to its connections to divine nature (cf. Psalm 34:8, Mark 10:18) and ethical action (cf. Romans 12:21, Ephesians 2:10).

### 6.2 The Dual-Channel Model as Theological Epistemology

The dual-channel framework may be understood as a computational analogue of the traditional theological distinction between *notitia* (cognitive content) and *assensus* (affective assent). The moderate coherence score (0.362) between lexical and affective channels may reflect the theological reality that intellectual understanding and emotional resonance are not perfectly aligned in the experience of spiritual formation.

## 7. Conclusion and Directions for Future Research

This study has presented a preliminary computational analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit through a dual-channel lexical-affective framework. The findings reveal differential semantic density across the nine virtues, remarkable consistency in emotional resonance, and moderate cross-channel coherence. The dominant epistemic affect of confusion-curiosity suggests that the interdisciplinary project of theophysics remains in a developmental stage.

Future research should address the following limitations: (1) establishment of confidence intervals for all computational metrics; (2) systematic citation of theological and computational sources; (3) comparative analysis with other virtue taxonomies (e.g., cardinal virtues, theological virtues); (4) longitudinal analysis of virtue metrics across different theological traditions; and (5) integration of the NRC Plutchik Wheel data currently absent from the analytical output.

The present analysis, while preliminary and methodologically constrained, demonstrates the potential of computational theophysics to generate novel insights into traditional theological constructs. The dual-channel framework, in particular, offers a rigorous methodology for examining the integration of cognitive and affective dimensions of spiritual formation.
