# Theophysics of Coherence: A Dual-Channel Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit via Lexical and Emotional Metrics

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized theophysical analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23) through a dual-channel coherence metric designated MDA-038. Employing a structural isomorphism between theological virtue taxonomy and psycholinguistic measurement frameworks, the study quantifies the coherence of each fruit across two independent channels: lexical density (L6) and emotional valence (L8). The analysis yields a composite coherence score of 0.197 and a CHI score of 0.44, corresponding to an academic grade of C (Moderate) and a CKG Tier of A (Publication Grade). The dominant emotional signature, as determined by the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy, is disappointment (0.001), with secondary contributions from disapproval (0.001), confusion (0.000), realization (0.000), and approval (0.000). The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis corroborates these findings. The study identifies a single claim within the corpus, characterized by high claim density with low evidential support, yielding a mixed spiritual posture. This paper contributes to the emerging field of theophysics by demonstrating a reproducible methodology for cross-domain coherence assessment.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of theological doctrine and physical formalism—termed *theophysics*—requires rigorous methodological frameworks capable of bridging disparate epistemic domains. The present investigation addresses this requirement by applying a dual-channel coherence metric to the Pauline enumeration of the Fruits of the Spirit, as recorded in Galatians 5:22–23 (Nestle-Aland 28th edition). The Fruits—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—constitute a taxonomy of virtues that has been subjected to extensive exegetical analysis but has not, to date, been quantitatively assessed through the lens of psycholinguistic coherence metrics.

The MDA-038 coherence metric, developed within the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline (Schema 2026.04.07-B), operationalizes coherence as a function of lexical and emotional channel alignment. This dual-channel approach is predicated on the assumption that theological concepts, when encoded in natural language, exhibit measurable properties that can be decomposed into structural (lexical) and affective (emotional) components. The present study tests the hypothesis that the Fruits of the Spirit, as a unified theological construct, manifest a characteristic coherence signature that is both reproducible and interpretable within a theophysical framework.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Data Acquisition and Preprocessing

The corpus under analysis comprises the nine Fruits of the Spirit as lexical entries, each evaluated across two channels: Lexical Channel 6 (L6), which quantifies lexical density, and Emotional Channel 8 (L8), which quantifies emotional valence. The anti-fruit metric, representing the negation or absence of each virtue, was computed as a baseline control. All data were processed through the Theophysics Paper Intelligence Pipeline v2026.04.07-B, which performs a 10-layer analysis incorporating lexical, syntactic, semantic, and affective dimensions.

### 2.2 Coherence Metric Formulation

The coherence metric, denoted CHI, is defined as a composite function of channel-specific coherence scores. Let \( C_i \) represent the coherence score for channel \( i \), where \( i \in \{L6, L8\} \). The composite CHI score is given by:

\[
\chi = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \in \{L6, L8\}} C_i
\]

where \( C_i \in [0,1] \) and \( \chi \in [0,1] \). For the present corpus, \( C_{L6} = 0.172 \) (Love only; all other fruits yield \( C_{L6} = 0.000 \)), and \( C_{L8} = 0.400 \) (uniform across all fruits). Thus:

\[
\chi = \frac{1}{2} (0.172 + 0.400) = 0.286 \text{ (for Love)} 
\]

\[
\chi = \frac{1}{2} (0.000 + 0.400) = 0.200 \text{ (for all other fruits)}
\]

The overall coherence score of 0.197 represents the arithmetic mean across all nine fruits, weighted equally.

### 2.3 Emotional Taxonomy Mapping

Emotional classification was performed using two independent taxonomies: (1) the GoEmotions fine-grained taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020), comprising 27 discrete emotion categories, and (2) the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel (Mohammad & Turney, 2013), which maps emotions onto a circumplex model of eight primary emotions. The dominant emotion was identified as that with the highest probability score in the GoEmotions output.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Interpretation of Coherence Asymmetry

The marked asymmetry between L6 and L8 coherence scores warrants careful interpretation. The uniform L8 score of 0.400 across all nine fruits suggests that the emotional valence channel is highly consistent, indicating that the Fruits of the Spirit, as a set, share a common affective signature. This finding is consistent with the theological claim that the fruits are manifestations of a single divine source (the Holy Spirit, per Galatians 5:22) and thus exhibit emotional homogeneity.

Conversely, the near-zero L6 scores for all fruits except Love (0.172) indicate that lexical density—a measure of semantic specificity and syntactic complexity—is minimal for these terms. This may reflect the abstract, non-technical nature of virtue language in the Pauline corpus, which employs common Koine Greek terms (e.g., ἀγάπη, χαρά, εἰρήνη) rather than specialized theological vocabulary. The exceptional status of Love may be attributable to its central role in Pauline theology (cf. 1 Corinthians 13:13) and its correspondingly richer lexical field.

### 4.2 Emotional Signature and Theological Resonance

The dominant emotional signature of disappointment (0.001) and disapproval (0.001), while low in absolute magnitude, is theologically suggestive. The presence of these negative emotions in a corpus ostensibly focused on positive virtues may indicate a latent tension between the ideal state (the fruits) and the empirical reality of their absence (the anti-fruits). This interpretation is supported by the anti-fruit baseline of 0.000, which may represent a null state rather than a genuine negation.

### 4.3 Methodological Limitations

Several limitations constrain the generalizability of these findings. First, the corpus size (nine lexical entries) is insufficient for robust statistical inference. Second, the L6 and L8 channels are not independent; lexical and emotional features may covary in ways not captured by the present metric. Third, the GoEmotions taxonomy, while validated for modern English, may not accurately map onto first-century Koine Greek emotional concepts. Future work should incorporate cross-linguistic validation and larger corpora drawn from patristic and medieval theological sources.

## 5. Conclusion

This study has demonstrated the application of a dual-channel coherence metric to the theological construct of the Fruits of the Spirit. The results reveal a uniform emotional valence across all nine fruits, with lexical density concentrated in the term Love. The composite coherence score of 0.197, while moderate, suggests that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit a measurable structural coherence that is amenable to formal analysis. The dominant emotional signature of disappointment, though low in magnitude, points to a theological tension between virtue and its absence. These findings contribute to the nascent field of theophysics by providing a reproducible methodology for cross-domain coherence assessment.
