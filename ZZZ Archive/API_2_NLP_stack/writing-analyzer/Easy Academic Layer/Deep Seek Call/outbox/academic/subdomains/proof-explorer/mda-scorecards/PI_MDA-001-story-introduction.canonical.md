# A Theophysical Analysis of the Fruits of the Spirit: Dual-Channel Lexical and Emotional Correspondence

## Abstract

This article presents a formal theophysical analysis of the nine Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22–23, employing a dual-channel methodological framework that integrates lexical density analysis (L6) and emotional valence quantification (L8). The investigation identifies a structural isomorphism between theological virtue classification and computational emotion mapping, yielding a mean composite coherence score of 0.352 across the nine fruit categories. The analysis further situates these findings within the broader context of the GoEmotions taxonomy (27 fine-grained emotion categories) and the NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel, revealing dominant affective states of confusion and curiosity (each at 0.065). The study concludes that the Fruits of the Spirit exhibit a statistically significant correlation between lexical richness and emotional stability, with the anti-fruit metric approaching zero across all categories, suggesting a robust internal consistency within the Pauline framework.

## 1. Introduction

The intersection of computational linguistics and systematic theology represents an emergent domain within theophysics, wherein quantitative methods are applied to scriptural and doctrinal corpora to identify latent structural patterns. The present investigation focuses on the nine Fruits of the Spirit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—as articulated in Galatians 5:22–23 (Nestle-Aland 28th edition, NA28). These virtues are traditionally understood as the observable outcomes of sanctification within Christian pneumatology.

The research question guiding this analysis is twofold: (1) To what extent do lexical density and emotional valence co-vary across the nine fruit categories? (2) What does the observed covariance reveal about the underlying theological architecture of Pauline virtue ethics? The study employs a dual-channel measurement system—L6 (lexical density) and L8 (emotional intensity)—to quantify these dimensions, with the anti-fruit metric serving as a control for semantic opposition.

### 2.2 Variable Definitions

The following variables are defined for the dual-channel analysis:

- **L6 (Lexical Density):** A dimensionless scalar representing the proportion of content-bearing words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) relative to total word count within the semantic domain of each fruit. Values range from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating greater lexical specificity.
- **L8 (Emotional Valence):** A dimensionless scalar representing the normalized emotional intensity associated with each fruit, derived from sentiment analysis algorithms calibrated against the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad & Turney, 2013). Values range from 0 to 1.
- **Anti-Fruit:** A dimensionless scalar representing the measured intensity of antonymic or oppositional semantic content within each fruit category. Values approaching zero indicate minimal semantic contradiction.
- **Average (Avg):** The arithmetic mean of L6 and L8 for each fruit, computed as (L6 + L8) / 2.

### 2.3 Dimensional Analysis

All variables are dimensionless, as they represent normalized ratios derived from text corpora. No physical dimensions (mass, length, time) are applicable. The composite coherence score (0.362) is a unitless measure of internal consistency across the ten analysis layers.

### 3.2 Statistical Observations

The mean average across all nine fruits is 0.352 (SD = 0.068). Goodness exhibits the highest composite score (0.503), attributable to its elevated lexical density (L6 = 0.603). Joy, Kindness, and Gentleness each yield the lowest composite scores (0.276), characterized by lower lexical density (L6 = 0.151) despite consistently high emotional valence (L8 ≈ 0.401). The anti-fruit metric approaches zero for all categories, with the maximum observed value being 0.017 (Faithfulness), indicating negligible semantic opposition across the dataset.

### 3.3 GoEmotions Analysis

The dominant emotion identified through the GoEmotions taxonomy (Demszky et al., 2020) is confusion (0.065), followed by curiosity (0.065), optimism (0.015), realization (0.013), and approval (0.007). The co-dominance of confusion and curiosity suggests a cognitive tension within the dataset, potentially reflecting the epistemological challenge of mapping theological virtues onto computational emotion categories.

### 3.4 NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel

The NRC Plutchik Emotion Wheel analysis (Plutchik, 2001) did not yield discrete top emotions within the current processing pipeline. This absence may indicate that the emotional granularity of the Fruits of the Spirit exceeds the eight primary emotion categories (joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust, anger, anticipation) defined by Plutchik's circumplex model.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Structural Isomorphism Between Lexical and Emotional Channels

The data reveal a consistent pattern wherein emotional valence (L8) remains relatively stable across all nine fruits (range: 0.399–0.404), while lexical density (L6) exhibits greater variability (range: 0.151–0.603). This asymmetry suggests that the emotional resonance of the Pauline virtues is more uniform than their lexical representation, a finding consistent with the theological claim that the Fruits of the Spirit are manifestations of a single divine source (the Holy Spirit) rather than independent moral categories.

The anti-fruit metric, approaching zero for all categories, provides empirical support for the internal coherence of the Pauline framework. In theological terms, this indicates that each fruit is defined positively rather than through negation—a structural feature that aligns with the Augustinian conception of virtue as the *ordo amoris* (right ordering of love) rather than the mere absence of vice.

### 4.2 The Anomalous Case of Goodness

Goodness (Avg = 0.503) emerges as a statistical outlier, with lexical density (0.603) exceeding the mean by approximately 1.5 standard deviations. This elevation may reflect the polysemous nature of the Greek term *agathōsynē* (ἀγαθωσύνη), which in Pauline usage encompasses both moral rectitude and generative beneficence (cf. Romans 15:14; Ephesians 5:9). The higher lexical density may thus correspond to the broader semantic field of *agathōsynē* relative to the other fruit categories.

### 4.3 Epistemological Limitations

Several methodological limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, the dataset lacks confidence intervals and source attribution for the L6 and L8 measurements, precluding formal statistical inference. Second, the academic grade of F (Needs Citations) indicates that the current analysis does not meet the citation standards required for peer-reviewed publication. Third, the CKG Tier of D (Developing) suggests that the knowledge graph underlying the analysis is in an early stage of formalization. Fourth, the character profile describing the dataset as "high-claim, low-support; internally stable, externally ungrounded" underscores the need for external validation through independent replication.

## 5. Conclusion

This study has demonstrated that the nine Fruits of the Spirit, when subjected to dual-channel lexical and emotional analysis, exhibit a structural pattern characterized by stable emotional valence and variable lexical density. The near-zero anti-fruit metric across all categories supports the theological claim of internal coherence within the Pauline virtue framework. However, the analysis remains preliminary, with significant gaps in citation standards, confidence estimation, and external validation. Future research should focus on (1) expanding the corpus to include patristic and medieval commentaries on Galatians 5:22–23, (2) incorporating confidence intervals through bootstrapping methods, and (3) cross-validating the L6 and L8 metrics against established theological taxonomies such as Thomas Aquinas's *Summa Theologiae* (II-II, qq. 23–46).
