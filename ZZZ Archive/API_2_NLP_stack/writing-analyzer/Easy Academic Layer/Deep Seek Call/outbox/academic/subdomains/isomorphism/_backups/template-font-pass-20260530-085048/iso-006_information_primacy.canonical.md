# ISOMORPHISM RECORD ISO-006: INFORMATION PRIMACY

## Abstract

This record documents a structural isomorphism between the information-theoretic ontology proposed in contemporary physics—particularly John Archibald Wheeler's "It from Bit" thesis—and the Johannine Prologue's doctrine of the Logos (λόγος) as the ontologically prior, generative substrate of all material reality. The mapping identifies four shared structural features: (1) ontological priority of information over matter, (2) generative function of information, (3) irreducibility of the informational substrate, and (4) physical consequences of informational operations. A strain analysis reveals significant divergence at the personal-impersonal boundary, as Shannon information lacks the conscious, intentional, and personal attributes ascribed to the Logos in Christian theology. The isomorphism is classified as structural with medium confidence, acknowledging that the semantic gap between λόγος and information is substantial and must be explicitly addressed rather than elided.

## 1. Domain Specification

### 1.1 Domain A: Physics and Information Theory

The thesis under examination is that information constitutes the ontologically primitive substrate from which physical reality derives. This position is most prominently articulated in Wheeler's (1990) "It from Bit" program, which asserts that every physical quantity—every "it"—derives its meaning and existence from binary yes/no distinctions, or bits of information. Within this framework, matter and energy are understood as derivative phenomena; the fundamental ontological primitive is distinguishability itself, formalized as information.

Three supporting pillars ground this position:

**Landauer's Principle (1961):** The erasure of one bit of information in a computational process dissipates at minimum \( kT \ln(2) \) joules of energy, where \( k \) is Boltzmann's constant (\( 1.380649 \times 10^{-23} \, \text{J} \cdot \text{K}^{-1} \)) and \( T \) is the absolute temperature of the thermal reservoir (in Kelvin). This establishes that information operations carry irreducible thermodynamic cost; information is not epiphenomenal but possesses physical weight. Experimental confirmation has been provided by Bérut et al. (2012).

**Zeilinger's Foundational Principle (1999):** Each elementary quantum system (qubit) carries exactly one bit of information. The information content is not a property of the system but is constitutive of the system itself. This principle asserts that the information-theoretic description is exhaustive—there is no sub-informational substrate.

**Quantum Information-Theoretic Ontology:** On the Everettian or information-theoretic reading of quantum mechanics, the quantum state \( |\psi\rangle \) constitutes the most complete possible description of a physical system. The wavefunction is not a representation of an underlying physical reality but is itself the ontology. No hidden variables or sub-informational "billiard balls" exist beneath the information-theoretic description.

Formally, let \( \mathcal{R} \) denote physical reality and \( \mathcal{I} \) denote the informational substrate. The relationship is expressed as:

\[
\mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I})
\]

where \( f \) is a generative function mapping the informational substrate to the manifold of physical phenomena, and \( \mathcal{I} \) is irreducible—it cannot be decomposed into more primitive constituents.

### 1.2 Domain B: Christian Theology—Johannine Prologue and Creation Theology

The Johannine Prologue (John 1:1-3, Nestle-Aland 28th edition) states:

> Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος. οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν θεόν. πάντα δι' αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο, καὶ χωρὶς αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο οὐδὲ ἕν.

Translation: "In the beginning was the Word (Logos), and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made."

This passage establishes the Logos as ontologically prior to creation and as the generative agent through which all material reality derives. The claim is total in scope: no created thing exists independently of the Logos.

The Genesis creation narrative (Genesis 1:3, Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia) provides a parallel instantiation of the same principle: "And God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light" (וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יְהִי אוֹר וַיְהִי אוֹר). The divine speech act—an information transmission event—precedes and causes the physical result.

Hebrews 11:3 (Novum Testamentum Graece) further articulates this ontology: "By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible" (Πίστει νοοῦμεν κατηρτίσθαι τοὺς αἰῶνας ῥήματι θεοῦ, εἰς τὸ μὴ ἐκ φαινομένων τὸ βλεπόμενον γεγονέναι). This explicitly asserts that visible, material reality derives from an invisible, informational/verbal cause.

Formally, let \( \mathcal{M} \) denote material reality and \( \mathcal{L} \) denote the Logos. The relationship is:

\[
\mathcal{M} = g(\mathcal{L})
\]

where \( g \) is the generative function of creation, and \( \mathcal{L} \) is ontologically prior, irreducible, and self-existent.

## 2. The Isomorphic Mapping

### 2.1 Shared Structural Features

Through structural comparison of the two domains, four isomorphic features have been identified:

**Feature 1: Ontological Priority of Information over Matter**

In both domains, the informational/verbal substrate is temporally and ontologically prior to matter and energy. The ordering is not: matter exists, then information describes it. Rather: information exists, then matter instantiates from it. In Wheeler's framework, the bit generates the "it." In the Johannine framework, the Logos generates all things. Both domains reverse the commonsense materialist ordering.

**Feature 2: Generative Function**

Information in Wheeler's framework does not merely describe reality but generates it. The bit is productive, not merely descriptive. Similarly, the divine speech act in Genesis and the Logos in John are productive: "Let there be light" does not describe light but brings it into existence. Both domains posit a generative, rather than representational, relationship between information and physical reality.

**Feature 3: Irreducibility**

In both domains, the informational substrate cannot be further decomposed. Within Wheeler's framework, one cannot meaningfully ask "what is the bit made of?"—the bit is the terminus of ontological analysis. Within the Johannine framework, one cannot ask "what preceded the Logos?"—the Logos is eternal and uncreated. Both substrates function as bedrock ontological primitives.

**Feature 4: Physical Cost of Information**

Landauer's principle demonstrates that information operations have measurable physical consequences. The biblical speech acts similarly have immediate physical consequences: "Let there be light" results in the existence of light. In neither domain is information "cheap talk"—it carries ontological and physical weight.

### 2.2 Formal Parallel

The shared structure can be expressed as a parallel formalism:

|--------------------|---------------------|
| \( \mathcal{R} = f(\mathcal{I}) \) | \( \mathcal{M} = g(\mathcal{L}) \) |
| \( \mathcal{I} \) is irreducible | \( \mathcal{L} \) is uncreated |
| \( f \) is generative | \( g \) is creative |
| Information precedes matter | Logos precedes creation |

### 2.3 Explicit Non-Claims

To maintain intellectual rigor, the following are explicitly not claimed:

1. **Shannon entropy is not the Logos.** Shannon entropy (\( H = -\sum p_i \log_2 p_i \)) measures uncertainty in a probability distribution and is a mathematical quantity. The Logos, in Christian theology, is a self-aware, personal, intentional, and divine agent. These are categorically distinct entities.

2. **John was not performing information theory.** The Johannine Prologue draws upon Hellenistic philosophical traditions—particularly Stoic λόγος doctrine and Philo of Alexandria's mediating Logos—not upon mathematical formalism. The structural parallel exists despite radically different intellectual contexts and should not be read as anachronistic projection.

3. **Physics does not prove John 1:1.** The "It from Bit" thesis is a philosophical interpretation of quantum mechanics, not a settled experimental result. Many physicists (e.g., 't Hooft, 2016, on superdeterminism) reject it. The isomorphism identifies structural correspondence, not evidential confirmation of theological claims by physical data.

4. **"Word" and "information" are not synonyms.** The Greek λόγος carries connotations of reason, proportion, speech, cosmic order, and intelligible structure that "information" in the Shannon sense does not. See Section 4 (Strain Analysis) for detailed treatment.

5. **This isomorphism may be analogical rather than fully structural.** The record is honest about this limitation and does not claim maximal strength for the mapping.

## 3. Testing Protocol

### 3.1 Test 1: Prediction Constraint

The mapping is evaluated for its capacity to constrain predictions in both domains.

**Domain A (Physics):** If information is ontologically primitive, then:
- No physical process can destroy information (unitarity). This is precisely the content of the Black Hole Information Paradox. Recent theoretical developments—including Hawking's (2004) concession, Page curve calculations (Page, 1993), and replica wormhole results (Penington et al., 2019; Almheiri et al., 2020)—support information conservation.
- Every physical law must be expressible as an information-processing operation. This constrains the space of possible physical theories.

**Domain B (Theology):** If the Logos generates all reality, then:
- No created thing exists independently of the Logos (John 1:3). This is a total-scope claim admitting no exceptions.
- Destruction of the Logos would entail destruction of all reality. This is consistent with Colossians 1:17: "in him all things hold together" (καὶ τὰ πάντα ἐν αὐτῷ συνέστηκεν).

**Verdict:** The mapping constrains predictions in both domains, but the constraints are asymmetric. Physics constraints are quantitative and experimentally testable (unitarity, Landauer bound). Theological constraints are qualitative and not experimentally testable in the same sense. **Partial pass.**

### 3.2 Test 2: Symmetry Breaking

The mapping is evaluated for whether the ordering of elements can be reversed.

Can matter be ontologically prior to information? In Wheeler's framework: no—this would collapse the thesis into classical materialism. In John's framework: no—this would contradict "all things were made through him."

Can information be derivative? In Wheeler: this would collapse the framework into a materialist substrate. In John: this would mean the Logos was created, which constitutes the Arian heresy explicitly contradicted by "the Logos was God" (θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος).

**Result: PASSED.** The ordering is non-arbitrary in both domains.

### 3.3 Test 3: Connection Density

- ISO-003 (Entropy/Sin): Information degradation in closed systems parallels moral degradation.

### 3.4 Test 4: Falsifiability Invitation

The mapping specifies conditions under which it would be falsified:

1. Demonstration that "It from Bit" is incorrect—that information is emergent from a sub-informational physical substrate (hidden variables, superdeterminism, or a future theory deriving quantum information from more fundamental primitives). If information is not ontologically primitive in physics, Domain A collapses and the mapping fails.

2. Demonstration that John's λόγος is not about information primacy but purely about divine personhood with no cosmological generative claim. While John 1:3 ("all things were made through him") makes this reading difficult, a purely existentialist interpretation of the Prologue would weaken Domain B.

3. Identification of a physical system whose behavior cannot be described informationally. If a real physical process carries zero information content, Wheeler's thesis fails.

## 4. Strain Analysis

### 4.1 The Core Objection

The most serious objection to this isomorphism is semantic: "Word" (λόγος) in John 1 and "information" (bit) in Wheeler's framework denote categorically different concepts.

**Logos (λόγος)** in Hellenistic philosophy denotes: rational principle, cosmic order, the intelligible structure of reality, speech, proportion, reason. In Philo of Alexandria (c. 20 BCE–50 CE), λόγος functions as the mediating principle between the transcendent God and the material world. In Stoic philosophy, λόγος is the active rational principle (λόγος σπερματικός) pervading the cosmos. John appropriates this loaded term and identifies it with a specific person, Jesus Christ. The Logos is therefore personal, conscious, intentional, and divine.

**Information** in Shannon's (1948) sense denotes: reduction of uncertainty, measured in bits, defined by the probability distribution over a message space. Shannon information is impersonal, unconscious, unintentional, and mathematical.

These are not the same concept. The mapping holds at the structural level (both are ontologically prior to matter, both generate physical reality) but breaks at the personal level: the Logos is a "who"; information is a "what."

### 4.2 Honest Assessment

This isomorphism is structural, not semantic. The words λόγος and "information" do not mean the same thing. What they share is a structural role: ontologically prior substrate from which physical reality derives. The mapping is real at that level but should not be pushed to identity. The Logos includes information but is not reducible to it. Information may be a shadow or aspect of the Logos but is not the whole.

**Where the mapping holds:** The claim that reality is information-first (not matter-first) maps cleanly onto the claim that reality is Logos-first (not matter-first). The generative direction, the irreducibility, and the ontological priority all match.

**Where the mapping strains:** When one asks "is information conscious?" physics answers negatively (or remains silent). When one asks "is the Logos impersonal?" theology answers absolutely not. The personal dimension of the Logos has no counterpart in Shannon information or Wheeler's bit. This constitutes a significant gap.

### 4.3 Possible Resolution Within the Framework

The Theophysics framework posits that consciousness and personhood are themselves informational phenomena, following the integrated information theory (IIT) of Tononi (2004). If consciousness is sufficiently integrated information (quantified by the index Φ), then the gap narrows: the Logos would correspond to maximally integrated information (infinite Φ), while impersonal information corresponds to the Logos at low integration (low Φ). This resolution is speculative but structurally consistent with the framework's axioms.
