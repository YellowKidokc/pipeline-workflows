# Isomorphism Registry: A Formal Registry of Cross-Domain Structural Correspondences in Theophysics

## Abstract

This document presents a systematic registry of structural isomorphism records documenting claimed cross-domain mappings between physical and theological frameworks within the Theophysics research program. Each record is evaluated against a standardized Four-Test Protocol (HYP-ISO-001) designed to discriminate genuine structural isomorphisms from mere analogies or arbitrary mappings. The registry currently contains thirty-six (36) records at various stages of testing and confidence assessment, ranging from high-confidence structural isomorphisms (e.g., ISO-001: Mathematics/Physics ↔ Trinity; ISO-002: Terminus Sui ↔ Grace) to low-confidence candidates and undetermined classifications. This document serves as the central index and methodological framework for the ongoing systematic investigation of cross-domain structural correspondences.

## 1. Introduction and Methodological Framework

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

The Isomorphism Registry constitutes a formal, living document within the Theophysics framework, designed to catalog and evaluate proposed structural correspondences between physical and theological domains. Each record (hereafter "ISO record") documents a claimed structural isomorphism, including the mapping function, swap test results, bidirectional predictions, falsification criteria, and an honest classification of the correspondence's nature and strength.

### 1.2 The Four-Test Protocol (HYP-ISO-001)

All records are evaluated against a standardized discrimination method, the Four-Test Protocol, which comprises the following criteria:

1. **Prediction Constraint**: Does the mapping generate testable predictions in both domains? A genuine structural isomorphism should constrain predictions bidirectionally, such that a confirmed prediction in one domain implies a corresponding prediction in the other.

2. **Symmetric Breaking**: Does breaking the structure in one domain produce an isomorphic breaking in the other? This test assesses whether the mapping preserves structural relations under perturbation or modification.

3. **Connection Density**: How many independent correspondences exist between the two domains? The protocol stipulates that seven (7) or more independent correspondences render the mapping statistically improbable as chance, thereby strengthening the case for genuine structural isomorphism.

4. **Falsifiability Invitation**: Does the mapping specify conditions under which it would be falsified? A genuine isomorphism must be vulnerable to empirical or logical refutation; mappings that cannot be falsified are classified as beliefs rather than structural claims.

## 4. Protocol for Adding New Records

The addition of new ISO records follows a standardized procedure to ensure methodological consistency and transparency:

1. **Template Acquisition**: Copy the file `ISOMORPHISM_TEMPLATE.md` as the foundational document for the new record.

2. **Naming Convention**: The record shall be named according to the format `ISO-[NNN]_[Short_Description].md`, where `[NNN]` is the next available sequential three-digit identifier and `[Short_Description]` is a concise descriptor of the proposed mapping.

3. **Complete Documentation**: Every field in the template must be filled. All four tests of the Four-Test Protocol must be applied and documented honestly, including negative results where applicable.

4. **Registry Update**: The README registry table (Section 2 of this document) must be updated to include the new record with its assigned ID, domains, status, confidence, and type.

5. **Visual Registry Integration**: The new record must be added to `Isomorphism_Registry.html` to maintain consistency across all registry formats.

## 5. Methodological Principles and Constraints

### 5.1 Falsifiability Requirement

A foundational principle governing this registry is that an isomorphism that cannot be falsified does not constitute a genuine isomorphism but rather a belief. This criterion is non-negotiable and applies uniformly across all records, regardless of confidence level or domain.

### 5.2 Classification Honesty

The classification "Analogy Only" is not considered a failure of the research program but rather an honest assessment of the nature of the correspondence. Such classifications are explicitly documented and maintained within the registry to prevent overinterpretation of weak correspondences.

### 5.3 The Swap Test

The swap test is mandatory for all records. This test assesses whether the roles of the two domains are interchangeable; if they are, the mapping is considered arbitrary and the record is classified accordingly. The swap test results are documented within each individual ISO record.

### 5.4 Observation Confirmation with Unsolved Mechanism

The status "OBSERVATION CONFIRMED, MECHANISM UNSOLVED" is recognized as a valid and productive classification. Within the Theophysics framework, empirical or structural observation precedes mechanistic explanation; records may therefore be maintained with high confidence in the observed correspondence even when the underlying mechanism remains unidentified.

The Isomorphism Registry provides a systematic, transparent, and falsifiable framework for investigating structural correspondences between physical and theological domains. The current registry of thirty-six records represents an ongoing research program that prioritizes methodological rigor, honest classification, and bidirectional predictive constraint. Future additions will continue to adhere to the Four-Test Protocol and the principles outlined herein.
