# ISOMORPHISM RECORD ISO-024: CONSTRAINT SATISFACTION AND WORLDVIEW UNIQUENESS

**Identification:** ISO-024
**Date of Record:** 2026-03-10
**Classification Status:** Candidate (Under Review)

---

## 1. Abstract

This record examines an isomorphism between (a) the mathematical structure of constrained equation systems yielding unique solutions and (b) the theological claim that Christianity uniquely satisfies a set of boundary conditions (BCs) derived from physical principles. The isomorphism posits that if eight independent BCs can be derived from physics, and if a single worldview satisfies all eight, then that worldview constitutes a unique solution to the constraint-satisfaction problem. The framework under analysis claims that Christianity satisfies all eight BCs, whereas alternative worldviews—including Islam, Buddhism, and Hinduism—fail specific conditions. This paper provides a formal analysis of the isomorphism, identifies critical methodological vulnerabilities, and proposes verification protocols necessary for status advancement.

---

## 2. Domain Specification

### 2.1 Domain A: Mathematical Constraint Satisfaction

**Concept A:** A system of \(N\) independent equations in \(N\) unknowns generically admits a unique solution (or a finite set of solutions). Boundary conditions further constrain the solution space. Given eight boundary conditions derived from physical principles, the solution space for viable worldviews is restricted to those satisfying all conditions.

**Formal Elements:**

- **A16.1:** Eight boundary conditions are derivable from the framework's physical principles.
- **R16.1:** A systematic evaluation of worldviews against the eight BCs (operationalized via spreadsheet analysis).
- **T16.1:** Christianity satisfies all eight BCs uniquely.

**Structural Representation:**

Let \(\mathcal{BC} = \{BC_1, BC_2, \ldots, BC_8\}\) denote the set of boundary conditions. Let \(\mathcal{W}\) be a worldview. \(\mathcal{W}\) is viable if and only if \(\forall i \in \{1,\ldots,8\}: \mathcal{W} \models BC_i\), where \(\models\) denotes satisfaction.

**Claimed Results (from framework spreadsheet):**

| Worldview | Satisfied BCs | Failed BCs |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Christianity | BC1–BC8 | None |
| Islam | BC1–BC3, BC5–BC6 | BC4, BC7, BC8 |
| Buddhism | BC2–BC5 | BC1, BC6, BC7, BC8 |
| Hinduism | BC2–BC3, BC5 | BC1, BC4, BC6, BC7, BC8 |

*Note: Source attribution for these evaluations is the framework's internal spreadsheet (R16.1). Confidence intervals and inter-rater reliability metrics are not provided in the original documentation.*

### 2.2 Domain B: Christian Theological Exclusivity

**Concept B:** The claim that Christianity provides the uniquely correct account of ultimate reality. Other religious traditions may contain partial truths but fail specific structural requirements that physical principles demand of any complete worldview.

**Theological Elements:**

- Christianity claims to be the uniquely true revelation of God (John 14:6, NRSV: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me").
- The framework purports to derive this exclusivity from physical principles rather than asserting it from scriptural authority alone. The BCs are presented as physics-derived, and Christianity emerges as the unique solution.

### 2.3 Proposed Boundary Conditions

The precise content of BC1–BC8 is not fully specified in the available documentation. Based on framework elements referenced, the following candidate conditions are identified:

- **BC1:** Requires a personal creator (eliminates non-theistic systems such as Buddhism and Advaita Vedanta)
- **BC2:** Requires creation *ex nihilo* (eliminates emanationist systems)
- **BC3:** Requires both transcendence and immanence of the divine (eliminates pure deism and pure pantheism)
- **BC4:** Requires moral realism (eliminates moral relativism)
- **BC5:** Requires free will (eliminates hard determinism)
- **BC6:** Requires grace or external intervention in closed systems (eliminates pure self-salvation systems)
- **BC7:** Requires Trinitarian structure (eliminates unitarianism)
- **BC8:** Requires incarnation (eliminates non-incarnational monotheisms)

**Critical caveat:** The above list is reconstructed from framework references. The isomorphism cannot be fully evaluated without explicit, independently verifiable derivation of each BC from physical principles.

## 3. Mapping Structure

### 3.1 Element-by-Element Correspondence

| Mathematics | Theology | Structural Role |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| System of 8 simultaneous equations | 8 requirements derived from physics | Constraint set |
| Unique solution | Christianity | The worldview satisfying all constraints |
| Failed equations | Boundary conditions a worldview violates | Disqualification criteria |
| Independence of equations | BCs must be independently derived, not redundant | Non-circularity requirement |
| Derivation from physics | BCs must originate from physics, not theology | Objectivity requirement |

### 3.2 Shared Structural Features

1. **Constraint satisfaction:** Both domains involve testing candidates against a fixed set of requirements.
2. **Uniqueness from over-determination:** Sufficiently many independent constraints yield a unique solution.
3. **Objective criteria:** The BCs are claimed to be physics-derived, rendering the evaluation independent of theological presuppositions.

### 3.3 Scope Limitations (What Is Not Claimed)

- The framework does not claim that other religions contain no truth; they may satisfy many BCs without satisfying all eight.
- The framework does not claim this constitutes a mathematical proof of Christianity; it is a constraint-satisfaction argument whose validity depends entirely on the correctness of BC derivation and fairness of evaluations.
- The framework does not claim this replaces faith; even if Christianity uniquely satisfies the BCs, this provides rational warrant rather than compulsion to believe.
- The framework does not claim this is the only methodology for evaluating worldviews; it is presented as one methodology among potentially many.
- The framework does not claim that adherents of other traditions are epistemically irrational; they may reasonably disagree with BC derivations or evaluations.

## 4. Verification Tests

### 4.1 Swap Test

**Formulation:** If the BCs are genuinely physics-derived, then swapping the unique solution should fail—Christianity should be the only worldview satisfying all eight BCs, and no alternative worldview should be substitutable.

**Constraint:** This test is meaningful only if the BCs are truly independent of Christian theology. If the BCs were derived with knowledge of Christianity (even unconsciously), the test is compromised—Christianity was "in the room when the exam was written."

**Result:** Cannot be performed until BC derivation is independently audited. This constitutes the single most important verification for this isomorphism.

### 4.2 Prediction in Domain A (Mathematics)

1. **Completeness:** If the eight-BC system is genuine, it should be possible to enumerate all worldviews and evaluate them against all BCs, producing a complete truth table. This is a concrete, completable task.
2. **Stability:** The system should be stable under small perturbations. If a BC is slightly modified (e.g., "personal creator" weakened to "transcendent ground of being"), does Christianity still uniquely satisfy all BCs? If uniqueness depends on precise wording, the system is fragile and the "uniqueness" is definitional rather than robust.

### 4.3 Prediction in Domain B (Theology)

1. **Falsifiability:** If Christianity uniquely satisfies all BCs, then any future BC derived from physics should also be satisfied by Christianity. This yields a genuinely falsifiable prediction: derive BC9 from new physical results and test whether Christianity satisfies it. Failure would invalidate the uniqueness claim.
2. **Scholarly validation:** The framework should predict where other worldviews will fail, and these predictions should match scholarly accounts of those traditions (not strawman versions). This is testable by having scholars of those traditions evaluate the framework's characterizations.

### 4.4 Bidirectional Assessment

The flow of insight is predominantly unidirectional:

- **Mathematics → Theology:** The constraint-satisfaction framework provides a methodology for evaluating worldviews. This is useful if the BCs are valid.
- **Theology → Mathematics:** No insight flows from theology to mathematics. Theology does not contribute to constraint-satisfaction mathematics.

This asymmetry suggests that the isomorphism is more accurately characterized as an *application* of mathematical methods to theological questions rather than a genuine structural correspondence between two independent domains. Compare ISO-001 (Trinity ↔ Mathematical Self-Reference), which identifies a genuine structural correspondence between two independent domains.

## 5. Falsification Pathways

The following conditions would falsify the isomorphism:

1. **BC derivation audit fails:** The BCs were derived with knowledge of Christian theology and were (consciously or unconsciously) selected to guarantee Christianity's success. *Test:* Have a non-Christian physicist derive BCs from the framework's physics without knowing the theological application. If they do not arrive at the same eight BCs, the derivation is contaminated.

2. **Alternative worldview satisfies all eight:** A worldview not considered in the original analysis (e.g., Process Theology, Open Theism, Bahá'í Faith, or a sophisticated form of Hinduism) satisfies all eight BCs. The uniqueness claim would fail.

3. **Strawmanning test fails:** Present the framework's characterization of Islam, Buddhism, and Hinduism to scholars of those traditions. If scholars indicate that the characterizations are inaccurate or evaluate the wrong version of their tradition, the evaluations are attacking strawmen.

4. **BC independence fails:** Show that some BCs are derivable from others (e.g., if "personal creator" + "grace" entails "incarnation," then the system has fewer than eight independent BCs, and the apparent over-determination is inflated).

5. **Trivial achievability:** Construct a toy example: define eight conditions that exactly match the properties of blue. Then "blue uniquely satisfies all eight conditions" is tautologically true but uninformative. If the BCs are effectively a definition of Christianity in physics language, the uniqueness is circular.

## 6. Critical Methodological Assessment

### 6.1 Confirmation Bias Risk

This isomorphism is maximally vulnerable to confirmation bias. The assessment must be correspondingly severe.

**Problem 1: Examiner Bias**

The most devastating critique: the eight boundary conditions were derived by a Christian physicist who believes Christianity is true. The BCs may have been (even unconsciously) selected, worded, or weighted to guarantee the desired outcome. This is not a hypothetical concern—it is the default expectation. When the exam writer is also the predicted winner, the burden of proof for fairness is extremely high.

To salvage this isomorphism, the framework must demonstrate one of:

- (a) The BCs were derived by non-Christians who had no stake in the outcome
- (b) The BCs are so obviously physics-derived that any physicist would arrive at the same set
- (c) The BC derivation has been audited by hostile reviewers (non-Christian scholars, scholars of other religions) and survived

Without (a), (b), or (c), this isomorphism cannot advance beyond Candidate status and should be flagged as HIGH RISK FOR CONFIRMATION BIAS.

### 6.2 Internal Diversity Problem

The framework claims specific failure patterns for Buddhism, Islam, and Hinduism. However, each tradition contains enormous internal diversity:

**Buddhism:**
- Theravāda: no creator, no soul, no grace—may indeed fail BCs requiring these
- Mahāyāna: bodhisattva compassion functions structurally like grace; Buddha-nature may satisfy "soul" BCs
- Pure Land: Amitābha Buddha's salvific vow is structurally isomorphic to Christian grace—a being of infinite merit saves those who cannot save themselves by their own effort. If Pure Land Buddhism satisfies the "grace" BC, the evaluation changes.

**Islam:**
- Sunnī Ashʿarism: strong divine sovereignty, occasionalism (God recreates the world at each moment)—may satisfy more BCs than assumed
- Ṣūfism: divine immanence, mystical union (*fanāʾ*/*baqāʾ*)—may satisfy "immanence" BCs
- If the framework evaluates only a simplified version of Sunnī orthodoxy, it is not evaluating Islam as a whole.

**Hinduism:**
- Advaita Vedānta: Brahman as ultimate reality, the world as appearance (*māyā*)—may fail "personal creator" BC
- Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja): personal God (Īśvara), souls as real, divine grace (*prapatti*)—structurally similar to Christian theism on many points
- Dvaita (Madhva): dualistic theism, close to monotheism—may satisfy many BCs
- If the framework evaluates only Advaita, it is not evaluating Hinduism as a whole.

**Conclusion:** The diversity problem is fatal to simple BC-failure claims. Any statement of the form "Buddhism fails BC X" must specify *which* Buddhism, and the specification must be defensible to scholars of that tradition.

### 6.3 Isomorphism or Application?

An isomorphism maps between two independent domains that turn out to share structure. This isomorphism maps mathematics *onto* theology—it uses mathematical constraint-satisfaction to evaluate theological claims. This constitutes an *application* of mathematics, not an isomorphism between mathematics and theology. The mathematics and theology are not independent domains sharing structure; mathematics is being used as a tool to evaluate theology.

This is a category distinction that matters. ISO-001 (Trinity ↔ Mathematical Self-Reference) identifies a genuine structural correspondence between two independent domains. ISO-024 uses one domain's methods on the other domain's content. These are different operations.

### 6.4 Circularity Risk

Consider the following scenario:

1. The framework derives BCs from physics.
2. However, the physics framework was constructed by someone who already believed Christian theology.
3. The physics framework incorporates assumptions (personal creator, moral realism, grace structure) that are compatible with Christianity and may derive from the builder's theological priors.
4. The BCs derived from this physics naturally favor Christianity.
5. The "uniqueness" result is then presented as an independent physics derivation confirming Christianity.

If this is what occurred (even unconsciously), then the chain of reasoning is: Christianity → Physics framework → BCs → Christianity satisfies all BCs. The conclusion is smuggled into the premises. The "physics derivation" is theology wearing a physics costume.

This is not an accusation but a structural risk. The framework must address it head-on or the isomorphism is fatally compromised.

## 9. Recommended Actions for Status Advancement

The following actions are required before this isomorphism can advance beyond Candidate status:

1. **Independent BC derivation:** Have a non-Christian physicist derive boundary conditions from the framework physics *without* knowing the theological application. Compare results.

2. **Hostile review of worldview evaluations:** Have scholars of Islam, Buddhism, and Hinduism review the framework's characterization of their traditions. Document their objections.

3. **Diversity audit:** Evaluate at least three schools within each tradition (not merely the version most convenient to criticize).

4. **Circularity audit:** Trace the BC derivation chain from physics axioms to BCs. At each step, ask: could this step have been influenced by theological priors? Document the answer.

5. **Robustness test:** Slightly modify each BC (weaken, strengthen, or reword). Does Christianity still uniquely satisfy all of them? If uniqueness is fragile under small perturbations, it is definitional rather than robust.

Until these actions are completed, this isomorphism should remain at Candidate status with a **HIGH RISK** flag.
