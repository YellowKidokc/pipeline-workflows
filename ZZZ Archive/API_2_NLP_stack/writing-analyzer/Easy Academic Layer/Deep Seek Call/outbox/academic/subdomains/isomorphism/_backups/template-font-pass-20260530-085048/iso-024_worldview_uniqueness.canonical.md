## ABSTRACT

This isomorphism record examines the structural correspondence between constraint-satisfaction problems in mathematical systems and the evaluation of worldviews against physics-derived boundary conditions. The central claim under investigation is that a system of eight independent boundary conditions (BCs), derivable from the framework's physical axioms, yields a unique solution in the domain of Christian theology. The analysis proceeds by formalizing the mapping between mathematical over-determination and theological exclusivity, subjecting the derivation to methodological scrutiny, and identifying critical vulnerabilities in the argumentative structure. Particular attention is given to the risk of circular reasoning, the problem of intra-traditional diversity within non-Christian worldviews, and the epistemological status of the boundary condition derivation. The isomorphism is provisionally classified as undetermined, with the recommendation that it remain at Candidate status pending independent audit of the BC derivation and peer review by scholars of the evaluated traditions.

## 1. DOMAIN SPECIFICATION

### 1.1 Domain A: Mathematical Constraint Satisfaction

Consider a system of \(N\) independent equations in \(N\) unknowns. Under generic conditions, such a system admits a unique solution (or a finite set of solutions). The imposition of boundary conditions further constrains the solution space. Formally, let \(\mathcal{E} = \{E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_N\}\) be a set of \(N\) independent equations, and let \(\mathcal{B} = \{B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_M\}\) be a set of \(M\) boundary conditions. The solution space \(\mathcal{S}\) is defined as:

\[
\mathcal{S} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^N : \forall i, E_i(x) = 0 \land \forall j, B_j(x) = 0\}
\]

For a well-posed system with \(N + M\) independent constraints, \(\mathcal{S}\) is either empty, a singleton, or a finite set. The uniqueness of the solution depends critically on the independence and non-redundancy of the constraints.

### 1.2 Domain B: Christian Theological Exclusivity

The theological claim under examination is that Christianity provides the uniquely correct account of ultimate reality, satisfying all structural requirements that physics imposes on any complete worldview. This exclusivity claim is grounded in scriptural sources (e.g., John 14:6: "Ἐγώ εἰμι ἡ ὁδὸς καὶ ἡ ἀλήθεια καὶ ἡ ζωή· οὐδεὶς ἔρχεται πρὸς τὸν πατέρα εἰ μὴ δι᾿ ἐμοῦ") and is here proposed to be derivable from physical principles rather than asserted from revelation alone.

Let \(\mathcal{BC} = \{BC_1, BC_2, \ldots, BC_8\}\) be the set of eight boundary conditions derived from the framework's physical axioms. Let \(\mathcal{W}\) be the set of all worldviews under consideration. A worldview \(W \in \mathcal{W}\) is deemed viable if and only if:

\[
\forall i \in \{1, \ldots, 8\}: W \models BC_i

where \(\models\) denotes satisfaction of the boundary condition.

## 3. BOUNDARY CONDITION SPECIFICATION

The precise content of BC1–BC8 is not provided in the available documentation. Based on the framework elements referenced, the following candidate boundary conditions are reconstructed:

- **BC1:** Requires a personal creator (eliminates non-theistic systems such as Buddhism and Advaita Vedanta)
- **BC2:** Requires creation *ex nihilo* (eliminates emanationist cosmologies)
- **BC3:** Requires divine transcendence *and* immanence (eliminates pure deism and pure pantheism)
- **BC4:** Requires moral realism (eliminates moral relativism and anti-realism)
- **BC5:** Requires libertarian free will (eliminates hard determinism)
- **BC6:** Requires grace or external intervention in closed systems (eliminates pure self-salvation systems)
- **BC7:** Requires Trinitarian structure (eliminates unitarian monotheisms)
- **BC8:** Requires incarnation (eliminates non-incarnational monotheisms)

**Epistemic caveat:** This reconstruction is provisional. The isomorphism cannot be fully evaluated without access to the original BC derivation documents.

## 4. METHODOLOGICAL ANALYSIS

### 4.1 The Problem of Circularity

The most significant methodological concern pertains to the derivation of the boundary conditions. The framework's physics was developed by a researcher who holds Christian theological commitments. The following circularity chain is structurally possible:

1. Christian theological presuppositions inform the construction of the physical framework.
2. The physical framework yields boundary conditions BC1–BC8.
3. Christianity satisfies all eight boundary conditions.
4. The result is presented as independent confirmation of Christianity from physics.

Formally, if the derivation chain is:

\[
\text{Christianity} \rightarrow \text{Physics Framework} \rightarrow \mathcal{BC} \rightarrow \text{Christianity satisfies } \mathcal{BC}
\]

then the conclusion is entailed by the premises, and the argument is circular. This is not an accusation of deliberate bias but a structural vulnerability that must be addressed.

**Required verification:** To salvage the isomorphism, one of the following must be demonstrated:

- (a) The BCs were derived by non-Christian physicists with no stake in the outcome.
- (b) The BCs are so evidently physics-derived that any competent physicist would arrive at the same set.
- (c) The BC derivation has been audited by hostile reviewers (non-Christian scholars, scholars of other religions) and survived.

Absent (a), (b), or (c), the isomorphism cannot advance beyond Candidate status.

### 4.2 The Problem of Intra-Traditional Diversity

The framework's claims regarding non-Christian worldviews fail to account for internal diversity within each tradition. This constitutes a methodological deficiency that undermines the reliability of the satisfaction assessments.

**Buddhism:** The claim that Buddhism fails BC1, BC6, BC7, and BC8 requires specification of which Buddhist tradition is being evaluated.

- Theravāda Buddhism: No creator deity, no permanent self (*anattā*), no grace — may indeed fail the specified BCs.
- Mahāyāna Buddhism: The bodhisattva ideal introduces compassion that functions structurally like grace; Buddha-nature (*tathāgatagarbha*) may satisfy "soul" BCs.
- Pure Land Buddhism: Amitābha Buddha's salvific vow (*praṇidhāna*) is structurally isomorphic to Christian grace — a being of infinite merit saves those who cannot save themselves. If Pure Land satisfies the grace BC, the evaluation changes.

**Islam:** The claim that Islam fails BC4, BC7, and BC8 requires specification.

- Sunni Ashʿarism: Strong divine sovereignty, occasionalist metaphysics (God recreates the world at each moment) — may satisfy more BCs than assumed.
- Sufism: Divine immanence, mystical union (*fanāʾ*/*baqāʾ*) — may satisfy immanence BCs.
- If the framework evaluates only a simplified version of Sunni orthodoxy, it does not evaluate Islam as a whole.

**Hinduism:** The claim that Hinduism fails BC1, BC4, BC6, BC7, and BC8 requires specification.

- Advaita Vedānta: Brahman as ultimate reality, world as *māyā* — may fail personal creator BC.
- Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja): Personal God (Īśvara), real souls, divine grace (*kṛpā*) — structurally similar to Christian theism on many points.
- Dvaita (Madhva): Dualistic theism, close to monotheism — may satisfy many BCs.

**Methodological requirement:** Any statement of the form "Buddhism fails BC X" must specify which school or tradition is being evaluated, and the characterization must be defensible to scholars of that tradition.

### 4.3 The Problem of Isomorphism vs. Application

A genuine isomorphism maps between two independent domains that exhibit shared structure. ISO-001 (Trinity ↔ Mathematical Self-Reference) exemplifies this: the structure of self-reference in mathematics corresponds to the structure of Trinitarian theology as two independent domains. ISO-024, by contrast, applies mathematical methods to theological content. Mathematics is used as a tool for evaluation, not as a domain with independent structure that mirrors theology.

This is a category distinction of methodological significance. The "isomorphism" in ISO-024 is more accurately described as a methodological application: constraint-satisfaction mathematics provides a framework for evaluating worldviews, but no structural correspondence between mathematics and theology is identified beyond the trivial fact that both involve satisfaction relations.

## 5. FALSIFICATION CONDITIONS

The isomorphism yields the following falsifiable predictions:

1. **BC derivation audit:** If a non-Christian physicist derives BCs from the framework's physics without knowledge of the theological application, and arrives at a different set of BCs, the derivation is contaminated and the isomorphism fails.

2. **Alternative worldview satisfaction:** If any worldview not considered in the original analysis (e.g., Process Theology, Open Theism, Baháʼí Faith, or a sophisticated form of Hinduism) satisfies all eight BCs, the uniqueness claim fails.

3. **Strawmanning test:** If scholars of Islam, Buddhism, or Hinduism judge the framework's characterizations of their traditions to be inaccurate or unrepresentative, the evaluations are invalid.

4. **BC independence test:** If some BCs are derivable from others (e.g., if "personal creator" + "grace" entails "incarnation"), then the system has fewer than eight independent constraints, and the apparent over-determination is inflated.

5. **Trivial satisfaction test:** If the BCs are effectively a definition of Christianity expressed in physical language, the uniqueness is tautological and uninformative.

---

## 6. SWAP TEST ANALYSIS

The swap test asks whether the unique solution can be replaced by another worldview. If the BCs are genuinely physics-derived, swapping should fail — Christianity should be the only worldview satisfying all eight BCs. However, the swap test is meaningful only if the BCs are independent of Christianity. If the BCs were derived with knowledge of Christianity (even unconsciously), the test is rigged.

**Result:** Cannot be performed until BC derivation is audited.

## 7. PREDICTIONS

### 7.1 Domain A (Mathematics)

- If the 8-BC system is genuine, it should be possible to enumerate all worldviews and check them against all BCs, producing a complete truth table.
- The system should be stable under small perturbations: if a BC is slightly modified (e.g., "personal creator" weakened to "transcendent ground of being"), Christianity should still uniquely satisfy all BCs. If uniqueness depends on precise wording, the system is fragile and the "uniqueness" is definitional.

### 7.2 Domain B (Theology)

- If Christianity uniquely satisfies all BCs, then any future BC derived from physics should also be satisfied by Christianity. This is a genuinely falsifiable prediction: derive BC9 from new physics results and check whether Christianity satisfies it.
- The framework should predict where other worldviews will fail, and the predicted failures should match scholarly accounts of those worldviews (not strawman versions).

---

## 8. BIDIRECTIONALITY ASSESSMENT

The flow of insight is predominantly one-directional:

- **Mathematics → Theology:** Constraint-satisfaction mathematics provides a methodology for evaluating worldviews. This is useful if the BCs are valid.
- **Theology → Mathematics:** No insight flows from theology to constraint-satisfaction mathematics.

This asymmetry distinguishes ISO-024 from genuine structural isomorphisms (e.g., ISO-001) where insight flows in both directions.

## 11. RECOMMENDED ACTIONS BEFORE STATUS UPGRADE

1. **Independent BC derivation:** Have a non-Christian physicist derive boundary conditions from the framework physics without knowledge of the theological application. Compare results.

2. **Hostile review of worldview evaluations:** Have scholars of Islam, Buddhism, and Hinduism review the framework's characterization of their traditions. Document their objections.

3. **Diversity audit:** Evaluate at least three schools within each tradition (not merely the version most convenient to criticize).

4. **Circularity audit:** Trace the BC derivation chain from physics axioms to BCs. At each step, ask whether the step could have been influenced by theological priors. Document the answer.

5. **Robustness test:** Slightly modify each BC (weaken, strengthen, or reword). Determine whether Christianity still uniquely satisfies all of them. If uniqueness is fragile under small perturbations, it is definitional rather than robust.
