# A Formal Theophysics Framework: Structural Isomorphisms Between Physical Law and Spiritual Coherence

## Abstract

This article presents a formal mathematical framework—designated the Theophysics Framework—that identifies ten structural isomorphisms between established physical laws and proposed spiritual principles governing what is termed "coherence" (χ). The framework is operationalized through a master equation integrating ten dimensionless variables across spatiotemporal domains. Seven empirical and formal tests are reported, including computational stability analysis, cosmological prediction against observational data, sensitivity analysis of multiplicative interdependence, application of Shannon's channel capacity theorem to truth transmission, Kolmogorov complexity analysis of truth versus deception, dynamical modeling of grace as a negentropic term, and correction of an initial mathematical error in quantum uncertainty modification. Results indicate that the framework produces stable solutions, yields a testable prediction regarding the Hubble tension (with a decisive falsification opportunity via the Euclid telescope in October 2026), and demonstrates structural consistency across all seven tests. The joint probability of the ten isomorphisms arising by chance is estimated at ≤10⁻²⁰ under generous assumptions. The framework does not claim proof of theological propositions but asserts that it has met the threshold for serious scientific consideration.

## 3. The Isomorphism Thesis: Formal Definition and Probability Analysis

### 3.1 Definition of Isomorphism

In the present context, an *isomorphism* between a physical law and a spiritual law is defined as a mapping that preserves four structural properties:

1. **Differential equation structure**: Both domains satisfy equations of identical mathematical form

**Background**: Shannon's channel capacity theorem (Shannon, 1948) establishes an upper bound on the rate at which information can be reliably transmitted through a noisy channel. For a relay chain of \(n\) intermediaries, each with fidelity \(f\), the total information preserved is \(f^n\).

**Methodology**: Truth transmission was modeled as a signal passing through 20 intermediaries, each operating at 90% fidelity. The preserved signal fraction was computed as:
