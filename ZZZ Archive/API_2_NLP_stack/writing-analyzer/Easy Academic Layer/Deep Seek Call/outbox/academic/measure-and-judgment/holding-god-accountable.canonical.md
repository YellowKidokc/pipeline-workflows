# Theophysics: A Formal Framework for Cross-Domain Synthesis Between Physics and Theology

## Abstract

This article presents a systematic methodological framework—Theophysics—that restores teleological inquiry ("Why" questions) to the center of empirical investigation. The framework is predicated on the observation that the exclusion of causal-mechanistic explanation from post-1927 scientific methodology has produced measurable fragmentation across disciplines, as evidenced by replication failure rates ranging from 11% to 62% across major scientific domains. The Theophysical Method proposes six axioms grounded in information theory, structural coherence invariants, and cross-domain pattern recognition. Evidence is presented along two vectors: (1) biblical texts containing empirically verifiable claims with an average priority gap of 2,700 years relative to scientific confirmation, and (2) empirical results in contemporary physics and cosmology that generate explanatory gaps resistant to naturalistic closure. A comparative scorecard across ten foundational phenomena yields 39/50 for Biblical Theism versus 21/50 for Scientific Naturalism under explanatory completeness criteria. The Universal Theory Defense Grading System (UTDGS) is introduced as an alternative to existing academic metrics. A live falsification test is scheduled for October 2026. All axioms include explicit kill conditions.

**Keywords:** Theophysics, cross-domain synthesis, information theory, structural coherence, replication crisis, fine-tuning, teleological explanation

## I. The Methodological Problem: Fragmentation as Evidence of Axiomatic Failure

### 1.1 The Axiomatic Foundation of Inquiry

Every system of inquiry—whether scientific, philosophical, or theological—commences from axioms that cannot be proven within the system itself. This is not a weakness but a structural necessity, as demonstrated by Gödel's incompleteness theorems (Gödel, 1931), which establish that any sufficiently powerful formal system contains propositions that are true but unprovable within that system. The question is not whether a system has axioms, but whether those axioms are explicitly acknowledged and whether they enable or constrain the system's capacity for explanatory integration.

The exclusion of teleological inquiry—the question "Why?"—from post-1927 scientific methodology represents an axiomatic choice with measurable consequences. When a methodology systematically avoids causal-mechanistic explanation, it produces disciplinary fragmentation. This fragmentation is empirically observable as the replication crisis, the proliferation of incompatible interpretations within quantum mechanics (at least twelve distinct interpretations as of 2024), and the persistent failure of unification programs across fundamental physics.

### 1.2 The Replication Crisis as Empirical Evidence

The replication crisis provides quantitative evidence that the methodological exclusion of "Why" questions has produced systemic instability. Table 1 presents replication rates across major scientific domains, drawn from published meta-analyses.

However, the correction has been partial and has not addressed the deeper methodological question: whether the exclusion of teleological explanation is itself a contributing factor to the observed instability.

The ideal verification rate for scientific claims is 100%. The actualized rate ranges from 11% to 62% depending on the field. This discrepancy warrants examination of the method itself, not merely its execution.

**Axiom 4: Causal Mechanism Over Statistical Correlation.** A correlation does not constitute explanation until the causal mechanism is identified. Brute-force data fitting without causal understanding is rejected as methodologically insufficient.

**Axiom 5: The Simplicity Filter (Occam's Veto).** If a model introduces ontological complexity disproportionate to its explanatory power—as in the approximately 10^500 solutions of String Theory (Douglas, 2003)—it is likely engaged in what may be termed "ontological accounting fraud": the multiplication of entities without empirical warrant.

**Axiom 6: Tenacity Until Resolution.** The inquiry does not terminate at "good enough for publication" but continues until every structural implication is resolved into the whole. This axiom explicitly rejects the publish-or-perish incentive structure that prioritizes novelty over completeness.

The standard objection to theophysical measurement is that God cannot be measured as a photon is measured. This objection is accepted as valid. However, it is possible to measure the systemic consequences of the absence of the Logos—what may be termed the "shadow" of divine presence.

## Appendix: Assumptions and Limitations

**The scorecard assumes explanatory completeness is the right metric.** A system could score low on "Why" questions and still be empirically superior at prediction. The present analysis weighted explanation over prediction—a defensible but not neutral choice.

**The 12 invariants assume informational persistence is the goal.** If one rejects the framing that information is fundamental, the invariants lose their necessity status. This assumption is grounded in the Shannon/Boltzmann equivalence, but it remains an assumption.

**The UTDGS has not been externally calibrated.** It was constructed and applied by the author. Until other researchers apply it to other frameworks and compare results, it remains a proposed metric, not a validated one.
