# The 7Q Framework: A Reverse-Order Epistemic Methodology for Theory Evaluation

## Abstract

This article presents the 7Q Framework, a reverse-order epistemic methodology for theory evaluation that inverts the conventional scientific method. Rather than commencing with a hypothesis and seeking confirmatory evidence—a process susceptible to confirmation bias—the 7Q approach begins with systematic destruction testing, wherein candidate theories are subjected to explicit falsification conditions prior to any constructive argumentation. The framework proceeds through seven sequential stages: destruction, forced consequences, foundational grounding, evidential support, precise statement, cross-domain generalization, and terminal naming. This paper argues that the reverse ordering confers epistemic advantages by ensuring that theoretical identity is earned through survival rather than assumed a priori. The framework is demonstrated through a worked example employing General Relativity as the test case, illustrating how each stage operates within a mature physical theory. The 7Q Framework constitutes a methodological contribution to theophysics, offering a structured protocol for evaluating claims that span physics and theology.

---

## 1. Introduction: The Problem of Assumed Identity

The conventional scientific method proceeds in a forward direction: hypothesis formation, data collection, confirmatory testing, and conclusion announcement (Popper, 1959). This sequence embeds a fundamental epistemic vulnerability: the hypothesis carries its identity—its name, its claim structure, its explanatory ambition—from the outset. The investigator is thus positioned to confirm what they already believe, introducing confirmation bias as a structural feature rather than an incidental contaminant (Nickerson, 1998).

The 7Q Framework proposes an inversion of this sequence. Rather than beginning with a named hypothesis and seeking its verification, the reverse method commences with systematic destruction: the explicit enumeration of conditions under which the candidate claim would fail. Identity is deferred to the terminal stage, awarded only after the claim has survived successive layers of epistemic scrutiny. The methodological thesis is as follows: *a claim earns its identity through demonstrated survival, not through antecedent assertion.*

This paper presents the 7Q Framework in formal detail, with each stage explicated through both abstract description and concrete application to General Relativity (GR). The choice of GR as the worked example is deliberate: it is a mature, empirically successful theory whose structure is well-understood, allowing the framework's operations to be demonstrated without ambiguity.

## 2. Stage 1 (Q7): Destruction—Explicit Enumeration of Falsification Conditions

### 2.1 Methodological Function

The initial stage of the 7Q Framework inverts the conventional starting point. Rather than stating what one believes, the investigator enumerates *every identifiable condition under which the candidate claim would be falsified*. This constitutes a mapping of what may be termed the "kill zone": the set of empirical, logical, and structural conditions whose satisfaction would entail the claim's invalidity.

The epistemic rationale is grounded in Popperian falsificationism (Popper, 1959), but extends beyond it. Where Popper emphasized empirical falsifiability as a demarcation criterion, the 7Q Framework demands a multi-dimensional death condition set encompassing:

1. **Self-refutation**: Does the claim undermine its own truth conditions?
2. **Infinite regress**: Does the claim's justification require an unending chain?
3. **Empirical contradiction**: Does available or theoretically possible evidence contradict the claim?
4. **Logical incoherence**: Does the claim violate the laws of non-contradiction or other logical constraints?
5. **Explanatory failure**: Does the claim fail to account for phenomena within its purported domain?

The governing principle may be stated as: *If one cannot specify how a claim could die, one cannot determine whether it is alive.*

### 2.2 Application to General Relativity

Prior to any exposition of GR's content, the following death conditions are specified:

1. **GR dies if** light does not bend in the presence of gravitational fields (contradiction of geodesic deviation).
2. **GR dies if** gravitational waves are not physically realizable (contradiction of linearized wave solutions).
3. **GR dies if** Global Positioning System (GPS) satellites require no relativistic correction (contradiction of gravitational time dilation).
4. **GR dies if** spacetime singularities are mathematically impossible (contradiction of the Penrose-Hawking singularity theorems).
5. **GR dies if** the equivalence principle is empirically false (contradiction of the theory's foundational axiom).

These conditions are enumerated before any constructive argument is advanced. The reader is positioned as an epistemic witness: these are the ways the theory could fail; subsequent stages will demonstrate whether it does.

## 3. Stage 2 (Q6): Force—Extraction of Necessary Consequences

### 3.1 Methodological Function

If the candidate claim survives the destruction stage—that is, if none of the enumerated death conditions are satisfied—the investigator proceeds to extract the claim's *necessary consequences*. This stage addresses the question: *What must the survivor necessarily produce if it is true?*

The distinction between contingent predictions and forced consequences is critical. Contingent predictions are those that might follow from a theory under specific auxiliary assumptions. Forced consequences are those that follow from the theory's core structure regardless of auxiliary conditions. A claim that generates no forced consequences is epistemically inert: it may survive destruction but explains nothing downstream.

The governing principle: *A surviving claim that generates no necessary consequences explains nothing.*

### 3.2 Application to General Relativity

If spacetime curvature is determined by mass-energy distribution (the Einstein field equations), then the following consequences are forced:

1. **Black holes must exist**: Extreme curvature produces event horizons where geodesic incompleteness obtains (Schwarzschild, 1916; Oppenheimer & Snyder, 1939).
2. **Gravitational waves must propagate**: Linearized perturbations of the metric yield wave solutions propagating at the speed of light (Einstein, 1916).
3. **Gravitational time dilation must occur**: The metric's temporal component varies with gravitational potential (Hafele & Keating, 1972).
4. **Light must follow curved geodesics**: Null geodesics in curved spacetime deviate from Euclidean straight lines (Eddington, 1919).

Einstein did not predict these phenomena as hypotheses; the mathematical structure of GR forced them as necessary consequences. The distinction is epistemologically significant: forced consequences carry greater confirmatory weight than contingent predictions.

## 4. Stage 3 (Q5): Ground—Foundational Tracing

### 4.1 Methodological Function

Having established that the claim survives destruction and generates necessary consequences, the investigator now traces the claim's *foundational dependencies*. This stage addresses the question: *What must already be true for this claim to hold?*

The tracing proceeds by identifying the chain of assumptions upon which the claim depends. This chain terminates at one of three endpoints:

1. **Axiom**: A foundational proposition that is assumed without proof (bedrock).
2. **Brute fact**: A proposition that is asserted without further justification (assertion).
3. **Circularity**: A proposition that depends upon the claim it is meant to support (loop).

The governing principle: *The reader sees the foundations not because they are claimed, but because the structure requires them.*

### 4.2 Application to General Relativity

GR's foundational dependencies trace to the following:

1. **Equivalence principle**: The local equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass (axiom; Einstein, 1907).
2. **Riemannian geometry**: The assumption that spacetime is a smooth, differentiable manifold with a metric tensor (mathematical framework; Riemann, 1854).
3. **Constancy of the speed of light**: The invariance of \(c\) in all inertial frames (empirical postulate; Michelson-Morley, 1887).
4. **Conservation of energy-momentum**: The vanishing of the covariant divergence of the stress-energy tensor (physical principle; Noether, 1918).

The chain terminates at the equivalence principle, which functions as an axiom—it is not derived from more fundamental principles. The smooth manifold assumption constitutes a hidden root that may prove invalid at quantum scales, where spacetime discreteness is hypothesized (Rovelli, 2004).

## 5. Stage 4 (Q4): Support—Evidential Confirmation After Survival

### 5.1 Methodological Function

Evidence enters the 7Q Framework at Stage 4, *after* the claim has survived destruction, demonstrated forced consequences, and had its foundations traced. This ordering is deliberate and epistemologically significant: evidence in the reverse framework functions as *confirmation* of an already-standing structure, not as *construction* of the argument.

The distinction is subtle but critical. In the forward method, evidence builds the case; in the reverse method, evidence confirms what has already survived. The confirmatory role is not diminished—indeed, it is arguably strengthened, because the evidence arrives after the reader has already witnessed the claim's survival through more fundamental tests.

The governing principle: *The evidence did not build the case; it confirmed what survived.*

### 5.2 Application to General Relativity

The following empirical confirmations are adduced:

1. **Mercury's perihelion precession**: Predicted value of 43 arcseconds per century (Einstein, 1915); confirmed within observational uncertainty (Clemence, 1947).
2. **Light bending during solar eclipse**: Predicted deflection of 1.75 arcseconds at the solar limb (Einstein, 1916); confirmed by Eddington's expedition (Dyson, Eddington, & Davidson, 1920).
3. **Gravitational wave detection**: Predicted by Einstein (1916); directly detected by LIGO (Abbott et al., 2016), with statistical significance exceeding 5.1σ.
4. **GPS relativistic corrections**: Predicted gravitational time dilation of approximately 45 microseconds per day (Hafele & Keating, 1972); operational confirmation through satellite navigation systems (Ashby, 2003).

By the time evidence is presented, the reader has already observed: what kills GR (Stage 1), what GR forces (Stage 2), and what GR stands on (Stage 3). The evidence functions as confirmation of a structure already demonstrated to be standing.

## 6. Stage 5 (Q3): State—Precise Formulation of the Surviving Claim

### 6.1 Methodological Function

Only at Stage 5 is the claim stated with precision. It is no longer a hypothesis; it is a *conclusion*—what remains after destruction, forced consequences, foundational tracing, and evidential confirmation. The reader has witnessed the entire process; the claim requires belief no longer, only acknowledgment as what survived.

The governing principle: *This is not a hypothesis; this is what is left.*

### 6.2 Application to General Relativity

The surviving claim is stated as follows:

> **The distribution of mass-energy determines the curvature of spacetime, and free-falling objects follow geodesics through that curvature.**

Formally, this is expressed through the Einstein field equations:

\[
R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}
\]

where:
- \(R_{\mu\nu}\) is the Ricci curvature tensor (units: m\(^{-2}\))
- \(R\) is the scalar curvature (units: m\(^{-2}\))
- \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is the metric tensor (dimensionless)
- \(\Lambda\) is the cosmological constant (units: m\(^{-2}\))
- \(G\) is Newton's gravitational constant (units: m\(^3\) kg\(^{-1}\) s\(^{-2}\))
- \(c\) is the speed of light (units: m s\(^{-1}\))
- \(T_{\mu\nu}\) is the stress-energy tensor (units: J m\(^{-3}\))

This statement appears at Stage 5, not Stage 1. The reader already knows what kills it, what it forces, what it stands on, and what data confirms it. The statement is a summary, not a starting point.

## 7. Stage 6 (Q2): Generalize—Cross-Domain Structural Isomorphism

### 7.1 Methodological Function

A claim that has survived the preceding five stages may still be domain-specific. Stage 6 tests whether the claim's *structure* appears across multiple domains. If the same formal architecture holds when variables are relabeled, this constitutes evidence of structural isomorphism—a strong indicator that the claim captures something real rather than merely local.

The governing principle: *This is not local; it survives across domains.*

### 7.2 Application to General Relativity

The Riemannian geometric structure upon which GR depends appears in multiple domains independent of physics:

1. **Machine learning**: Manifold learning algorithms assume data lie on low-dimensional Riemannian manifolds embedded in high-dimensional spaces (Tenenbaum, de Silva, & Langford, 2000).
2. **Economics**: Utility surfaces in consumer theory exhibit curvature properties analogous to spacetime curvature (Debreu, 1972).
3. **Information theory**: Fisher information geometry employs Riemannian metrics on statistical manifolds (Amari & Nagaoka, 2000).

These are not analogies; they are structural isomorphisms. The mathematical architecture GR depends upon—Riemannian geometry on smooth manifolds—exists independently of physical spacetime. This cross-domain presence strengthens the claim that GR's structure is not an artifact of physical contingency but reflects a deeper mathematical reality.

## 8. Stage 7 (Q1): Name—Identity Earned Through Survival

### 8.1 Methodological Function

The terminal stage awards the claim its name. Every layer has been examined: destruction conditions (Stage 1), forced consequences (Stage 2), foundational dependencies (Stage 3), evidential confirmation (Stage 4), precise statement (Stage 5), and cross-domain generalization (Stage 6). Identity is not assumed; it is earned. Everything that could have died is dead; what remains has been tested, traced, supported, stated, and generalized.

The governing principle: *Now you know what it is, because you watched everything else fail.*

### 8.2 Application to General Relativity

After all preceding stages, the theory is named:

> **General Relativity: a foundational causal theory of spacetime curvature, derived from the equivalence principle, confirmed across 100+ years of empirical testing, with one known open question at quantum scales.**

The name carries meaning because it was earned through the full 7Q process. The reader has witnessed the theory's survival through systematic destruction testing, observed its forced consequences, traced its foundations, reviewed its confirmatory evidence, examined its precise formulation, and recognized its cross-domain structural presence.

## 9. The Full Sequence: Summary and Epistemic Implications

The 7Q Framework proceeds through seven stages in reverse order:

| Stage | Designation | Function | Transition |
|-------|-------------|----------|------------|
| 1 (Q7) | Destroy | Enumerate death conditions | unknown → vulnerable |
| 2 (Q6) | Force | Extract necessary consequences | vulnerable → constrained |
| 3 (Q5) | Ground | Trace foundational dependencies | constrained → grounded |
| 4 (Q4) | Support | Adduce confirmatory evidence | grounded → supported |
| 5 (Q3) | State | Formulate precise claim | supported → stated |
| 6 (Q2) | Generalize | Demonstrate cross-domain isomorphism | stated → located |
| 7 (Q1) | Name | Award identity through survival | located → defined (earned) |

The epistemic trajectory is: death condition → consequence → dependency → evidence → assertion → location → identity.

The framework's central epistemic claim is that this reverse ordering confers advantages over the forward method. Specifically:

1. **Confirmation bias is structurally mitigated**: Identity is deferred, so the investigator cannot prematurely commit to a named hypothesis.
2. **Falsification is prioritized**: Destruction conditions are specified before constructive argumentation begins.
3. **Evidence plays a confirmatory rather than constructive role**: Evidence confirms what has already survived more fundamental tests.
4. **Identity is earned**: The claim's name carries the weight of demonstrated survival.

---

## 10. Conclusion

The 7Q Framework offers a structured methodology for theory evaluation that inverts the conventional scientific method. By beginning with systematic destruction testing and deferring identity to the terminal stage, the framework aims to produce conclusions that are epistemically robust—not because they were believed from the outset, but because they survived everything that could kill them.

The worked example of General Relativity demonstrates the framework's applicability to mature physical theories. Future work will extend the framework to theological claims, where the reverse ordering may prove particularly valuable given the epistemological challenges inherent in theology as a discipline.
