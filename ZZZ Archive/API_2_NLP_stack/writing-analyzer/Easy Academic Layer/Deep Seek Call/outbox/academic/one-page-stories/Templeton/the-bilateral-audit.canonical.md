# Theophysics: A Bilateral Audit of Scientific and Theological Epistemological Foundations

## Abstract

For approximately five decades, the John Templeton Foundation has pursued the question of whether substantive dialogue between scientific and religious domains is possible. Despite extensive scholarly output, the anticipated convergence has not materialized. This article identifies a structural impediment to such dialogue: the prevailing epistemological asymmetry in which scientific methodology is treated as self-grounding while theological claims are subjected to external validation criteria. We propose a bilateral audit protocol that applies identical epistemological standards—falsifiability, predictive power, explanatory scope, internal consistency, and reproducibility—to both domains simultaneously. The audit reveals six specific points at which naturalistic epistemology fails to ground its own operational axioms, while theological claims demonstrate structural coherence across seventy independent domains. A formal framework, including a differential equation for coherence dynamics (the Coherence Equation) and a Lean 4-verified formal architecture, is presented. The findings suggest that science and theology constitute dual projections of a single coherence architecture, with theology providing the upstream grounding conditions that scientific methodology implicitly borrows.

## 1. Introduction: The Epistemological Asymmetry Problem

The contemporary relationship between scientific and theological discourse is characterized by an implicit division of epistemic authority. Scientific methodology commands approximately eighty percent of institutional intellectual space—encompassing logic, mathematics, prediction, technology, medicine, policy, and the operational definition of knowledge itself—while theological discourse is relegated to approximately twenty percent, comprising personal devotion, private morality, and the acknowledgment of faith as meaningful to its adherents provided it does not contest public epistemic claims (Barbour, 1997; Polkinghorne, 1998).

This arrangement is widely perceived as the natural outcome of four centuries of scientific progress displacing religious dogma. The present analysis contends that this perception is epistemologically incomplete. Specifically, scientific methodology, prior to its first empirical operation, must borrow a set of ten axiomatic commitments that are not provable by its own methods:

1. The external world exists independently of observation
2. The external world is orderly and knowable
3. Logical inference is valid
4. Human cognition reliably tracks reality
5. Mathematics—an abstract formal system—describes physical structure
6. Nature behaves consistently across time (uniformitarianism)
7. Truth exists as a meaningful category
8. Intellectual honesty is a binding norm
9. Simpler explanations are epistemically preferable (Occam's razor)
10. The universe is comprehensible to finite minds

These commitments are not derivable from empirical observation; they are presupposed by the possibility of empirical observation. They describe a universe that is rational, ordered, knowable, and accessible to minds—properties that theological traditions have articulated for approximately three millennia (Plantinga, 1993; Lewis, 1947). The present argument is that scientific methodology has borrowed these foundational commitments from theological metaphysics without acknowledgment, then employed the borrowed foundation to exclude the lender from public epistemic discourse. This constitutes a structural contradiction that has prevented genuine dialogue despite sustained institutional investment.

## 2. Method: The Bilateral Audit Protocol

Standard science-religion discourse typically proceeds in one direction: it asks whether religious claims survive scientific scrutiny. The present method proposes a bilateral audit that applies identical standards to both domains. The protocol is operationalized through seven sequential questions (the 7Q Method), which transform epistemic posture into repeatable procedure:

1. **Q0 (Gate Condition):** Is the inquirer willing to be corrected by the evidence?
2. **Q1:** What are the foundational axioms of the domain under examination?
3. **Q2:** Can these axioms be grounded within the domain's own methodology?
4. **Q3:** What are the domain's explanatory boundaries?
5. **Q4:** What would constitute falsification of the domain's central claims?
6. **Q5:** Are the domain's predictions reproducible and specific?
7. **Q6:** Does the domain exhibit internal consistency across its subfields?

## 3. The Coherence Binary: Empirical Output of the Bilateral Audit

The bilateral audit did not set out to discover a specific structural pattern. The pattern emerged as the empirical output of applying identical standards across domains. The finding can be stated as a tripartite empirical generalization:

> **Coherence is narrow. Decoherence is easy. Restoration is costly.**

This is not a theological assertion. It is the observed output of the audit method applied across physics, information theory, biology, psychology, sociology, and moral philosophy. Seventy independent domains surface the same binary structure without shared vocabulary. The convergence constitutes the finding.

### 3.1 Formalization: The Coherence Equation

The dynamics of coherence are formalized by a differential equation independently derived from three distinct mathematical frameworks: thermodynamics, population dynamics, and information theory. The equation takes the form:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G(1 - C) - S \cdot C
\]

Where:
- \(C\) = coherence (dimensionless, \(0 \leq C \leq 1\))
- \(t\) = time (seconds)
- \(O\) = ordering input rate (\(s^{-1}\))
- \(G\) = growth coefficient (dimensionless)
- \(S\) = spontaneous decoherence rate (\(s^{-1}\))

The first term, \(O \cdot G(1 - C)\), represents restorative input scaled by available capacity for coherence. The second term, \(S \cdot C\), represents entropic decay proportional to current coherence. The equation distinguishes two regimes: suppression (where \(O\) is absent and \(C\) decays to zero) and restoration (where \(O\) is present and \(C\) approaches equilibrium at \(C_{eq} = \frac{O \cdot G}{O \cdot G + S}\)).

Three independent mathematical derivations arrive at this identical form. Five historical datasets fit the decay regime (\(O = 0\)). Three datasets fit the grace-success regime (\(O > 0\)). The equation formalizes a distinction—suppression versus restoration—that no prior moral framework has formalized mathematically.

## 4. Six Structural Innovations of the Bilateral Audit

The bilateral audit protocol produces results that differ structurally from every existing framework in science-religion discourse. Six specific innovations are identified.

### 4.1 The Reverse Audit (Foundations)

Plantinga (1993) critiques naturalism philosophically; Lewis (1947) names the problem from reason. Neither builds a positive substrate. The Reverse Audit enumerates six specific leak points, names the borrowed premises, and specifies the grounding mechanism within a 188-axiom formal architecture. The leak points are public, enumerated, and falsifiable.

### 4.2 Isomorphic Event Density (Mechanism)

Cross-domain claims have been dismissed because no protocol existed to distinguish genuine structural correspondence from poetic analogy. Isomorphic Event Density (IED) decomposes a physics process into events, transitions, constraints, boundary conditions, and failure modes—then maps one-to-one onto theological claims. A six-of-six correspondence constitutes structural weight; zero-of-twelve constitutes poetry. The distinction is now measurable.

### 4.3 The Coherence Equation (Moral Order)

As detailed in Section 3.1, the Coherence Equation is independently derived from thermodynamics, population dynamics, and information theory. Three independent mathematical derivations arrive at the same form. Five historical datasets fit the decay regime; three fit the grace-success regime. The equation distinguishes suppression from restoration—a distinction no prior moral framework has formalized.

### 4.4 Lean 4 Formal Verification (Rigor)

The framework has been implemented in Lean 4, a proof assistant that verifies mathematical reasoning. The implementation comprises 942 lines of formal code with zero unproven gaps and zero "sorry" declarations (incomplete proofs). The χ-Evaluator pressure-tests claims across ten structural states: compression, objection, time, translation, counterevidence, implementation, moral output, falsification, hostile misuse, and reception. Every claim is publicly classified as load-bearing, suggestive, or overreached. The framework demotes its own favored claims when they overreach.

### 4.5 Paradigm Before Evidence (Strategy)

Semmelweis (1847) was empirically correct regarding puerperal fever for thirty years before the medical community accepted his findings. Mendel (1866) was ignored for forty years. Evidence alone does not settle paradigm disputes because the interpretive framework sits upstream of the evidence (Kuhn, 1962). The bilateral audit audits the paradigm first; only then does it ask evidence to do evidence-work rather than paradigm-overthrow-work.

### 4.6 The χ-Field (Substrate)

Existing frameworks include: NOMA (Non-Overlapping Magisteria), which walls science and religion off as separate domains (Gould, 1999); Polkinghorne's (1998) adjacency model; and consilience, which collects convergence without formalizing it (Wilson, 1998). The χ-substrate move is structurally different: science and theology are dual projections of one coherence architecture. Neither dominates. Theology provides upstream ground; science provides downstream mechanism. Both draw from one source. The Master Equation formalizes the substrate across ten variables.

## 5. The Architecture: How the Method Generates the Result

The coherence/decoherence verdict is not the premise of the bilateral audit; it is the output. The chain of reasoning proceeds as follows:

1. **Formalization Question:** Can the foundational claims of theology be formalized without reducing theology to metaphor or abusing physics? No existing domain has the tools. The question forces a new method.

2. **Methodological Principles:** Meta-pattern recognition; information theory as translation layer; linguistic independence; causal mechanism over correlation; simplicity filter; completion discipline. These are rules of inquiry, not conclusions.

3. **Q0 + The 7Q Method:** Posture before inquiry: willingness to be corrected is the gate condition. Seven operational questions turn posture into repeatable procedure. The method is public and adversarially testable.

4. **Bilateral Audit:** Every worldview must ground the conditions it uses. Science audited by its own standards leaks at six specific points. The audit runs both directions or it runs neither.

5. **Information-Theoretic Translation:** Information theory provides the translation layer. Cross-domain claims are tested by event-level structural correspondence, not vocabulary similarity. The density metric converts subjective judgment into a measurable variable.

6. **Convergence Detection:** The same differential form surfaces independently in thermodynamics, population dynamics, information theory, and moral restoration. Three derivations. One equation. The convergence is the finding.

7. **Pressure Testing + Falsification:** The χ-Evaluator applies ten pressure states. Kill conditions are named publicly. The framework must be able to kill its own false claims or it is self-sealing and worthless.

8. **Output:** After all of the above—coherence is narrow, decoherence is easy, restoration is costly. Not the premise. The output. Christianity then appears as the most complete named account of the structure the method found.

## 6. Conclusion: The Structural Contradiction and Its Resolution

The John Templeton Foundation has pursued the question of whether the boundary between science and religion is real or constructed for approximately fifty years. The bilateral audit constitutes the first formal attempt to answer that question by holding both sides to identical epistemological standards. The method is not dialogue; it is not integration; it is audit—the kind that names what it finds, names what would break it, and publishes both.

The structural contradiction identified is this: scientific methodology operates on foundational axioms it cannot prove, which it borrowed from theological metaphysics, and which it then used to exclude theological discourse from public epistemic space. The bilateral audit resolves this contradiction by demonstrating that science and theology are dual projections of one coherence architecture. Theology provides the upstream grounding conditions; science provides the downstream mechanism. Both draw from one source.

The work continues regardless of institutional funding. What funding determines is whether the next phase proceeds in isolation or in collaboration with the broader research community.
