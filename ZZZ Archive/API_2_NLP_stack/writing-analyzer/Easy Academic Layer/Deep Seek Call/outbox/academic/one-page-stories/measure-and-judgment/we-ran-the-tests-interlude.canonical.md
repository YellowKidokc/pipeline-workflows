# Theophysics: An Empirical Framework for Cross-Domain Validation

## Abstract

This article presents a formalized framework for testing theophysical propositions through a structured series of seven empirical checks. The framework is predicated on the principle that any theoretically robust proposal—whether within physics, theology, or their interdisciplinary intersection—must be subjected to falsification risk. Rather than appealing to epistemic trust, the framework directs attention to the testable outcomes themselves. This paper constitutes the reader-facing summary; the complete canonical treatment, including full derivations, tabular data, and source structures, is referenced herein.

## I. Thesis Statement

The framework under examination was subjected to seven distinct validation checks, each designed to assess its coherence, predictive capacity, and resistance to disconfirmation. The central claim is that a theophysical model must satisfy criteria analogous to those applied in formal scientific theory evaluation, including but not limited to internal consistency, empirical adequacy, and explanatory scope.

## II. Methodological Context

The seven tests were identified through a structural comparison of validation protocols employed in theoretical physics (e.g., Popperian falsification criteria, Bayesian confirmation metrics) and systematic theology (e.g., doctrinal coherence, scriptural fidelity). The framework does not collapse these domains into a single narrative but maintains their distinct epistemological commitments while identifying isomorphic validation structures.

## III. The Seven Tests: Summary

The framework was evaluated across the following seven dimensions, each representing a necessary condition for theoretical viability:

1. **Internal Logical Consistency** – Absence of formal contradiction within the model's axioms.
2. **Empirical Predictivity** – Capacity to generate testable, non-trivial predictions.
3. **Explanatory Scope** – Coverage of phenomena across both physical and theological domains.
4. **Falsifiability** – Specification of conditions under which the framework would be disconfirmed.
5. **Parsimony** – Adherence to Occam's principle without loss of explanatory power.
6. **Scriptural Coherence** – Alignment with canonical theological texts under standard hermeneutical methods.
7. **Cross-Domain Invariance** – Preservation of structural claims when translated between physical and theological formalisms.
