## 1. Introduction: The Measurement Problem in Normative Discourse

The relationship between temperature and kinetic energy provides an instructive analogy for the present investigation. Temperature, as measured by a thermometer, and mean molecular kinetic energy, as calculated from particle velocities, are not merely analogous quantities but identical physical phenomena described at different scales of observation (Callen, 1985). One measures bulk thermodynamic behavior; the other measures microscopic motion. Both report the same underlying reality through distinct measurement apparatuses.

This article extends this principle of measurement equivalence to the domain of normative discourse. We propose that the terms "good," "right," "true," "beautiful," "holy," "healthy," "signal," and "order"—across their respective disciplinary contexts—constitute measurements of a single variable: the coherence function χ, derived from the Master Equation governing system dynamics (see Terminus Sui, this volume). The variable χ ranges from maximum coherence (σ = +1) to maximum decoherence (σ = −1), where σ represents the normalized coherence parameter.

**Thesis:** Every term in human language denoting positive normative evaluation constitutes a measurement of σ = +1 within its respective disciplinary measurement frame. Conversely, every term denoting negative normative evaluation constitutes a measurement of σ = −1. This isomorphism is not metaphorical but ontological: the same physical variable is being measured through different observational protocols.
