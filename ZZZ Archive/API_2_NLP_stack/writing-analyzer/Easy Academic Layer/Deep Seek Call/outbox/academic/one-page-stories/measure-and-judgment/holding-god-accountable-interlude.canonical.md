# The Theophysical Method: An Academic Framework for Cross-Domain Validation

## Abstract

This article presents a formal methodological framework—designated the *Theophysical Method*—for subjecting biblical theism to the same epistemic standards and falsification protocols conventionally applied within theoretical physics. The central thesis posits that if the construct "God" corresponds to an ontologically real entity, that construct must withstand the same rigorous scrutiny, predictive testing, and structural coherence demanded of physical theories. The framework operates as a bilateral audit: the theological domain is tested against physical criteria, and the physical domain is reciprocally tested against theological constraints. This paper delineates the structural architecture of this method across four sequential moves—The Theophysical Method, The Airlock, New Metrics, and The Evidence—and establishes the epistemological warrants for such cross-domain validation.

---

## 1. Introduction: The Theophysical Method

The present inquiry addresses a foundational question: *What occurs when biblical theism is evaluated according to the same methodological criteria that it claims reality obeys?* This question presupposes a symmetry of epistemic accountability between theological and physical domains. If the proposition "God is real" is to be treated as a truth-claim with ontological weight, it must, by logical necessity, survive the same falsification protocols applied to physical theories (Popper, 1959; Kuhn, 1962).

The Theophysical Method is thus defined as a bilateral testing framework: the theological construct is subjected to physical scrutiny, and the physical framework is reciprocally subjected to theological constraints. This bidirectional structure distinguishes the method from both apologetic theology (which assumes the truth of its object) and scientistic reductionism (which excludes theological claims *a priori*). The method operates under the epistemic principle that any claim to reality—whether physical or theological—must demonstrate predictive coherence, internal consistency, and empirical accountability (Lowe, 2023).

## 2. The Airlock: Structural Architecture in Four Moves

The Theophysical Method is organized into four sequential structural moves, each serving a distinct epistemic function within the overall validation protocol.

### 2.1 Move One: The Theophysical Method

The first move establishes the operational definition of the method itself. The central question—*What happens if biblical theism is tested by the same method it claims reality obeys?*—functions as the methodological axiom from which all subsequent analysis proceeds. This move explicitly rejects epistemic asymmetry: theological claims are not granted *a priori* exemption from physical scrutiny, nor are physical claims granted exemption from theological coherence constraints.

### 2.2 Move Two: The Airlock

The second move, designated *The Airlock*, names the load-bearing structural components of the framework prior to requiring the reader's engagement with the full derivation. This move serves a procedural function analogous to the airlock mechanism in pressurized systems: it mediates between distinct epistemic environments (the theological and the physical) without permitting uncontrolled contamination of either domain. The Airlock establishes that the framework's structural integrity depends upon the sequential, non-reductive integration of both domains.

### 2.3 Move Three: New Metrics

The third move introduces *New Metrics*—measurement criteria that emerge from the bilateral testing process itself. These metrics are not imported from either domain *a priori* but are derived from the interaction between theological and physical constraints. The framework tests God and then gets tested back; the metrics of validation are thus co-constituted through this reciprocal process.

### 2.4 Move Four: The Evidence—Bible to Science

The fourth move establishes the evidentiary bridge from biblical text to scientific observation. This move does not collapse the theological and physical domains into a single narrative but rather identifies structural isomorphisms between them. The evidentiary chain proceeds from scriptural claims (treated as propositional truth-claims subject to formal analysis) through to empirically testable physical predictions.

## 3. New Metrics: Operationalization of the Framework

The New Metrics component operationalizes the bilateral testing protocol. The framework tests God—that is, it subjects theological propositions to physical falsification criteria—and then permits the physical framework to be tested back against theological coherence constraints. This reciprocal testing generates metrics that are irreducible to either domain alone.

The formal structure of this bilateral audit can be expressed as follows:

Let \( T \) represent the set of theological propositions derived from biblical theism, and let \( P \) represent the set of physical propositions derived from empirical science. The Theophysical Method defines a validation function \( V \) such that:

\[
V(T, P) = \begin{cases}
\text{validated}, & \text{if } T \cap P \neq \emptyset \text{ and } \neg\exists t \in T \text{ such that } t \text{ is falsified by } p \in P \\
\text{provisionally validated}, & \text{if } T \cap P \neq \emptyset \text{ with residual anomalies} \\
\text{falsified}, & \text{if } \exists t \in T \text{ such that } t \text{ is falsified by } p \in P
\end{cases}
\]

where \( t \) and \( p \) denote individual propositions within their respective domains, and the intersection \( T \cap P \) denotes propositions that are structurally isomorphic across domains (Lowe, 2023).

## 4. Methodological Context and Epistemic Justification

The isomorphism between theological and physical structures was identified through systematic structural comparison of propositional claims across both domains. This comparison proceeds by identifying formal correspondences between scriptural claims (treated as propositional statements with truth-values) and physical laws (treated as empirically validated regularities). The identification of such isomorphisms does not presuppose the truth of either domain but rather establishes the conditions under which cross-domain validation becomes epistemically meaningful.

## 5. Conclusion and Research Trajectory

The Theophysical Method establishes a formal framework for bilateral validation between biblical theism and theoretical physics. By subjecting theological claims to physical falsification criteria and physical claims to theological coherence constraints, the method generates new metrics of validation that are irreducible to either domain alone. The framework does not collapse the theological and physical domains into a single narrative but rather identifies structural isomorphisms that permit cross-domain testing.

The research trajectory proceeds from the present methodological exposition to the bilateral audit itself, detailed in the subsequent document *The Bilateral Audit* (Lowe, 2023). This audit applies the Theophysical Method to specific propositional claims drawn from biblical theism and theoretical physics, evaluating each against the other under the framework's validation criteria.
