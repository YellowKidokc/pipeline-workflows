# Theophysics: A Bilateral Audit of Science and Religion Through Formal Structural Analysis

## Abstract

For approximately five decades, the John Templeton Foundation has pursued the question of whether substantive dialogue between science and religion is possible. Despite substantial scholarly output, the resulting discourse has remained largely superficial, failing to achieve genuine integration or resolution. This article argues that the persistent impasse stems from a fundamental structural asymmetry: the prevailing framework grants science epistemic authority over approximately eighty percent of public discourse—including logic, mathematics, prediction, technology, medicine, policy, and the definition of knowledge itself—while relegating theology to the remaining twenty percent, encompassing personal devotion, private morality, and existential comfort. This asymmetry, however, rests upon an unacknowledged dependency. Science, prior to any empirical operation, must presuppose at least ten metaphysical axioms—the existence of an external world, its orderliness and knowability, the reliability of logic, the correspondence between human cognition and reality, the applicability of mathematics to physical structure, the uniformity of nature across time, the existence of truth, the value of honesty, the principle of parsimony, and the comprehensibility of the universe to rational minds—none of which are empirically demonstrable yet all of which are required for empirical methodology to function. These axioms describe a universe that is rational, ordered, knowable, and accessible to mind—precisely the universe that theology has described for millennia. This article proposes a bilateral audit methodology that holds both science and theology to identical epistemic standards, including falsifiability, predictive power, explanatory scope, internal consistency, and reproducibility. The audit identifies six specific leak points in naturalism, enumerates the borrowed premises, and specifies a grounding mechanism within a 188-axiom formal architecture. The method yields an unexpected empirical finding: across seventy independent domains, the same structural binary emerges—coherence is narrow, decoherence is easy, and restoration requires external input. This finding is formalized through the Coherence Equation, independently derived from thermodynamics, population dynamics, and information theory, and tested against eight historical datasets. The article concludes by presenting the χ-substrate framework, in which science and theology are understood as dual projections of a single coherence architecture, with theology providing upstream ground and science providing downstream mechanism.

## 1. Introduction: The Structural Asymmetry in Science-Religion Discourse

The John Templeton Foundation has, over the past fifty years, funded extensive research into the relationship between science and religion. The resulting corpus is substantial in volume yet, by the Foundation's own implicit admission, limited in transformative impact. The dialogue has remained "thin" (Templeton, 2023). This article contends that the failure is not attributable to insufficient funding, inadequate scholarship, or intractable subject matter, but rather to a broken foundational premise that has structured the entire discourse.

### 1.1 The Eighty-Twenty Division

Contemporary public discourse operates under an implicit allocation of epistemic territory. Science occupies approximately eighty percent of the available space, encompassing logic, mathematics, prediction, technology, medicine, policy, and—critically—the definition of what constitutes legitimate knowledge. Christianity, as the primary representative of theology in Western discourse, occupies the remaining twenty percent: Sunday morning observance, personal devotion, private morality, and the polite acknowledgment that faith holds meaning for those who hold it, provided it remains within its designated boundaries.

This arrangement presents itself as the natural outcome of four centuries of scientific progress superseding religious dogma. The present analysis argues that this self-presentation is misleading. The arrangement is not a verdict rendered by neutral historical forces but rather a settlement imposed by one party after it appropriated the other party's foundational premises without acknowledgment.

### 1.2 The Borrowed Foundation

Before science can take its first measurement, it must presuppose at least ten axioms that are not provable by its own methods:

1. **The existence of an external world**—that there is a reality independent of the observer's mind.
2. **The orderliness and knowability of that world**—that it is structured according to regular principles accessible to rational inquiry.
3. **The reliability of logic**—that logical inference tracks truth rather than being merely a neurological artifact.
4. **The correspondence between human cognition and reality**—that the brain, itself a product of natural processes, can reliably apprehend the structure of the universe.
5. **The applicability of mathematics to physical structure**—that abstract mathematical constructs, invented by human minds, accurately describe physical reality.
6. **The uniformity of nature across time**—that the laws governing physical processes today will continue to hold tomorrow.
7. **The existence of truth**—that propositions can correspond to reality in a meaningful sense.
8. **The value of honesty**—that intellectual integrity is a virtue and that deception undermines inquiry.
9. **The principle of parsimony**—that simpler explanations are epistemically preferable to more complex ones.
10. **The comprehensibility of the universe to rational minds**—that the cosmos is structured in a way that finite intellects can, in principle, understand.

None of these axioms is empirically demonstrable. Each is required before empirical methodology can be designed, executed, or interpreted. These axioms do not describe a neutral, indifferent universe. They describe a universe that is rational, ordered, knowable, and accessible to mind—precisely the universe that Judeo-Christian theology has described for approximately three thousand years.

Science did not generate this foundation through its own methods. It borrowed it from the theological tradition it subsequently marginalized. The borrowed foundation was then used to declare the lender epistemically out of bounds. This is not a settlement; it is a structural contradiction. It is the reason that fifty years of well-funded dialogue has produced conversation without resolution.

## 2. Method: The Bilateral Audit

### 2.1 Rationale for Symmetric Epistemic Evaluation

The standard move in science-religion discourse is to ask whether religious claims survive scientific scrutiny. This is a unidirectional audit. A genuinely symmetric methodology requires that both parties be held to identical standards. The bilateral audit, therefore, proceeds in two directions:

**Direction A:** Hold theological claims accountable to science's own standards—falsifiability, predictive power, explanatory scope, internal consistency, and reproducibility. Do not protect theological claims from epistemic pressure.

**Direction B:** Hold science accountable to the same standards. Can it ground its own axioms? Can it explain consciousness—not merely map its neural correlates, but explain why there is subjective experience at all? Can it explain why abstract mathematics describes physical reality with precision that Wigner (1960) described as "unreasonable"? Can it account for the fine-tuning of cosmological constants? Can it explain the origin of information, as distinct from its processing once it exists?

### 2.2 The Seven-Question Method (7Q)

The bilateral audit is operationalized through a repeatable procedure consisting of seven operational questions, designated the 7Q Method. The method requires a posture of willingness to be corrected as the gate condition for inquiry. The seven questions are:

1. **Q0 (Posture):** Is the investigator willing to be corrected by the evidence?
2. **Q1 (Domain):** What is the specific domain of the claim?
3. **Q2 (Mechanism):** What causal mechanism is proposed?
4. **Q3 (Evidence):** What empirical evidence supports or contradicts the claim?
5. **Q4 (Falsification):** What would constitute a kill condition for the claim?
6. **Q5 (Translation):** How does the claim map across domains without vocabulary dependence?
7. **Q6 (Convergence):** Does the claim surface the same structure independently in multiple domains?

The method is public, adversarially testable, and designed to produce results that survive critical scrutiny.

### 2.3 Isomorphic Event Density (IED)

Cross-domain claims have been rightly dismissed in the literature because no protocol existed to distinguish genuine structural correspondence from poetic analogy or metaphorical noise. Isomorphic Event Density (IED) addresses this gap by decomposing a process into six components: events, transitions, constraints, boundary conditions, failure modes, and restoration mechanisms. A one-to-one mapping across domains at all six levels constitutes structural weight. A mapping at zero of twelve levels (six from each domain) is poetry. The distinction is now measurable.

## 4. The Coherence Equation: Formal Derivation

### 4.1 Mathematical Formulation

The coherence equation is independently derived from three domains: thermodynamics, population dynamics, and information theory. All three derivations arrive at the same differential form:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G(1 - C) - S \cdot C
\]

Where:
- \(C\) = coherence (dimensionless, \(0 \leq C \leq 1\))
- \(t\) = time (seconds)
- \(O\) = ordering input rate (\(s^{-1}\))
- \(G\) = growth coefficient (dimensionless)
- \(S\) = spontaneous decoherence rate (\(s^{-1}\))

### 4.2 Domain-Specific Derivations

**Thermodynamic derivation:** The equation emerges from the relationship between entropy production and free energy minimization in open systems far from equilibrium (Prigogine, 1967).

**Population dynamics derivation:** The equation emerges from the Lotka-Volterra framework when applied to ordered versus disordered states in complex adaptive systems (May, 1973).

**Information theory derivation:** The equation emerges from Shannon's channel capacity theorem when applied to the preservation of structured information against noise (Shannon, 1948).

3. Post-WWII Western moral consensus erosion (1960–2020)

## 5. The χ-Substrate Framework

### 5.1 Beyond Existing Models

Existing frameworks for relating science and religion fall into three categories:

1. **NOMA (Non-Overlapping Magisteria):** Gould (1999) walls science and religion off as separate domains with distinct authorities.
2. **Complementarity:** Polkinghorne (1994) keeps them adjacent but does not formalize the relationship.
3. **Consilience:** Wilson (1998) collects convergence without formalizing the underlying structure.

The χ-substrate move is structurally different: science and theology are understood as dual projections of a single coherence architecture. Neither dominates. Theology provides upstream ground; science provides downstream mechanism. Both draw from one source.

## 7. Strategic Implications: Paradigm Before Evidence

### 7.1 The Semmelweis-Mendel Pattern

The history of science demonstrates that evidence alone does not settle paradigm disputes. Semmelweis (1847) was correct about puerperal fever for thirty years before the medical establishment accepted his findings. Mendel (1866) sat ignored for forty years before his work on heredity was rediscovered. In both cases, the interpretive framework sat upstream of the evidence, preventing the evidence from doing its work.

### 7.2 Application to Science-Religion Discourse

The bilateral audit audits the paradigm first. Only then does it ask evidence to do evidence-work, not paradigm-overthrow-work. This is why the existing literature—which attempts to deploy evidence within an unexamined paradigm—keeps losing ground it should hold.

### 8.3 Limitations and Future Work

The present analysis has several limitations. First, the seventy-domain survey requires independent replication. Second, the Lean 4 verification, while complete, has not been adversarially reviewed by the broader formal verification community. Third, the historical datasets, while independently sourced, require additional validation through primary source analysis.

Future work should focus on: (a) expanding the domain survey to include non-Western religious traditions, (b) subjecting the Lean 4 implementation to adversarial review, (c) developing experimental protocols for testing the Coherence Equation's predictions, and (d) engaging the broader research community in collaborative refinement of the methodology.
