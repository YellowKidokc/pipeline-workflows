# The 7Q Method: An Eight-Layer Epistemic Framework for Cross-Domain Inquiry with Forward and Reverse Applications

## Abstract

This article presents the 7Q Method, a formal epistemic framework comprising eight layers of inquiry—seven sequential questions preceded by a foundational presuppositional condition (Q0)—designed for the classification, evaluation, and falsification of claims across disciplinary boundaries. The method integrates forward-direction classification with reverse-direction proof analysis, incorporating five distinct death conditions for claim termination. A weighted, multiplicative evidence synthesis procedure yields a composite truth score modulated by framework compliance. The method is demonstrated through worked examples drawn from general relativity (GR) and is proposed as a universal epistemic tool applicable to physics, theology, consciousness studies, information theory, mathematics, biology, and economics. Structural isomorphisms across domains are identified through topological, boundary condition, and conservation law preservation under variable substitution.

---

## 1. Introduction: The Presuppositional Condition (Q0)

### 1.1 Operational Definition

The 7Q Method commences not with a question but with a precondition: the inquirer cannot constitute the ground of inquiry. Formally, Q0 is defined as an external constraint on the epistemic posture of the investigator, lying outside the ordered set {Q1, Q2, ..., Q7}. The operational requirement is stated as follows:

\[
\text{posture(decided)} \implies \text{dead framework}
\]
\[
\text{posture(open)} \implies \text{breakthrough potential}
\]

This condition is not a normative appeal to intellectual humility as a virtue; rather, it is an operational necessity. Historical analysis of ossified frameworks reveals a common failure mode: the investigator arrived at the inquiry with conclusions predetermined. Positivism, for example, arrived decided that metaphysics was meaningless; behaviorism arrived decided that consciousness was irrelevant to psychological explanation. Both frameworks subsequently exhibited structural stagnation.

### 1.2 Historical Precedent

The Q0 condition finds empirical support in the investigative practices of paradigm-shifting scientists. Einstein (1915) did not set out to disprove Newtonian gravitation; he sought to resolve a genuine tension between the principle of relativity and the observed constancy of the speed of light. Planck (1900) did not seek to introduce quanta; the blackbody radiation data compelled their postulation. In both cases, the investigator maintained an open posture toward evidence that disconfirmed prior theoretical commitments.

Q0 is acknowledged to be the weakest operational element of the framework in terms of enforceability—it cannot be scored, only acknowledged—yet it is argued to be the most important structural element, as its violation precludes the proper functioning of all subsequent questions.

## 2. Q1: Definition and Classification

### 2.1 Q1.1—Classification

The first formal question (Q1) requires the inquirer to name the claim under investigation, classify its type, and assign it a tier within a knowledge hierarchy. Misclassification at Q1 propagates errors through all subsequent questions. The classification types are:

- **Causal**: The claim asserts that one entity or process produces a change in another.
- **Descriptive**: The claim characterizes a state or structure without asserting causation.
- **Ontological**: The claim concerns the nature of being or existence.
- **Mathematical**: The claim is expressed in formal mathematical terms.
- **Predictive**: The claim generates testable predictions about future observations.
- **Mechanistic**: The claim specifies the mechanism by which an effect is produced.
- **Normative**: The claim prescribes what ought to be the case.

### 2.2 Q1.2—Tier Assignment

Claims are assigned to one of four tiers in a knowledge hierarchy:

| Tier | Category | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| Foundational | Axiom, postulate, definition | The equivalence principle |
| Derived | Theorem, corollary, hypothesis | The Schwarzschild solution |
| Constraint | Boundary condition | Asymptotic flatness |
| Support | Evidence bundle, connection, bridge | Observational data sets |

The critical distinction is between foundational and derived claims. A hypothesis treated as an axiom will never be traced to its dependencies, because axioms, by definition, do not have dependencies—they *are* dependencies.

### 2.3 Worked Example: General Relativity

Application of Q1 to general relativity yields a reclassification from descriptive to causal. Standard textbook presentations (e.g., Misner, Thorne, & Wheeler, 1973) treat GR as a description of spacetime geometry. Q1 reclassifies it: GR asserts that mass-energy *causes* curvature. This is a causal claim—a stronger commitment than "spacetime is curved." The reclassification immediately sharpens the kill conditions:

\[
\text{classify(GR)} = \text{causal} > \text{descriptive}
\]
\[
\text{Causal claim} \implies \text{removing the cause should remove the effect}
\]

This causal formulation is testable in ways that a purely descriptive formulation is not.

## 3. Q2: Domain Anchoring and Isomorphism Testing

### 3.1 Q2.1—Domain Anchor

Q2 establishes the primary domain, additional domains, and scale of operation for the claim. The critical question is whether the claim appears in more than one domain, as cross-domain presence triggers the isomorphism test. Recognized domains include:

- Physics
- Theology
- Consciousness studies
- Information theory
- Mathematics
- Biology
- Economics

Scale of operation ranges from quantum through molecular, neural, individual, social, civilizational, to cosmic.

### 3.2 Q2.2—Isomorphism Test

The isomorphism test distinguishes between analogy and structural identity. An isomorphism is defined as follows:

\[
\text{iso}(A, B) \iff \forall \text{ relations } R: R_A \leftrightarrow R_B \text{ under variable substitution}
\]

If the mathematics preserves topology, boundary conditions, and conservation laws under variable substitution, the relationship is an isomorphism, not an analogy. Analogies suggest similarity; isomorphisms demonstrate structural identity. The same structural relationships holding across domains—the same tensor equations, the same conservation laws—constitute structure, not metaphor.

**Examples of cross-domain isomorphisms:**

- Riemannian geometry appears in physics (GR), machine learning (manifold learning), economics (curved utility surfaces), and information theory (Fisher information geometry). The same mathematical structure appears in all four domains.

## 4. Q3: Precision and Negation

### 4.1 Q3.1—Precision

Q3 forces the claim into a single precise statement, classified by certainty level and scope:

- **Certainty levels**: Proven, well-supported, tentative, speculative
- **Scope**: Universal, domain-specific, context-dependent

The opponent restatement test is applied: can a serious, informed opponent restate the claim accurately? If not, the claim is insufficiently precise for testing.

### 4.2 Q3.2—Negation Test

Q3 automatically generates the logical negation of the claim. Hidden bundled claims surface through this operation:

\[
\neg(A \land B) = \neg A \lor \neg B
\]

If the negation decomposes the original claim into independently testable components, bundled claims hidden within a single statement have been identified.

**Worked example—GR negation:**

\[
\neg(\text{GR}) = \neg(\text{mass-energy curves spacetime}) \lor \neg(\text{objects follow geodesics})
\]

This yields two independently killable components, identified automatically through the negation test.

## 5. Q4: Evidence Channels and the Completeness Factor

### 5.1 Q4—Channel 1: Phenomenon Strength (PS)

Phenomenon Strength quantifies what is reliably observed:

\[
\text{PS} = \text{reproducibility} \times 0.4 + \text{effect size} \times 0.3 + \text{measurement quality} \times 0.3
\]

Reproducibility receives the highest weight because unrepeatable observations do not constitute evidence.

### 5.2 Q4—Channel 2: Explanatory Depth (ED)

Explanatory Depth quantifies mechanistic understanding:

\[
\text{ED} = \text{mechanism clarity} \times 0.4 + \text{constraint consistency} \times 0.3 + \text{scope} \times 0.3
\]

The "why-penalty" operates as follows: if ED = 0, evidence is capped at 50% regardless of PS. This formalizes the observation that pre-1927 scientists who asked "why" moved the needle, while post-1927 instrumentalism correlates with disciplinary fragmentation.

### 5.3 Q4—Channel 3: Experiential Coherence (EC)

Experiential Coherence quantifies stable downstream effects:

\[
\text{EC} = \text{internal consistency} \times 0.25 + \text{longitudinal stability} \times 0.25 + \text{behavioral transformation} \times 0.25 + \text{intersubjective pattern} \times 0.25
\]

This channel does not measure subjective intensity ("I feel strongly") but rather whether the evidence produces consistent downstream effects across time, behavior, and multiple agents.

### 5.4 The Completeness Factor (CF)

The three channels multiply rather than add:

\[
\text{CF} = (0.5 + 0.5 \times \text{ED}) \times (0.5 + 0.5 \times \text{EC})
\]
\[
E_{\text{final}} = \text{PS} \times \text{CF}
\]

Zero in one channel cannot be compensated by maximum values in another. If ED = 0 and EC = 0, then CF = 0.25, capping evidence at 25%. If ED = 1 and EC = 1, then CF = 1.0, allowing full credit.

## 6. Q5: Dependency Chain and Fragility Mapping

### 6.1 Q5.1—Chain Terminus

Q5 traces the dependency chain to its terminus. Terminus types are:

- **Axiom**: Self-evident—solid ground
- **Brute fact**: Accepted without proof—honest but ungrounded
- **Circularity**: Loops back—STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITY FLAGGED
- **Open**: Never terminates—INCOMPLETE FLAGGED

### 6.2 Q5.2—Fragility Map

For each dependency, the consequence of its failure is assessed:

- **Immediate collapse**: Critical dependency
- **Graceful degradation**: Claim still partially works
- **Independent survival**: Dependency was not actually needed

**Worked example—GR dependencies:**

GR depends on: equivalence principle, Riemannian geometry, \(c = \text{const}\), energy-momentum conservation, and the smooth manifold assumption. Q5 surfaces the hidden dependency: the smooth manifold assumption is unverified at the Planck scale (\(\sim 10^{-35}\ \text{m}\)), where spacetime may be discrete. This has been identified as the root of the quantum gravity problem (Rovelli, 2004).

## 7. Q6: Prediction Generation and Cross-Domain Forcing

### 7.1 Q6.1—Predictions

Q6 separates confirmed predictions from untested ones. Generative maturity is quantified as:

\[
\text{generative maturity} = \frac{\text{confirmed predictions}}{\text{total predictions}}
\]

If Q6(claim) = \(\emptyset\), this constitutes an explanatory failure signal—the claim occupies conceptual space without generating knowledge.

**Worked example—GR predictions:**

GR forced the prediction of black holes (Schwarzschild, 1916), gravitational waves (detected 2015), and GPS corrections (daily application). All are consequences generated by the mathematics that Einstein himself did not initially plant.

### 7.2 Q6.2—Cross-Domain Forcing

If a Q6 consequence appears in another domain and preserves the same mathematical structure under variable substitution, an isomorphism candidate has been detected. This creates a detection loop with Q2:

\[
\text{Q6 consequence} \in \text{domain}_B \implies \text{flag for Q2 isomorphism test}
\]
\[
\text{Q2} \leftrightarrow \text{Q6 form a detection loop}
\]

## 8. Q7: Death Conditions and Adversarial Testing

### 8.1 Death Condition #1: Self-Refutation

\[
P \implies \neg P
\]

The claim destroys itself when stated. The denial presupposes what it denies. Example: "There is no truth" is itself a truth claim; "Logic is unreliable" requires logic to state.

### 8.2 Death Condition #2: Infinite Regress

\[
\text{answer}(Q) \implies \text{new instance of } Q \implies \infty
\]

The claim pushes the question backward without resolution. Each answer generates a new instance of the same question. This differs from a deep dependency chain—regress means the chain never terminates by design.

### 8.3 Death Condition #3: Empirical Contradiction

\[
\text{prediction(claim)} \neq \text{observation} \implies \text{falsified}
\]

This is the traditional Popperian (1959) mode of falsification. It is necessary but not sufficient: a claim can survive empirical testing and still fail on other conditions.

### 8.4 Death Condition #4: Logical Incoherence

\[
\text{claim} \land \neg(\text{consequence of claim}) \implies \text{incoherent}
\]

The claim contradicts something already established in its own chain—a prior dependency, a forced consequence, or itself at a different point in the argument. This differs from self-refutation (which is immediate) by catching delayed contradictions that only appear when the full chain is traced.

### 8.5 Death Condition #5: Explanatory Failure

\[
\text{Q6(claim)} = \emptyset
\]

The claim cannot answer forced downstream questions from Q6. It stops generating consequences. Kuhn (1962) described this as anomaly accumulation; the 7Q system formalizes it. A theory that stops generating is dying, even if the data still fit.

### 8.6 Adversarial Test

A claim that has survived adversarial testing by informed opponents is categorically different from one examined only by sympathetic parties. The 7Q system requires this field to be declared. A claim with no adversarial history is unproven, not confirmed.

## 9. Synthesis: Truth Score and Modulation

### 9.1 Truth Score (T)

The composite truth score is derived from six variables, all drawn from the 7Q fields:

\[
T = w_1 S + w_2 E + w_3 L + w_4 D + w_5 P + w_6 C
\]

Where:
- \(S \leftarrow\) Q7 survivability
- \(E \leftarrow\) Q4 \(E_{\text{final}}\) with why-penalty
- \(L \leftarrow\) Q3 precision \(\times\) Q4 type
- \(D \leftarrow\) Q5 dependency stability
- \(P \leftarrow\) Q6 confirmed prediction ratio
- \(C \leftarrow\) Q2 cross-domain coherence

Every variable comes from fields the inquirer has already filled. No external judgment is required.

\[
T = \sum w_i V_i \quad \text{where } V \in \{S, E, L, D, P, C\}
\]
\[
\text{Maps to: } T = \chi, \text{ confidence } = \kappa, \text{ cluster variance } = \rho
\]

### 9.2 Modulator

\[
T_{\text{enhanced}} = T \times (0.6 + 0.4 \times \text{7Q score})
\]

Zero 7Q markers yield 60% of the truth score; full 7Q compliance yields 100%. This modulation reflects the principle that structured epistemic practice constitutes better epistemic practice.

### 9.3 Hard Caps

Three hard caps enforce completeness:

- If ED < 0.3: \(\max(T) = 0.70\) (no explanation)
- If Q7 < 0.3: \(\max(T) = 0.60\) (unfalsifiable)
- If Q6 < 0.3: \(\max(T) = 0.70\) (no consequences)

These caps prevent gaming the system by compensating for missing dimensions.

## 10. Conclusion

The 7Q Method provides a formal, eight-layer epistemic framework for the classification, evaluation, and falsification of claims across disciplinary boundaries. By integrating forward-direction classification with reverse-direction proof analysis, incorporating five distinct death conditions, and employing a weighted, multiplicative evidence synthesis procedure, the method offers a systematic approach to epistemic evaluation that transcends individual disciplinary conventions. The framework's capacity to detect structural isomorphisms across domains—through topological, boundary condition, and conservation law preservation under variable substitution—positions it as a potential universal tool for interdisciplinary inquiry.
