# The Moral-Physical Dictionary: A Formal Mapping of Coherence Across Disciplinary Measurement Frames

## Abstract

This article presents a formal isomorphism between evaluative terminology across moral, physical, informational, biological, aesthetic, and theological domains. Through structural comparison of measurement frameworks, we demonstrate that terms conventionally treated as domain-specific—including "good," "order," "signal," "health," "beauty," and "holiness"—constitute distinct observational reports of a single underlying variable: the coherence function χ, as defined by the Master Equation. Conversely, terms such as "evil," "disorder," "noise," "disease," "ugliness," and "sin" correspond to maximal decoherence (σ = −1). The proposed mapping is supported by etymological analysis, dimensional consistency across measurement protocols, and the formal equivalence of thermodynamic entropy, information-theoretic noise, and theological conceptions of separation from the Logos. We argue that if this isomorphism obtains, moral relativism is not merely philosophically untenable but physically incoherent, as it would require the denial of measurable decoherence processes.

## 1. Introduction: The Unity of Evaluative Measurement

The relationship between temperature and kinetic energy is not one of analogy but of ontological identity. Temperature represents a bulk thermodynamic measurement of mean molecular kinetic energy; the two quantities are related by the equipartition theorem, \( \langle E_k \rangle = \frac{3}{2} k_B T \), where \( k_B \) is the Boltzmann constant. A thermometer and a particle detector report the same underlying physical reality through different observational frames.

We propose that an analogous identity obtains across a broader set of evaluative domains. The physicist measures entropy \( S \); the communications engineer measures signal-to-noise ratio (SNR); the physician assesses disease burden; the ethicist evaluates moral wrongness; the theologian identifies sin. The central thesis of this article is that these disparate measurements constitute distinct observational reports of a single underlying variable: the coherence function χ, derived from the Master Equation governing system evolution.

### 1.1 Definition of the Coherence Function

Let χ represent the coherence function of a system, defined over the interval \( \sigma \in [-1, +1] \), where:

- \( \sigma = +1 \) corresponds to **maximum coherence**: perfect phase alignment, minimal entropy, maximal structural integrity, optimal information transmission, and maximal Logos coupling.
- \( \sigma = -1 \) corresponds to **maximum decoherence**: complete phase randomization, maximal entropy, structural disintegration, total information loss, and maximal separation from the Logos.

The Master Equation governing χ takes the general form:

\[
\frac{d\chi}{dt} = -\gamma(\chi - \chi_{\text{eq}}) + \mathcal{F}(t)
\]

where \( \gamma \) is the decoherence rate constant (with units of inverse time, s⁻¹), \( \chi_{\text{eq}} \) is the equilibrium coherence value for a closed system (tending toward −1 by the Second Law), and \( \mathcal{F}(t) \) represents external negentropic forcing (e.g., information input, energy injection, or divine action).

## 3. Formal Equivalence Demonstration

### 3.1 Thermodynamic Frame

The Second Law of Thermodynamics states that for a closed system, entropy cannot decrease:

\[
\frac{dS}{dt} \geq 0
\]

where \( S = -k_B \sum_i p_i \ln p_i \). In terms of χ, this becomes:

\[
\frac{d\chi}{dt} \leq 0 \quad \text{for } \mathcal{F}(t) = 0
\]

Thus, maximal decoherence (σ = −1) corresponds to thermodynamic equilibrium, and maximal coherence (σ = +1) corresponds to a maximally ordered, low-entropy state.

### 3.2 Information-Theoretic Frame

Shannon's channel capacity theorem gives:

\[
C = B \log_2(1 + \text{SNR})
\]

where SNR is the signal-to-noise ratio. In the coherence frame, SNR ∝ χ, such that:

\[
\lim_{\chi \to +1} C \to \infty \quad \text{(perfect transmission)}
\]
\[
\lim_{\chi \to -1} C \to 0 \quad \text{(complete noise)}
\]

Thus, "truth" (maximal information fidelity) and "falsehood" (maximal information corruption) are measurements of χ in the informational frame.
