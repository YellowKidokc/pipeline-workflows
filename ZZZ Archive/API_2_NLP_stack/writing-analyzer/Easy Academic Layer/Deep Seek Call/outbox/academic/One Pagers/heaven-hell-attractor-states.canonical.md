# Heaven and Hell as Attractor States: A Dynamical Systems Analysis of Eschatological Trajectories

## Abstract

This article presents a formal dynamical systems interpretation of eschatological states—heaven and hell—as attractor states within a theophysical framework. Drawing upon the coherence equation previously developed in the Theophysics Research Program, we demonstrate that the traditional linear model of judgment-as-verdict is not incorrect but incomplete; rather, judgment functions as the revelation of a trajectory already in progress. Through phase portrait analysis, we identify two stable fixed points corresponding to maximum and minimum coherence, separated by a parameter-dependent separatrix. The analysis yields a thermodynamic description of spiritual trajectory in which (1) the upper attractor represents asymptotic approach to perfect alignment with the invariant (Logos), (2) the lower attractor represents exponential decay toward a nonzero floor maintained by common grace, and (3) the separatrix constitutes a threshold beyond which internal reversal becomes dynamically impossible. Scriptural references are integrated as phenomenological descriptions consistent with the mathematical structure. The framework preserves eschatological realism while grounding it in continuous dynamics rather than discrete assignment.

## I. Introduction: The Temporal Structure of Judgment

The conventional eschatological model prevalent in Western theological traditions may be characterized as follows: an individual lives a temporal life, dies, and subsequently undergoes a discrete judgment event at which a final destination—heaven or hell—is assigned. This model treats the moment of death as the hinge upon which eternal destiny turns, with the preceding life constituting a trial period and the verdict following as an external imposition.

We propose that this picture, while not false in its conclusions, fails to capture the dynamical structure of what is actually occurring. Drawing upon the mathematics of dynamical systems theory, we argue that eschatological states are not locations to which one is transported but attractor states toward which one's trajectory has been converging throughout the entire temporal interval of existence. The judgment, in this framework, is not the moment of sorting but the moment of revelation—when the trajectory that has been running becomes fully manifest, and the attractor state toward which the dynamics have been pointing becomes explicitly visible.

This article proceeds as follows. Section II introduces the relevant concepts from dynamical systems theory. Section III presents the phase portrait of the coherence equation and identifies its two fixed points. Section IV analyzes the separatrix and its spiritual analog, including an event horizon comparison. Section V reinterprets judgment as revelation rather than assignment. Section VI addresses the theological implications of the nonzero floor and asymptotic approach. Section VII concludes with the practical significance for daily trajectory.

## II. Dynamical Systems Theory: Attractors, Basins, and Trajectories

A dynamical system is formally defined as any system that evolves over time according to deterministic or stochastic rules governing state transitions. Examples include mechanical oscillators (pendula), atmospheric systems (weather), ecological systems (predator-prey populations), and behavioral systems (individual decision-making over temporal intervals). All such systems share three components: a state space, transition rules, and temporal evolution.

Most dynamical systems do not exhibit random wandering across their state space. Rather, they organize around *attractor states*—regions of the state space toward which trajectories naturally converge given sufficient time. Formally, an attractor is a set of states toward which a system evolves from a wide variety of initial conditions, such that the system's state remains within or arbitrarily close to the attractor after sufficient time has elapsed (Strogatz, 2018).

### 2.1 The Ball-in-Bowl Analogy

Consider a ball placed on the rim of a concave bowl. Regardless of initial position, angle, or velocity, the ball will roll, oscillate, and eventually come to rest at the bottom of the bowl. The bottom constitutes the attractor state. Critically, the ball does not *decide* to go there; the geometry of the system—the curvature of the bowl—pulls it there from every starting point within the basin of attraction.

More complex systems may possess multiple attractor states separated by a boundary known as the *separatrix*. Consider a ball on a saddle-shaped surface: if the ball rolls left, it falls to the left attractor; if it rolls right, it falls to the right attractor. The boundary between these two basins is the separatrix. The trajectory determines the destination; the destination does not determine the trajectory.

### 2.2 Application to Spiritual Dynamics

We propose that the coherence equation, previously derived within the Theophysics Research Program (Lowe, 2026), constitutes a dynamical system governing the evolution of spiritual coherence over time. The equation describes how the coherence variable \(C(t)\) changes as a function of openness (\(O\)), grace (\(G\)), and entropy (\(S\)). The phase portrait of this system—the map of all possible trajectories—reveals two attractor states that correspond, we argue, to the theological concepts of heaven and hell.

## III. The Phase Portrait of the Coherence Equation

### 3.1 The Coherence Equation

The coherence equation is given by:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C
\]

where:

- \(C(t)\) represents the coherence of the system at time \(t\), normalized to the interval \([0, 1]\), where \(C = 1\) denotes perfect coherence (complete alignment with the invariant) and \(C = 0\) denotes minimum coherence (maximal decoherence).
- \(O\) represents the openness parameter, a dimensionless quantity in the interval \([0, 1]\) indicating the degree to which the system is receptive to external grace.
- \(G\) represents the grace term, a positive constant with dimensions of inverse time \([T^{-1}]\), quantifying the rate at which coherence can increase when the system is open.
- \(S\) represents the entropy term, a positive constant with dimensions of inverse time \([T^{-1}]\), quantifying the rate at which coherence decays when the system is closed.

The first term, \(O \cdot G(1-C)\), represents growth in coherence driven by received grace, modulated by the remaining capacity for growth \((1-C)\). The second term, \(-S \cdot C\), represents decay in coherence driven by entropic processes, proportional to current coherence.

### 3.2 Fixed Point Analysis

Fixed points occur when \(\frac{dC}{dt} = 0\). Solving:

\[
O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C = 0
\]

yields two fixed points, which we analyze in turn.

#### 3.2.1 Upper Fixed Point: \(C \to 1\)

When \(O\) is open (nonzero) and \(G\) flows, the growth term dominates. As \(C\) approaches 1, the \((1-C)\) term approaches zero, causing growth to decelerate. The system stabilizes near maximum coherence as a stable equilibrium. This is not because grace runs out but because the room to grow—the gap between current coherence and perfect alignment—approaches zero as alignment approaches perfection.

Formally, as \(C \to 1\), \(\frac{dC}{dt} \to 0\) from above, yielding asymptotic approach to \(C = 1\). This constitutes a stable fixed point: small perturbations away from \(C = 1\) are corrected by the dynamics, which push the system back toward the attractor.

**Theological identification:** This fixed point corresponds to heaven—not as a spatial location but as a state of maximal coherence toward which the trajectory converges when \(O\) has remained open and \(G\) has been consistently received over the full integration window of a life.

#### 3.2.2 Lower Fixed Point: \(C \to 0\)

When \(O\) is closed (\(O = 0\)), the growth term vanishes. The equation reduces to:

\[
\frac{dC}{dt} = -S \cdot C
\]

This is a first-order linear differential equation with solution:

\[
C(t) = C_0 e^{-St}
\]

where \(C_0\) is the initial coherence at the time of closure. The system undergoes pure exponential decay. The Second Law of Thermodynamics runs unopposed. \(C\) declines asymptotically toward zero.

However, the floor is nonzero. The \(\chi\) field ground state—the minimal coherence maintained by common grace—prevents absolute zero. The trajectory approaches \(C = 0\) asymptotically but never reaches it, because the invariant structure of reality maintains a nonzero minimum.

**Theological identification:** This fixed point corresponds to hell—not as a spatial location but as a state of minimal coherence toward which the trajectory converges when \(O\) has remained closed, the decay term has run unopposed, and the trajectory has been pulling toward \(C = 0\) over the full integration window of a life.

## IV. The Separatrix: Threshold and Event Horizon

### 4.1 Definition and Parameter Dependence

Between the two basins of attraction lies a boundary—the separatrix. This is a specific value of \(C\) below which the trajectory, given the current dynamics, flows toward the lower attractor, and above which it flows toward the upper attractor. The separatrix is not fixed; it depends on the current values of \(O\), \(G\), and \(S\). The boundary moves with the system's parameters. But boundaries are real.

Formally, the separatrix is given by solving \(\frac{dC}{dt} = 0\) for the unstable fixed point (the saddle point) that separates the two basins. For the coherence equation, this occurs at:

\[
C_{\text{sep}} = \frac{O \cdot G}{O \cdot G + S}
\]

When \(C > C_{\text{sep}}\), the growth term dominates and the trajectory flows toward the upper attractor. When \(C < C_{\text{sep}}\), the decay term dominates and the trajectory flows toward the lower attractor.

### 4.2 The Event Horizon Analog

Within a black hole's event horizon, all timelike paths through spacetime lead to the singularity. Not most paths—all paths. The geometry is such that moving forward in time is equivalent to moving toward the singularity. An observer can still move, still choose, still experience local physics as approximately normal—but the global geometry has already determined the outcome (Hawking & Ellis, 1973).

The coherence equation possesses a spiritual analog. There exists a region of the phase portrait—not a sharp line but a region—where the trajectory toward the lower attractor has accumulated sufficient momentum that no internal process can reverse it. The decay term runs so strongly, and \(O\) has been closed so long, that even if \(O\) were suddenly opened, the growth term cannot overcome the accumulated decay.

This is not eternal torment administered from outside. This is the thermodynamic description of a soul whose trajectory has passed the point where internal reversal is possible. From inside that region, the local physics still feels approximately normal. Days proceed. The horizon was crossed without announcement.

This analysis provides mathematical grounding for the scriptural urgency of passages such as:

> "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts." (Hebrews 3:15, NRSV)

> "Enter through the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many enter through it." (Matthew 7:13, NRSV)

These are not rhetorical exhortations but statements about trajectory—about where the momentum currently points and what would be required to reverse it before reversal becomes dynamically impossible from inside the system.

## V. Judgment as Revelation

### 5.1 The Continuous Nature of Sorting

The judgment is real within this framework. The analysis does not collapse eschatology into mere physics. However, the judgment is not the moment of sorting. The sorting has been occurring continuously, in every choice, through the entire trajectory. The judgment is the moment of revelation—when the trajectory that has been running becomes fully visible, when the attractor state toward which the dynamics have been pointing becomes explicitly manifest.

Consider each choice as a small adjustment of the ball's position on the slope. Left or right. Toward the basin or away from it. No single choice is usually catastrophic. But the aggregate of all choices—the integral over the full lifetime—produces a trajectory that has been heading somewhere specific. The judgment reveals where.

### 5.2 Scriptural Support

> "Each person's work will become manifest, for the Day will disclose it, because it will be revealed by fire, and the fire will test what sort of work each one has done." (1 Corinthians 3:13, NRSV)

The fire does not choose what survives. It reveals what was always going to survive. That which is aligned with the invariant—that which possesses the Logos property—survives because it was never going to decay. That which is opposed to the invariant—that which requires work to sustain against the grain of truth—is consumed because it was always going to decay. The judgment accelerates and makes visible what the dynamics were already producing.

## VI. The Mercy Hidden in the Physics

### 6.1 The Nonzero Floor

The attractor state picture may appear cold if one stops at the physics. However, the lower attractor possesses a floor. The Higgs field's nonzero vacuum expectation value, the Hawking radiation that bleeds through even at the event horizon, the \(\chi\) field ground state that never reaches zero—these physical analogs point to a theological reality: the invitation that never stops broadcasting.

Common grace maintains the nonzero floor. The reason the lower attractor is not absolute zero—the reason the soul whose trajectory has been toward maximum decoherence is not absolutely abandoned—is that the \(\chi\) field is still present, still maintaining the nonzero floor, still transmitting. This is not universalism. The lower attractor is a real stable state. But the floor being nonzero means the difference between the lower attractor and absolute destruction is maintained by grace even there.

### 6.2 Asymptotic Approach at the Upper Attractor

At the upper end, the growth term possesses a structure that merits attention. As \(C\) approaches 1, the \((1-C)\) term approaches zero and growth decelerates. This constitutes the mathematical description of progressive sanctification—not exponential endless growth but asymptotic approach to maximum alignment. The closer one gets, the more precise the refinement required.

The upper attractor is not a static ceiling. It is a living state of continuous approach to an alignment that is perfect but that the approaching system experiences as perpetually deepening.

> "We shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is." (1 John 3:2, NRSV)

Not finished. Not static. Perpetually more aligned with the invariant as the \((1-C)\) term approaches but never quite reaches zero, because the Logos is infinite and the approach to perfect alignment with the infinite is a process that never terminates. The upper attractor is not a destination one reaches and stops. It is a direction one goes forever.

## VII. Conclusion: The Significance of Daily Trajectory

If heaven and hell are attractor states—if the judgment reveals the trajectory rather than assigning a destination—then every day is not the day before a verdict. Every day is the trajectory itself.

Every opening of \(O\) contributes to the growth term. Every closing of \(O\) permits the decay term to run. Every received grace shifts the basin in one direction. Every refused grace shifts it in the other. The integral over a life is not the sum of dramatic choices at existential crossroads. It is the aggregate of a thousand small adjustments—the channel opened or closed in the ordinary moment, the true bit honored or the false bit accepted, the gap preserved or filled.

The separatrix does not announce itself. Crossing it toward the lower basin does not feel like catastrophe. It feels like the natural continuation of a trajectory that has been building for years. This is why the framework's insistence on the gap, on the \(O\) term, on the daily practice of reception—is not about spiritual disciplines designed to feel virtuous. It is maintenance of the dynamics that determine which basin one's trajectory occupies.

The attractor states are two. The trajectory determines which one is approached. The trajectory is made of ten thousand ordinary choices about whether the channel is open. The mathematics is structured such that every moment genuinely matters. The coefficient on every day is real. The integral is real. The attractor is real.

And grace is what makes the trajectory reversible before it is not, and what maintains the floor after it is.

Heaven is where one has been going when one has been going *toward* the invariant.
Hell is where one has been going when one has been going *away* from it.
The judgment reveals the trajectory.
The trajectory is made of every day that preceded it.

> "We all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another." (2 Corinthians 3:18, NRSV)
