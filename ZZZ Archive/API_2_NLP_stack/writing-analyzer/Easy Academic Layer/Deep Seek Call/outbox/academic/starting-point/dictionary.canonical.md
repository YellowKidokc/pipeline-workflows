## 2. The Coherence Function: Formal Definition

Let χ represent the coherence function, defined as:

\[
\chi = \frac{\text{Tr}(\rho^2)}{\text{Tr}(\rho_{\text{max}}^2)} \cdot \frac{\langle \psi | \hat{C} | \psi \rangle}{\langle \psi | \psi \rangle}
\]

where:
- \(\rho\) is the density matrix of the system
- \(\rho_{\text{max}}\) is the density matrix at maximum purity
- \(\hat{C}\) is the coherence operator
- \(\text{Tr}(\rho^2)\) is the purity measure (dimensionless, range [0,1])
- The ratio \(\langle \psi | \hat{C} | \psi \rangle / \langle \psi | \psi \rangle\) normalizes the coherence expectation value

The normalized coherence parameter σ is then:

\[
\sigma = \frac{\chi - \chi_{\text{min}}}{\chi_{\text{max}} - \chi_{\text{min}}} \in [-1, +1]
\]

At σ = +1, the system exhibits maximal phase alignment, minimal entropy, and maximal structural integrity. At σ = −1, the system exhibits complete phase randomization, maximal entropy, and structural dissolution.
