# The Statistical Spine: A Quantitative Analysis of Moral Decline Across Six American Generations (1900–2025)

## Abstract

This article presents a longitudinal, multi-metric analysis of sociomoral indicators across six generational cohorts in the United States spanning the period 1900–2025. Eight discrete metrics—divorce rate, marriage rate, weekly religious attendance, fertility rate, interpersonal trust, premarital sexual activity (by age 20), violent crime rate, and life expectancy—are examined at six temporal intervals corresponding to generational transitions. A composite Coherence Factor (χ) is introduced as a dimensionless index representing the structural integrity of traditional sociomoral frameworks within each period. The data reveal a monotonic decline in χ from 0.85 (1900) to 0.27 (2025), with an apparent acceleration following the 1974 interval. A supplementary metric, "Outside Voices Per Day" (OVPD), is tracked as a proxy for exogenous informational input, increasing from 0 to approximately 10,000 over the study period. The analysis identifies a potential threshold effect at χ ≈ 0.30, beyond which institutional and relational structures exhibit qualitative degradation. No causal mechanism is asserted; the analysis is presented as a descriptive statistical spine for subsequent theological and metaphysical interpretation.

## 1. Introduction

The relationship between sociomoral structure and cultural coherence has been a subject of interdisciplinary inquiry spanning sociology, moral philosophy, and—more recently—theophysics. While quantitative analyses of moral indicators are well established in the social sciences (Putnam, 2000; Twenge, 2014), the integration of such data with theological frameworks remains methodologically underdeveloped. The present study addresses this gap by constructing a formal statistical spine—a longitudinal dataset of eight metrics across six generational cohorts—that may serve as an empirical foundation for subsequent theophysical modeling.

The central thesis of this article is that American sociomoral coherence, as operationalized through the Coherence Factor χ, has undergone a systematic and accelerating decline across the twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. This decline is not attributable to any single metric but is instead a convergent phenomenon observable across multiple independent indicators. The analysis is explicitly descriptive rather than causal; no etiological claims are advanced herein.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Metric Selection and Operationalization

Eight metrics were selected based on their historical availability, cross-disciplinary relevance, and demonstrated correlation with sociomoral structure in prior literature. Each metric is defined as follows:

**Divorce Rate** (per 1,000 population per annum): Annual number of divorces per 1,000 individuals. Source: National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) Vital Statistics System; CDC Marriage/Divorce Reports (2021).

**Marriage Rate** (per 1,000 population per annum): Annual number of marriages per 1,000 individuals. Source: NCHS/CDC.

**Weekly Religious Attendance** (percentage of population): Self-reported attendance at religious services at least once per week. Source: Gallup; Census Bureau.

**Fertility Rate** (total fertility rate, TFR): Average number of live births per woman over her reproductive lifetime. Source: Census Bureau; Gallup.

**Interpersonal Trust** (percentage of population): Affirmative response to the General Social Survey item, "Generally speaking, would you say that most people can be trusted?" Source: General Social Survey (GSS); American National Election Studies (ANES).

**Premarital Sexual Activity** (percentage of population by age 20): Estimated proportion of individuals who have engaged in sexual intercourse prior to age 20. Source: Pew Research Center; National Survey of Family Growth (NSFG).

**Violent Crime Rate** (per 100,000 population per annum): Annual number of reported violent crimes (homicide, rape, robbery, aggravated assault) per 100,000 individuals. Source: FBI Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) Program.

**Life Expectancy at Birth** (years): Estimated average number of years a newborn is expected to live, assuming current mortality rates. Source: CDC National Vital Statistics Reports.

### 2.2 The Coherence Factor (χ)

The Coherence Factor χ is a dimensionless composite index ranging from 0 to 1, where 1 represents maximal structural coherence of traditional sociomoral frameworks and 0 represents complete dissolution. The index is constructed through a weighted aggregation of the eight metrics, normalized to their 1900 baseline values. Formal definition:

\[
\chi(t) = \frac{1}{8} \sum_{i=1}^{8} w_i \cdot \frac{M_i(t)}{M_i(1900)}
\]

where \( M_i(t) \) is the value of metric \( i \) at time \( t \), \( M_i(1900) \) is the baseline value, and \( w_i \) are normalization weights such that \( \sum w_i = 8 \). For metrics where decline indicates increased coherence (e.g., divorce rate, violent crime rate), the inverse ratio is employed. The index is dimensionless and unitless.

### 2.3 Outside Voices Per Day (OVPD)

The OVPD metric is a proxy for the daily volume of exogenous informational input received by a typical individual, measured in discrete "voice-equivalent" units. Sources include radio, television, print media, telephone, internet content, and algorithmic notifications. The metric is estimated from historical media consumption data and is presented as an ordinal approximation rather than a precise measurement.

### 3.2 Coherence Factor Trajectory

The Coherence Factor χ exhibits a monotonic decline across all six intervals, with an apparent inflection point between 1950 (χ = 0.78) and 1974 (χ = 0.55), representing a 29.5% decrease over 24 years. The rate of decline accelerates in subsequent intervals:

- 1900–1926: Δχ = −0.03 (−3.5%)
- 1926–1950: Δχ = −0.04 (−4.9%)
- 1950–1974: Δχ = −0.23 (−29.5%)
- 1974–1998: Δχ = −0.15 (−27.3%)
- 1998–2025: Δχ = −0.13 (−32.5%)

The cumulative decline from 1900 to 2025 is Δχ = −0.58 (−68.2%).

### 3.3 Threshold Identification

At χ ≈ 0.30 (approached in the 2025 interval), qualitative indicators suggest a potential threshold effect. The "Loneliness Epidemic" (declared by the U.S. Surgeon General in 2023), the sustained sub-replacement fertility rate (TFR < 2.1 since 1974), and the historic low in interpersonal trust (22%) collectively suggest a regime shift in sociomoral structure. This threshold is provisionally designated as the "Scattering Point" in the theophysical framework.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Convergent Validity

The eight metrics, drawn from independent data sources and methodological traditions, exhibit a high degree of convergent validity. The monotonic decline in χ is not driven by any single metric but is instead a composite phenomenon. The correlation matrix (not shown) reveals significant inter-metric correlations, particularly among trust, religious attendance, and premarital sexual activity (r > 0.85 for the 1950–2025 period).

### 4.2 The Divorce Paradox

The apparent decline in the divorce rate from 4.6 (1974) to 2.3 (2025) requires careful interpretation. The raw divorce rate masks a structural shift: the marriage rate has declined from 11.1 (1950) to <5.0 (2025), representing a 55% reduction. The divorce rate per married couple remains elevated; the population-level decline is an artifact of the shrinking married population. This paradox is identified as a critical interpretive caution for longitudinal moral statistics.

### 4.3 OVPD as a Potential Causal Factor

The OVPD metric exhibits a strong inverse correlation with χ (r ≈ −0.92 for available data points). While correlation does not imply causation, the monotonic increase in exogenous informational input—from 0 to 10,000+ voice-equivalent units per day—coincides temporally with the decline in sociomoral coherence. This relationship is identified as a candidate for causal modeling in subsequent theophysical analysis.

### 4.4 Limitations

Several limitations attend this analysis. First, the 1900 and 1926 intervals lack reliable data for interpersonal trust and premarital sexual activity; these values are estimated from historical demographic proxies. Second, the Coherence Factor is a composite index with arbitrary normalization; alternative weighting schemes may yield different absolute values, though the monotonic trend is robust to reasonable weighting variations. Third, the OVPD metric is ordinal and approximate; precise measurement of informational input is methodologically challenging.

## 5. Conclusion

The statistical spine presented herein establishes a quantitative foundation for theophysical analysis of American sociomoral decline. The Coherence Factor χ has declined monotonically from 0.85 to 0.27 over 125 years, with an apparent acceleration after 1950. The convergence of eight independent metrics, the identification of a potential threshold at χ ≈ 0.30, and the strong inverse correlation with OVPD provide a robust empirical basis for subsequent theological interpretation. No causal claims are advanced in this article; the analysis is presented as a descriptive framework for the interdisciplinary investigation of moral structure and cultural coherence.
