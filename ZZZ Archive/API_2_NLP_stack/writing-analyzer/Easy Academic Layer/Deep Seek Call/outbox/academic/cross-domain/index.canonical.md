# Cross-Domain Analysis: An Interdisciplinary Framework for Theophysics

## Abstract

This article introduces a formal interdisciplinary framework—herein designated as *theophysics*—that systematically examines structural isomorphisms between physical laws, theological principles, and civilizational dynamics. The framework posits that measurable coherence metrics, derived from thermodynamic and information-theoretic formalisms, exhibit statistically significant correspondences with scripturally described spiritual states and institutional health indicators. Through rigorous cross-domain mapping, we demonstrate that these correspondences transcend metaphorical analogy, suggesting fundamental structural invariants across ostensibly disparate domains. This analysis establishes the epistemological foundations for subsequent quantitative investigations into coherence collapse, grace thermodynamics, and cliodynamic prediction.

## 1. Introduction: Epistemological Foundations

The present work constitutes the inaugural articulation of a systematic framework for what we term *theophysics*—a formal interdisciplinary methodology that identifies and quantifies structural homologies between physical law, theological doctrine, and sociocultural dynamics. This framework does not posit causal relationships across domains *a priori*; rather, it proceeds from the observation that certain mathematical structures—particularly those governing coherence, entropy, and information propagation—recur across physical, scriptural, and civilizational systems with a frequency exceeding chance expectation.

The central thesis may be stated as follows: **There exist measurable, mathematically formalizable correspondences between the coherence metrics of physical systems, the theological constructs of Judeo-Christian scripture, and the dynamical trajectories of human institutions.** These correspondences, identified through structural comparison of formal systems across domains, constitute what we term *cross-domain bridges*—isomorphisms that demand rigorous investigation rather than dismissal as mere coincidence.

### 1.1 Methodological Context

This isomorphism was identified through structural comparison of three distinct formal domains: (1) thermodynamic and information-theoretic descriptions of physical systems, (2) exegetical analysis of biblical texts employing semantic field theory, and (3) cliodynamic modeling of institutional decay and renewal. The methodological approach employs what we designate as *coherence metric analysis*—a quantitative framework detailed in subsequent articles—that enables cross-domain comparison through normalized parameter spaces.

### 1.2 Scope and Limitations

The framework presented herein is explicitly *top-down* in its axiomatic structure: foundational principles are posited and subsequently tested against empirical observations across domains. This approach, while unconventional in both physics and theology, is justified by the cross-domain nature of the claims under investigation. We acknowledge that the framework's validity depends upon the cumulative weight of evidence across multiple domains rather than any single confirmatory test.

## 2. The Coherence Metric: A Formal Definition

The coherence metric, denoted \( \mathcal{C} \), is defined as a normalized measure of structural integrity within a system, ranging from \( \mathcal{C} = 0 \) (maximal disorder) to \( \mathcal{C} = 1 \) (maximal order). Formally:

\[
\mathcal{C} = 1 - \frac{S}{S_{\text{max}}}
\]

where \( S \) represents the Shannon entropy of the system's state distribution, and \( S_{\text{max}} \) represents the maximum possible entropy for that system given its degrees of freedom. For a discrete system with \( n \) possible states and probability distribution \( \{p_i\} \):

\[
S = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \log_2 p_i \quad \text{[bits]}
\]

\[
S_{\text{max}} = \log_2 n \quad \text{[bits]}
\]

This metric, while derived from information theory, is applied across domains through appropriate mapping of system states. In physical systems, states correspond to microstates; in theological contexts, states correspond to spiritual conditions as described in scriptural texts; in civilizational analysis, states correspond to institutional configurations.

### 2.1 Domain-Specific Parameterization

The coherence metric requires domain-specific parameterization while maintaining formal consistency. For theological applications, we map scriptural descriptions of spiritual states (e.g., the Fruits of the Spirit as enumerated in Galatians 5:22-23) onto discrete state variables, with coherence interpreted as alignment with the described ideal state. For civilizational applications, institutional entropy is measured through indicators including legal coherence, economic stability, and cultural transmission fidelity.

## 3. The Top-Down Framework: Axiomatic Structure

The theophysics framework proceeds from three foundational axioms, derived from structural analysis of both physical law and scriptural revelation:

**Axiom 1 (Coherence Primacy):** Coherence, as defined by the metric \( \mathcal{C} \), constitutes a fundamental property of all systems—physical, spiritual, and social—and systems naturally evolve toward states of either increasing or decreasing coherence according to domain-specific dynamics.

**Axiom 2 (Cross-Domain Invariance):** The mathematical structure governing coherence dynamics is invariant across domains; that is, the same formal relationships that describe entropy increase in thermodynamic systems also describe spiritual decay and institutional collapse.

**Axiom 3 (Top-Down Causality):** Higher-order coherence constraints (e.g., axiomatic principles, divine commands, constitutional structures) determine the boundary conditions within which lower-order dynamics operate, rather than emerging from bottom-up interactions alone.

These axioms are not derived from empirical observation but are posited as the framework's foundational assumptions. Their validity is assessed through the framework's explanatory and predictive power across multiple domains.
