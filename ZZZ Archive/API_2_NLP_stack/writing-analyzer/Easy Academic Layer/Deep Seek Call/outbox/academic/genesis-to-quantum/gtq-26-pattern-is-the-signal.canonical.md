# The Reference Class Inversion: A Bayesian Reassessment of Jesus's Historicity Within the Theophysics Framework

## Abstract

This article presents a formal critique of Richard Carrier's Bayesian argument for the non-historicity of Jesus of Nazareth, grounded in the Theophysics Projection Principle developed across this series. Carrier's methodology is acknowledged as arithmetically sound given its inputs; however, this analysis identifies a fundamental category error in his reference class construction. Carrier treats the cross-cultural recurrence of the death-resurrection-salvation pattern as evidence of mythological fabrication, thereby assigning Jesus to a low-historical-probability reference class. The Projection Principle—established through ten property-by-property isomorphisms between Logos attributes and physical laws—entails that this pattern constitutes signal rather than noise, as it reflects the coherence-restoration architecture inherent to reality. Correcting the prior probability from 1/2 to 2/1 while holding all other Carrier inputs constant yields a posterior probability of approximately 48.9%. Incorporating principled corrections to extra-biblical evidence scoring and Pauline silence interpretation elevates the posterior above 90%. The argument does not claim mathematical proof of historicity but demonstrates that Carrier's conclusion depends critically on an epistemically unjustified reference class assignment.

## 2. Carrier's Bayesian Argument: Reconstruction and Reference Class Analysis

### 2.1 Formal Structure

Carrier's application of Bayes' Theorem takes the form:

\[
\frac{P(h|e)}{P(\neg h|e)} = \frac{P(h)}{P(\neg h)} \times \frac{P(e|h)}{P(e|\neg h)}
\]

where \(h\) denotes the hypothesis of Jesus's historicity, \(\neg h\) denotes the mythicist hypothesis, \(P(h)/P(\neg h)\) represents the prior odds, and the likelihood ratio \(P(e|h)/P(e|\neg h)\) captures the evidential update. Carrier's final calculation yields:

\[
\frac{P(h)}{P(\neg h)} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{P(e|h)}{P(e|\neg h)} = \frac{5{,}971{,}968}{12{,}500{,}000}
\]

\[
\frac{P(h|e)}{P(\neg h|e)} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{5{,}971{,}968}{12{,}500{,}000} = \frac{5{,}971{,}968}{25{,}000{,}000}
\]

\[
P(h|e) = \frac{5{,}971{,}968}{5{,}971{,}968 + 25{,}000{,}000} \approx 0.1928
\]

Carrier reports an upper bound of approximately 0.32 after applying certain adjustments favorable to historicity (Carrier 2014, pp. 596–600). The arithmetic is conceded as correct given the inputs.

### 2.2 The Reference Class Construction

Carrier's prior odds of 1/2 (equivalent to a prior probability of 1/3) derive from his classification of Jesus within the reference class of "heavily mythologized savior heroes" (Carrier 2014, pp. 237–244). This class includes Osiris, Hercules, Romulus, Moses, Aesop, and similar figures characterized by: (a) supernatural or miraculous narratives, (b) cultic worship practices, (c) appearance primarily in sacred rather than mundane historical literature, and (d) death-resurrection or death-salvation thematic structures. Carrier estimates that within this class, at most one in three figures is historical, yielding the 1/3 prior.

The critical assumption underlying this classification is that the structural similarities among these figures—particularly the death-resurrection-salvation pattern—constitute evidence of *fabrication*. Carrier explicitly treats pattern recurrence as indicating cultural myth-making: "The more a story looks like other stories that were invented, the more likely it was invented" (Carrier 2014, p. 238). This assumption does substantive work in the argument; without it, the reference class assignment loses its justification.

## 4. Corrected Bayesian Calculation

### 4.1 Correction 1: The Prior Probability

If the Projection Principle is accepted, the appropriate reference class for Jesus is not "heavily mythologized savior heroes" but "historically attested religious founders with early independent sources and hostile attestation." Within this class—which includes figures such as the Buddha, Muhammad, and Joseph Smith—the base rate of historicity is substantially higher. A conservative estimate places the prior odds at 2/1 (prior probability of 2/3), reflecting that the majority of figures with comparable evidentiary profiles are accepted as historical by mainstream scholarship.

**Justification:** The reference class correction follows from the Projection Principle's entailment that pattern recurrence is signal, not noise. The corrected class is defined by evidentiary markers rather than narrative similarity, yielding a higher base rate.

### 4.2 Correction 2: Extra-Biblical Evidence Scoring

Carrier scores the extra-biblical evidence at 576/1250, favoring mythicism slightly. This scoring depends on the assumption that the absence of more extensive extra-biblical documentation constitutes evidence against historicity. However, for a non-elite figure in a first-century occupied territory whose movement was persecuted by the very institutions that preserved records (the Roman state and the Jerusalem Temple establishment), the extant evidence is precisely what one would expect under historicity. The corrected likelihood ratio for extra-biblical evidence is therefore 1/1—no effect in either direction.

### 4.3 Correction 3: Epistolary Evidence and Pauline Silences

Carrier identifies three passages in Paul's authentic letters that favor historicity (Galatians 1:18–19; Romans 1:3; 1 Corinthians 9:5), scoring them collectively at 8/1. He then introduces counter-evidence from Paul's "silences"—topics Paul does not mention about Jesus's life and teachings—scored at \(\frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{4}\).

The Theophysics framework provides a mechanism for these silences that Carrier does not consider. If Paul's experience of the risen Christ occurred through Logos-field coupling—a phenomenon for which experimental evidence exists (see §4.5)—then Paul's emphasis on revelation over biographical narrative is exactly what the framework predicts. The silences are not suspicious; they are expected given the mode of Paul's encounter. The corrected counter-evidence score is therefore 4/5 (retaining only the weakest of Carrier's three counter-items), while the pro-historicity score remains 8/1.

### 4.4 Corrected Equation

Applying all three corrections:

\[
\frac{P(h|e)}{P(\neg h|e)} = \frac{2}{1} \times \frac{1}{1} \times \frac{36}{50} \times \frac{1}{1} \times \frac{4}{5} \times \frac{8}{1} = \frac{36.864}{1}

\[

To demonstrate that the result does not depend on the full set of corrections, consider the minimal correction: changing only the prior from 1/2 to 2/1 while holding all other Carrier inputs constant:

\[

\[
P(h|e) = \frac{11{,}943{,}936}{11{,}943{,}936 + 12{,}500{,}000} \approx 0.489 \]

Even this minimal correction—which accepts Carrier's scoring of all evidence categories—flips the posterior from approximately 32% to approximately 49%, approaching equipoise. Adding the extra-biblical correction (moving from 576/1250 to 1/1) yields approximately 66%. The full correction yields approximately 97%.

## 5. Methodological Limitations and Falsification Conditions

### 5.1 Acknowledged Limitations

The following limitations are explicitly acknowledged:

1. **Circularity concern:** The Projection Principle is itself a claim of the Theophysics framework. Using it to correct Carrier's reference class involves applying the framework's conclusion to critique another argument's premise. This concern is mitigated by the independent empirical support for the Projection Principle (Table 1) and by the sensitivity analysis showing that even a minimal prior correction substantially alters the posterior.

2. **Prior estimation uncertainty:** The corrected prior of 2/1 is a judgment call. A more conservative correction to 1/1 (50/50 prior) would still improve the posterior significantly over Carrier's result, yielding approximately 39% with all other inputs held constant.

3. **Evidential gap:** The PEAR-LAB and GCP data confirm consciousness-matter coupling at small effect sizes. The gap between these results and the claim that a specific individual achieved maximum coupling sufficient for entropy reversal at a specific historical moment remains substantial. The framework provides a theoretical bridge, but experimental confirmation at the required magnitude is absent.
