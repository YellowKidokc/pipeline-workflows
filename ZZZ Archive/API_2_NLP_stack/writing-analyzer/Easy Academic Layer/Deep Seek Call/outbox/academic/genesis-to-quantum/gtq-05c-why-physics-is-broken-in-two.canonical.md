### 2. Comparative Analysis: The Properties of Each Framework

#### 2.1 General Relativity: The Logos-Imprint

General Relativity, as formalized by Einstein's field equations,

\[G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}\]

where \(G_{\mu\nu}\) is the Einstein tensor representing spacetime curvature, \(\Lambda\) is the cosmological constant, \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is the metric tensor, \(G\) is Newton's gravitational constant, \(c\) is the speed of light in vacuum, and \(T_{\mu\nu}\) is the stress-energy tensor, exhibits the following properties:

- **Determinism:** Given complete initial conditions, the future state of the system is calculable with perfect precision. No probability distributions are required.
- **Continuity:** Spacetime is modeled as a smooth, differentiable manifold with no discrete structure.
- **Observer-Independence:** The moon exists at the position GR predicts regardless of whether any observer is measuring it. There is no observer problem in GR.
- **Exactness:** Solutions to the field equations yield exact values, not probability amplitudes.

These properties—order, law, coherence, determinism—are consistent with the theological concept of the Logos as articulated in John 1:3: "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made" (English Standard Version). The physical world, if created through a perfectly coherent Logos, should carry the signature of that coherence. GR, at the large scale, preserves this signature.

#### 2.2 Quantum Mechanics: The Fracture Signature

Quantum Mechanics, as operationalized by the Born Rule,

\[P(a) = |\langle a|\psi\rangle|^2\]

where \(P(a)\) is the probability of obtaining measurement outcome \(a\), \(|\psi\rangle\) is the quantum state vector, and \(\langle a|\) is the eigenstate corresponding to outcome \(a\), exhibits the following properties:

- **Probabilism:** The best possible prediction is a probability distribution. This is not a matter of epistemic ignorance but is claimed by the Copenhagen interpretation to be the actual structure of quantum reality.
- **Discontinuity:** Quantum transitions occur in discrete, discontinuous jumps (e.g., quantum leaps, wavefunction collapse).
- **Observer-Dependence:** The act of measurement changes the system. The observer is not external and neutral but is entangled with the observed system.
- **Indeterminacy:** Particles do not possess definite positions or momenta until measured; they exist in superposition states described by probability amplitudes.

These properties—noise, probability, observer-dependence, discontinuity—are not properties of the Logos as described in the Johannine prologue. They are, however, precisely what a receiver produces when it has been decoupled from a perfectly ordered source: a clean signal read through a broken instrument.
