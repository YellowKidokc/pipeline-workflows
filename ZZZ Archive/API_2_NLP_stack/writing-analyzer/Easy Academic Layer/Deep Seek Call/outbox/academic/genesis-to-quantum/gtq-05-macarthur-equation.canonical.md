# MacArthur and the Equation: A Two-Phase Resolution of the Calvinist-Arminian Antinomy via Coherence Dynamics

## Abstract

The present article proposes a formal resolution to the sixteen-century theological dispute between Calvinist and Arminian soteriological frameworks. Drawing upon a dynamical systems model of spiritual coherence, we demonstrate that the apparent antinomy between divine sovereignty and human responsibility dissolves when salvation is understood as a two-phase process governed by a single differential equation. Phase 1 (monergistic) describes God's unilateral action upon a spiritually closed system, corresponding to the Reformed position articulated by John MacArthur. Phase 2 (synergistic) describes genuine human response within a grace-opened channel, corresponding to the Arminian position. Both phases are shown to be sequentially necessary and mathematically derivable from the coherence dynamics equation $\dot C = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C$. The resolution preserves the plain reading of all load-bearing proof texts from both traditions without requiring reinterpretation. Kill conditions are specified for empirical falsification.

## I. Introduction: The Persistence of the Antinomy

For approximately sixteen centuries, Christian theology has sustained a dialectical tension between two apparently irreconcilable propositions: (1) that God sovereignly elects individuals to salvation prior to any human action, and (2) that human beings possess genuine libertarian agency in responding to divine grace. This tension has generated an extensive literature spanning Augustine of Hippo (354–430 CE), Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274), Martin Luther (1483–1546), John Calvin (1509–1564), Jacobus Arminius (1560–1609), John Wesley (1703–1791), and contemporary figures including John MacArthur.

The standard resolution within Reformed orthodoxy has been to characterize this tension as an *antinomy*—a pair of propositions that appear contradictory but are both true, with their reconciliation lying beyond finite human comprehension (MacArthur, 1988). The Arminian tradition has typically resolved the tension by emphasizing prevenient grace as the condition that enables but does not compel human response (Wesley, 1740). Both approaches, however, require interpretive softening of the opposing tradition's clearest proof texts.

The present article argues that this softening is unnecessary. We demonstrate that the Calvinist and Arminian positions describe sequential phases of a single dynamical process, formalized by the coherence dynamics equation. This equation, derived from structural comparison of soteriological models with open-system thermodynamics, partitions salvation into two distinct dynamical regimes: a monergistic phase (Phase 1: $s: -1 \rightarrow 0$) in which God acts unilaterally on a closed system, and a synergistic phase (Phase 2: $s: 0 \rightarrow +1$) in which human response is genuinely efficacious within a grace-opened channel.

## II. Thesis Statement

**Primary claim:** The Calvinist-Arminian antinomy resolves into a two-phase dynamical process governed by the equation $\dot C = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C$, where each tradition accurately describes one phase without requiring reinterpretation of the other's proof texts.

**Secondary claims:**
1. At surrender parameter $s = -1$, the coupling function $\alpha(s) = (1+s)/2$ equals zero, yielding pure decay dynamics $\dot C = -S \cdot C$—this corresponds to MacArthur's "dead in trespasses and sins" (Ephesians 2:1).
2. The transition $s: -1 \rightarrow 0$ is monergistic: God acts unilaterally on $s$ to open the system, corresponding to effectual calling and irresistible grace.
3. At $s > 0$, the growth term $O \cdot G(1-C)$ requires both human openness ($O$) and divine grace ($G$), corresponding to Wesley's synergistic account of prevenient grace enabling genuine choice.
4. The grammatical asymmetry in Romans 9:22–23 (active verb for election, passive for reprobation) reflects the dynamical asymmetry: salvation requires external input; destruction is endogenous.

## III. The Calvinist Position: MacArthur's Formulation

John MacArthur's soteriology represents a rigorous articulation of Reformed orthodoxy. His position rests on four pillars, each grounded in scriptural exegesis:

### III.1 Divine Election as Pre-Creation Choice

MacArthur (1988) states: "The doctrine of election simply means that God, uninfluenced and before creation, predetermined certain people to be saved." This claim is anchored in Ephesians 1:4 (NA28): *καθὼς ἐξελέξατο ἡμᾶς ἐν αὐτῷ πρὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου* ("just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world"). The election is unconditional—not contingent upon divine foreknowledge of human response, which would render God's choice derivative rather than sovereign.

### III.2 Total Inability

Ephesians 2:1 (NA28) states: *καὶ ὑμᾶς ὄντας νεκροὺς τοῖς παραπτώμασιν καὶ ταῖς ἁμαρτίαις ὑμῶν* ("And you were dead in your trespasses and sins"). MacArthur interprets "dead" (*νεκρούς*) as denoting complete spiritual incapacity: the unregenerate person cannot initiate or even cooperate with salvific grace. The first move must originate entirely outside the system.

### III.3 Effectual Calling

The divine call to salvation is not merely persuasive but efficacious. When God calls the elect, they necessarily respond—not under coercion but because their nature has been transformed. This is grounded in passages such as John 6:37 (NA28): *πᾶν ὃ δίδωσίν μοι ὁ πατὴρ πρὸς ἐμὲ ἥξει* ("All that the Father gives Me will come to Me").

### III.4 Simultaneous Affirmation of Human Responsibility

Notably, MacArthur (1988) simultaneously affirms that "all men are commanded to repent," that "everyone is held culpable and guilty for not repenting," and that "they're unable to respond apart from the intervening sovereign grace of God." This threefold affirmation—command, culpability, inability—constitutes the antinomy he refuses to resolve:

> "God's sovereign election and man's exercise of responsibility in choosing Jesus Christ seem opposite and irreconcilable truths—and from our limited human perspective they are opposite and irreconcilable. That is why so many earnest, well-meaning Christians throughout the history of the church have floundered trying to reconcile them." (MacArthur, *Ephesians Commentary*)

## IV. The Arminian Position: Wesley's Formulation

The Arminian tradition, as articulated by John Wesley, emphasizes the universality of divine grace and the genuineness of human response. Its key scriptural anchors include:

### IV.1 Universal Divine Intention

John 3:16 (NA28): *Οὕτως γὰρ ἠγάπησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν κόσμον* ("For God so loved the world"). The object of divine love is *κόσμον* (the world), not a predetermined subset. Second Peter 3:9 (NA28): *μὴ βουλόμενός τινας ἀπολέσθαι ἀλλὰ πάντας εἰς μετάνοιαν χωρῆσαι* ("not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance").

### IV.2 Genuine Human Choice

Joshua 24:15 (BHS): *בַּחֲרוּ לָכֶם הַיּוֹם* ("Choose for yourselves today"). Revelation 3:20 (NA28): *ἰδοὺ ἕστηκα ἐπὶ τὴν θύραν καὶ κρούω· ἐάν τις ἀκούσῃ τῆς φωνῆς μου καὶ ἀνοίξῃ τὴν θύραν, εἰσελεύσομαι πρὸς αὐτόν* ("Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him").

### IV.3 Prevenient Grace

Wesley's distinctive contribution is the doctrine of *prevenient grace*—grace that precedes and enables human response. This grace restores sufficient libertarian capacity that every person can genuinely respond to or resist the divine offer. The response, however, remains genuinely the person's own.

## V. The Failure of Traditional Resolution Strategies

Both traditions, when confronted with the other's strongest proof texts, resort to interpretive strategies that soften the plain reading:

**Calvinist strategy:** "Any" in 2 Peter 3:9 is interpreted as "any of the elect." "World" in John 3:16 is interpreted as "the elect throughout the world."

**Arminian strategy:** "Chose" in Ephesians 1:4 is interpreted as "foreknew" (i.e., God chose those He foresaw would believe). "Dead" in Ephesians 2:1 is interpreted as "morally sick" rather than "spiritually incapacitated."

Both strategies exhibit the same hermeneutical pattern: reading one's systematic conclusion into the text to preserve internal consistency. This pattern suggests that the underlying problem is not exegetical but structural—both traditions are operating with an implicit single-phase model of salvation that cannot accommodate the full range of scriptural data.

## VI. The Two-Phase Resolution: Conceptual Framework

The resolution proposed here is structural rather than hermeneutical. Salvation is modeled as a two-phase process, with each tradition accurately describing one phase:

### Phase 1: Monergistic ($s: -1 \rightarrow 0$)

The spiritually dead system ($s = -1$) cannot initiate its own recovery. God acts unilaterally to open the system, creating the possibility of response where none existed. This phase is entirely monergistic—God alone acts. **Calvinist territory.**

### Phase 2: Synergistic ($s: 0 \rightarrow +1$)

Once the system is open, grace flows through the channel. The human agent now possesses genuine capacity to respond—to surrender, resist, or cooperate. Growth requires both divine grace and human openness. **Arminian territory.**

The two camps are not contradicting each other; they are describing sequential phases of the same event and disputing which phase constitutes "salvation." The resolution requires both phases as sequentially necessary.

## VII. The Coherence Dynamics Equation

### VII.1 Master Equation

The dynamics of spiritual coherence are governed by:

$$\frac{dC}{dt} = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C$$

**Variable definitions:**

| Symbol | Definition | Range | Dimension |
|--------|------------|-------|-----------|
| $C$ | Coherence with God (spiritual alignment, closeness) | $[0, 1]$ | Dimensionless (normalized) |
| $O$ | Openness (human reception variable) | $[0, 1]$ | Dimensionless |
| $G$ | Grace (divine input; sovereign, external) | $[0, \infty)$ | $T^{-1}$ (rate parameter) |
| $S$ | Entropy/sin (decay pressure; always active post-Fall) | $[0, \infty)$ | $T^{-1}$ (rate parameter) |
| $t$ | Time | $[0, \infty)$ | $T$ |

**Term analysis:**

- **Growth term:** $O \cdot G(1-C)$ — requires both human openness ($O$) and divine grace ($G$). The factor $(1-C)$ represents remaining capacity for growth, ensuring logistic saturation as $C \to 1$.
- **Decay term:** $S \cdot C$ — always active post-Fall, proportional to current coherence. Represents the entropic pull of sin.

### VII.2 The Surrender Parameter and Coupling Function

The surrender parameter $s$ ranges from $-1$ (fully closed, autonomous) to $+1$ (fully surrendered). The coupling function $\alpha(s)$ determines what fraction of grace $G$ actually reaches the system:

$$s \in [-1, +1], \quad \alpha(s) = \frac{1+s}{2} \in [0, 1]$$

Effective grace input is $\alpha(s) \cdot G$. At $s = -1$, $\alpha = 0$; at $s = +1$, $\alpha = 1$.

### VII.3 Phase 1: The Dead System ($s = -1$)

At $s = -1$, the coupling function equals zero. No grace reaches the system. The master equation reduces to:

$$\left.\frac{dC}{dt}\right|_{s=-1} = -S \cdot C$$

**Dynamical analysis:** This is a first-order linear decay equation. The equilibrium $C^* = 0$ is stable. The system cannot generate positive $\dot C$ from internal dynamics alone. It is dynamically dead—not metaphorically but mathematically.

**Theological mapping:** This corresponds precisely to MacArthur's "dead in trespasses and sins" (Ephesians 2:1). The system at $s = -1$ cannot initiate its own recovery. The first move must come from outside—this is monergism.

**Phase 1 transition ($s: -1 \rightarrow 0$):** God acts unilaterally on $s$ to open the system. The person at $s = -1$ did not choose this transition; dead systems do not choose. Every Reformed verb (wills, draws, grants, calls, appoints, prepares, causes, chooses) describes $G$ acting on a closed system to crack it open.

### VII.4 Phase 2: The Open System ($s > 0$)

For $s > 0$, the coupling function $\alpha(s) > 0$, and the growth term becomes active:

$$\left.\frac{dC}{dt}\right|_{s>0} = \alpha(s) \cdot O \cdot G \cdot (1-C) - S \cdot C$$

**Dynamical analysis:** The growth term requires both $O$ and $G$. Neither alone produces growth. The system now has genuine dynamical choice: the person can increase $O$ (surrendering deeper), decrease $O$ (resisting), or maintain $O$ (stasis). Each choice produces a different trajectory.

**Theological mapping:** This corresponds to the Arminian insistence that human response is genuinely efficacious. The $O$ variable is real—it multiplies $G$ and shapes the trajectory. "Whoever believes" (John 3:16) operates here: belief corresponds to increasing $O$, which enables the growth term to overcome the decay term.

## IX. The Double Predestination Question

MacArthur (1988) rejects double predestination—the thesis that God actively reprobates the non-elect. His position: God actively elects the saved and passively "passes by" the rest, allowing them to continue in their own trajectory.

### IX.1 Grammatical Evidence from Romans 9

Romans 9:22–23 (NA28) exhibits a striking grammatical asymmetry:

- **Vessels of wrath:** *κατηρτισμένα εἰς ἀπώλειαν* ("fitted for destruction") — passive participle (*κατηρτισμένα*), indicating preparation by another agent or by circumstances.
- **Vessels of mercy:** *ἃ προητοίμασεν εἰς δόξαν* ("which He prepared beforehand for glory") — active verb (*προητοίμασεν*), indicating direct divine action.

### IX.2 Dynamical Mapping

The equation maps this asymmetry cleanly:

$$\text{Election: } G \rightarrow \Delta s > 0 \quad (\text{active})$$
$$\text{Reprobation: } s \text{ remains at } -1 \Rightarrow \dot C < 0 \quad (\text{passive, endogenous})$$

At $s = -1$ with $\alpha = 0$, the equation is pure decay: $\dot C = -S \cdot C$. The system decays on its own trajectory. No divine push is needed. God sustains common grace ($G > 0$ always) while the system's own dynamics carry it downward.

**Active election:** God acts on $s$ to produce the $-1 \rightarrow 0$ transition. Active verb. God prepares, chooses, calls.

**Passive reprobation:** The transition does not occur. The system remains at $s = -1$. Decay does its own work. Passive verb. Fitted for destruction by its own trajectory.

The asymmetry is not arbitrary. Salvation requires external input; destruction requires only the absence of that input. Romans 9:22–23 exhibits grammatical asymmetry because the grammar reflects the actual dynamics.

## X. Augustine's Four-State Mapping

Twelve centuries prior to the formalization of this equation, Augustine of Hippo (354–430 CE) mapped four states of the human will that correspond precisely to specific $s$ regimes:

| State | Augustine's Terminology | $s$ Value | Dynamics |
|-------|------------------------|-----------|----------|
| Pre-Fall | *Posse peccare, posse non peccare* (able to sin, able not to sin) | Variable | Channel open; entropy not yet accumulated; genuine choice in both directions |
| Post-Fall (apart from grace) | *Non posse non peccare* (unable not to sin) | $s = -1$ | Locked in decay; coupling zero; cannot stop downward trajectory from inside |
| Under Grace | *Posse non peccare* (able not to sin) | $0 \leq s \leq +1$ | Channel reopened; person can choose to receive or resist; Phase 2 active |
| In Glory | *Non posse peccare* (unable to sin) | $s = +1$ | Perfect coupling; no internal resistance; no competing signals |

### X.1 The Paradox of Freedom

Augustine's framework generates a paradox that troubled subsequent theology: how is *non posse peccare* (inability to sin) more free than *posse peccare, posse non peccare* (ability to sin or not)?

The equation provides an answer. At $s = +1$, the coupling is perfect ($\alpha = 1$). The person's will and God's will produce identical output—not because the person is overwritten but because they are fully transparent to grace. Paul's statement in Galatians 2:20 (NA28) captures this: *ζῶ δὲ οὐκέτι ἐγώ, ζῇ δὲ ἐν ἐμοὶ Χριστός* ("It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me"). The "freedom" to choose sin was always the freedom to choose decay ($\dot C < 0$). Losing it is not losing freedom; it is gaining freedom *from* the entropic pull of sin.

## XI. Philippians 2:12–13 as Summary Equation

MacArthur's frequently preached passage, Philippians 2:12–13 (NA28), states the entire equation in one sentence:

> *μετὰ φόβου καὶ τρόμου τὴν ἑαυτῶν σωτηρίαν κατεργάζεσθε· θεὸς γάρ ἐστιν ὁ ἐνεργῶν ἐν ὑμῖν καὶ τὸ θέλειν καὶ τὸ ἐνεργεῖν ὑπὲρ τῆς εὐδοκίας.*

**Mapping:**

| Phrase | Equation Mapping |
|--------|------------------|
| "Work out your salvation" (*κατεργάζεσθε*) | Maintain $O$; keep the channel open; your job, and it is real |
| "With fear and trembling" (*μετὰ φόβου καὶ τρόμου*) | Entropy $S$ is always active; complacency closes the channel |
| "For it is God who works in you" (*θεὸς γάρ ἐστιν ὁ ἐνεργῶν ἐν ὑμῖν*) | $G$ is the power source; you are not generating coherence but receiving it |
| "Both to will and to work" (*καὶ τὸ θέλειν καὶ τὸ ἐνεργεῖν*) | Even the willingness to respond is grace-enabled; God provides the opening, the power, and the desire |

This is not antinomy. It is one equation with two terms and two phases, both real, both necessary, both taught by Scripture.

## XII. Kill Conditions and Falsification

The equation either dissolves the antinomy or it does not. The following conditions specify empirical and hermeneutical tests:

### XII.1 Load-Bearing: Two-Phase Partition

**Condition:** Calvinist proof texts must slot into Phase 1; Arminian proof texts must slot into Phase 2—without either side reinterpreting the other's plain reading.

**Kill condition:** Any clearly load-bearing proof text from either side cannot be slotted into its phase without diluting the plain reading. If "chose us before the foundation of the world" (Ephesians 1:4) cannot be Phase 1 verbatim, or "whoever believes" (John 3:16) cannot be Phase 2 verbatim, the equation merely renames the antinomy.

**Status:** Confirmed for Ephesians 1:4, Ephesians 2:1, John 3:16, Revelation 3:20. **Confidence: HIGH**

### XII.2 Load-Bearing: Monergism at $s = -1$

**Condition:** At $s = -1$, the coupling function is zero, and only the decay term runs. The dead system cannot reverse itself; the first move must come from outside.

**Kill condition:** A coherent dynamical regime can be specified in which a system at $s = -1$ produces nonzero $\dot C > 0$ from internal dynamics alone—in which case the "dead don't choose" claim has a counterexample and Phase 1 monergism becomes optional.

**Status:** Confirmed. **Confidence: HIGH**

### XII.3 Load-Bearing: Synergism at $s > 0$

**Condition:** The growth term $O \cdot G(1-C)$ requires both factors. Real openness and real grace. Neither alone produces growth.

**Kill condition:** The growth term can be derived without the $O$ factor, OR the $O$ factor turns out to be a function of $G$ alone (i.e., openness is fully determined by grace)—in which case Phase 2 collapses back into Phase 1 and the synergistic reading evaporates.

**Status:** Confirmed. **Confidence: HIGH**

### XII.4 Suggestive: Romans 9 Grammatical Asymmetry

**Condition:** Active verb for vessels of mercy, passive for vessels of wrath. The equation maps this: salvation requires external input; destruction is endogenous.

**Kill condition:** Greek-text scholars find the active/passive distinction in Romans 9:22–23 is not load-bearing for the dynamics—e.g., a divine passive that implies God's active reprobation despite the surface grammar.

**Status:** Open. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

### XII.5 Suggestive: Augustine's Four-State Mapping

**Condition:** Augustine's four states correspond to discrete $s$ regimes.

**Kill condition:** Careful Augustinian scholarship shows the four states do not correspond to discrete $s$ regimes—e.g., if "in glory" is not a stable fixed point at $s = +1$ but a different dynamical regime altogether.

**Status:** Open. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

### XII.6 Destructive: Antinomy Beyond Phases

**Kill condition:** A load-bearing Calvinist or Arminian proof text exists that does not fit either Phase 1 or Phase 2 even in principle—in which case the antinomy is not two-phase dynamics but a third structure the equation does not capture.

**Status:** Open. **Severity: FRAMEWORK-LEVEL**

## XIII. Conclusion

The Calvinist-Arminian antinomy, sustained for sixteen centuries, resolves into a two-phase dynamical process governed by the coherence dynamics equation $\dot C = O \cdot G(1-C) - S \cdot C$. Phase 1 ($s: -1 \rightarrow 0$) is monergistic: God acts unilaterally on a closed system, corresponding to the Reformed position. Phase 2 ($s: 0 \rightarrow +1$) is synergistic: genuine human response operates within a grace-opened channel, corresponding to the Arminian position. Both phases are sequentially necessary, both are scripturally attested, and neither requires reinterpretation of the other's proof texts.

The equation describes the mechanism of *how* the transition works. It does not answer *why* God initiates it for some and not others. The initial conditions—who receives the effectual call—are set by a Person with reasons the equation does not access. The mystery of election itself remains; what dissolves is the apparent contradiction between sovereignty and responsibility.

MacArthur (1988) counseled leaving the harmonizing to God. The equation suggests that God has already done so—in one equation, with both terms present, both necessary, and neither alone sufficient.
