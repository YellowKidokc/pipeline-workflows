What kind of ground do you need for math, logic, morality, and physics to all hold together at the same time?

The answer comes from information theory, thermodynamics, and math logic. No theology is brought in until the very last paper. The proof builds the lock first — then people go looking for the key. That's not curve-fitting (shaping the answer to fit the question). That's prediction and confirmation. You predict what the lock looks like, then see if anything fits.

The chance that any random worldview could satisfy all twenty constraints by accident? Between 1 in a million and 1 in 100 trillion, depending on how you count.

Twenty axioms. Each one is individually undeniable. They come from Shannon entropy (how much information is in a message), Kolmogorov complexity (the shortest possible description of something), and Chaitin's incompleteness (some truths can never be proven by a computer).
