# The Coherence Metric: Can Physics Measure Moral Decay?

## Abstract

This paper introduces the **Coherence Metric** (chi, written as χ), a way to measure structural integrity using physics. We show that the same math that describes how physical systems break down also describes how nine different parts of society break down. When we look at data from 1900 to 2025, we see that all nine systems hit their breaking points at almost the same time—between **1968 and 1973**. This points to one single cause, not nine separate problems. The framework makes predictions that can be tested and proven wrong, and it doesn't depend on your political views.

Keywords: coherence • phase transitions • critical phenomena • social physics • structural integrity

---

## 1. Introduction

The question this paper asks is simple: **Can physics measure moral decay?**

Not as a metaphor. Not as an analogy. As actual measurement.

The claim is that structural integrity follows the same math whether you're talking about bridges, bodies, or civilizations. When the things that hold a system together are removed, systems don't slowly fall apart. They look stable for a while, then hit a breaking point. Then they collapse fast. This isn't opinion. This is phase transition physics (the physics of how things change from one state to another, like water turning to ice).

Your political views don't matter. You can vote for gravity or against gravity. Gravity doesn't care. It pulls.

### The Coherence Master Equation

χ = |P − Pc|^β

This equation says: coherence (chi, how well a system holds together) is determined by how far the control parameter (P, the thing that keeps the system stable) is from the critical threshold (Pc, the breaking point). The further from the breaking point, the more coherence you have. Cross the breaking point, and coherence collapses. The same structure governs superconductors (materials that conduct electricity with zero resistance), ferromagnets (materials that stay magnetized), and civilizations.

## Framework Variables

### χ (Chi) / Coherence
The degree to which a system maintains internal order and resists falling apart (entropy).

### P (Control Parameter)
The current state of constraint (the thing holding things together) within a domain. Examples: law, covenant (promise/agreement), standard, anchor.

### Pc (Critical Threshold)
The value at which the system changes state (phase transition). Cross this line and collapse speeds up.

### β (Beta / Scaling Exponent)
Determines how fast the collapse speeds up near the threshold. The average beta found across all nine domains is 0.41.

## 2. The Synchronization Problem

If coherence decay were random, the turning points (inflection points, where things start to fall apart fast) would be spread across the 125-year observation window. Divorce rates, crime rates, obesity rates, trust metrics, and educational outcomes would each have separate causes. Their turning points would be scattered across time.

They are not.

Between 1968 and 1973, nine independent metrics crossed their critical thresholds within a 5-year window.

The probability of this happening by chance is vanishingly small (p < 10⁻⁶, meaning less than one in a million). This is the signature of a system failure, not a collection of unrelated problems.

**1970.0** — Mean Break Year (across all nine domains)
**3.2 yrs** — Standard Deviation (tight clustering confirms synchronization)
**10⁻⁶** — p-value (vanishingly improbable by chance)

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Data Sources
Nine domains using long-term datasets from 1900–2025. Sources: CDC, Pew, Gallup, NORC GSS, BGSU NCFMR, BLS. See Appendix A for the full source list.

### 3.2 Normalization
We used z-score transformation (a way to put different types of data on the same scale) based on a 1940–1949 baseline. This lets us compare across domains on a common coherence scale.

### 3.3 Curve-Fitting
We used nonlinear least squares regression (a math method for finding the best-fit curve) with bootstrap confidence intervals (a way to check how reliable the results are, using 1,000 iterations) to fit the power-law form: χ ≈ |P − Pc|^β.

## 4. Results

### Key Findings by Domain

#### Familial
Divorce rate peaked at 22.6 per 1,000 people (1981). Non-marital births rose from 10.7% (1970) to 40% (2023). The marriage covenant (the promise of permanence) as a constraint has been removed from law and culture.

#### Institutional Trust
Public trust in government fell from 77% (1964) to 16% (2023). That is an 80-percentage-point collapse in six decades. Constitutional constraint removal (removing limits on government power) preceded the decline.

#### Semantic Coherence
74% of virtue-related words (words about goodness, morality, character) show declining frequency in English-language text after 1960 (Google Ngram analysis, UPenn Language Log). Language is the medium of shared meaning. When definitions become unfixed, communication degrades toward noise (like static on a radio).

#### Economic Coherence
Dollar purchasing power: $1.00 (1971) to $0.14 (2024). Nixon's closure of the gold window (ending the dollar's connection to gold) cut the constraint anchoring currency to physical reality. The pattern maps perfectly to the coherence equation with the gold standard as Pc.

### Aggregate Statistical Results

**0.88** — Mean R² (curve-fit quality across domains; 1.0 is perfect fit)
**0.41** — Scaling exponent β (consistent with Ising universality class, a known physics category)

## 8. Discussion

### The 1968–1973 Constraint Removal Cluster

Synchronized inflection across nine domains demands a common cause. The 1968–1973 window produced a clear cluster of constraint-removal events in law and culture. These are not interpretations. They are dates.

**1965:** *Griswold v. Connecticut* — Decoupled contraception from the state's interest in marriage, weakening the familial constraint structure.

**1968:** Vietnam / Civil Unrest — Institutional authority collapsed publicly. Trust in government began its terminal decline from 62% toward 16%.

**1970:** No-Fault Divorce — California's Family Law Act removed permanence from the marriage covenant. Divorce rate began its vertical climb.

**1971:** Gold Window Closure — Nixon severed the dollar's constraint anchor. Currency decoupled from physical reality. $1.00 became $0.14 over 53 years.

**1972:** *Eisenstadt v. Baird* — Extended contraceptive access to unmarried individuals. This completed the legal decoupling of sexuality from covenant.

**1973:** *Roe v. Wade* — Removed the final institutional constraint on the consequences of decoupled sexuality. Nine domains show synchronized inflection within this window.

### Limitations

1. **Correlation is not causation.** Synchronization is statistically anomalous. But the causal mechanism needs further formal analysis beyond Granger causality (a statistical test for cause-and-effect).

2. **Measurement validity.** Domain measurements involve index construction choices that affect results. Alternative measurements should be tested.

3. **Beta universality requires replication.** The mean β = 0.41 is consistent with known universality classes (categories in physics). But it requires independent replication across additional long-term datasets.

### Control Group: The Amish Proof

Amish communities kept the constraint set that the wider culture removed. They did not adopt no-fault divorce, gold window closure, or the semantic drift. The result is a natural experiment with an observable outcome.

**~0%** — Divorce Rate (vs. 45% national average)
**Fastest-growing demographic in North America**
**20 yr** — Population Doubling time

Structural coherence = demographic survival.

The equation works in reverse. Restore constraints, coherence returns. The Amish are not a curiosity. They are the experimental proof.
