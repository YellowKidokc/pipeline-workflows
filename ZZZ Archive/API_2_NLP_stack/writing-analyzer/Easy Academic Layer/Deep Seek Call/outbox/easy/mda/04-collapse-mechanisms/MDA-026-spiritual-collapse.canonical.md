### The Institutional Hemorrhage

By 1965, Mainline church membership started dropping in real numbers — from 31 million members in 1965 down to 25 million by 1988. This happened while the US population was growing. This wasn't a slow drift. It was an institutional hemorrhage (a massive, uncontrolled loss).

The "checking out" of America's elite from traditional Protestant moral views happened at almost the exact same time as the decline of virtue words. The cultural gatekeepers (the people who shape what society talks about) stopped using the language of virtue in 1962. Then they stopped going to the institutions that taught virtue in 1965.

## 5.2 The Psychological Pivot: From Anxiety to Medication (1963)

"The population began to manage the stress of the collapsing social order not through ritual or community (which were declining) but through chemistry."

The 1950s were called the "Age of Anxiety." But back then, people saw anxiety through a psychoanalytic or existential lens — it was a burden you had to carry or analyze. The medicalization of this state (turning it into a medical problem) was a huge shift in how culture handled distress.

The threshold for this change is marked by the introduction of **Valium (diazepam) in 1963**. It was marketed as a solution to the "psychic tension" of modern life. Valium sales exploded. It became the most prescribed drug in the United States between 1969 and 1982, peaking in 1978 with 2.3 billion pills sold.

### From Stoic Endurance to Pharmacological Management

This 1963 threshold (happening at the same time as the SAT peak and Mainline stagnation) signals the move from "stoic endurance" (a virtue ethic — living by moral character) to "pharmacological management" (a therapeutic ethic — treating problems with medicine). The population began to manage the stress of the collapsing social order not through ritual or community (which were declining) but through chemistry.

Think of it like this: **Thermodynamics / Judgment** — Like heat flowing from hot to cold — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system. Once the culture crossed this threshold, it couldn't go back on its own.

While the diagnostic shift from "Anxiety" to "Depression" as the main cultural ailment would happen later (in the 1980s), the mechanism of managing the self shifted decisively in 1963.
