# The Arc of Decline: 125 Years in Charts

This is a data-driven look at five generations of one family: Samuel (1900), Henry (1926), William (1950), Thomas (1974), Jacob (1998), and Jacob again (2025). The numbers tell a story that is hard to argue with.

Each generation was shaped by the one before it. Each inherited a world with certain rules and limits. Those limits changed over time. Some were lost. New freedoms appeared that earlier generations never wanted or missed. The direction is clear. The end point is not.

We look at three areas: **Family** (the basic unit of society), **Society** (trust, faith, and community), and **Economic** (money, debt, and living conditions).

## 1900: Samuel Lowe

**The Baseline - Before the Acceleration**

In 1900, America was a nation of small, tight circles. Seventy-six million people lived across 3.5 million square miles. Most people never traveled more than fifty miles from home. There was no radio, no telephone in rural Ohio, no moving pictures. The only voices Samuel heard belonged to people standing near him.

This was not deprivation. It was simply the shape of life before technology shrank the world. The world was small, but it felt whole. There were no images of other lives to breed discontent. No advertising to manufacture desire. No news to generate fear.

The constraints were hard and real. You could not rush a harvest or speed a letter across the county. Marriage was for life because divorce meant social death. The church was the only social institution that existed. Faith was not optional — it was the framework everything else ran on.

### Family Structure

- **Divorce Rate (per 1,000):** 0.7 (Baseline)
- **Marriage Age (M/F):** 26/22 (Baseline)
- **Cohabitation:** ~0% (Baseline)

### Society & Faith

- **Church Attendance:** ~95% (Near Universal)

### Technology & Connection

- **Households with Phone:** <1% (Baseline)
- **Households with Electricity:** <3% (Baseline)

## 1926: Henry Lowe

**The Builder - The First Intrusions**

By 1926, America had grown to 117 million people. Cities had exploded. New York held six million. Chicago held three. The Model T had put twenty million cars on American roads. What once was a long trip was now a two-hour drive. Distance was starting to die.

The deeper change was invisible: radio. The Lowe family bought a Radiola in 1924. For the first time, outside voices entered the home. A man talking in New York. An orchestra in Chicago. The complete isolation of Samuel's world had been broken. Other voices were now possible. Other lives. Other ways of being.

The family still listened together — one hour a night around the radio cabinet. It was still communal. Still shared. But the silence that had forced people inward was shrinking. The door had opened a crack. It would never close again.

### Family Structure

- **Divorce Rate (per 1,000):** 1.6 (+129% from 1900)
- **Marriage Age (M/F):** 24/21 (Stable)
- **Cohabitation:** ~0% (Still Rare)

### Society & Faith

- **Church Attendance (Rural):** ~90% (Still Universal)
- **Population:** 117M (+54% from 1900)

### Technology & Connection

- **Automobiles:** 20M (from 8,000)
- **Hours Media/Day:** ~1 (First Intrusion)

## 1950: William Lowe

**Peak Coherence - The Golden Moment**

America in 1950 had never been richer, safer, or more confident. The war was over. The Depression was memory. Half the world's manufacturing output came from American factories. The GI Bill sent eight million veterans to college. Suburbs bloomed on former farmland — each house with a yard, a garage, a TV antenna.

This was the peak. Not of wealth alone, but of coherence. Church attendance hit 55% weekly — the highest in American history. Divorce had stabilized. Families were marrying younger because they could afford to. The Baby Boom was underway: 3.7 children per woman by 1957. The middle class was growing fast.

Television arrived in a rush — 9% of households in 1950, 87% by 1960. No technology had ever spread that fast. But in 1950, it still felt controlled. The family gathered together to watch one shared screen. No one knew yet that this was the beginning of the end.

### Family Structure

- **Divorce Rate (per 1,000):** 2.6 (+271% from 1900)
- **Marriage Age (M/F):** 23/20 (Younger - Optimism)

### Society & Faith

- **Church Attendance:** 55% (PEAK - Historical High)
- **"No Religion":** 2% (Near Zero)
- **Median Income (2024$):** $42K (30% above 1940)

### Technology & Connection

- **TV Hours/Day (if owned):** 4.5 (New Environment)

## 1974: Thomas Lowe

**The Breaking - Everything Comes Apart**

Everything Bill had taken for granted came apart. It happened fast. Vietnam ended not with victory but with helicopters evacuating the embassy roof. 58,000 American boys were dead. Nothing was gained. Nixon resigned in August 1974 — the first president to do so. Watergate confirmed what many suspected: the people in charge will lie to you. Trust in government fell from 77% to 36% in a single decade.

The sexual revolution had arrived. Ten million women were on the Pill. For the first time in human history, sex was separated from pregnancy. The biological constraint that had shaped every generation — the simple, hard fact that sex could mean a baby — was gone. California passed no-fault divorce in 1969. Most states followed. By 1974, you could end a marriage simply because you wanted to.

Why marry if you don't have to? Why wait if there is no cost? The old structure was built on hard limits. The limits were gone. Everything that followed was the result.

### Family Structure

- **Divorce Rate (per 1,000):** 4.6 (+557% from 1900)
- **Marriage Age (M/F):** 24/22 (+117% from 1950)

### Society & Faith

- **Church Attendance:** 40% (-27% from Peak)
- **"No Religion":** 7% (-53% from Peak)
- **Violent Crime (per 100k):** 461 (+187% from 1960)
- **Murder Rate (per 100k):** 9.8 (+93% from 1960)

### Economic Reality

- **Consumer Credit:** $197B (Debt Rising)

### Technology Saturation

- **TV Hours/Day:** 6+ (Always On)
- **Homes with TV:** 98% (Universal)
- **Cable:** Emerging (Fragmentation Begins)

## 1998: Jacob Lowe

**The Aftermath - Rich, Comfortable, Untethered**

America in 1998 was rich, comfortable, and untethered. The Cold War was over. The Soviet Union had collapsed. The stock market was booming. Unemployment sat at 4.5%. The federal budget was running a surplus. Bill Clinton was being impeached for lying about an affair — and his approval ratings barely moved. The country had learned to separate public performance from private life. Just another boundary erased.

The internet was emerging. AOL, Yahoo, early Amazon. Twenty-six percent of households had access. By 2024, the question would be meaningless — the internet as common as electricity. Any question answered instantly. Any desire finding an outlet. You could talk to anyone, anywhere, anytime. Connection had never been easier.

But infinite connection was not the same as depth. Jake's parents divorced when he was five. He shuttled between states — different schools, different neighborhoods. He didn't know where he was from. Home was wherever the screen was glowing.

### Family Structure

- **Divorce Rate (per 1,000):** 4.0 (+471% from 1900)
- **Marriage Age (M/F):** 27/25 (Delayed/Skipped)
- **Cohabiting Couples:** 5.5M (10x from 1970)
- **Adults Living Alone:** 25M (from 4M in 1950)

### Society & Faith

- **Church Attendance:** 35% (-36% from Peak)
- **"No Religion":** 14% (from 2% in 1950)
- **Violent Crime (per 100k):** 566 (+252% from 1960)
- **Median Income (2024$):** $70K (Prosperity)

### Economic Reality

- **Consumer Credit:** $1.37T (7x from 1974)
- **Federal Debt:** $5.6T (12x from 1974)

### Technology Saturation

- **Cable Channels:** 50+ (Infinite Choice)

## 2025: Jacob Lowe

**The Choice - Connected, Isolated, Searching**

In 2023, the Surgeon General declared a loneliness epidemic. Social isolation increases your risk of early death by 26%. That is as deadly as smoking fifteen cigarettes a day. Americans have fewer friends than ever. Fewer marriages. Fewer children. Fewer real connections of any kind. The average American spends 7.5 hours a day on screens — more than with other people, more than sleeping.

And yet everyone is connected. Always online. Always reachable. AI girlfriends are now a billion-dollar industry. Dating apps have collapsed — not because people found love, but because they gave up. Infinite connection has produced infinite loneliness.

The birth rate has fallen to 1.6 children per woman — the lowest in American history. South Korea is at 0.7. The future is not being born. Something is deeply wrong. Most people feel it. The numbers confirm it.

### Family Structure

- **Divorce Rate (per 1,000):** 2.3 (-54% from 1900 | HISTORIC LOW — but fewer marry)
- **Adults Living Alone:** 37M (from 4M in 1950)
- **Couples Met Online:** 65%+ (from 0% in 1990)

### Society & Faith

- **Church Attendance:** 30% (-45% from Peak)
- **"No Religion":** 28% (from 2% in 1950)
- **Trust in Government:** <20% (-74% from Peak)

### Technology Saturation

- **AI Companion Users:** 30M+ (Replacing Humans)
- **Reachability:** Always Reachable, No Escape
