## What Coherence Means in Physics

In physics, **coherence** means the parts of a system act together. They are aligned. They move in sync.

This is not a metaphor. It is measurable.

In a laser, light waves are coherent when they share the same phase and frequency. In a superconductor, electrons are coherent when they form Cooper pairs. In a ferromagnet, atomic spins are coherent when they all point the same way.

Think of coherence like a tuning fork — when you strike it near other objects, they start vibrating at the same frequency. One source of perfect pitch brings everything else into harmony.

## The Order Parameter (chi)

Physicists measure coherence using an **order parameter**. This is a number that equals **zero** in the disordered (high-entropy) phase, is **non-zero** in the ordered (low-entropy) phase, and changes **sharply** at the phase transition.

Phase Transition — Plain English

This equation says: when temperature T is below the critical point Tc, the order parameter chi equals the distance from the critical point, raised to the power beta. The further below Tc, the stronger the order. When T rises above Tc, order disappears — chi drops to zero.

T = temperature | Tc = critical temperature | beta = critical exponent (typically 0.3–0.5)

This transition is **sudden, not gradual.** It is **universal across systems** —same math, different materials. And it is **predictable** once Tc is known.

## A Concrete Example: The Superconductor

Below Tc — Ordered

Electrons form Cooper pairs. Electrical resistance equals zero. The system shows quantum coherence at a scale you can see and measure.

Above Tc — Disordered

Cooper pairs break apart. Resistance returns. Coherence is lost.
