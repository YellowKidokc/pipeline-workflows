## Objections, Answered

### "They're just genetically different."

They are not an ethnic group — they accept converts. Their 85–90% retention rate means most chose to stay *after* seeing the alternative during Rumspringa (a period when teens experience the outside world). And their genetics are actually *worse* than average because of founder effects (limited gene pool from small starting group) and inherited disorders. They win in spite of biology, not because of it.

### "It only works because they're small."

They have grown 48 times in a century — from 8,000 to 385,000 — across 622 settlements in 32 states. The model grows by splitting: when a district gets bigger than the buggy travel distance, it divides. There is no theoretical population limit.

### "I wouldn't want to live like that."

That is not the question this study answers. The question is whether constraint produces order. Your personal preference does not change the data. And 85–90% of people raised in the system *do* want to live like that, after experiencing the alternative.

### "They free-ride on the outside world."

They pay taxes and take no benefits. They give more to the system than they take. Their model does not require isolation; it requires *constraint*. Remove the constraint, the result disappears with it.

### "This is cherry-picking. They have problems too."

They do. Genetic diseases from the small starting population. Limited formal education (balanced by 100% literacy and 5% business failure). Occasional abuse cases (at rates lower than the surrounding population). None of these reverse a clear advantage that holds across *every* measured area.
