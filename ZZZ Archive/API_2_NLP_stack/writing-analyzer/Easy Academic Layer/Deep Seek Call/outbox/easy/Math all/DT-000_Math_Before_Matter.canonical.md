# Page Zero: The Biaxiosum

This paper was written by a man who believes in God. That sentence should not make you ignore everything that follows. But in today's academic world, it often does.

## My Position & Where I'm Coming From

I am an independent researcher in Oklahoma City. I don't work for a university. I don't have a physics degree. I work in rental properties and the stock market. For the last sixteen months, I've built a mathematical framework with five AI systems as research partners. We've produced over 1,300 papers. I believe Jesus Christ is my Lord and Savior. I believe consciousness is fundamental — it's not something that just pops out of brain activity. I believe the universe is structured by something that looks exactly like what theologians call the Logos (the divine reason that orders everything).

**None of those beliefs appear in this paper.**

## The Central Claim

Mathematical structure came before physical reality. Math came first. Matter came second. This is not a religious argument. It is a logical one. Every physicist alive uses math as if it came before matter. This paper just asks them to be honest about what that means.

My position: I believe mathematical truth and divine truth are two different views of the same underlying structure. That belief is why I do this research. But it does not appear in the argument. The argument stands or falls on logic, not on my religion.

## The Biaxiosum Rule

> Tell me where you're starting from before you criticize where I'm starting from. If you believe math is a human invention, say so — and then explain why the universe obeyed it for 13.8 billion years before humans existed. If you believe math is an abstract object that exists in some non-physical realm, say so — and then explain how something that can't push or pull anything still makes bridges stay up and planets stay in orbit. Wherever you start, you end.

## Section 1: The Problem Everyone Skips

Every physicist uses mathematics as if it came before the physical world. The equations come first. The predictions follow. The experiments confirm. This way of working assumes, without saying it, that mathematical structure exists on its own — separate from the physical systems it describes.

Nobody argues about this when they're actually doing physics. The argument only starts when someone asks the obvious question: **if math is just a human invention — a useful made-up story, a language game, a cultural thing — then why did the universe obey it for 13.8 billion years before any human existed to invent it?**

This is not a trick question. It is a logical one. And the standard answers don't hold up.

### Answer 1: Math Is Invented (Nominalism)

The nominalist (someone who believes only physical things exist) says mathematical objects don't really exist. Numbers, sets, and functions are human constructions — useful labels we stick on patterns we see. There is no "2" floating in empty space. There is just the word "2" and our habit of counting.

The Problem

The universe was counting long before we were. Hydrogen atoms bonded in pairs 13.8 billion years ago. The inverse-square law (gravity gets weaker by the square of the distance) governed how things pulled on each other before any mind wrote it down. The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter was π when the first stars formed. None of this waited for us.

The nominalist has to explain how a human invention retroactively (backward in time) controlled the behavior of a universe that existed 13.8 billion years before its inventors. No nominalist has done this. The usual response is to change the claim: "Well, the patterns were always there; we just named them." But that gives away the argument. If the patterns were always there, then what you're calling an "invention" is actually a discovery. You have abandoned nominalism.

### Answer 2: Math Is Abstract (Platonism)

The Platonist (someone who believes ideas are real things) says math exists in an abstract realm — outside space and time, neither physical nor mental. Numbers are real objects, but they have no location, no mass, no ability to cause anything.

The Causal Problem

If mathematical objects can't cause anything, how do they make things obey them? Why does a bridge that breaks the load-bearing equations collapse? Why does an orbit that breaks Kepler's laws fix itself? Abstract objects, by definition, cannot push, pull, or constrain anything. They are causally inert (they can't make anything happen).

The Platonist faces a trap. Either mathematical objects can cause things (which contradicts them being abstract) or something else that can cause things puts mathematical law into action (which requires identifying what that something is). The standard Platonist has no answer to this.

### Answer 3: Math Is Useful Fiction (Fictionalism)

The fictionalist (someone who believes math is a useful story) says mathematical statements are literally false — there are no numbers, no sets, no functions — but they're useful tools. We pretend math is true because the pretending gives us accurate predictions.

The Prediction Problem

Why does a false theory give true predictions? In every other area, a false premise leads to false conclusions. A fake map of London will not get you to Paddington Station. A fake theory of chemistry will not produce working medicines. If math is fiction, it is the only fiction in history that reliably predicts physical reality to fourteen decimal places.

## Section 2: The Asymmetry

The three standard positions fail because they all try to make math depend on something else — on human minds (nominalism), on an abstract realm (Platonism), or on a fiction (fictionalism). But the dependency runs the other way.

| Property | Mathematical Truth | Human Invention |
|---|---|---|
| **Existence** | Necessary — true in all possible worlds | Contingent — depends on inventor |
| **Duration** | Eternal — true before and after time | Temporal — begins when invented |
| **Stability** | Immutable — never changes | Mutable — revised, updated, discarded |
| **Scope** | Universal — same everywhere | Local — varies by culture and context |
| **Substrate** | Immaterial — not physical | Material — requires brains, paper, computers |
| **Role** | Foundational — grounds everything else | Derivative — depends on prior knowledge |
| **Reliability** | Infallible — cannot produce false results from true premises | Fallible — errors are routine |

The Categorical Mismatch

Every property of mathematical truth is the opposite of every property of human invention. This is not a small difference. It is a complete mismatch. Calling them the same thing is not simplicity. It is confusion.

## Section 3: The 13.8 Billion Year Gap

The strongest single piece of evidence that math came first is time-based.

- The universe is approximately 13.8 billion years old
- *Homo sapiens* appeared approximately 300,000 years ago
- Written mathematics appeared approximately 5,000 years ago
- Formal math systems (axioms and proofs) appeared approximately 150 years ago

The Gap

**For the first 13,799,700,000 years**, the universe operated according to mathematical law with no human mind to observe, formalize, or invent anything. Gravity followed the inverse-square law. Electromagnetic radiation obeyed Maxwell's equations. Quantum processes followed the Schrödinger equation. Heat and energy systems obeyed the laws of entropy (the tendency of things to become more disordered).

The nominalist has three options:

1. **Option 1:** The universe did not obey mathematical law before humans. This is proven false by evidence.
2. **Option 2:** The universe obeyed mathematical law, but that law was not "math" until humans named it. This is just playing with words.
3. **Option 3:** Mathematical law is something we project backward onto a lawless universe. This makes all of physics fictional and all engineering just lucky guesses.

The gap is the argument. 13.8 billion years of the universe doing math without a mathematician. The simplest explanation is that mathematical structure was there first. Everything else was built on it.

## Section 4: Wigner's Question, Answered

In 1960, Eugene Wigner published "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences." He noticed that mathematical ideas developed for purely abstract reasons — with no physical use in mind — kept turning out to describe physical reality with amazing precision.

- **Non-Euclidean geometry** (geometry not on flat surfaces) was developed as a mathematical curiosity in the 19th century. Einstein used it to describe how space bends in 1915.
- **Complex numbers** (numbers involving the square root of -1) were invented as algebraic abstractions. Quantum mechanics requires them as fundamental.
- **Group theory** (the study of symmetry) was developed as pure mathematics. Particle physics is built on it.

Wigner called this "unreasonable" because he had no explanation for it. If math is a human invention, there is no reason it should match physical reality so perfectly.

The Resolution

But if math came before physics — if physical reality is made from mathematical structure rather than the other way around — then Wigner's mystery disappears. Math describes physics perfectly because physics is built from math. The effectiveness is not unreasonable. It is unavoidable.

## Section 5: The Five Limitation Theorems

Five independent results from five independent fields all point to the same structural conclusion: **closed systems cannot prove themselves correct.**

| Theorem | Year | What It Proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Gödel** | 1931 | No consistent formal system can prove its own consistency |
| **Tarski** | 1933 | No formal language can define its own truth |
| **Turing** | 1936 | No algorithm can determine whether an arbitrary program will stop running |
| **Second Law** | 1850s | No closed system can reverse its own entropy (disorder) |
| **Landauer** | 1961 | Processing information always costs some minimum amount of energy |

Unforced Convergence

Five different mathematicians and physicists, working in five different fields, across a century of research, arrived at the same structural conclusion. This is not forced agreement. This is unforced convergence — the kind that matters.

**What this means for math coming first:** If no system can prove itself correct, then the mathematical structure that controls physical reality cannot be a product of physical reality. It must come from outside. Whatever "outside" means, it has the properties of mathematical truth: necessary, eternal, immutable, universal. Math before matter.

## Section 7: Falsification Criteria

This paper can be proven wrong by any of the following:

1. **Contingent Mathematical Truth:** Show a mathematical truth that could be different. Show that 2+2 could equal 5 in some possible world.
2. **Temporal Mathematical Truth:** Show a mathematical truth that changes with time. Show that a mathematical statement was false before a certain date and true after.
3. **Local Mathematical Truth:** Show a mathematical truth that is local. Show that a mathematical theorem holds in one culture or location but not another.
4. **Causal Abstract Objects:** Explain how abstract objects can cause things to happen without giving them both abstractness and the ability to act.
5. **Alternative to Wigner:** Give another explanation for why math works so well that does not assume math came first.

None of these have been done. The paper stands until one is.

## Section 8: The Burden Framework

This paper does not ask for agreement. It asks for engagement. Specifically, it asks anyone who disagrees to do one of three things:

Move 1: Attack the Method

Show that the property comparison uses bad categories, or that the properties listed are not really properties of mathematical truth. If the asymmetry table is wrong, the argument falls apart.

Move 2: Attack the Data

Show that one or more of the seven property matches is wrong — that mathematical truth is, in fact, contingent, temporal, mutable, or local. If even one property can be reversed, the categorical mismatch gets much weaker.

Move 3: Produce a Better Explanation

Offer another explanation for all seven properties, the 13.8 billion year gap, Wigner's effectiveness, the five limitation theorems, and the causal problem that uses fewer steps than "mathematical structure came first." Simplicity is a scientific principle, not a religious one.

A misquote of Stephen Hawking is not Move 1, 2, or 3. Saying "you don't have a degree" is not Move 1, 2, or 3. Saying "everyone agrees" is not Move 1, 2, or 3. The argument is structural. It must be defeated structurally.

**Until one of these three moves succeeds, the paper stands.** Not because the author is stubborn. Because that is how evidence works.
