# The 7Q Framework — Reverse Direction

## Start With Destruction. End With a Name.

The normal scientific method starts with a guess and tries to prove it right. This method starts with everything that could destroy your idea — and whatever survives gets its name at the very end. You don't have to trust the author. You watched everything else die.

### Traditional (Forward)

Start with guess → collect evidence → try to prove it → announce the answer.

**Problem:** You assume the identity of the idea right from the start. The guess already has a name. This makes you biased toward what you already believe. You're trying to prove something you already think is true.

### Reverse (7Q)

Start with destruction → pull out consequences → trace the foundations → add evidence → say what survived → place it in the bigger picture → name it last.

**Advantage:** The idea earns its identity. It doesn't assume it. The conclusion is what's still standing after everything else failed. You didn't prove it — you watched it survive.

The normal method starts with a seed and hopes it grows. The reverse method starts with a forest fire and names what's still standing.

## 1. from Q7 — Destroy

### Show Every Way It Could Die

**Function:** Define failure FIRST. Before you build anything, map the kill zone.

#### The Axe Falls First

unknown → vulnerable

You don't start by saying what you believe. You start by listing **every way your idea can die.** Self-refutation (it contradicts itself). Infinite regress (it keeps asking "why" forever). Empirical contradiction (real-world data says it's wrong). Logical incoherence (it doesn't make sense). Explanatory failure (it can't explain anything). Five death conditions, named clearly, before you do anything else.

"If you can't name how it dies, you can't know if it's alive."

#### Physics Example — General Relativity, Reversed

Before saying what General Relativity (GR) claims, start here: **GR dies if** light doesn't bend around mass. GR dies if gravitational waves don't exist. GR dies if GPS doesn't need relativistic correction. GR dies if spacetime singularities (points where gravity becomes infinite) are impossible. GR dies if the equivalence principle (gravity and acceleration feel the same) is wrong. Five kill conditions, laid out before any argument is made.

The question for the reader: "These are the ways this idea dies. Watch what happens next."

## 2. from Q6 — Force

### Extract What the Survivor Produces

**Function:** If this survives step 1, what does it NECESSARILY generate?

#### What It Produces

vulnerable → constrained

The kill conditions from step 1 didn't fire. The idea survived. Now ask: **what does the survivor necessarily produce?** Not what you hope it produces — what it MUST produce if it's true. Predictions. Constraints. Consequences in other fields. If it produces nothing, it's a dead tree — standing but fruitless.

"A surviving claim that generates nothing downstream explains nothing."

#### What GR Forces

If spacetime curves around mass, then: **black holes must exist** (extreme curvature). **Gravitational waves must propagate** (ripples in curvature). **Time must run slower near mass** (metric distortion). **Light must bend around galaxies** (geodesic paths through curved space). Einstein didn't predict most of these — the math forced them.

The question for the reader: "This is not a guess anymore. This is what must follow."

## 3. from Q5 — Ground

### Show What It Stands On

**Function:** Trace the foundations. Where does the chain end?

#### The Root System

constrained → grounded

You've shown it can't be easily killed. You've shown what it forces. Now show **what it stands on.** What assumptions are required? What must already be true? Trace the chain down. Does it end at bedrock (a basic truth you accept without proof), at assertion (a brute fact you just state), or does it loop back on itself (circularity)?

"The reader now sees the foundations — not because you claimed them, but because the structure required them."

#### What GR Stands On

Equivalence principle (gravity = acceleration). Riemannian geometry (smooth curved surfaces). Constancy of c (the speed of light is always the same). Conservation of energy-momentum (energy can't be created or destroyed). The chain ends at the equivalence principle — **a basic truth, not a derived result.** And the smooth manifold assumption is the hidden root that might be wrong at quantum scales.

The question for the reader: "Now you see what holds it up. You decide if those foundations are solid."

## 4. from Q4 — Support

### Bring Evidence AFTER Survival

**Function:** Evidence confirms what survived — it doesn't build the argument.

#### Evidence After Survival

grounded → supported

Notice the order. In the forward direction, evidence comes early — you try to prove your guess. **In reverse, evidence comes AFTER the structure has already survived destruction.** Evidence here is confirmation, not construction. The argument was already built by steps 1-3. Now you're showing the data agrees.

"The evidence didn't build the case. It confirmed what survived."

#### Evidence Arrives Late — And It's Devastating

Mercury's precession (the way its orbit shifts): predicted exactly. Light bending: confirmed in 1919. Gravitational waves: detected in 2015. GPS corrections: working every day. By this point in the reverse argument, the reader has already seen: what kills it, what it forces, what it stands on. **The evidence landing now isn't persuasion — it's confirmation of a structure that was already standing.**

The question for the reader: "The structure survived. The data matches. This is not persuasion — this is confirmation."

## 6. from Q2 — Generalize

### Show It Holds Across Domains

**Function:** The survivor isn't trapped in one field. Place it in the broader landscape.

#### Multiple Soils, Same Tree

stated → located

The claim survived, produced consequences, has foundations, was confirmed by data, and has been precisely stated. "This isn't local. It survives across domains."

The question for the reader: "This isn't local. It survives across domains."
