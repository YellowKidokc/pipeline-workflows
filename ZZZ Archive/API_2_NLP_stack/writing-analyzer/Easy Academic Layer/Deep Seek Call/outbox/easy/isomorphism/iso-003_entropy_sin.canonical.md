# ISO-003: Entropy and Sin — The Same Shape of Decay

## Domains

**Domain A:** Physics — Thermodynamics (the study of heat, energy, and disorder)
**Domain B:** Christian Theology — Hamartiology (the study of sin)

**Concept A:** The Second Law of Thermodynamics — in any isolated system (no energy in or out), disorder always increases. Order breaks down unless you add energy from outside.

**Concept B:** Sin as moral entropy — moral order breaks down without outside help (grace). God's moral reality (the "coherence field") never changes, but our ability to connect with it gets worse over time if we close ourselves off.

---

## The Mapping

**Mathematical Form A:**

dS/dt ≥ 0 in isolated systems

Where S = thermodynamic entropy (measured in joules per kelvin, J/K). This means: in any system that is completely sealed off from the outside, disorder never decreases on its own. Order turning into disorder is the natural direction of things. The only way to make a local area more orderly is to bring in energy from outside the system — but that increases disorder somewhere else. The total disorder of the universe plus the system never goes down.

**Mathematical Form B:**

dD/dt > 0 where D = moral disorder in systems decoupled from grace

This means: in any person or community that is completely closed off from outside moral input (grace), moral disorder keeps getting worse. The coherence field C is conserved (div(C) = 0 — God's moral reality doesn't degrade or change). But the system's ability to receive and line up with that coherence gets worse over time. Sin is not destroying the moral field. Sin is the system losing its ability to connect with it.

**Shared Structure:**

Both describe the same kind of irreversible breakdown in closed systems. You need outside input to reverse it.

1. **Directionality** — Both processes only go one way in closed systems. Entropy increases. Moral disorder increases. Neither one spontaneously reverses on its own.
2. **Closure is the problem** — The breakdown isn't caused by what's inside the system. It's caused by the system being closed. An open thermodynamic system (one that can receive energy) can decrease local entropy. An open moral system (one receiving grace) can decrease moral disorder.
3. **External input reverses degradation** — In physics: you need energy from outside. In theology: you need grace. Both restore local order, but the source pays the cost.
4. **Total conservation** — In physics: the total entropy of the universe plus the system never decreases. In theology: div(C) = 0 — the coherence field is conserved. What changes is the system's ability to connect, not the field itself.
5. **Irreversibility without intervention** — A broken egg does not put itself back together. A fallen person does not fix themselves. Both need work from outside the system.

**What Is NOT Claimed:**

- NOT claiming sin IS entropy — sin works on moral coherence. Entropy works on thermodynamic microstates (tiny particle arrangements). They use completely different measurement units (J/K vs. the coherence variable C).
- NOT claiming moral decay can be measured in joules per kelvin — the match is in the *shape* of the degradation (topology), not the measurement units (metrics).
- NOT claiming thermodynamics has moral content — the Second Law is morally neutral. The parallel is in the structure of how closed systems break down.
- NOT claiming this proves total depravity — it shows that SOME degree of moral decay is inevitable in closed systems. But how fast and how far depends on the coupling coefficient α(u) (how open the system is to grace).
- NOT claiming all entropy is bad — thermodynamic entropy enables heat flow, chemical reactions, and life itself. The parallel is specifically about ISOLATED system entropy, where no outside input compensates.
- NOT claiming this is a new idea — the entropy/sin parallel has been noted before. What is new is the formal structural mapping with the coupling coefficient and the conservation of the coherence field.

## Tests

**Swap Test:** Can you put moral decay in the physics slot?

No. Thermodynamic entropy is measurable in joules per kelvin using Boltzmann's equation S = k_B ln(Ω). Moral entropy works on the coherence variable C, which cannot be reduced to counting microstates (or if it can, we don't know how yet). You cannot plug moral disorder into a heat engine calculation and get a meaningful answer.

However, the *shape* is identical:

- Steady increase in closed systems: yes (both)
- Reversal only through external input: yes (both)
- Conservation of the source field: yes (both — total energy is conserved, total coherence is conserved)
- Degradation is of the system's connection, not the field: yes (both — entropy doesn't destroy energy, sin doesn't destroy God's moral reality)

The swap test PASSES at the shape level (topological) and FAILS at the measurement level (metric). This is exactly what you expect for a structural isomorphism — same shape, different substance.

**Swap test result: PASSED (topological). FAILED (metric). This is expected for a cross-domain structural isomorphism.**

**Prediction in Domain A (Physics):**

- No perpetual motion machines. Every attempt to build a self-sustaining closed system that maintains or increases its own order will fail. This has been tested for about 170 years with zero exceptions.
- Every living system maintains order only by importing energy from outside (food, sunlight) and exporting entropy (heat, waste). Life is an open system. Closed biological systems die.
- The heat death of the universe (maximum entropy, no usable energy left) is the thermodynamic endpoint of a system with no external input. If the universe is a closed system, heat death is inevitable.

**Prediction in Domain B (Theology):**

- No self-salvation (Pelagianism fails). A person fully closed to grace cannot reverse their own moral decay, for the same structural reason a closed thermodynamic system cannot reverse its entropy. Pelagianism is the moral equivalent of a perpetual motion machine.
- Every morally ordered community maintains that order only by staying open to external moral input (grace, revelation, worship). Communities that close themselves to these inputs degrade morally over time, no matter how good they started. History confirms this pattern again and again.
- "Total depravity" in Reformed theology maps to heat death — the endpoint of a moral system with zero grace coupling (α = 0) for enough time. Not that every action is maximally evil, but that no part of the system is untouched by the decay. Just like heat death doesn't mean zero energy — it means zero usable energy.
- Sanctification (the process of becoming more holy) maps to refrigeration — local entropy decrease achieved by connecting to an external energy source. The cost is paid by the source (Christ), not the system.

**Bidirectional:** Yes.

- Physics → Theology: The Second Law limits which salvation theories are structurally possible. Any theology that claims you can save yourself violates the structural equivalent of energy conservation.
- Theology → Physics: The doctrine of sin predicts that any physical system modeling moral agents must include an entropy-like variable that increases in isolation. This can be tested in computer models of agents and social dynamics simulations.

**Falsification:**

1. **In Physics:** Show thermodynamic entropy spontaneously decreasing in a truly isolated system (not a tiny random fluctuation, but a sustained large-scale decrease). This would break the Second Law and remove the structural need for external input.
2. **In Theology:** Show a moral community that maintains or increases its moral order over many generations with zero external moral input — no revelation, no grace, no contact with any moral source beyond its own internal resources. If such a community exists, the moral entropy thesis fails.
3. **Break the shape:** Show that the structural features (steady increase, dependence on closure, reversal by external input, conservation of the source field) do not actually match between the two domains. This would require showing that one domain has a structural feature the other lacks at the shape level.
