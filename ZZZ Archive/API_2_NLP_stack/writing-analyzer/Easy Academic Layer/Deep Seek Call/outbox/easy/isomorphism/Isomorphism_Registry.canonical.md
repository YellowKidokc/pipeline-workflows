## ISO-002: Terminus Sui ↔ Grace

"Terminus Sui" means "self-terminating" or "self-rescuing." Six famous impossibility theorems show that no system can save itself. When you flip these theorems around, they create a five-part specification for what a "rescue from the outside" must look like. This specification maps perfectly onto the Bible's description of grace.

**The Six Impossibility Theorems:**
1.  **Gödel:** A system can't prove its own consistency.
2.  **Tarski:** A language can't define its own truth.
3.  **Turing:** A program can't predict if it will stop running.
4.  **Clausius:** An isolated system can't reverse its own entropy (disorder).
5.  **Landauer:** You can't erase information without spending energy from outside.
6.  **Leibniz:** A contingent (dependent) thing can't explain its own existence.

**The Five Properties of Grace (from Ephesians 2:8-9):**
- **G1 Externality:** "not of yourselves" — the rescue comes from outside.
- **G2 Sufficiency:** "gift of God" — it's complete, not partial.
- **G3 Asymmetric cost:** The cost is borne by the source, not the receiver.
- **G4 Merit-independence:** "not of works" — you don't earn it.
- **G5 Temporal priority:** "chosen before the foundation" — it was planned before you needed it.

**Shared Structure: The Inability to Self-Rescue**

Both domains describe systems that are structurally unable to fix themselves. The five properties needed for rescue are the logical opposites of the six impossibility theorems. Six theorems from three centuries and six different fields all point to the same specification, which matches a 2,000-year-old theological claim point-for-point.

Think of it like a car with a dead battery. The car cannot jump-start itself (Gödel). It can't tell you it's dead (Tarski). It can't fix its own engine (Turing). The battery's energy is running down and can't be reversed (Clausius). You can't get a spark from nothing (Landauer). The car can't explain why it exists (Leibniz). The rescue has to come from another car (externality), with a complete charge (sufficiency), and the other car's battery does the work (asymmetric cost). You don't earn the jump-start (merit-independence), and the other car was already there before you needed it (temporal priority).

**What this does NOT claim:** Grace is a theorem. The six theorems were intended to prove grace. This proves Christianity is true. The coupling coefficient α(u) can be measured in a theology lab.

**The Swap Test (PASSED):** Each property maps to a specific theorem. Externality is not the same as sufficiency (Gödel vs. Tarski/Turing). Asymmetric cost is not the same as merit-independence (Landauer vs. Clausius). Temporal priority is not the same as externality (Leibniz requires a prior ground, not just an external one). It's a one-to-one mapping.

**Predictions in Math/Physics:**
- No closed formal system will ever prove its own consistency.
- No self-contained AI can verify its own alignment (this is the "alignment problem").
- Every closed system eventually needs an external audit, energy source, or truth.

**Predictions in Theology:**
- Pelagianism (the idea you can save yourself) is like a perpetual motion machine—it doesn't work.
- Semi-Pelagianism (the idea you can start the process yourself) is like a system that can partially validate itself—still impossible.
- Any coherent theory of salvation must have all five G-properties.
- The Reformation was right: for coherence, the coupling coefficient α(u) must be zero (total surrender).

**How to Falsify This (Break ANY ONE):**
- A closed system proves its own consistency (breaks Gödel → G1).
- A formal language defines its own truth predicate (breaks Tarski → G2).
- A program decides its own halting (breaks Turing → G2).
- An isolated system decreases its own entropy (breaks Clausius → G4).
- A computation erases information at zero energy cost (breaks Landauer → G3).
- A contingent thing explains its own existence (breaks Leibniz → G5).
