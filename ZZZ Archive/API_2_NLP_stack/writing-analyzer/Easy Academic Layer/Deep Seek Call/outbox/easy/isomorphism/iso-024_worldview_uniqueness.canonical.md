# ISO-024: Worldview Uniqueness

## Isomorphism Record

**ID:** ISO-024
**Date:** 2026-03-10
**Status:** Candidate

---

## Domains

**Domain A:** Mathematics — A system of 8 equations with 8 unknowns usually has only one solution. Boundary conditions (rules that limit what counts as a valid answer) shrink the possible solutions. If physics gives us 8 boundary conditions, and only one worldview meets all 8, then that worldview is the unique solution. The claim: Christianity is the only worldview that meets all 8 boundary conditions. Other worldviews fail specific ones.

**Domain B:** Christian Theology — The idea that Christianity is the one true account of ultimate reality. Other religions have some truth, but they fail certain structural requirements that physics demands from any complete worldview.

---

## The Mapping

**Mathematical Form A:**

Elements:

- A16.1: Eight boundary conditions can be derived from the framework's physics
- R16.1: A spreadsheet that checks worldviews against the 8 boundary conditions
- T16.1: Christianity satisfies all 8 boundary conditions uniquely

Structure: Let BC = {BC1, BC2, BC3, BC4, BC5, BC6, BC7, BC8} be the set of boundary conditions. Let W be a worldview. W is viable only if it satisfies every single boundary condition from BC1 to BC8.

Claimed results from the framework's spreadsheet:

- Christianity: satisfies BC1 through BC8 (all 8)
- Islam: fails BC4, BC7, BC8
- Buddhism: fails BC1, BC6, BC7, BC8
- Hinduism: fails BC1, BC4, BC6, BC7, BC8
- (Other worldviews are presumably checked the same way)

**Mathematical Form B:**

Theological structure:

- Christianity claims to be the one true revelation of God
- John 14:6 — "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me"
- The framework claims to DERIVE this exclusivity from physics, not from the Bible. The boundary conditions come from physics, and Christianity just happens to be the unique solution.

**What Are the 8 Boundary Conditions?**

This is the key question. We cannot evaluate the isomorphism without knowing what BC1 through BC8 actually are. Based on the framework, they likely include conditions like:

- A boundary condition requiring a personal creator (this eliminates Buddhism and some forms of Hinduism)
- A boundary condition requiring creation out of nothing (this eliminates systems where the universe flows from God like water from a fountain)
- A boundary condition requiring God to be both beyond the universe AND present within it (this eliminates pure deism, where God is distant, and pure pantheism, where God is everything)
- A boundary condition requiring moral facts to be real (this eliminates moral relativism)
- A boundary condition requiring free will (this eliminates hard determinism)
- A boundary condition requiring grace, or outside help, for closed systems (this eliminates systems where you save yourself)
- A boundary condition requiring a Trinitarian structure (this eliminates unitarianism)
- A boundary condition requiring incarnation, God becoming human (this eliminates monotheisms without incarnation)

**IF** the boundary conditions are these, then the critique below applies with full force.

**Element-by-Element Mapping:**

| Mathematics | Theology | Structural Role |
|---|---|---|
| System of 8 equations | 8 requirements from physics | The set of rules |
| Unique solution | Christianity | The one worldview that meets all rules |
| Failed equations | Boundary conditions a worldview violates | Reasons for disqualification |
| Equations must be independent | Boundary conditions must come from different physics, not repeat each other | Prevents cheating |

**Shared Structure:**

1. **Constraint satisfaction** — Both domains test candidates against a fixed set of requirements
2. **Uniqueness from over-determination** — Enough independent rules force a single answer
3. **Objective criteria** — The boundary conditions are claimed to come from physics, not theology, making the evaluation fair

**What Is NOT Claimed:**

- NOT claiming other religions have no truth — they may satisfy many boundary conditions, just not all 8
- NOT claiming this is a mathematical proof of Christianity — it is an argument based on meeting requirements, and it depends entirely on whether the boundary conditions are correct and the evaluations are fair
- NOT claiming this replaces faith — even if Christianity uniquely meets the boundary conditions, this does not force belief; it gives rational support
- NOT claiming this is the only way to evaluate worldviews — it is ONE method, not THE method
- NOT claiming Muslims, Buddhists, or Hindus are irrational — they may reasonably disagree with the boundary condition derivations or the evaluations

## Tests

**Swap Test:** Can you swap the unique solution?

If the boundary conditions truly come from physics, then swapping should fail — Christianity should be the only worldview meeting all 8, and no other worldview should fit. But this swap test only works if the boundary conditions were created without knowing about Christianity. If the boundary conditions were made WITH knowledge of Christianity (even without realizing it), then the test is rigged — Christianity was in the room when the exam was written.

**Swap test result: CANNOT BE PERFORMED UNTIL THE BOUNDARY CONDITION DERIVATION IS AUDITED.** The swap test requires knowing that the boundary conditions were created without theological input. This is the single most important check for this isomorphism.

**Prediction in Domain A (Mathematics):**

- If the 8-boundary-condition system is real, then it should be possible to list ALL worldviews and check them against ALL boundary conditions, making a complete truth table. This is a concrete, doable task.
- The system should be stable under small changes: if a boundary condition is slightly modified (for example, "personal creator" is weakened to "transcendent ground of being"), does Christianity still uniquely meet all boundary conditions? If the uniqueness depends on exact wording, the system is fragile and the "uniqueness" is just a trick of definitions.

**Prediction in Domain B (Theology):**

- If Christianity uniquely meets all boundary conditions, then any future boundary condition derived from physics should also be met by Christianity. This is a genuinely testable prediction: derive BC9 from some new physics result and check whether Christianity satisfies it. If it doesn't, the uniqueness claim fails.
- The framework should be able to predict WHERE other worldviews will fail, and the predicted failures should match what scholars of those worldviews actually say (not simplified, unfair versions). This is testable by having scholars of those traditions review the framework's descriptions.

**Bidirectional:** Weak and problematic.

- Mathematics → Theology: The constraint-satisfaction framework gives a method for evaluating worldviews. This is useful IF the boundary conditions are valid.
- Theology → Mathematics: No insight flows from theology to mathematics here. Theology does not help with constraint satisfaction mathematics.
- The flow is one-directional: mathematics provides the method, theology provides the content. The "isomorphism" is really a mathematical method applied to a theological question, not a correspondence between two independent domains.

**Falsification:**

1. **Boundary condition derivation audit fails:** The boundary conditions were created with knowledge of Christian theology, and were (consciously or unconsciously) chosen to guarantee Christianity's success. This is the most likely failure mode. To test: have a non-Christian physicist derive boundary conditions from the framework's physics without knowing the theological application. Do they arrive at the same 8 boundary conditions? If not, the derivation is contaminated.
2. **Another worldview satisfies all 8:** A worldview not considered in the original analysis (for example, Process Theology, Open Theism, Bahá'í Faith, or a sophisticated form of Hinduism) satisfies all 8 boundary conditions. The uniqueness claim fails.
3. **Strawmanning test fails:** Present the framework's description of Islam, Buddhism, and Hinduism to scholars of those traditions. If the scholars say "that's not what we believe" or "you're evaluating the wrong version of our tradition," then the evaluations are attacking strawmen (simplified, unfair versions of the real thing).
4. **Boundary condition independence fails:** Show that some boundary conditions can be derived from others (for example, if "personal creator" plus "grace" forces "incarnation," then the system has fewer than 8 independent boundary conditions, and the apparent over-determination is inflated).
5. **The mathematics is trivially achievable:** Construct a toy example: define 8 conditions that exactly match the properties of blue. Then "blue uniquely satisfies all 8 conditions" is true by definition but tells you nothing. If the boundary conditions are effectively a definition of Christianity in physics language, the uniqueness is circular.

## Honest Assessment

**This is the isomorphism most vulnerable to confirmation bias (the tendency to look for evidence that supports what you already believe). The assessment must be correspondingly severe.**

**Problem 1: Who Wrote the Exam?**

The most devastating critique: the 8 boundary conditions were created by a Christian physicist who believes Christianity is true. The boundary conditions may have been (even unconsciously) chosen, worded, or weighted to guarantee the desired outcome. This is not a hypothetical concern — it is the default expectation. When the exam writer is also the predicted winner, the burden of proof for fairness is extremely high.

To salvage this isomorphism, the framework must show one of:

- (a) The boundary conditions were created by non-Christians who had no stake in the outcome
- (b) The boundary conditions are so obviously from physics that any physicist would arrive at the same set
- (c) The boundary condition derivation has been audited by hostile reviewers (non-Christian scholars, scholars of other religions) and survived

Without (a), (b), or (c), this isomorphism cannot advance beyond Candidate status, and honestly, it should probably be flagged as HIGH RISK FOR CONFIRMATION BIAS.

**Problem 2: Which Buddhism? Which Hinduism? Which Islam?**

The framework claims:

- Buddhism fails BC1, BC6, BC7, BC8
- Islam fails BC4, BC7, BC8
- Hinduism fails BC1, BC4, BC6, BC7, BC8

But each of these traditions contains enormous internal diversity:

**Buddhism:**

- Theravada: no creator, no soul, no grace — may indeed fail boundary conditions requiring these
- Mahayana: bodhisattva compassion functions structurally like grace; Buddha-nature may satisfy "soul" boundary conditions
- Pure Land: Amida Buddha's salvific vow is structurally the same as Christian grace — a being of infinite merit saves those who cannot save themselves by their own effort
- If Pure Land Buddhism satisfies the "grace" boundary condition, the evaluation changes

**Islam:**

- Sunni Ash'arism: strong divine sovereignty, occasionalism (God recreates the world at each moment) — may satisfy more boundary conditions than assumed
- Sufism: divine immanence (God present within the world), mystical union (fana/baqa) — may satisfy "immanence" boundary conditions
- If the framework is evaluating only a simplified version of Sunni orthodoxy, it is not evaluating Islam as a whole

**Hinduism:**

- Advaita Vedanta: Brahman as ultimate reality, the world as appearance — may fail "personal creator" boundary condition
- Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja): personal God, souls as real, divine grace — structurally similar to Christian theism on many points
- Dvaita (Madhva): dualistic theism, close to monotheism — may satisfy many boundary conditions
- If the framework is evaluating only Advaita, it is not evaluating Hinduism as a whole

**The diversity problem is fatal to simple boundary condition failure claims.** Any statement of the form "Buddhism fails BC X" must specify WHICH Buddhism, and the specification must be defensible to scholars of Buddhism.

**Problem 3: Is This an Isomorphism or an Application?**

An isomorphism maps between two independent domains that turn out to share structure. This isomorphism maps mathematics ONTO theology — it uses mathematical constraint-satisfaction to evaluate theological claims. That is an application of mathematics, not an isomorphism between mathematics and theology. The mathematics and theology are not independent domains sharing structure; mathematics is being used as a TOOL to evaluate theology.

This is a category distinction that matters. ISO-001 (Trinity ↔ Mathematical Self-Reference) identifies a genuine structural correspondence between two independent domains. ISO-024 uses one domain's methods on the other domain's content. These are different things.

**Problem 4: The Circularity Risk in Detail**

Consider this scenario:

1. The framework derives boundary conditions from physics
2. But the physics framework was built by someone who already believed Christian theology
3. The physics framework incorporates assumptions (personal creator, moral realism, grace structure) that are compatible with Christianity and may come from the builder's theological beliefs
4. The boundary conditions derived from this physics naturally favor Christianity
5. The "uniqueness" result is then presented as an independent physics derivation confirming Christianity

If this is what happened (even unconsciously), then the chain of reasoning is: Christianity → Physics framework → Boundary conditions → Christianity satisfies all boundary conditions. The conclusion is smuggled into the premises. The "physics derivation" is theology wearing a physics costume.

This is not an accusation but a structural risk. The framework must address it head-on or the isomorphism is fatally compromised.

## Recommended Actions Before Status Upgrade

1. **Independent boundary condition derivation:** Have a non-Christian physicist derive boundary conditions from the framework physics WITHOUT knowing the theological application. Compare results.
2. **Hostile review of worldview evaluations:** Have scholars of Islam, Buddhism, and Hinduism review the framework's description of their traditions. Document their objections.
3. **Diversity audit:** Evaluate at least 3 schools within each tradition (not just the version most convenient to criticize).
4. **Circularity audit:** Trace the boundary condition derivation chain from physics axioms to boundary conditions. At each step, ask: could this step have been influenced by theological beliefs? Document the answer.
5. **Robustness test:** Slightly modify each boundary condition (weaken, strengthen, or reword). Does Christianity still uniquely satisfy all of them? If the uniqueness is fragile under small changes, it is definitional rather than robust.

Until these actions are completed, this isomorphism should remain at Candidate status with a HIGH RISK flag.
