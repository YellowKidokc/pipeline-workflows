## Section I: The Problem with Bottom-Up Theories

### 1.1 The Endless Debate

For 60 years, scholars have argued about American decline:

- Conservatives blame the 1960s counterculture
- Progressives blame capitalism and racism
- Libertarians blame government expansion
- Communitarians blame individualism

**They are all wrong—and all right.**

Each one points to a *symptom* and mistakes it for the *cause*. This is like arguing whether the fever or the cough is killing the patient, while ignoring the real infection underneath.

### 1.2 Why Bottom-Up Fails

Bottom-up theories:

- **Start with observations** (divorce rates, trust surveys, crime statistics)
- **Propose causes** (cultural shifts, policy changes, technology)
- **Argue about which cause is most important**

This method *cannot settle the debate* because:

- Many symptoms can come from the same root cause
- Correlation does not mean causation
- The cause level is too shallow

You cannot diagnose a system failure just by listing its symptoms.

### 1.3 The Alternative: Top-Down Derivation

Mature physics works differently:

- **Set starting rules** (principles that cannot be broken without contradiction)
- **Derive predictions** (what must happen if the rules are true)
- **Check with evidence** (do observations match predictions?)

Einstein did not observe time slowing down and then invent relativity. He derived time slowing down from starting rules (light always travels the same speed, gravity and acceleration feel the same) and then looked for proof.

**Theophysics applies this method to social systems for the first time.**
