## Phase I — State + Entropy + Time

### 1. Measurement (Collapse)

Start here. Before time, before entropy, before structure — there is a system in superposition (all possibilities exist at once). Something selects a basis (a framework for measuring). The state resolves (becomes one definite outcome). Everything after follows from this.

**State:** Y ~ p(y) — superposed / unresolved (not yet decided)

**Operation:** Mb = measurement in basis b

**Update:** Y → yi with pi (the system picks one outcome with a certain probability)

**Entropy:** Spost < Spre — conditional entropy reduces (uncertainty goes down after measurement)

**Path:** Non-invertible — information loss to environment (you can't undo it)

**Result:** Single event ⇒ global state update (non-reversible)

### 2. Pre-Measurement (The Undivided Domain)

If collapse produces irreversibility (you can't go back), what existed before collapse? A domain where no basis has been selected. Maximum entropy under constraints (maximum uncertainty within the rules). All outcomes latent (hidden but possible). This is the pre-measurement state — and it requires no observer to exist.

**Pre-measure:** H(Y) maximal under constraints (maximum uncertainty)

**Constraint:** No Mb ⇒ no selection (no measurement means no choice is made)

**Structure:** ∀b, outcomes latent (for every possible basis, all outcomes are still possible)

**Interpretation:** "No decision" ≡ no basis selection

### 3. Dual Frames (Two Valid Perspectives)

Sections 1 and 2 give us two domains — one where all states coexist, one where they resolve one after another. These are not contradictions. They are different projections (viewpoints) of the same joint distribution (shared probability pattern), valid under their own algebras (math systems). Both true. Neither complete alone.

**Frames:** FΣF = global (all states co-present) · FΣD = local (sequential)

**Mapping:** π : ΣF → ΣD (a function that maps fundamental to derivative)

**Consistency:** TruthF ≠ TruthD — both valid under respective σ-algebras (both are true in their own math frameworks)

**Resolution:** Different projections of same joint distribution — no contradiction

### 4. Time (The Arrow)

Collapse (Section 1) produces irreversibility. Irreversibility produces ordering. Ordering is time. Time does not exist in the fundamental space — it emerges when measurement forces transitions into a sequence. The arrow of time is the entropy gradient (the slope of increasing disorder) created by collapse.

**Define:** ΔS = S(t + Δt) - S(t) (change in entropy over a time step)

**Arrow:** ΔS ≥ 0 in ΣD (entropy always increases or stays the same in our universe)

**Event:** Transition induces ordering: t0 < t1 < ... < tn (events get arranged in sequence)

**Interpretation:** Time ≡ ordered information increase — entropy gradient

## Phase II — Structure + Limits

### 5. Symmetry Breaking (The Fracture)

If collapse created time and irreversibility (Sections 1, 4), then the derivative space ΣD is not the original reality — it is the broken-symmetry phase (the version after the original symmetry was broken). The laws we measure are effective laws (rules that work in this broken version), valid only post-break. The coupling (connection) between spaces was severed at the transition.

**Model:** ΣD = broken-symmetry phase of ΣF

**Order parameter:** φ ≠ 0 after break (a measurable quantity becomes non-zero after the break)

**Coupling:** Corig → C0 — severance (the original connection gets cut down to zero)

**Observation:** Laws in ΣD = effective — post-break (the rules we see are only the rules for the broken version)

### 6. Triadic Closure (The Minimum Structure)

The substrate (underlying reality) requires a generator (source), a state (what exists), and a relation (connection between them). Two components cannot close over transformations (they can't complete their own changes) — the system leaks. Three is the minimum for self-consistent evolution (change that doesn't break itself). This is a structural claim about closure, not a theological assertion.

**Require:** Generator (G), State (S), Relation (R)

**Binary fails:** {G, S} lacks closure over transformations (two parts can't complete the loop)

**Triad:** T = (G, S, R) ⇒ Closure(T) → stable dynamics (three parts can close the loop and create stable change)

**Claim:** Minimal structure for self-consistent evolution ≥ 3

### 7. Observer Constraints (Not Consciousness)

Collapse requires a measurement operator (Section 1), but the operator is not consciousness. The observer selects the basis (the framework) — the system selects the outcome (the result). Information extracted is bounded by the interaction (limited by how they connect), not by the observer's awareness. This constrains all claims about "consciousness causes collapse."

**Measurement:** outcome ~ p(y | b, interaction) (the result depends on the basis and the interaction)

**Not required:** human consciousness ∉ necessary set (human awareness isn't needed)

**Observer role:** selects b, not yi (the observer picks the framework, not the result)

**Information:** I(outcome; system) constrained by interaction, not intent (what you can learn depends on how you interact, not what you intend)

If the fundamental frame (Section 3) holds all states co-present, then a choice made "later" in the derivative frame can condition statistics "earlier" (affect the probabilities of past events) — without violating causality (the rule that cause comes before effect). This is not backward causation. It is an update of the joint distribution across frames. The dual-frame structure predicted this.

**Experiment:** Choice at t2 conditions statistics at t1 (a later choice affects earlier probabilities)

**Formal:** p(y | choicet2) ≠ p(y) marginally (the probability changes when you account for the later choice)

**Constraint:** No signal to past — no causal violation (you can't send a message backward in time)

**Interpretation:** Update of joint distribution, not backward causation

**States:** C0 = sparse, mediated, fragile · C1 = distributed, direct, stable

**Dynamics:** Same ΣF, ΣD — different C ⇒ different observables (same parts, different connections, different results)

**Conclusion:** One system, regime-dependent behavior

Sections 1–9 describe the architecture. This section describes the repair. If the substrate broke through symmetry-breaking (Section 5), then restoration requires the reverse sequence — localization into the broken phase, coherent stabilization under its constraints, release of accumulated energy, and irreversible update of the coupling constant. **Why:** Stabilization accumulates potential across the substrate boundary. Release converts it into the coupling update.

**Why:** This is the point of irreversibility. The old coupling constant is replaced. The system cannot return to C0.

**Step 5 — Propagation:** C1 ⊗ ΣD — new coupling available across derivative space

**Why:** The update is not local. C1 propagates to all entangled subsystems in ΣD. Activation still requires Q = 1 (Section χ).

**Irreversible:** cost → ∞ in volume limit (you can't undo it, the cost becomes infinite)

**Observable:** ΔC visible only in cross-substrate processes (the change in coupling only shows up in processes that cross between substrates)
