### The Institutional Hemorrhage

By 1965, Mainline church membership started to drop in real numbers. It went from 31 million members in 1965 down to 25 million by 1988. This happened while the overall population was growing. That's a huge reversal. This wasn't just a slow drift. It was an institutional hemorrhage (a massive, unstoppable loss).

The moment when America's elite stopped believing in traditional Protestant morality lined up almost perfectly with the moment they stopped using words about virtue. The cultural gatekeepers (the people who shape what society thinks and talks about) stopped speaking the language of virtue in 1962. Then they stopped going to the institutions that taught virtue in 1965.

- 1955: ~70%

- 1965: 31 million peak
- 1988: 25 million

## 5.2 The Psychological Pivot: From Anxiety to Medication (1963)

"The population began to manage the stress of the collapsing social order not through ritual or community (which were declining) but through **chemistry**."

The 1950s were called the "Age of Anxiety." But back then, people saw anxiety through a psychoanalytic (talking about your childhood) or existential (what does life mean?) lens. It was a burden you had to carry or analyze. The *medicalization* (turning a normal human experience into a medical problem) of this state was a huge shift in how culture handled distress.

The threshold for this change is marked by the introduction of **Valium (diazepam) in 1963**. It was marketed as a solution to the "psychic tension" of modern life. Valium sales skyrocketed. It became the most prescribed drug in the United States between 1969 and 1982. It peaked in 1978 with 2.3 billion pills sold.

### From Stoic Endurance to Pharmacological Management

This 1963 threshold (which happened at the same time as the SAT peak and Mainline stagnation) signals the move from "stoic endurance" (a virtue ethic) to "pharmacological management" (a therapeutic ethic). The population began to manage the stress of the collapsing social order not through ritual or community (which were declining) but through chemistry.

The main cultural ailment would later shift from "Anxiety" to "Depression" in the 1980s. But the *mechanism* of managing the self changed decisively in 1963.
