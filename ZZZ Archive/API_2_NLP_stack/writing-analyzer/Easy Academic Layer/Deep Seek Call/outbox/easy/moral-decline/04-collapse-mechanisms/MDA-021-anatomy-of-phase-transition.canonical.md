Union membership at 34.8%. The gold standard tied money to something real. Personal savings rates were high. Home ownership was growing through programs (VA loans, FHA) that built wealth rather than draining it.

## What Made It Work (The Physics)

Let's rewind. I want to name what was actually happening using the language of the equation, because this isn't nostalgia — it's thermodynamics (the science of how energy and order flow in a system).

Like heat flowing from hot to cold — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system. That's thermodynamics. In any coherent (well-ordered) system, you need three things working together:

### First: A Strong Negentropic Source (G)

Something that continuously pumps order into the system against the natural drift toward disorder (entropy). In America's peak window, that source was spread across multiple reinforcing institutions — the church provided moral order, the family provided relational order, the legal system provided behavioral order, the economic system provided material order. These weren't separate. They were _coupled_ (linked together). The church reinforced the family. The family reinforced civic participation. Civic participation reinforced trust in institutions. Trust reinforced economic stability. A positive feedback loop of coherence.

### Second: High Observer Receptivity (O)

The population was tuned in to the signal. Church attendance wasn't just a social habit — it was a weekly reset. Families ate dinner together. Neighbors knew each other's names. The observer function (the ability to receive and respond to the coherence signal) was high because the social setup _demanded_ engagement with the sources of order.

### Third: Managed Entropy (S)

Disorder never goes to zero — that's the second law of thermodynamics. But in a well-functioning system, disorder is absorbed, processed, and released before it builds up past the breaking point. Social deviance existed in the 1940s and 1950s. But it was contained by institutional response — not perfectly, not justly in every case, but structurally. The system had ways to handle disorder without being destroyed by it.

The equation says this setup produces rising coherence: dC/dt > 0. That means the change in coherence over time is positive. And that's what the data shows. From 1900 to 1958, in every measurable area, the trend lines moved toward greater integration.

## What Was Already Fragile

Here's where I need to push back against the "good old days" reading. Because the equation also reveals something that story misses: the system was _brittle_.

High coherence held up by outside enforcement — rather than by internal belief — is structurally weak. Think of it like a crystal: highly ordered, but one sharp hit in the right place shatters the whole thing.

Three specific weaknesses were already there in the peak window:

1

### Conformity as a Substitute for Conviction

Much of the behavioral order of the 1940s and 1950s was held up by social pressure, not by genuine moral growth. People went to church because that's what you did. People stayed married because divorce carried shame. When the outside pressure was removed — as it would be, catastrophically, in 1968-1973 — the behavioral order collapsed almost overnight. That speed of collapse tells you the internal order was lower than the outside measurements suggested.

In equation terms: the observed C (coherence) was artificially inflated by institutional enforcement. The _actual_ C — the coherence held by individual conviction — was lower. The gap between observed and actual coherence is the measure of the system's fragility.

2

### Justice Deficits as Entropy Seeds

Jim Crow. Gender subordination. The systematic exclusion of entire groups from the coherence system didn't just harm those groups — it planted seeds of disorder in the system itself. A moral system that says "all men are created equal" while enforcing racial hierarchy contains an internal contradiction. That contradiction is stored disorder, waiting for a trigger to release.

Like a shattered glass — you can't unshatter it, and you can't hide the pieces. Every shard is still there, accounted for. What broke stays broken until someone comes with glue from outside. That's the weak force analogy for moral conservation. The civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s was, in this framework, not the cause of moral decline but the _exposure_ of a disorder reservoir that had been building for generations. The system had to process this disorder. The question was whether it could do so without falling apart. The answer, as we'll see in Part 4, was no — because the disorder released by fixing injustice happened at the same time as disorder released from many other sources.

3

### The Single-Signal Problem

Almost all of the order input came through a single cultural channel: Protestant Christianity filtered through American civic religion. There was no backup signal. No backup plan. No second source of order that could keep the system going if the main source weakened. When church attendance started falling, there was nothing to catch the system.

Like a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal. That's the information / Logos analogy. In information theory terms: the system had no error correction. A single-source signal with no backup is maximally vulnerable to noise. When the noise came — and it came from every direction at once — the signal was overwhelmed.
