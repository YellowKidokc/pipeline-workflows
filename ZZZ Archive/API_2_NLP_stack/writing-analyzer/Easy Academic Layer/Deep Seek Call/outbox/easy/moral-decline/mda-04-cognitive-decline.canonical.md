## 4.2 Grade Inflation and the Loss of Standards

### Fiat Excellence

While objective test scores (like the SAT) were going down, something strange was happening with grades. Grades were going up. This is called "grade inflation," and it started in the **mid-1960s**.

Before the mid-60s, the average college GPA (grade point average) was about 2.5, which is a C+. Starting in the mid-60s, GPAs began to rise steadily. The rise sped up during the Vietnam War era, partly because professors gave higher grades to protect male students from being drafted into the military.

So here's the picture: objective scores (SATs) were falling, but subjective rewards (grades) were rising. We moved from "Excellence defined from the outside" (Did you get the answer right?) to "Affirmation defined from the inside" (How do you feel about your effort?).

This was the birth of **Fiat Standards**. Think of it like this: we stopped measuring the "Physical Reality" of what a student could actually do, and started printing "Paper Excellence" instead. The educational system stopped being a filter that sorted students by ability. It became a machine for making students feel good about themselves.

### The Great Divergence: Objective vs. Subjective

#### Falling — Objective Capability

SAT Verbal: 478 in 1963 → 424 in 1980

SAT Math: 502 in 1963 → 466 in 1980

#### Rising — Subjective Validation

College GPA: ~2.5 before 1965 → steady rise after

Self-Esteem replaced Virtue as the goal. External standards were replaced by internal validation.

Source: College Board SAT Historical Data; Wirtz Commission (1977)

This split between falling objective scores (SATs down) and rising subjective rewards (GPAs up) marks the beginning of what some call the "culture of narcissism." It also marks the breakdown of objective standards. This is the semantic shift from "virtue" (excellence defined from the outside) to "self-esteem" (excellence defined from the inside). The educational system stopped being a filter for competence and became a mechanism for affirmation. This shift began in earnest around 1965.

### The Theophysics of the "Brain Drain"

When a society loses its "Thick" moral vocabulary — words like *Fortitude* (courage to do what's right even when it's hard) and *Prudence* (wisdom to make good decisions) — it loses the "Software" needed for high-level thinking. Rigorous thought requires the ability to tell yourself "No." It requires impulse control. The 1962 Semantic crash destroyed that impulse control.

Think of it like this: **Information / Logos** — "Like a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal." When we lost the thick moral vocabulary, we added static to the channel. The signal got garbled.

By 1963, the tools were rusting. We were entering an era where we would have more "experts" than ever before, but less actual ability to solve our problems.

**Critical Threshold Year (Pc): 1963**

SAT Peak / Start of Decline

The year the American mind began its long, subsidized retreat from reality.

### The Cognitive Cascade

**1962** — Semantic Threshold Crossed. Thick virtue terms (like Fortitude and Prudence) become critically marginalized. The "software" of moral reasoning is corrupted.

**1963** — SAT Scores Peak. Verbal: 478. Math: 502. This is the high-water mark. A 17-year decline begins immediately.

**Mid-1960s** — Grade Inflation Begins. Average college GPA of ~2.5 begins steady rise. Accelerates during Vietnam War era.

**~1965** — Affirmation Replaces Filtration. Education shifts from being a competence filter to a self-esteem mechanism. "Virtue" yields to "self-esteem."

**1977** — Wirtz Commission Confirms. A later analysis confirms that real deterioration in rigorous academic preparation contributed significantly to the slide.

**1980** — SAT Trough. Verbal: 424. Math: 466. End of 17-year decline. The damage is done.
