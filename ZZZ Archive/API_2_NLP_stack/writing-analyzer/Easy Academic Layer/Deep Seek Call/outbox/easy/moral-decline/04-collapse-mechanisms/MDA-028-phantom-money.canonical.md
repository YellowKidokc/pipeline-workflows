# Phantom Money, Phantom Morals

## Baseline: 1971 (The Last Anchored Moment)

On August 15, 1971, President Nixon cut the dollar loose from gold. This one decision sent shockwaves through every American institution that had been built on the idea that money had *stable value*.

**Before 1971:**

- $1 USD = 1/35th of an ounce of gold
- Money represented *claims on something real*
- Inflation was held back by physical reality
- You couldn't borrow forever against a limited supply of gold

**Economic Coherence in 1971: 76**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Debt-to-GDP Ratio | 38% |
| Real Wage Growth | 2.1%/yr |
| Net Worth to Income | 4.2x |
| Corporate Profit Margins | 7.8% |
| Top 1% Wealth Share | 35% |
| Home Price to Income | 3.0x |

These numbers weren't perfect. But they were *held in check by reality*. You couldn't claim more value than you produced for very long — the gold standard forced a correction.

## The Severing: 1971–1980

When the gold standard broke, it wasn't just a money change. It was a **reality constraint removal**.

For the first time in American history, the government could create wealth *symbolically* instead of *productively*.

The Federal Reserve immediately started what it called "monetary accommodation." The rest of us call it: printing money to keep spending beyond what we produce.

**The Mechanism**

1. Government wants to spend more than it collects in taxes
2. Instead of raising taxes, it borrows from the Fed
3. The Fed creates new money (from nothing) to buy government bonds
4. That new money flows into the economy
5. Prices rise (inflation), but wages don't keep up
6. Net result: real wealth moves from wage-earners to asset-holders and the government

This isn't capitalism. It isn't socialism. It's **extraction through monetary deception**.

**By 1980:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Debt-to-GDP Ratio | 42% |
| Real Wages | Flat |
| Net Worth to Income | 4.1x |
| Top 1% Wealth Share | 37% |

Notice: the economy was growing on paper, but workers were *losing ground* even as productivity increased.

The last decade flipped the basic economic equation.

**Before Inversion (pre-1980):**
Productive work → real income → savings → real wealth
Risk: too much debt → correction → reset

**After Inversion (2014+):**
