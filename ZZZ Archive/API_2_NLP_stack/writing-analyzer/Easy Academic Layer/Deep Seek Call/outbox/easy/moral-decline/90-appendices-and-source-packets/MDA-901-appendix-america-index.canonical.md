# Critical Thresholds

## 1962 — Engel v. Vitale

The Supreme Court removes prayer from public schools. Within one generation, every FACTS metric starts going downhill.

## 1968 — Phase Transition Begins

SAT scores peak. Social trust starts dropping. The divorce rate speeds up. This is the turning point across many different datasets that don't depend on each other.

## 1971 — Nixon Shock

The dollar is cut loose from gold. Money loses its real backing. Real wages stop keeping up with productivity. Economic morality becomes a phantom — like heat flowing from hot to cold, you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system.

## 1973 — Roe v. Wade

The sanctity of life is redefined by a court decision. Moral consequence gets separated from personal choice by an official rule.

## 2008 — Financial Crisis & Smartphone Saturation

Trust in institutions collapses below the point where it can recover. Algorithms start capturing your attention. The observer — you — starts dissolving.

## A Note on Methodology

This series applies thermodynamic modeling to social data. This is not a metaphor. It's a structural match. When disorder (entropy) increases in a closed system, it does not reverse on its own. The same math that governs phase transitions in physics — ice turning to water, water turning to steam — maps exactly onto the observed social data: slow decay, then faster decay past critical thresholds, then no going back without energy from outside the system.

Every claim has a source. Every metric tracks over time. The FACTS framework was built to be testable: if any pillar showed recovery, the argument would weaken. None has.
