## 03 · The Patterns

### Gravity

Gravity is the weakest force in nature. It's a hundred thousand trillion trillion times weaker than electromagnetism. And yet it shaped every galaxy. It never spikes. It never rushes. It reaches everywhere. You can't block it. It doesn't push — it curves the path so that everything, if you stop fighting it, bends toward center.

Like the slope of a hill — you don't get pushed, the ground itself tilts so you naturally move toward the bottom. Grace changes the shape of the ground under your feet.

I thought: that's patience. Written in geometry. Not *like* patience. Patience.

### The Strong Force

The strong force is the strongest force in existence. The harder you try to pull two quarks (tiny particles inside protons and neutrons) apart, the stronger it gets. When you're close inside the bond — total freedom. But try to leave? The universe would rather create new matter than let the bond break.

Like a parent holding a child's hand — loose grip when you're walking together, iron grip when you try to run into traffic. The closer you stay, the more freedom you feel.

I thought: that's love.

### Light

Light sustains itself. It needs no medium, no carrier, no permission. A photon (a particle of light) from 13 billion years ago arrives with its original information intact. It travels at maximum speed. Nothing is faster. And you cannot refuse it — if it touches your retina, you see.

Like a flashlight in a dark room — it doesn't create what's there, it reveals what was always there. You can't have half-light. It either reaches the corner or it doesn't.

I thought: that's truth.

### Entropy

Entropy (the measure of disorder) is the universal tendency toward disorder. Things fall apart. Relationships fracture. Systems decay. Every closed system trends toward chaos. Nothing is exempt.

Like heat flowing from hot to cold — it only moves one direction, and you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system.

I thought: that's sin. Described by physics 3,000 years after Moses described it in words.
