# The Seven Questions: A Claim Becomes Inspectable When You Ask What Must Be True Underneath It

Every claim is like a tree. This method asks whether the roots can actually hold the tree up.

**David Lowe - FP-029 - POF 2828**

---

## 01 - The Question

### If a claim is true, what else must be true underneath it?

A claim is not just a sentence. It has hidden parts: assumptions, definitions, mechanisms, tests, limits, consequences, and failure conditions. Most arguments hide those layers.

The goal is not to make every claim sound smart. The goal is to make every claim inspectable (able to be looked at closely and tested).

— Faith Through Physics

The 7Q method refuses to hide the structure. It turns a claim around until every load-bearing surface (the parts that hold the claim up) is visible.

---

## 02 - Proof by Classification

### Inquiry becomes repeatable when the roots are named

**Q1 — Name the claim**  
Find the sentence you are actually trying to defend before anything else moves.

**Q2 — Expose assumptions**  
Ask what the claim already takes for granted about reality, language, and method.

**Q3 — Define the mechanism**  
Do not stop at labels. Ask how the claim is supposed to work.

**Q4 — Identify kill conditions**  
List the tests that would break the claim if they failed honestly.

The last three questions finish the job: what the claim produces, what survives inspection, and what identity the surviving structure earns. The sequence is a machine, not a slogan.
