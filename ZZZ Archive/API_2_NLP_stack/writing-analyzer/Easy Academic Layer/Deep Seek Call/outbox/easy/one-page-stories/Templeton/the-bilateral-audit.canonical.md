The 80% science owns only works because the 20% is true. That is the structural contradiction nobody names.

Before science takes its first measurement, it must borrow ten things it cannot prove with its own method. That the outside world exists. That it is orderly and can be known. That logic works. That is not a fair settlement. That is a structural contradiction. And it is the reason fifty years of Templeton funding has produced talk without resolution. You cannot have a real conversation between two sides when one side is using ideas it took from the other without saying so.

The Method

## Hold Both Sides to the Same Standard

The usual move in science-religion talk is to ask whether religious claims survive scientific testing. That is one direction. It is only half an audit. The full audit runs the other direction too.

Hold God accountable to science's own standards — falsifiability (can it be proven wrong?), predictive power (does it predict things before they happen?), explanatory scope (how much does it explain?), internal consistency (does it contradict itself?), reproducibility (can you get the same result again?). Run the full test. Do not protect theological claims from pressure.

Then hold science accountable to the same standard. Can it ground its own starting assumptions? Can it explain consciousness — not just map which brain parts light up, but explain why there is something it *feels like* to be a brain? Can it explain why abstract mathematics describes physical reality with precision that is almost ridiculous? Can it account for the fine-tuning of the universe's basic settings? Can it explain where information comes from, not just how it is processed once it exists?
