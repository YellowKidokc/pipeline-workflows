# The Salvation Algorithm: A Six-Link Test of Justice, Mercy, and Substitution

Grace does not cancel justice. It answers justice without letting the whole system fall apart.

## 01 — The Question

### If good and evil are real, what kind of world are we in?

This claim sounds simple to say, but it's hard to live by consistently. People protest when they're betrayed. They condemn cruelty. They demand justice the moment someone wrongs them. That reaction isn't just something we learn from society. It's a signal that moral reality is real and important.

If morality is real, then the universe contains more than just matter moving around. It contains accountability — the idea that we are responsible for our actions.

The salvation question starts here: Can justice stay justice if mercy walks into the room?

## 02 — Proof by Moral Weight

### Six links, one chain

This section keeps the chain short. It doesn't try to replace the full explanation. It only shows the pressure points that make the conclusion impossible to avoid.

**Link 1 — Moral weight**

We live as if cruelty, betrayal, and justice are real — not just optional preferences we can ignore.

**Link 2 — Jurisdiction**

A true judge cannot dismiss guilt by pretending the wrong never happened. Like heat flowing from hot to cold — you can't make it go backwards without spending energy from outside the system. (This is the Thermodynamics / Judgment analogy: judgment only moves one direction, and you can't undo it on your own.)

**Link 3 — Substitution**

If someone else bears the cost, mercy can appear without erasing the offense. Like a shattered glass — you can't unshatter it, and you can't hide the pieces. Every shard is still there, accounted for. What broke stays broken until someone comes with glue from outside. (This is the Weak Force / Moral Conservation analogy.)

**Link 4 — Outside rescue**

A closed system cannot generate the term it needs to heal itself from decay. Like a radio signal through static — the clearer the channel, the more of the message gets through. Sin is the static. Truth is the signal. (This is the Information / Logos analogy.)

That is the chain in compact form: justice is real, guilt is real, mercy must not lie, and rescue must come from outside the system that failed.

## 03 — The Answer

### Grace meets justice without collapsing either one

The Gospel is not an escape from justice. It is justice and mercy meeting without collapse.
